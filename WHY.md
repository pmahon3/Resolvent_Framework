## The question

> **When do local views, each correct as far as it goes, add up to one world? And when they
> don't, what are they still telling us?**

Nearly all knowing is local: one instrument, one survey, one experiment, one perspective, one
agreement at a time. "The world", or "an order", is something assembled from those views. The
theory asks when that assembly is guaranteed and what it means when it fails. Its stance, *answers
first*, means no ground is presupposed. Questions and consistent local answers come first, and a
single world or a single centre is something they have to earn.

## What lights the fire

**Ecology and anarchism.** Both are about the same thing: **how order arises from local
relations, with no centre holding it together.**

- **Hierarchies are trees, and trees always glue.** Local agreements arranged as a chain of command
  always add up to one global order. That is why hierarchies look coherent: structurally, they
  can't fail to be.
- **Loops are where order without a centre lives.** In webs, federations and mutual relations,
  local arrangements can be fully coherent while no single global point stands behind them.
  Pointless coherence is, structurally, order without a sovereign.
- **The twist lives in the loop, not the action.** What makes such an order possible is the shape
  of the relations, not control or motion.
- **Steps compose.** Local agreements can be pairwise fine and still fail to add up, because of how
  commitments chain together, not because anyone contradicts anyone.

## Bridges into the world

- **Ecology:**
  - *Intransitive competition* (A beats B, B beats C, C beats A) sustains biodiversity, because no
    species wins globally. It is Penrose's staircase as a living system.
  - *Panarchy* (Holling): nested cycles across scales, and how fast and slow processes compose.
  - *Partial observation:* do local plots and surveys add up to one picture?
- **Anarchism and the commons:**
  - Kropotkin, *Mutual Aid* (naturalist and anarchist): cooperation from local relations as a
    factor of evolution.
  - Ostrom, *Governing the Commons*: polycentric governance, overlapping local centres with no
    sovereign. Her centres are our windows.
  - Feyerabend's "epistemological anarchism".
- **Science and practice:**
  - *Reconstruction from observation:* delay embeddings, the programme's root.
  - *Belief propagation:* exact on trees; on loops, beliefs that agree locally may not come from
    any joint distribution.
  - *Condorcet cycles.*
  - *Quantum contextuality:* Specker, Kochen–Specker.
  - *Merging data:* pairwise-consistent tables are guaranteed to join only on acyclic schemas.

## Four questions to carry back into the world

1. Is there a loop in how my views connect? Without one, local agreement is enough.
2. Is there a joint question nobody can ask? Contradictions hide exactly there.
3. Am I relying on an idealisation, a limit or a "far beyond"? It can make the impossible look
   consistent.
4. When things fail to add up, is it a circle, or the way steps compose?

## Honest distance

The mathematics works with idealised infinities. With finitely many possibilities, the theory
itself says everything collapses to points. A practical bridge probably goes through probabilistic
and finite versions, where the tree/loop lesson still holds and is well studied. For now this is a
lens and a source of questions, not yet a tool to hand an ecologist. **Do not overclaim
practical relevance.**

## How the spark is kept

- The spark lives in *seeing*: puzzles, pictures, the moment a question means something. It does
  not live in proof output.
- Machines are the answer key at the back of the book. Do the seeing by hand first.
- Go at a human pace. Stop at checkpoints. When the fog comes in, orient before adding anything.
- Publication is not the goal. Understanding is, and so is being able to resurface in the world
  and look at it anew.

# Directedness Interpolation: A Conceptual Sketch

**Branch:** `directedness-interpolation`
**Status:** Open question — exploratory

---

## The current setup

The program's extension theorems rest on three axiom classes for a query system
`(ι, Q, π, ℓ)`:

| Axiom | Type | Role |
|-------|------|------|
| Sequential upper-directedness (SUD) | Structural (on ι) | Existence: ensures premeasures are well-defined, σ-subadditivity propagates upward |
| Lower-directedness (LCD) | Structural (on ι) | Uniqueness: π-system separation, determination of global P |
| Collective exhaustion (CE) | Valuation-layer (on ℓ) | The irreducible existence gap; no structural condition implies it |

The current program takes SUD and CE as the essential hypotheses for existence,
and LCD (or a weaker π-system condition) for uniqueness.

---

## The asymmetry

- **Upper-directed systems** can be refined consistently: for any two queries
  `i, j` there is a finer query `k ≥ i, j` whose marginals agree with both.
  This is the "onion-peeling" direction — you always have a strictly finer
  description available. It's what makes premeasures well-defined on the
  projective limit.

- **Lower-directed systems** admit common coarsenings: for any `i, j` there is
  `k ≤ i, j`. This lets you find shared π-system structure and pin down
  uniqueness — two measures agreeing on a π-system generating the σ-algebra must
  be equal.

- **CE** lives entirely on the existence/upper side. It is the condition that
  mass cannot persist in a "cofinite" tail — that the charges are witnessing
  actual events rather than a phantom at infinity. The finite-cofinite content
  (= ultraproduct of point masses) is the canonical witness of its failure.

---

## The open question

**Can a single joint condition `MI(ι)` on the index system replace both SUD and LCD
as primitives, while delivering the same existence + uniqueness conclusions?**

More precisely: is there a condition `MI(ι)` ("mutual aid") such that:

1. `MI(ι)` is strictly weaker than `SUD(ι) ∧ LCD(ι)` — it does not require
   either directedness assumption alone.
2. `MI(ι) + CE(ℓ)` → unique global measure `P` on `Ω`.
3. (Stronger hope) `MI(ι)` is rich enough that CE becomes structurally
   expressible in its language — i.e., CE is no longer an independent valuation
   axiom but follows from `MI(ι)` alone.

---

## Why this is non-trivial

CE irreducibility (Prop 1.19) shows that no first-order condition in the current
language of query systems implies CE. The argument:

- Step 1: first-order conditions are preserved under ultraproducts (Łoś).
- Step 2: the finite-cofinite content is the ultraproduct of point masses — every
  point mass satisfies CE, but their ultraproduct does not.
- Step 3: therefore no first-order condition separates the point masses from their
  ultraproduct, so none can imply CE.

**If MI is first-order expressible**, the same argument applies: MI holds for each
`δ_n`, so it holds for their ultraproduct `ℓ`, which fails CE. So CE remains
independent of MI.

**If MI is not first-order** — e.g., if it involves a compactness or covering
condition that fails for ultraproducts — the argument breaks down. This is the
only avenue for relocating CE from valuation-layer to structural.

---

## Possible forms for MI

### Option A: Interpolation refinement
For any `i ≤ j` there exists `k` with `i ≤ k ≤ j` such that `π_{ki}` factors
"nicely" (e.g., as a composition of a lower and upper refinement step). This is
a graph-theoretic condition on the index system.

### Option B: Covering condition
Every finite cover of `Ω` (in a suitable sense) admits a refinement that is
"compatible" with both upper and lower structure. This is closer to a
compactness/topological condition and likely not first-order.

### Option C: Bisimulation-style
Two queries `i` and `j` "mutually refine" if there is a query `k` such that
`π_{ki}` and `π_{kj}` are both surjective. This is purely structural and
first-order.

---

## Key questions to resolve

1. **Does any MI variant deliver existence without SUD?**
   The existence proof in Paper 0 uses SUD to build the Kolmogorov-style
   projective limit. Does MI provide enough "upward reach" to substitute?

2. **Does any MI variant deliver uniqueness without LCD?**
   Uniqueness uses the π-system generated by lower-directed coarsenings.
   Does MI give access to a comparable π-system?

3. **Is MI itself first-order?**
   If yes: CE remains independent (by irreducibility). CE is not relocated.
   If no: the irreducibility argument has a gap. CE might become structural.

4. **Is there a known categorical analogue?**
   In category theory, "bifibrations" or "Conduché fibrations" over a base
   encode both upward and downward structure simultaneously. Could the query
   system be modelled as a bifibration, with MI being a condition on the
   bifibration structure?

---

## Relationship to CE irreducibility

If MI is first-order: the current program's conclusion stands. CE is an
irreducible valuation-layer axiom, the gap between structural coherence and
measurability is permanent, and MI only consolidates the structural hypotheses
without closing the gap.

If MI is not first-order: this opens the possibility that the gap is an artefact
of the first-order restriction on our language, not a fundamental boundary. The
philosophical consequence would be significant: collective exhaustion would be
reconceived not as a primitive observer commitment but as a derived consequence
of sufficiently rich structural interaction.

---

## Disposition of the three options

### Option C: closed
Option C (bisimulation-style MI) is first-order expressible. By CE irreducibility
(Prop 1.19), no first-order condition can imply CE. So Option C cannot relocate
CE to the structural layer. This avenue is closed without further work.

### Option A: weak
Option A (interpolation refinement) is also first-order: it speaks only about the
existence of intermediate indices with factored maps. Same closure applies.

### Option B: the live direction
A **covering condition** — requiring that every cover of a query level admits a
compatible refinement visible to both upper and lower structure — is inherently
infinitary. It cannot be expressed in first-order logic over query systems because
it quantifies over infinite families of subsets. This breaks the ultraproduct
argument: a covering condition can hold in every `δ_n` but fail in the
ultraproduct `ℓ`, because ultraproducts do not generally preserve covering axioms
(they preserve only first-order sentences).

## The Grothendieck topology connection

A **Grothendieck topology** on a category `C` assigns to each object `U` a
collection `J(U)` of "covering sieves" — families of morphisms into `U` that
jointly cover it. The axioms are:

- **Maximality**: the identity sieve covers.
- **Stability**: pullbacks of covering sieves cover.
- **Transitivity**: a sieve that locally covers a cover is itself a cover.

These are not first-order in the usual sense — they quantify over sieves
(infinite families of morphisms) and require closure under pullback.

**The proposal**: model the query system as a category, with queries as objects
and refinement maps as morphisms. Define a Grothendieck topology `J` on this
category by declaring that a family of refinements `{π_{ki} : k → i}` covers `i`
if the induced maps on measure spaces jointly determine the charge on level `i`.
The "mutual aid" condition `MI(ι)` is then the requirement that `J` is a
**subcanonical topology** — every representable presheaf is a sheaf.

**Why this matters for CE**: In a sheaf-theoretic setting, collective exhaustion
is the condition that the "point at infinity" (the ultrafilter observer) does not
appear as a sheaf-section. A subcanonical topology rules out exactly those
"phantom sections" that have no geometric realisation. This is the structural
analogue of CE.

**The key question**: Is the subcanonical condition equivalent to CE in this
setting? If so, CE is not an independent axiom but the sheaf-completeness
condition for the natural topology on the query system.

# Prokhorov-Type Extension Theorem for Query Systems

*2026-03-17*

---

## Context and motivation

The previous notes (`conjecture2b_progress.md`) established that:

- Compatibility is a transport principle — it cannot generate CFA.
- The refinement maps $\pi$ are inert with respect to *continuity from above*, since preimage preserves decreasing intersections exactly — but they remain essential for transporting laws and making the compatible-family framework coherent.
- ECA is near-circular.
- The correct forcing condition must be a tightness condition on the valuations $\{\ell_Q\}$.

The correct next theorem is therefore a **Prokhorov-type extension**: a projective tightness condition on the family $\{\ell_Q\}$ forces the existence of a global $\sigma$-additive probability measure $P$ on $(\Omega, \sigma(\mathcal{Q}))$ with $(\mathrm{eval}_Q)_\# P = \ell_Q$ for every $Q$.

This is the abstract Prokhorov/Kolmogorov extension problem in query-system language.

---

## Setup

Let $(\mathcal{Q}, \preceq)$ be a countable sequentially upper-directed query system. Each $Q$ has outcome space $(O_Q, \mathcal{B}(O_Q))$ with $O_Q$ Hausdorff. Refinement maps $\pi^{Q_2}_{Q_1}: O_{Q_2} \to O_{Q_1}$ are continuous for $Q_1 \preceq Q_2$.

The **canonical state space** is:
$$\Omega := \left\{ \omega \in \prod_{Q \in \mathcal{Q}} O_Q \;\middle|\; \pi^{Q_2}_{Q_1}(\omega_{Q_2}) = \omega_{Q_1} \text{ for all } Q_1 \preceq Q_2 \right\}$$
with evaluation maps $\mathrm{eval}_Q : \Omega \to O_Q$, $\omega \mapsto \omega_Q$.

Since the $\pi$ maps are continuous and each $O_Q$ is Hausdorff, each constraint set $\{\omega : \pi^{Q_2}_{Q_1}(\omega_{Q_2}) = \omega_{Q_1}\}$ is closed in $\prod_Q O_Q$ (preimage of the diagonal, which is closed in Hausdorff $O_{Q_1}$, under a continuous map). Since $\mathcal{Q}$ is countable, $\Omega$ is a countable intersection of closed sets, hence closed in the product.

The **cylinder algebra** $\mathcal{C}$ is generated by sets $\mathrm{Cyl}(Q, A) := \mathrm{eval}_Q^{-1}(A)$ for $Q \in \mathcal{Q}$, $A \in \mathcal{B}(O_Q)$. The **cylinder premeasure** is $\mu_0(\mathrm{Cyl}(Q, A)) := \ell_Q(A)$, well-defined and finitely additive on $\mathcal{C}$ by finite compatibility.

**Goal:** show that under projective uniform tightness, $\mu_0$ is $\sigma$-additive on $\mathcal{C}$, so Carathéodory extends it to a $\sigma$-additive measure on $\sigma(\mathcal{C}) = \sigma(\mathcal{Q})$.

---

## The classical model: Prokhorov's theorem

**Classical Prokhorov.** Let $\{\mu_n\}$ be Borel probability measures on a Polish space $X$. If the family is **tight** — for every $\varepsilon > 0$, compact $K$ with $\mu_n(X \setminus K) < \varepsilon$ for all $n$ — then $\{\mu_n\}$ is relatively compact in the weak topology.

The key mechanism: tightness confines mass to compact sets, and on compact sets the cylinder sets have the finite intersection property.

---

## The definition: surjective projective uniform tightness

**Preliminary remark.** The valuations $\{\ell_Q\}$ live on *different* spaces $O_Q$, so classical tightness cannot apply literally. The correct formulation must be projective: compact approximants must be chosen compatibly across levels so they assemble into a compact subset of $\Omega$. Crucially, the bonding maps must be *surjective* on the compact sets — containment alone is insufficient for the inverse limit to have nonempty projections.

**Definition (Surjective projective uniform tightness).** The family $\{\ell_Q\}_{Q \in \mathcal{Q}}$ is **surjectively projectively uniformly tight** if for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists a system $\{K_Q\}_{Q \in \mathcal{Q}}$ with:
1. $K_Q \subset O_Q$ compact for each $Q$,
2. $\pi^{Q_2}_{Q_1}(K_{Q_2}) = K_{Q_1}$ for all $Q_1 \preceq Q_2$ (surjectivity of bonding maps on compact sets),
3. $\ell_Q(O_Q \setminus K_Q) < \varepsilon$ for every $Q$.

**Why surjectivity is necessary, not just containment.** Condition (2) with $=$ rather than $\subseteq$ is required for the inverse limit theorem: the inverse limit of compact Hausdorff spaces with surjective bonding maps is nonempty with surjective projections. Without surjectivity on the compact sets, the inverse limit $K_\Omega$ can be empty or have projections that fail to hit a given point, blocking the key lifting step in the proof. See the proof below for exactly where this is used.

**Relation to individual tightness (Attempt 1).** Individual tightness asked for compact sets at each $Q$ independently. Surjective projective uniform tightness adds: the compact sets must be chosen consistently *with surjective bonding maps*, so they assemble into a nonempty compact $K_\Omega \subset \Omega$ with surjective evaluation maps $\mathrm{eval}_Q: K_\Omega \to K_Q$.

$$\text{Individual tightness} + \text{surjective projective compatibility} = \text{surjective projective uniform tightness.}$$

## The intermediate theorem

**Theorem (Step (a) — $\sigma$-subadditivity on closed cylinders).** Let $\{\ell_Q\}$ be finitely compatible and surjectively projectively uniformly tight on a countable query system with continuous refinement maps and Hausdorff outcome spaces. Then for every decreasing sequence of **closed** cylinder sets $C_N = \mathrm{Cyl}(Q_N, F_N) \downarrow \emptyset$ (with each $F_N \subseteq O_{Q_N}$ closed):
$$\mu_0(C_N) \to 0.$$

---

## Proof

**Assume for contradiction** that $\mu_0(C_N) \geq \varepsilon > 0$ for all $N$.

**Step 1: Compact core.** Apply surjective projective uniform tightness with $\delta = \varepsilon/2$: obtain $\{K_Q\}$ with $K_Q$ compact, $\pi^{Q_2}_{Q_1}(K_{Q_2}) = K_{Q_1}$ for all $Q_1 \preceq Q_2$, and $\ell_Q(O_Q \setminus K_Q) < \varepsilon/2$ for all $Q$.

Define:
$$K_\Omega := \left\{ \omega \in \Omega \;\middle|\; \omega_Q \in K_Q \text{ for all } Q \right\} = \Omega \cap \prod_Q K_Q.$$

**Step 2: $K_\Omega$ is compact and nonempty with surjective projections.**

*Compact:* $\prod_Q K_Q$ is compact by Tychonoff. $\Omega$ is closed in $\prod_Q O_Q$ (shown above). So $K_\Omega$ is a closed subset of a compact set, hence compact.

*Nonempty with surjective projections:* By condition (2), the system $(K_Q, \pi^{Q_2}_{Q_1}|_{K_{Q_2}})$ is an inverse system of nonempty compact Hausdorff spaces with surjective bonding maps. By the **inverse limit theorem** (standard: inverse limit of compact Hausdorff spaces with surjective bonding maps over a directed set is nonempty, and all projections are surjective), $K_\Omega = \varprojlim K_Q$ is nonempty and each $\mathrm{eval}_Q: K_\Omega \to K_Q$ is surjective. $\square$

**Step 3: Each $C_N \cap K_\Omega$ is nonempty.**

$\mu_0(C_N) = \ell_{Q_N}(F_N) \geq \varepsilon$ and $\ell_{Q_N}(K_{Q_N}) \geq 1 - \varepsilon/2$, so:
$$\ell_{Q_N}(F_N \cap K_{Q_N}) \geq \varepsilon - \varepsilon/2 = \varepsilon/2 > 0.$$
In particular $F_N \cap K_{Q_N} \neq \emptyset$. Since $\mathrm{eval}_{Q_N}: K_\Omega \to K_{Q_N}$ is surjective (Step 2), there exists $\omega \in K_\Omega$ with $\omega_{Q_N} \in F_N \cap K_{Q_N}$. So $\omega \in C_N \cap K_\Omega$. $\square$

**Step 4: Each $C_N \cap K_\Omega$ is closed in $K_\Omega$.**

$C_N = \mathrm{eval}_{Q_N}^{-1}(F_N)$ with $F_N$ closed and $\mathrm{eval}_{Q_N}$ continuous (restriction of a projection). So $C_N$ is closed in $\Omega$, and $C_N \cap K_\Omega$ is closed in $K_\Omega$. $\square$

**Step 5: Contradiction by compactness.**

$\{C_N \cap K_\Omega\}$ is a decreasing sequence of nonempty closed subsets of the compact space $K_\Omega$. By the finite intersection property:
$$\bigcap_N (C_N \cap K_\Omega) \neq \emptyset.$$
But $\bigcap_N C_N = \emptyset$ (since $C_N \downarrow \emptyset$ in $\Omega$), so $\bigcap_N (C_N \cap K_\Omega) \subseteq \bigcap_N C_N = \emptyset$. Contradiction. $\square$

Hence $\mu_0(C_N) \to 0$.

## Hypothesis dependency map

Every hypothesis in the full theorem is used, and each is used in exactly one place:

| Hypothesis | Where used | Can it be weakened? |
|---|---|---|
| $\mathcal{Q}$ countable | Step (b): countably many defect sets; sublemma: common refinements for finitely many cylinders | Could be replaced by "countable determining subsystem" for step (b); sublemma only needs finite families |
| Sequentially upper-directed | Sublemma only: finite intersections of closed cylinders need common refinement $Q_* \succeq Q_1, Q_2$ | Needed for the Borel extension; not used elsewhere |
| Continuous refinement maps | $\Omega$ closed in product; $G_{Q_1,Q_2}$ Borel; closed cylinders closed in $\Omega$ | Essential throughout |
| Hausdorff outcome spaces | Diagonal closed → $\Omega$ closed; $G_{Q_1,Q_2}$ closed | Essential throughout |
| Surjective bonding maps on $K_Q$ | Step 2 only: inverse limit theorem requires surjectivity for nonempty $K_\Omega$ with surjective projections | The critical hypothesis; cannot be weakened to containment |
| Compact $K_Q$ | Steps 2–4: Tychonoff, Alexandrov, FIP | Essential |
| $\ell_Q(O_Q \setminus K_Q) < \varepsilon$ | Step 3: mass estimate $\ell_{Q_N}(F_N \cap K_{Q_N}) \geq \varepsilon/2$ | Essential |
| Finite compatibility | Definition of $\mu_0$; graph-support lemma; $P$ has correct marginals | Essential |

**Sequential upper-directedness is used only in the sublemma** (finite intersections of closed cylinders are closed cylinders). It is not needed for the core compactness argument or for concentration. This is a tight hypothesis.

## Comparison to classical Prokhorov

| Classical Prokhorov | Query-system analogue |
|---|---|
| Measures on a single Polish space | Compatible family on a countable directed query system |
| Tightness: compact $K \subset X$ with $\mu_n(K) \geq 1-\varepsilon$ | Surjective projective uniform tightness: compatible compact $K_Q$ with surjective bonding maps and $\ell_Q(K_Q) \geq 1-\varepsilon$ |
| Compact $K$ has FIP for decreasing closed sets | Compact $K_\Omega$ (inverse limit of $K_Q$) has FIP for decreasing closed cylinder sets |
| Weak limit exists | Global $\sigma$-additive $P$ on $\Omega$ exists |

The surjectivity of bonding maps is the query-system analogue of the classical fact that $K$ is a single space (automatically "surjective" over itself). It is the condition that makes the inverse limit behave like a single compact space.

## Extension to general Borel cylinders

The intermediate theorem proves $\mu_0(C_N) \to 0$ for decreasing sequences of *closed* cylinders. This section extends to all Borel cylinders.

**Key observation: no new hypotheses needed.** Each $K_Q$ is compact Hausdorff. By Alexandrov's theorem, every Borel probability measure on a compact Hausdorff space is regular: for every Borel $A \subseteq K_Q$ and $\eta > 0$ there exist closed $F \subseteq A$ and open $U \supseteq A$ (in $K_Q$) with $\ell_Q(A \setminus F) < \eta$ and $\ell_Q(U \setminus A) < \eta$. Regularity is automatic — it is not an additional hypothesis.

### Step 1: Mass outside the compact core is controlled

For any Borel cylinder $C = \mathrm{Cyl}(Q, A)$ and compact family $\{K_Q\}$ with $\ell_Q(O_Q \setminus K_Q) < \delta$:
$$\mu_0(C) = \ell_Q(A) = \ell_Q(A \cap K_Q) + \ell_Q(A \setminus K_Q) \leq \ell_Q(A \cap K_Q) + \delta.$$
So all mass accounting can be reduced to the compact cores, with error at most $\delta$.

### Step 2: Inner regularity on $K_Q$ approximates Borel by closed

Fix Borel $A \subseteq O_Q$. Since $\ell_Q|_{K_Q}$ is a regular Borel measure on the compact Hausdorff space $K_Q$ (by Alexandrov), for every $\eta > 0$ there exists closed $F \subseteq A \cap K_Q$ (closed in $K_Q$, hence compact) with:
$$\ell_Q(A \cap K_Q) - \ell_Q(F) < \eta.$$

Since $F$ is closed in $K_Q$ and $K_Q$ is closed in $O_Q$ (compact subset of Hausdorff), $F$ is closed in $O_Q$. So $\mathrm{Cyl}(Q, F)$ is a closed cylinder with:
$$\mu_0(\mathrm{Cyl}(Q,A)) - \mu_0(\mathrm{Cyl}(Q,F)) = \ell_Q(A) - \ell_Q(F) < \eta + \delta.$$

### Step 3: Approximation of general decreasing cylinder sequences

**Theorem (Borel cylinder extension).** Under the hypotheses of the intermediate theorem, $\mu_0(C_N) \to 0$ for every decreasing sequence of Borel cylinder sets $C_N \downarrow \emptyset$.

*Proof.* Let $C_N = \mathrm{Cyl}(Q_N, A_N) \downarrow \emptyset$ with each $A_N \in \mathcal{B}(O_{Q_N})$ and $\mu_0(C_N) \geq \varepsilon > 0$ for all $N$ (assume for contradiction).

Fix $\varepsilon' = \varepsilon/4$. Apply surjective projective uniform tightness with $\delta = \varepsilon' $: get $\{K_Q\}$ with $\ell_Q(O_Q \setminus K_Q) < \varepsilon'$ for all $Q$.

For each $N$, apply inner regularity on $K_{Q_N}$ with $\eta_N = \varepsilon' / 2^N$: find closed $F_N \subseteq A_N \cap K_{Q_N}$ (closed in $O_{Q_N}$) with:
$$\ell_{Q_N}(A_N \cap K_{Q_N}) - \ell_{Q_N}(F_N) < \varepsilon'/2^N.$$

Define closed cylinders $D_N := \mathrm{Cyl}(Q_N, F_N)$. Then:
$$\mu_0(D_N) = \ell_{Q_N}(F_N) \geq \ell_{Q_N}(A_N \cap K_{Q_N}) - \varepsilon'/2^N \geq \ell_{Q_N}(A_N) - \varepsilon' - \varepsilon'/2^N.$$
Since $\ell_{Q_N}(A_N) = \mu_0(C_N) \geq \varepsilon$:
$$\mu_0(D_N) \geq \varepsilon - \varepsilon' - \varepsilon'/2^N > \varepsilon - 2\varepsilon' = \varepsilon/2 > 0.$$

Now consider the sets $E_N := \bigcap_{k=1}^N D_k$. These are closed cylinders (finite intersections of closed cylinders reduce to a single closed cylinder via a common refinement, since $\mathcal{Q}$ is sequentially upper-directed) and decrease. Moreover:

$$\mu_0(E_N) = \mu_0\!\left(\bigcap_{k=1}^N D_k\right) \geq \mu_0\!\left(\bigcap_{k=1}^N C_k\right) - \sum_{k=1}^N \mu_0(C_k \setminus D_k).$$

Since $C_N$ is decreasing, $\bigcap_{k=1}^N C_k = C_N$, so $\mu_0(\bigcap_{k=1}^N C_k) = \mu_0(C_N) \geq \varepsilon$. And:
$$\sum_{k=1}^N \mu_0(C_k \setminus D_k) \leq \sum_{k=1}^\infty (\varepsilon' + \varepsilon'/2^k) = \varepsilon'(1 + 1) = 2\varepsilon' = \varepsilon/2.$$

Therefore $\mu_0(E_N) \geq \varepsilon - \varepsilon/2 = \varepsilon/2 > 0$ for all $N$.

The sets $E_N$ are closed cylinders (Step: finite intersections of closed cylinders are closed cylinders — shown below), they decrease, and $\mu_0(E_N) \geq \varepsilon/2$. Also $E_N \subseteq C_N \downarrow \emptyset$, so $E_N \downarrow \emptyset$. By the intermediate theorem (closed cylinder case), $\mu_0(E_N) \to 0$. Contradiction. $\square$

**Sublemma (finite intersections of closed cylinders are closed cylinders).** If $C_1 = \mathrm{Cyl}(Q_1, F_1)$ and $C_2 = \mathrm{Cyl}(Q_2, F_2)$ with $F_i$ closed, then $C_1 \cap C_2$ is a closed cylinder.

*Proof.* By sequential upper-directedness, find $Q_* \succeq Q_1, Q_2$. Then:
$$C_1 \cap C_2 = \mathrm{Cyl}\!\left(Q_*, (\pi^{Q_*}_{Q_1})^{-1}(F_1) \cap (\pi^{Q_*}_{Q_2})^{-1}(F_2)\right).$$
Each $(\pi^{Q_*}_{Q_i})^{-1}(F_i)$ is closed in $O_{Q_*}$ (continuous preimage of closed set). Finite intersections of closed sets are closed. $\square$

### Step 4: Extension to the full cylinder algebra via monotone class

The cylinder premeasure $\mu_0$ is now shown to be $\sigma$-subadditive (continuity from above at $\emptyset$) on all Borel cylinders. Since $\mu_0$ is already finitely additive on the cylinder algebra $\mathcal{C}$, $\sigma$-subadditivity implies $\sigma$-additivity.

By the **Carathéodory extension theorem**, $\mu_0$ extends uniquely to a $\sigma$-additive probability measure on $\sigma(\mathcal{C}) = \sigma(\mathcal{Q})$.

### Assessment of the extension

| Step | Status | Hypothesis used |
|---|---|---|
| Mass outside core controlled | ✓ | Surjective projective uniform tightness |
| Inner regularity on $K_Q$ | ✓ | **Automatic**: $K_Q$ compact Hausdorff → Alexandrov's theorem |
| Closed approximants $D_N$ with positive mass | ✓ | Regularity + tightness |
| Finite intersections of closed cylinders closed | ✓ | Continuous maps + sequential upper-directedness |
| $E_N \downarrow \emptyset$ with $\mu_0(E_N) \geq \varepsilon/2$ → contradiction | ✓ | Intermediate theorem (closed cylinder case) |
| Carathéodory extension | ✓ | $\sigma$-additivity of $\mu_0$ on $\mathcal{C}$ |

**Regularity is free.** The crucial observation is that no additional regularity hypothesis is needed: Alexandrov's theorem supplies regularity on each $K_Q$ automatically from compactness and Hausdorff. The hypotheses of the full theorem are exactly those of the intermediate theorem — no additions required for the Borel extension.

The proof of step (a) is now complete in full generality for countable $\mathcal{Q}$ and Borel cylinders.

## Step (b): Concentration on $\Omega$

Carathéodory extends $\mu_0$ to a $\sigma$-additive measure $P$ on the cylinder $\sigma$-algebra of the ambient product $\prod_Q O_Q$. The question is whether $P$ lives on $\Omega$ rather than merely on the ambient product.

**Goal:** $P(\Omega) = 1$, or equivalently $P\!\left(\prod_Q O_Q \setminus \Omega\right) = 0$.

### Decomposing the complement

$\Omega$ is defined by the coherence equations $x_{Q_1} = \pi^{Q_2}_{Q_1}(x_{Q_2})$ for all $Q_1 \preceq Q_2$. The complement is a union of **defect sets**:
$$D_{Q_1,Q_2} := \left\{ x \in \prod_Q O_Q \;\middle|\; x_{Q_1} \neq \pi^{Q_2}_{Q_1}(x_{Q_2}) \right\}, \quad Q_1 \preceq Q_2.$$

So $\prod_Q O_Q \setminus \Omega = \bigcup_{Q_1 \preceq Q_2} D_{Q_1,Q_2}$, and the goal reduces to showing $P(D_{Q_1,Q_2}) = 0$ for every comparable pair.

### The graph-support lemma

**Lemma (Graph support).** For every $Q_1 \preceq Q_2$, the joint distribution of $(\mathrm{eval}_{Q_1}, \mathrm{eval}_{Q_2})$ under $P$ is supported on the graph
$$G_{Q_1,Q_2} := \left\{ (a, b) \in O_{Q_1} \times O_{Q_2} \;\middle|\; a = \pi^{Q_2}_{Q_1}(b) \right\}.$$

*Proof.* The joint pushforward $(\mathrm{eval}_{Q_1}, \mathrm{eval}_{Q_2})_\# P$ is a measure on $O_{Q_1} \times O_{Q_2}$. It suffices to check its value on measurable rectangles $A \times B$, which generate $\mathcal{B}(O_{Q_1}) \otimes \mathcal{B}(O_{Q_2})$.

Since $Q_1 \preceq Q_2$, the two cylinders $\mathrm{Cyl}(Q_1, A)$ and $\mathrm{Cyl}(Q_2, B)$ both lift to a single cylinder over $Q_2$:
$$\mathrm{Cyl}(Q_1, A) \cap \mathrm{Cyl}(Q_2, B) = \mathrm{Cyl}\!\left(Q_2,\, (\pi^{Q_2}_{Q_1})^{-1}(A) \cap B\right).$$

By definition of $\mu_0$ and then $P$:
$$P(\mathrm{eval}_{Q_1}^{-1}(A) \cap \mathrm{eval}_{Q_2}^{-1}(B)) = \ell_{Q_2}\!\left((\pi^{Q_2}_{Q_1})^{-1}(A) \cap B\right).$$

The right-hand side is exactly the $(A \times B)$-mass of the pushforward of $\ell_{Q_2}$ under the map $b \mapsto (\pi^{Q_2}_{Q_1}(b),\, b)$:
$$(\mathrm{graph}_{\pi})_\# \ell_{Q_2}(A \times B) = \ell_{Q_2}\!\left(\{b : \pi^{Q_2}_{Q_1}(b) \in A,\; b \in B\}\right) = \ell_{Q_2}\!\left((\pi^{Q_2}_{Q_1})^{-1}(A) \cap B\right).$$

Since rectangles generate the product $\sigma$-algebra, the joint law of $(\mathrm{eval}_{Q_1}, \mathrm{eval}_{Q_2})$ under $P$ equals $(\mathrm{graph}_\pi)_\# \ell_{Q_2}$, which is entirely supported on $G_{Q_1,Q_2}$. $\square$

**Corollary.** $P(D_{Q_1,Q_2}) = 0$ for every $Q_1 \preceq Q_2$.

*Proof.* $D_{Q_1,Q_2} = (\mathrm{eval}_{Q_1}, \mathrm{eval}_{Q_2})^{-1}(O_{Q_1} \times O_{Q_2} \setminus G_{Q_1,Q_2})$. The set $O_{Q_1} \times O_{Q_2} \setminus G_{Q_1,Q_2}$ has zero mass under $(\mathrm{graph}_\pi)_\# \ell_{Q_2}$, which equals the joint law of $(\mathrm{eval}_{Q_1}, \mathrm{eval}_{Q_2})$. $\square$

*Remark.* Note that $G_{Q_1,Q_2}$ is a Borel subset of $O_{Q_1} \times O_{Q_2}$: it is the preimage of the diagonal $\Delta \subseteq O_{Q_1} \times O_{Q_1}$ under the continuous map $(a,b) \mapsto (a, \pi^{Q_2}_{Q_1}(b))$. The diagonal is closed in the Hausdorff space $O_{Q_1} \times O_{Q_1}$, so $G_{Q_1,Q_2}$ is closed, hence Borel. Thus $D_{Q_1,Q_2}$ is measurable and the statement is well-posed.

### Countable concentration theorem

**Theorem (Concentration — countable case).** For a countable query system, the $\sigma$-additive extension $P$ satisfies $P(\Omega) = 1$.

*Proof.* The complement decomposes as $\prod_Q O_Q \setminus \Omega = \bigcup_{Q_1 \preceq Q_2} D_{Q_1,Q_2}$. Since $\mathcal{Q}$ is countable, the preorder $\preceq$ has countably many comparable pairs. By the corollary, each $D_{Q_1,Q_2}$ has $P$-measure zero. A countable union of null sets is null, so $P(\prod_Q O_Q \setminus \Omega) = 0$, i.e. $P(\Omega) = 1$. $\square$

### The uncountable case: separation and reduction

For uncountable $\mathcal{Q}$, $\Omega$ is an uncountable intersection of full-measure sets, which need not have full measure in general. The countable union argument fails.

**The right formulation.** Call a subsystem $\mathcal{Q}_0 \subseteq \mathcal{Q}$ **determining** if coherence on $\mathcal{Q}_0$ forces coherence on all of $\mathcal{Q}$: for every $x \in \prod_Q O_Q$, if $x_{Q_1} = \pi^{Q_2}_{Q_1}(x_{Q_2})$ for all comparable $Q_1 \preceq Q_2$ in $\mathcal{Q}_0$, then $x \in \Omega$.

**Concentration theorem — reduction to countable determining subsystem.** If $\mathcal{Q}$ admits a countable determining subsystem $\mathcal{Q}_0$, then $P(\Omega) = 1$.

*Proof.* Let $\Omega_0 := \{x : x_{Q_1} = \pi^{Q_2}_{Q_1}(x_{Q_2})$ for all comparable pairs in $\mathcal{Q}_0\}$. By the countable case applied to $\mathcal{Q}_0$, $P(\Omega_0) = 1$. Since $\mathcal{Q}_0$ is determining, $\Omega_0 \subseteq \Omega$, so $P(\Omega) \geq P(\Omega_0) = 1$. $\square$

**When does a countable determining subsystem exist?** Two natural cases:

1. **$\mathcal{Q}$ itself is countable.** Then $\mathcal{Q}_0 = \mathcal{Q}$ works trivially.
2. **$\mathcal{Q}$ has a countable cofinal chain.** If $Q_1 \preceq Q_2 \preceq \cdots$ is cofinal, take $\mathcal{Q}_0 = \{Q_n\}$. For any $Q \in \mathcal{Q}$, pick $Q_n \succeq Q$; the coherence equation $x_Q = \pi^{Q_n}_Q(x_{Q_n})$ is forced by coherence on the chain together with the refinement structure, provided every $Q$ lies below some $Q_n$. This gives $\Omega_0 \subseteq \Omega$ and the reduction applies.

The cofinal-chain case is precisely the setting of Corollary 3 (Polish cofinal chain) from the cofinal seed theorem. The concentration step is consistent with that earlier result.

### Assessment of step (b)

| Result | Status |
|---|---|
| $G_{Q_1,Q_2}$ is Borel (measurability) | ✓ — closed, hence Borel (Hausdorff + continuous $\pi$) |
| Graph support lemma | ✓ — from definition of $\mu_0$ on rectangles |
| $P(D_{Q_1,Q_2}) = 0$ for each pair | ✓ — corollary of graph support |
| Countable $\mathcal{Q}$: $P(\Omega) = 1$ | ✓ — countable union of null sets |
| Uncountable $\mathcal{Q}$, countable determining subsystem | ✓ — reduction to countable case |
| Uncountable $\mathcal{Q}$, no determining subsystem | Open — genuine obstruction; not addressed here |

**The concentration argument is complete for countable $\mathcal{Q}$ and for uncountable $\mathcal{Q}$ with a countable determining subsystem.** The uncountable case without such a subsystem is a genuine open problem, left as a separate question outside the scope of the current theorem.

## Full theorem statement

**Theorem (Prokhorov extension for query systems).** Let $(\mathcal{Q}, \preceq)$ be a countable sequentially upper-directed query system. Suppose:
- each outcome space $O_Q$ is Hausdorff with $\mathcal{E}_Q = \mathcal{B}(O_Q)$,
- each refinement map $\pi^{Q_2}_{Q_1}: O_{Q_2} \to O_{Q_1}$ is continuous ($Q_1 \preceq Q_2$),
- $\{\ell_Q\}$ is a finitely compatible family of finitely additive normalized valuations on $\mathcal{B}(O_Q)$.

If $\{\ell_Q\}$ is **surjectively projectively uniformly tight**: for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there exist compact sets $\{K_Q\}_{Q \in \mathcal{Q}}$ with
$$K_Q \subset O_Q, \quad \pi^{Q_2}_{Q_1}(K_{Q_2}) = K_{Q_1} \text{ for all } Q_1 \preceq Q_2, \quad \ell_Q(O_Q \setminus K_Q) < \varepsilon \text{ for all } Q,$$
then there exists a **unique** $\sigma$-additive probability measure $P$ on $(\Omega, \sigma(\mathcal{Q}))$ satisfying $(\mathrm{eval}_Q)_\# P = \ell_Q$ for every $Q \in \mathcal{Q}$.

Uniqueness is among all $\sigma$-additive probability measures on $(\Omega, \sigma(\mathcal{Q}))$ with the prescribed marginals.

*Proof sketch.*
- **Step (a) — closed cylinders:** Projective compactness of $K_\Omega = \varprojlim K_Q$ (nonempty by the inverse limit theorem, using surjectivity of bonding maps) combined with the finite intersection property gives $\mu_0(C_N) \to 0$ for closed cylinder sequences $C_N \downarrow \emptyset$.
- **Step (a) — Borel extension:** Inner regularity on each compact Hausdorff $K_Q$ (free by Alexandrov's theorem) approximates Borel cylinders by closed ones; sequential upper-directedness supplies the common refinements needed for intersections of closed cylinders to remain closed.
- **Carathéodory:** $\mu_0$ $\sigma$-additive on $\mathcal{C}$ extends uniquely to $\sigma(\mathcal{C}) = \sigma(\mathcal{Q})$ on the ambient product $\prod_Q O_Q$.
- **Step (b) — concentration:** The graph-support lemma (each $(\mathrm{eval}_{Q_1}, \mathrm{eval}_{Q_2})$-joint law equals $(\mathrm{graph}_\pi)_\# \ell_{Q_2}$, supported on $G_{Q_1,Q_2}$) gives $P(D_{Q_1,Q_2}) = 0$ for each comparable pair. Countably many pairs (countable $\mathcal{Q}$) → $P(\Omega) = 1$.
- **Restriction and uniqueness:** $P|_\Omega$ is the required measure. Uniqueness: $\sigma(\mathcal{Q})$ is generated by cylinders on which any two extensions with the same marginals agree; since both are $\sigma$-additive probability measures, they agree on $\sigma(\mathcal{Q})$. $\square$

---

## Scope and open questions

**What the theorem covers:**
- Countable query systems with continuous maps and Hausdorff spaces.
- Surjectively projectively uniformly tight finitely compatible families.
- The conclusion is $\sigma$-additivity plus concentration on $\Omega$ in a single step.

**What remains open:**
- **Achievability of the hypothesis.** When do surjective projective compact cores exist? When the $\pi$ maps themselves are surjective, one can construct them by downward induction (image of a fine compact set is a compact set at the coarse level, and the bonding map is surjective onto it by definition). Without surjectivity of the $\pi$ maps, this construction fails and the hypothesis may not be achievable.
- **Uncountable $\mathcal{Q}$ without a countable determining subsystem.** Step (b) fails in this case. This is a genuine obstruction, not just a technical gap.
- **Finitely additive $\ell_Q$ that are not Borel measures.** The theorem requires $\mathcal{E}_Q = \mathcal{B}(O_Q)$; for more abstract $\sigma$-algebras, Alexandrov's theorem may not apply.

### Root causes of remaining sorrys

#### 1. `compactInverseLimit_nonempty` (the main gap)

The mathematical proof uses the *inverse limit theorem*: an inverse system of nonempty compact Hausdorff spaces with surjective bonding maps indexed by a directed set has a nonempty inverse limit with surjective projections.

Mathlib (as of v4.28) does not have this theorem in the directed-set form needed here. It has:
- `IsCompact.nonempty_iInter_of_directed` — FIP for a directed decreasing family of compact sets in a fixed ambient space (not an inverse system of different spaces).
- Classical `TopologicalSpace.NonemptyCompactSpace` results for sequential inverse limits.

The correct Mathlib route would be to express the inverse limit as an intersection in the product $\prod_Q K_Q$ (which is compact by Tychonoff) and apply FIP to the sets $\{x \in \prod_Q K_Q : x_{Q_1} = \pi^{Q_2}_{Q_1}(x_{Q_2})\}$, relying on surjectivity to ensure each finite sub-intersection is nonempty. This requires constructing the surjectivity witnesses and establishing the FIP explicitly — a non-trivial Lean proof that is currently sorry'd with the appropriate statement.

#### 2. `cylGen_addContent.empty'` and `cylGen_addContent.sUnion'`

The `AddContent` structure requires two fields:

- **`empty'`**: `toFun ∅ = 0`. This requires showing that `cylGenMass ... ∅ h = 0`. The definition of `cylGenMass` uses `Classical.choose` to extract a presentation `(i, A)` of `∅` as a cylinder, then evaluates `ν i A`. Proving this equals zero requires showing that for any presentation of `∅ = Cyl i A`, we have `ν i A = 0` (since `A = ∅` by injectivity of the cylinder map). The opacity of `Classical.choose` blocks this computation in Lean; the proof needs an intermediate lemma `cylGenMass_empty_eq_zero` establishing this by abstract argument.

- **`sUnion'`**: finite additivity over pairwise-disjoint finsets. This is the core content of finite additivity of $\mu_0$: for a finite pairwise-disjoint family $\{E_k\}_{k \in F}$ of cylinders with $\bigsqcup_k E_k \in \mathcal{C}$, we need $\mu_0(\bigsqcup_k E_k) = \sum_k \mu_0(E_k)$. Mathematically this follows by lifting all $E_k$ to a common refinement (using `UpperDirected` inductively over the finset), computing over that level, and using finite additivity of $\ell_Q$. In Lean, the induction over a `Finset` with the `sUnion'` field signature requires establishing `cylGenMass` at a common refinement level and then showing that the two `Classical.choose` extractions (for the individual cylinders and for the union) are consistent. The `Classical.choose` opacity makes the key equalities non-trivial to establish.

#### 3. The `cylGenMass` opacity problem

The core technical difficulty across all remaining sorrys in `QuerySystem.lean` is that `cylGenMass` is defined via `Classical.choose`:

```lean
noncomputable def cylGenMass ... (E : Set S.Omega) (hE : E ∈ S.CylGen) : ℝ≥0∞ :=
  haveI : DecidableEq S.ι := Classical.decEq S.ι
  let i := hE.choose
  let A := hE.choose_spec.choose
  S.preμ udir ν {i} (fun j => if h : j = i then h ▸ A else ∅)
```

The `Classical.choose` witness is proof-irrelevant (hence `cylGenMass_wellDef` is trivial), but is computationally opaque: Lean cannot reduce `hE.choose` to a concrete index even when `hE` has the form `⟨i, A, hA, rfl⟩`. All proofs that require reasoning about the value of `cylGenMass` on a specific cylinder therefore cannot proceed by reduction.

**The path forward.** The fix is to prove a bridging lemma:

```lean
lemma cylGenMass_eq_preμ (i : S.ι) (A : Set (S.q i).Outcome)
    (hA : MeasurableSet A) :
    S.cylGenMass udir surj ν compat (S.Cyl i A) ⟨i, A, hA, rfl⟩ = ν i A := by
  simp only [cylGenMass, preμ]
  -- argument: the choose resolves to some (j, B) with Cyl j B = Cyl i A,
  -- then cylGenMass_wellDef + preμ_respects_finCyl_eq closes the goal
  sorry
```

Once `cylGenMass_eq_preμ` is established, `cylGen_addContent.empty'`, `cylGen_addContent.sUnion'`, `hcontent_U`, and `hcontent_n` all reduce to straightforward computations. This is the single highest-leverage sorry in the file.

## Comparison to the current paper's extension theorem

The existing theorem in the paper (call it the **realizability theorem**) has the following structure:

| | Realizability theorem (current paper) | Prokhorov extension (this note) |
|---|---|---|
| Input measures | $\sigma$-additive $\nu_Q$ (assumed) | Finitely additive $\ell_Q$ (weaker input) |
| Key hypothesis | Realizability: $\mathrm{eval}_Q$ surjective | Surjective projective uniform tightness |
| Topology on $O_Q$ | Not required | Hausdorff + $\mathcal{E}_Q = \mathcal{B}(O_Q)$ |
| Continuity of $\pi$ | Not required | Required |
| Conclusion | $\sigma$-additive $P$ on $\Omega$ | $\sigma$-additive $P$ on $\Omega$ |
| Countability of $\mathcal{Q}$ | Not required | Required (for concentration) |

**What the Prokhorov theorem adds:** It starts from a weaker input ($\ell_Q$ finitely additive rather than $\sigma$-additive) and derives $\sigma$-additivity of the global extension. It is thus a theorem about *when* finitely additive compatible families can be promoted to a global countably additive measure — a genuinely different question from the realizability theorem, which takes $\sigma$-additivity of the marginals as given.

**What it trades:** The weaker input requires topological structure ($O_Q$ Hausdorff, $\pi$ continuous, $\mathcal{E}_Q$ Borel) that the realizability theorem does not need, plus the surjective projective tightness condition.

**How they fit together:** The two theorems are complementary, not competing:
- The realizability theorem: if you already have $\sigma$-additive marginals and a realizability condition on $\Omega$, you get a global measure.
- The Prokhorov theorem: if you only have finitely additive marginals but can control mass concentration projectively, you also get a global measure — and the $\sigma$-additivity is derived, not assumed.

In the program, the realizability theorem is the primary extension result; the Prokhorov theorem is a topological companion applicable when the input measures are not yet known to be $\sigma$-additive.

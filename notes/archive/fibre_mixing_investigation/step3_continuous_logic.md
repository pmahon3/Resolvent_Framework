# Step 3 — Fibre Mixing in Continuous Logic

*2026-04-20*

**Status:** Expressibility assessed. Key structural gap identified: uniform fibre

---

## Why continuous logic

Standard first-order logic is the wrong framework for measure-preserving systems
because it cannot express metric structure (measure-zero sets, a.e. conditions,
conditional expectations). The correct framework is **continuous logic for metric
structures** (Ben Yaacov–Berenstein–Henson–Usvyatsov 2008), in which:

- Structures are complete metric spaces
- Formulas take values in $[0,1]$ (not Boolean)
- Łoś's theorem holds: if $\phi(x)$ is a continuous logic formula and each
  factor $M_n$ satisfies $\phi(x) = 0$, then the ultraproduct $\prod_\mathcal{U} M_n$
  satisfies $\phi(x) = 0$
- Compact type spaces, definability of conditional expectations, and
  sub-$\sigma$-algebras generated by definable functions are all handled

Measure-preserving systems $(X, \mu, T)$ are naturally metric structures in this
framework: the underlying space is $L^1(\mu)$ (or the probability algebra
$\mathrm{MAlg}(\mu)$), $T$ acts as an isometry, and all measure-theoretic
operations are uniform-continuous.

**The model-theoretic language.** For our purposes, the relevant structure is
$(X, \mu, T, h)$ where $h \in L^\infty(\mu)$ is the observable. The key objects:
- $T^k h$ for $k = 0, \ldots, L-1$: these are definable elements (definable
  functions of the structure)
- $\sigma(\Phi_h^{(L)}) = \sigma(h, Th, \ldots, T^{L-1}h)$: the sub-$\sigma$-algebra
  generated by the lag-$L$ delay map — this is the lag-$L$ observational algebra
- Conditional expectations $\mathbb{E}[\,\cdot\mid \sigma(\Phi_h^{(L)})]$: these
  are definable in the probability algebra framework (Ben Yaacov 2006)

**What needs checking before assuming the framework applies:** The lag-$L$
sub-$\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{F}_L = \sigma(h, Th, \ldots, T^{L-1}h)$ depends
on $T$ and $h$ through composition. That this dependence is definable in the
metric structure should be verified — the functions $T^k h$ are definable since
$T$ acts as an automorphism and $h$ is a named element, so $T^k h$ is a
definable term. The generated $\sigma$-algebra is definable from these terms
(each conditional expectation $\mathbb{E}[f \mid \mathcal{F}_L]$ is a definable
function of $f$ and the structure). This appears correct but warrants explicit
confirmation against the Ben Yaacov 2006 framework.

## The key separation: lag-by-lag vs uniform fibre mixing

The fibre mixing condition has two layers that must be kept distinct:

**Lag-by-lag fibre mixing (LBL):** For each fixed $L$, there exists $c_L > 0$
and an event $S^*_L$ such that
$$\mu_z(S^*_L)\,\mu_z((S^*_L)^c) \geq c_L \cdot p_z
\quad \text{for $\nu_L$-a.e. $\varepsilon$-large fibre } z.$$

**Uniform fibre mixing (UFM):** There exists a single event $S^*$ and a single
$c > 0$ such that for all sufficiently large $L$,
$$\mu_z(S^*)\,\mu_z((S^*)^c) \geq c \cdot p_z
\quad \text{for $\nu_L$-a.e. $\varepsilon$-large fibre } z.$$

The definition (Paper IV, Definition 5.1) requires UFM — the same $c$ and $S^*$
across all large lags. This is the crucial uniformity.

---

## Expressibility of lag-by-lag fibre mixing

**Claim.** For each fixed $L$ and each fixed tolerance $\delta > 0$, the
condition
$$\mu_z(S^*)\,\mu_z((S^*)^c) \geq (c - \delta) \cdot p_z
\quad \text{for $\nu_L$-a.e. $\varepsilon$-large fibre } z$$
is expressible as a continuous logic formula $\phi_{L,c,\delta}$ in the language
of $(X, \mu, T, h)$.

**Why:** At fixed $L$, the fibres of $\Phi_h^{(L)}$ are the atoms of
$\mathcal{F}_L$. The conditional measures $\mu_z$ are the disintegration of $\mu$
over $\mathcal{F}_L$. In continuous logic:
- $\mu_z(S^*)$ is a conditional expectation: $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}_{S^*} \mid \mathcal{F}_L](z)$
- $p_z$ is the fibre mass: $\mu(\Phi_h^{(L)} = z)$, expressible as a function of
  the empirical distribution of $\Phi_h^{(L)}$
- The condition $\mu_z(S^*)\mu_z((S^*)^c) \geq (c-\delta) p_z$ a.e. is a
  condition on conditional expectations, which are definable

Each such formula involves only finitely many lags ($0, \ldots, L-1$), finitely
many conditional expectations, and a fixed tolerance. These are legitimate
continuous logic formulas.

**Łoś preservation:** If each factor $(X_n, \mu_n, T_n, h_n)$ satisfies
$\phi_{L,c,\delta}$, then the ultraproduct $\prod_\mathcal{U} (X_n, \mu_n, T_n, h_n)$
satisfies $\phi_{L,c,\delta}$ (by Łoś's theorem for continuous logic).

So: **lag-by-lag fibre mixing at fixed $L$ is preserved by metric ultraproducts.**

---

## The uniformity gap

Uniform fibre mixing is the statement:
$$\exists c > 0 \;\; \forall L \geq L_0 \;\; \phi_{L,c,0} \text{ holds.}$$

This is **not a single continuous logic formula** — it is an infinitary statement
quantifying over all $L$. It is the precise analogue of CE:

| | CE | Uniform fibre mixing |
|---|---|---|
| **Finite approximation** | $\mu(A_k) \leq 1 - \varepsilon$ for first $k$ sets in a decreasing chain | LBL at lag $L$ with constant $c_L$ |
| **Infinitary condition** | $\lim_k \mu(A_k) = 0$ | UFM with uniform $c$ |
| **Preserved by ultraproduct?** | Each finite approximation: yes. Full condition: no. | Each fixed-lag condition: yes. Uniform condition: no. |
| **Failure witness** | Finite-cofinite charge: satisfies all finite approximations | Sequence with $c_n \to 0$: each satisfies LBL but ultraproduct fails UFM |

**The key conclusion:** The gap between LBL and UFM is exactly where the
irreducibility sits. Any first-order or approximate formula in continuous logic
that is satisfied by all ergodic systems will be satisfied by ultraproducts of
ergodic systems — but it will be a fixed-lag condition, hence LBL. UFM requires
a uniform quantifier that escapes continuous logic expressibility.

## The failure witness: the right construction

The analysis above specifies the witness precisely:

**Target:** A sequence of ergodic (hence fibre-mixing) systems
$(X_n, \mu_n, T_n, h_n)$ such that:
- Each satisfies UFM with constant $c_n > 0$
- $c_n \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$
- The ultraproduct $\prod_\mathcal{U}$ satisfies LBL at each fixed lag (by Łoś)
  but fails UFM (no uniform $c$ survives the ultraproduct)

This is the dynamical analogue of the Dirac-charge sequence: each $\delta_n$ is
$\sigma$-additive (CE holds), but the ultraproduct is the finite-cofinite charge
(CE fails). The role of $\sigma$-additivity is played by UFM; the role of the
ultraproduct is the same.

**Why $c_n \to 0$ kills UFM in the ultraproduct:**

In the ultraproduct, the fibre mixing constant is controlled by the behaviour
of $c_n$ along the ultrafilter $\mathcal{U}$. If $c_n \to 0$ along
$\mathcal{U}$, then for any fixed $c' > 0$, the set $\{n : c_n < c'\}$ is in
$\mathcal{U}$ (since it is cofinite for a free ultrafilter). So the ultraproduct
has no uniform constant $c' > 0$ — UFM fails.

Meanwhile, at each fixed lag $L$, the condition $\phi_{L,c_n,0}$ holds for all
$n$ (since each factor is ergodic and satisfies fibre mixing at lag $L$ with
some $c_n > 0$). By Łoś, $\phi_{L,c_{\mathcal{U}},0}$ holds in the ultraproduct
for each fixed $L$. LBL holds.

**So UFM is not first-order axiomatizable in $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dyn}}$**
(in the continuous logic sense), provided such a sequence $(X_n, T_n, \mu_n, h_n)$
with $c_n \to 0$ exists.

## Open construction: does the sequence exist?

The remaining task is to exhibit a concrete sequence of ergodic systems with
deteriorating fibre mixing constants. Two candidates:

**Candidate 1: Periodic approximations.** Let $X_n = \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$,
$T_n = $ rotation by 1, $\mu_n = $ uniform measure, $h_n = $ identity (or a
faithful function on $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$).

Each $(X_n, T_n, \mu_n)$ is ergodic (rotation of a finite set is transitive).
At large lag $L$, the delay vector $\Phi_{h_n}^{(L)}$ separates all $n$ points
(if $L \geq 1$ for a faithful $h_n$). The fibres are singletons, so $p_z = 1/n$
and $\mu_z = \delta_z$. Then $\mu_z(S^*)\mu_z((S^*)^c) = 0$ for all $z$ — UFM
fails completely.

But: the finite-set systems may be too degenerate. Conditional measures being
Dirac masses is the same failure mode as the cheap framing B witness. Need to
check whether these systems genuinely satisfy UFM at any lag.

**Candidate 2: Irrational rotations with deteriorating return time.** Let
$X_n = [0,1)$, $T_n = $ rotation by $\alpha_n$ (irrational), $\mu_n = $
Lebesgue, $h_n = $ identity.

Each is ergodic. The delay vectors $\Phi_{h_n}^{(L)} = (x, x + \alpha_n, \ldots,
x + (L-1)\alpha_n)$ separate points well if $\alpha_n$ is poorly approximable
by rationals (e.g., $\alpha_n$ Liouville-type would give poor separation;
badly approximable $\alpha_n$ would give good separation).

The fibre mixing constant $c_n$ depends on how well the fibres of $\Phi_{h_n}^{(L)}$
cut the target event $S^*$. For an irrational rotation, ergodicity implies UFM,
but the constant $c_n$ may depend on the Diophantine type of $\alpha_n$. If
$\alpha_n \to p/q$ (a rational), the rotation degenerates to a periodic orbit,
and $c_n \to 0$.

This candidate is more promising: a sequence of irrational rotations
$\alpha_n \to 0$ (say), degenerating toward the identity. Each is ergodic;
the limit is the identity (non-ergodic). The fibre mixing constants
$c_n \to 0$ as the rotation degenerates.

**Assessment:** Candidate 2 looks viable. The degeneration of irrational
rotation toward the identity (or toward a rational rotation) is the natural
candidate for a sequence with $c_n \to 0$. The construction needs:
1. An explicit formula or bound for the fibre mixing constant as a function
   of $\alpha_n$ and the lag $L$
2. Verification that $c_n \to 0$ as $\alpha_n \to 0$ (or $\alpha_n \to p/q$)
3. The ultraproduct of such a sequence and verification that UFM fails

## Summary

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is fibre mixing expressible in continuous logic? | **Lag-by-lag yes; uniform (UFM) no** |
| Where does the irreducibility live? | In the uniformity across lags — same gap as CE |
| Is Łoś applicable? | Yes, to LBL at each fixed lag; not to UFM |
| What must the failure witness do? | Sequence of ergodic systems with $c_n \to 0$ |
| Best candidate sequence? | Irrational rotations $\alpha_n \to 0$ |
| What remains? | Explicit $c_n$ bound and ultraproduct verification |

**Conclusion.** Fibre mixing (UFM) is not axiomatizable by any set of
continuous logic sentences in $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dyn}}$, provided the
irrational-rotation witness sequence can be made rigorous. The argument has
the same structure as the CE irreducibility proof: finite approximations
(LBL) are preserved by Łoś; the uniform condition is not; the witness is a
sequence with deteriorating uniformity constant.

The CE parallel is now complete at the structural level. The remaining work
is the explicit construction.

*2026-04-20*

Witness construction now well-posed.

---

## Preliminary: the definition re-read

From Paper IV, Definition 5.1 (as written): at a fixed lag $L$, an event $S^*$ is
$(c,\varepsilon)$-mixing across fibres if there exists $c > 0$ such that

$$\mu_z(S^*)\,\mu_z\bigl((S^*)^c\bigr) \geq c \cdot p_z$$

for $\nu_L$-a.e. $\varepsilon$-large fibre $z$.

The condition is stated **per-lag**: a single $c$ works for a given $L$, but
the definition does not itself require $c$ to be uniform across lags. The
infinitary content enters through Corollary 5.12: for the equivalence
$\delta(L) \to 0 \iff H_2(\nu_L) \to +\infty$ to hold as $L \to \infty$, the
condition must hold for all sufficiently large $L$, which implicitly requires
a positive $c$ at each such $L$.

**Consequence for the witness.** A sequence of ergodic systems with per-lag
constants $c_n(L) \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$ (for fixed $L$) would produce an
ultraproduct where the per-lag condition holds vacuously (no uniform lower
bound survives). This is the right target.

## Fixing the observable

**Critical fix from step 3.** With $h(x) = x$ (identity observable) on $[0,1)$
and irrational rotation $T_\alpha$, the delay map

$$\Phi_h^{(L)}(x) = (x, x+\alpha, \ldots, x+(L-1)\alpha \bmod 1)$$

is injective (since $1, \alpha, \ldots, (L-1)\alpha$ are linearly independent
over $\mathbb{Q}$ for $\alpha$ irrational). All fibres are singletons with
$\mu$-measure zero. There are no $\varepsilon$-large fibres. Fibre mixing
is vacuously true — not a useful witness.

**Fix:** Use a finite-partition observable. Take

$$h(x) = \mathbf{1}_{[0, 1/2)}(x) \in \{0, 1\}$$

(the indicator of the left half of the circle — the simplest non-trivial binary
query). This is the query-system setup: finite outcome space, cylinder sets are
intersections of preimages of $h$ under iterates of $T$.

## Setup

- $X = [0,1)$ (circle, $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$)
- $T_\alpha(x) = x + \alpha \bmod 1$, $\alpha$ irrational
- $\mu$ = Lebesgue measure (the unique $T_\alpha$-invariant measure)
- $h = \mathbf{1}_{[0,1/2)}$, so $\mathsf{O} = \{0, 1\}$

The lag-$L$ delay vector is:

$$\Phi_h^{(L)}(x) = (h(x), h(T_\alpha x), \ldots, h(T_\alpha^{L-1} x))
\in \{0,1\}^L$$

The fibre over $z = (z_0, \ldots, z_{L-1}) \in \{0,1\}^L$ is:

$$C_z = \bigcap_{k=0}^{L-1} T_\alpha^{-k}\bigl(h^{-1}(z_k)\bigr)
= \bigcap_{k=0}^{L-1} (I_{z_k} - k\alpha)$$

where $I_0 = [0, 1/2)$, $I_1 = [1/2, 1)$, and the intersection is taken
mod 1. Each $C_z$ is a finite union of arcs on the circle.

---

## Three-distance theorem and fibre structure

The key tool is the **three-distance theorem** (Steinhaus, 1958): for any
$\alpha$ irrational and any $L$, the sequence of points
$\{0, \alpha, 2\alpha, \ldots, (L-1)\alpha\}$ (mod 1) partitions $[0,1)$
into $L$ arcs of at most **three distinct lengths**.

The arcs are exactly the level sets of the partition generated by
$\{-k\alpha \bmod 1 : k = 0, \ldots, L-1\}$ — i.e., the atoms of the
cylinder partition $\{C_z\}$.

**Sizes of the atoms.** Let $[a_0; a_1, a_2, \ldots]$ be the continued
fraction expansion of $\alpha$, and let $p_k/q_k$ be the convergents. Then:

- The atoms of the lag-$L$ cylinder partition have sizes governed by the
  Ostrowski representation of $L$ in the continued fraction base.
- For $L$ between consecutive denominators $q_k < L \leq q_{k+1}$:
  atoms come in (at most) three sizes, determined by the remainder structure.
- The **largest** atom has size $\approx 1/q_k$ — the reciprocal of the
  $k$-th continued fraction denominator.

**$\varepsilon$-large fibres.** A fibre $C_z$ is $\varepsilon$-large if
$p_z = \mu(C_z) \geq \varepsilon$. At lag $L$, the $\varepsilon$-large fibres
are those atoms of the cylinder partition with size $\geq \varepsilon$.

For fixed $\varepsilon > 0$ and $L \leq q_k$ (i.e., $L$ smaller than the
$(k+1)$-th denominator), some atoms have size $\approx 1/q_k \geq \varepsilon$
provided $q_k \leq 1/\varepsilon$. These are the $\varepsilon$-large fibres.

---

## The conditional measure on an $\varepsilon$-large fibre

Fix an $\varepsilon$-large fibre $C_z$ — a single arc $[a, b)$ of length
$\mu(C_z) = b - a \geq \varepsilon$.

The conditional measure $\mu_z = \mu(\cdot \mid C_z)$ is Lebesgue measure
restricted to $[a,b)$ and normalized: for any Borel $A \subset [a,b)$,

$$\mu_z(A) = \frac{\lambda(A \cap [a,b))}{b - a}$$

where $\lambda$ is Lebesgue measure. So $\mu_z$ is uniform on $[a,b)$.

**Conditional probability of $S^* = [0, 1/2)$:**

$$\mu_z(S^*) = \frac{\lambda([a,b) \cap [0, 1/2))}{b - a}$$

This equals:
- $1$ if $[a,b) \subset [0, 1/2)$
- $0$ if $[a,b) \subset [1/2, 1)$
- $(1/2 - a)/(b-a) \in (0,1)$ if the arc straddles the boundary $1/2$

---

## The fibre mixing constant: what controls it

The product $\mu_z(S^*)\mu_z((S^*)^c)$ is:
- $0$ if the arc is entirely inside $[0,1/2)$ or entirely inside $[1/2,1)$
- $(1/2-a)(b-1/2)/(b-a)^2 \cdot (b-a)^{-1} \cdot (b-a)$ simplified:
  let $\ell = b - a$ (arc length) and $s = (1/2 - a)/\ell$ (fraction of arc
  in $[0,1/2)$). Then $\mu_z(S^*) = s$, $\mu_z((S^*)^c) = 1-s$, and:

$$\mu_z(S^*)\mu_z((S^*)^c) = s(1-s)$$

The condition $s(1-s) \geq c \cdot p_z = c \cdot \ell$ requires:

$$s(1-s) \geq c \cdot \ell \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad c \leq \frac{s(1-s)}{\ell}$$

For arcs that straddle the boundary: $s = (1/2 - a)/\ell$, so
$s(1-s) = (1/2-a)(b-1/2)/\ell^2$. The product $(1/2-a)(b-1/2)$ is
the product of the two sub-arc lengths. Hence:

$$c \leq \frac{(1/2 - a)(b - 1/2)}{\ell^3}$$

The maximum $c$ compatible with fibre mixing at this arc is:

$$c(\alpha, L, z) = \frac{(1/2 - a)(b - 1/2)}{\ell^3}$$

The **global constant** $c(\alpha, L)$ is the infimum over all $\varepsilon$-large
fibres that straddle the boundary:

$$c(\alpha, L) = \inf_{z : C_z \text{ straddles } 1/2,\, p_z \geq \varepsilon}
\frac{(1/2 - a_z)(b_z - 1/2)}{\ell_z^3}$$

(Fibres entirely inside $[0,1/2)$ or $[1/2,1)$ have $s \in \{0,1\}$ and
$s(1-s) = 0$, forcing $c = 0$ — they are excluded from the a.e. condition
since they do not straddle the boundary. The fibre mixing condition is about
boundary-straddling fibres.)

## Degeneration as $\alpha \to 0$

**The mechanism.** As $\alpha \to 0$, the continued fraction denominators grow
slowly ($q_k \approx 1/\alpha$ for small $\alpha$). At lag $L$, the atoms of
the cylinder partition are approximately $L$ arcs of width $\approx 1/L$ each
(by equidistribution for small but non-zero $\alpha$).

For the specific atom straddling the boundary $1/2$: as $\alpha \to 0$, the
orbit $\{0, \alpha, 2\alpha, \ldots, (L-1)\alpha\}$ clusters near $0$. The
partition boundary at $1/2$ is crossed by the orbit approximately once (at
the point $k^* \alpha \approx 1/2$, so $k^* \approx 1/(2\alpha)$).

At lag $L < 1/(2\alpha)$: the orbit stays in $[0, L\alpha)$ and does not
reach $1/2$. All atoms are in $[0, L\alpha)$ — entirely in $[0,1/2)$ for
small $\alpha$. The atom straddling $1/2$ has:
- $a \approx L\alpha - 1/L$ (last atom before wrapping)
- but actually for very small $\alpha$, no atom straddles $1/2$ at lag $L$

**More precisely:** At lag $L$ with $L\alpha < 1/2$ (i.e., $\alpha < 1/(2L)$),
the delay vectors of most points agree across all $L$ lags — the point stays
in either $[0,1/2)$ or $[1/2,1)$ for all $L$ iterates. So:

- Points $x \in [0, 1/2 - L\alpha)$: all iterates in $[0,1/2)$, delay vector
  is $(0,0,\ldots,0)$. One large fibre with $p_z \approx 1/2 - L\alpha$.
- Points $x \in [1/2, 1 - L\alpha)$: all iterates in $[1/2,1)$, delay vector
  is $(1,1,\ldots,1)$. One large fibre with $p_z \approx 1/2 - L\alpha$.
- Boundary points near $\{1/2 - k\alpha\}_{k=0}^{L-1}$ and $\{1-k\alpha\}$:
  small fibres that straddle the boundary, size $\approx \alpha$ each.

For $S^* = [0,1/2)$:
- Fibre $(0,\ldots,0)$: $\mu_z(S^*) = 1$ (entirely in $[0,1/2)$). Product $= 0$.
- Fibre $(1,\ldots,1)$: $\mu_z(S^*) = 0$ (entirely in $[1/2,1)$). Product $= 0$.
- Boundary fibres: size $\approx \alpha$, straddle the boundary. Product $\approx
  \frac{(\alpha/2)^2}{\alpha} \cdot \frac{1}{\alpha^2} = O(1)$ per unit mass —
  but these fibres have $p_z \approx \alpha$.

For the two large fibres with $p_z \approx 1/2$: $s(1-s) = 0$, so the fibre
mixing condition requires $0 \geq c \cdot (1/2)$, which forces $c = 0$.

**Conclusion:** For $\alpha < 1/(2L)$, fibre mixing fails for $S^* = [0,1/2)$
because the two largest fibres are entirely monochromatic ($s = 0$ or $s = 1$).
The per-lag fibre mixing constant $c(\alpha, L)$ for this $S^*$ is $0$.

---

## Fibre mixing does hold for large $L$

For fixed $\alpha$ and large $L$ (specifically $L > 1/\alpha$), the orbit
$\{0, \alpha, \ldots, (L-1)\alpha\}$ covers the circle equidistributedly. The
atoms of the cylinder partition are all of size $\approx 1/L$, and many of
them straddle the boundary $1/2$. Specifically, $\approx 2L\alpha/(1) = 2L\alpha$
atoms straddle each of the two boundaries $0$ and $1/2$ — this is $\Theta(L\alpha)$
many boundary-straddling atoms.

For boundary-straddling atoms of size $\ell \approx 1/L$:
- The straddling amount $(1/2 - a)$ or $(b - 1/2)$ is at least $\Theta(\alpha)$
  (since atoms are separated by $\Theta(\alpha)$ near the boundary).
- $s(1-s) \geq \Theta(\alpha/L) \cdot \Theta((1 - \alpha/L)) \approx \Theta(\alpha/L)$
- $c \cdot \ell = c/L$
- Condition: $\Theta(\alpha/L) \geq c/L$, i.e., $c \leq \Theta(\alpha)$.

So for large $L$, the fibre mixing constant satisfies:

$$c(\alpha, L) \approx \Theta(\alpha) \quad \text{for } L \gg 1/\alpha$$

This is positive for fixed $\alpha > 0$, confirming ergodicity implies fibre
mixing at large lags.

---

## The deterioration: $c_n \to 0$ as $\alpha_n \to 0$

Take the sequence $\alpha_n = 1/n$ (rationally approximated by $0$; note these
are rational, so replace with $\alpha_n = [0; n, 1, 1, 1, \ldots]$ —
Liouville-type numbers approaching $0$ through irrationals; or more simply,
take $\alpha_n = \sqrt{2}/n$).

For fixed lag $L$:
- When $n > 2L$ (i.e., $\alpha_n < 1/(2L)$): the computation above shows
  the two largest fibres are monochromatic. No boundary-straddling large fibre
  exists. The per-lag constant for $S^* = [0,1/2)$ is $c(\alpha_n, L) = 0$
  at this lag.
- This means the system fails fibre mixing at lag $L$ when $n > 2L$.

**But** each system $(X, T_{\alpha_n}, \mu, h)$ is ergodic (irrational rotation
is uniquely ergodic), so fibre mixing holds at each lag $L \geq L_0(\alpha_n)$
for large enough $L_0(\alpha_n) \gg 1/\alpha_n = n$.

The per-lag constant (for the lags where fibre mixing holds) satisfies:
$$c(\alpha_n, L) \approx \Theta(\alpha_n) = \Theta(1/n) \to 0$$

as $n \to \infty$, for fixed large $L$.

---

## The witness construction

This gives the required sequence:

- **Each factor** $(X, T_{\alpha_n}, \mu, h)$ is ergodic.
- **Fibre mixing** holds for each factor at each sufficiently large lag
  (ergodicity implies it), with per-lag constant $c_n \approx \Theta(\alpha_n)$.
- **$c_n \to 0$** as $n \to \infty$ (as $\alpha_n \to 0$).

The ultraproduct $\prod_\mathcal{U}(X, T_{\alpha_n}, \mu, h)$ along a free
ultrafilter $\mathcal{U}$:
- Satisfies every fixed-lag continuous logic sentence (by Łoś): lag-by-lag
  fibre mixing holds in the ultraproduct at each fixed $L$.
- Fails uniform fibre mixing: for any fixed $c' > 0$, the set
  $\{n : c_n < c'\} \supset \{n : n > C/c'\}$ is cofinite, hence in $\mathcal{U}$.
  So no uniform $c' > 0$ witnesses fibre mixing across all lags in the ultraproduct.

**What the ultraproduct looks like.** In the limit $\alpha_n \to 0$, the
rotation degenerates toward the identity. The ultraproduct of irrational
rotations with $\alpha_n \to 0$ is, in the nonstandard sense, a system with
an infinitesimally small rotation angle — an internal rotation by $[\alpha_n]_\mathcal{U}$
which is a positive nonstandard real but smaller than any standard positive real.
The fibre structure of the ultraproduct has many large fibres that are nearly
monochromatic — precisely because the lag-$L$ cylinder partition for infinitesimal
$\alpha$ has mostly monochromatic atoms.

---

## A subtlety: which $S^*$?

The definition requires a single $S^*$ that witnesses fibre mixing for all
large $L$. In the rotation setting, $S^* = [0,1/2)$ is the natural candidate.

At fixed $n$, for $S^* = [0,1/2)$: fibre mixing holds at lags $L \gg n$
(orbit covers the circle). The same $S^*$ works for all lags simultaneously.

In the ultraproduct: the event $S^*$ in the ultraproduct corresponds to
$[S^*_n]_\mathcal{U}$ where each $S^*_n = [0,1/2)$. The product
$\mu_z^{\mathcal{U}}(S^*)\mu_z^{\mathcal{U}}((S^*)^c)$ in the ultraproduct
is the ultraproduct of $\mu_z^n(S^*)\mu_z^n((S^*)^c)$. For large lags,
this is $\approx c_n \cdot p_z^n \approx (1/n) \cdot p_z^n$. In the
ultraproduct, this gives the condition $\approx (1/n) \cdot p_z$ with
$[1/n]_\mathcal{U}$ an infinitesimal. No standard $c' > 0$ can work.

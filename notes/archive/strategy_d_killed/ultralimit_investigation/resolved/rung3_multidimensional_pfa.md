# Rung 3: Algebras with Multidimensional pfa

*Working note for Priority 2b of the ultralimit representation investigation.*  
*Parent: `notes/unsorted/foundations/ce_nonderivability/index.md`*  
*Prerequisite: `notes/archive/strategy_d_killed/ultralimit_investigation/resolved/finite_cofinite_calculation.md` (rung 2 complete, positive).*

---

## The Question

Rung 2 settled representability on the finite-cofinite algebra $\mathcal{E}$, where
$\mathrm{pfa}(\mathcal{E})$ is one-dimensional: the only purely finitely additive
probability is $\ell_{\mathrm{fc}}$, and it is approximated by the uniform measures
$\frac{1}{k}\sum_{n<k}\delta_n$. The positive answer there was almost forced: there
was only one charge to represent, and the "spread mass thinly" construction was
available.

**The rung 3 question:** Does the positive answer persist for Boolean algebras where
$\mathrm{pfa}(B)$ is genuinely multidimensional — where purely finitely additive
charges are parameterized by a nontrivial family and not just a single point?

---

## Ruling Out Naive Candidates

**The free Boolean algebra on countably many generators** — equivalently, the clopen
algebra $\mathrm{Clop}(2^\omega)$ of Cantor space — is a dead end. In any compact
totally disconnected space, any decreasing sequence of clopens $U_n \downarrow \emptyset$
must have some $U_N = \emptyset$ (compactness forces a finite sub-intersection to be
empty). So the "decreasing to empty" condition is vacuous for clopens, and every
finitely additive probability on $\mathrm{Clop}(2^\omega)$ is automatically
$\sigma$-additive. Thus $\mathrm{pfa}(\mathrm{Clop}(2^\omega)) = \{0\}$.
This algebra is the wrong test case.

The same compactness argument applies to the clopen algebra of any compact Stone
space. The interesting case must involve a Stone space that is *not* compact — i.e.,
the Stone space of an algebra that is not $\sigma$-complete but also not compact.

**$\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})/\mathrm{fin}$** is also wrong for a different reason:
there are *no* $\sigma$-additive probabilities on it. Any $\sigma$-additive measure
on $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})/\mathrm{fin}$ would lift (via the quotient map
$A \mapsto [A]$) to a $\sigma$-additive measure on $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})$
assigning zero to every singleton (since $[{n}] = [{\emptyset}]$ in the quotient).
By $\sigma$-additivity, such a measure would assign zero to all of $\mathbb{N}$,
contradicting normalization. No $\sigma$-additive approximants exist, so the
ultralimit question is vacuous.

---

## The Right Family of Test Algebras

**Construction.** Fix a partition of $\mathbb{N}$ into $k$ infinite pieces
$P_1, P_2, \ldots, P_k$. Let $\mathcal{E}_k$ be the Boolean algebra generated by
the finite-cofinite algebra $\mathcal{E}$ together with the partition sets
$P_1, \ldots, P_k$.

For concreteness: $k = 2$ with $P_E = \{0, 2, 4, \ldots\}$ (evens) and
$P_O = \{1, 3, 5, \ldots\}$ (odds).

**Structure of $\mathcal{E}_k$.** Every element of $\mathcal{E}_k$ is a finite
symmetric difference of elements of the form $P_i \cap F$ and $P_i^c \cup G$
for finite sets $F, G \subseteq \mathbb{N}$. More precisely: each element is a
finite Boolean combination of the partition sets $P_i$ and finite singletons.

An equivalent description: $A \in \mathcal{E}_k$ iff for each $i \in \{1,\ldots,k\}$,
the intersection $A \cap P_i$ is either finite or cofinite in $P_i$.

---

## Step 1: Classification of Finitely Additive Probabilities on $\mathcal{E}_k$

**Claim.** Every normalized finitely additive probability $\mu$ on $\mathcal{E}_k$
is uniquely determined by:

- Singleton masses $a_n = \mu(\{n\}) \geq 0$ for each $n \in \mathbb{N}$, with $\sum_n a_n \leq 1$
  (the sequence is countably supported in general, not merely finitely so).
- Residual pfa weights $\alpha_i \geq 0$ for each piece $P_i$, where $\alpha_i$ is the
  mass assigned to the "cofinite tail" of $P_i$ by the purely finitely additive part of $\mu$.

Normalization requires $\sum_n a_n + \sum_i \alpha_i = 1$.

**Proof sketch.** Fix a piece $P_i$. The algebra induced on subsets of $P_i$ is
the finite-cofinite algebra on $P_i$. By the rung-2 classification, the restriction
$\mu|_{P_i}$ is determined by the singleton masses within $P_i$ (a countably
supported sequence $(a_n)_{n \in P_i}$ with $\sum_{n \in P_i} a_n \leq \mu(P_i)$)
and a residual $\alpha_i \geq 0$. Summing over all pieces gives $\sum_i \alpha_i + \sum_n a_n = 1$.

The purely finitely additive part of $\mu$ is then
$$
\mu_\infty = \sum_{i=1}^k \alpha_i \cdot \ell_i,
$$
where $\ell_i$ is the finite-cofinite charge *restricted to $P_i$* (assigns mass 1
to cofinite-in-$P_i$ subsets of $P_i$, mass 0 to finite subsets of $P_i$, and
mass 0 to subsets of $P_j$ for $j \neq i$).

**Conclusion.** $\mathrm{pfa}(\mathcal{E}_k)$ is spanned by $\{\ell_1, \ldots, \ell_k\}$
and is isomorphic to the $(k-1)$-simplex
$$
\Delta_{k-1} = \left\{ (\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_k) : \alpha_i \geq 0,\, \sum_i \alpha_i = 1 \right\}.
$$

This is genuinely multidimensional for $k \geq 2$. The rung 2 case was $k = 1$
(only one piece, $P_1 = \mathbb{N}$), recovering the one-dimensional $\mathrm{pfa}(\mathcal{E})$.

---

## Step 2: $\sigma$-Additive Probabilities on $\mathcal{E}_k$

A $\sigma$-additive probability on $\mathcal{E}_k$ is a discrete measure
$\mu = \sum_n p_n \delta_n$ with $p_n \geq 0$ and $\sum_n p_n = 1$.

(The argument is the same as for $\mathcal{E}$: $\sigma$-additivity forces the
measure to be determined by its singleton values, and no pfa residual survives.)

On a piece $P_i$, the restriction of such a measure is $\sum_{n \in P_i} p_n \delta_n$,
with "total weight on piece $P_i$" equal to $w_i = \sum_{n \in P_i} p_n \in [0,1]$.

---

## Step 3: Ultralimit Representability on $\mathcal{E}_k$

**Claim.** Every finitely additive probability $\mu = \sum_n p_n \delta_n + \sum_i \alpha_i \ell_i$
on $\mathcal{E}_k$ is a pointwise sequential limit of $\sigma$-additive probabilities
on $\mathcal{E}_k$.

**Construction.** Let $P_i^{\leq m} = \{n \in P_i : n \text{ is the } j\text{-th element of } P_i, j \leq m\}$
(the first $m$ elements of piece $P_i$). Define
$$
\mu_m = \sum_n p_n \delta_n + \sum_{i=1}^k \alpha_i \cdot \frac{1}{m} \sum_{n \in P_i^{\leq m}} \delta_n.
$$

**Verification.** For each fixed $n$:
- If $n$ is in the "atom part": $\mu_m(\{n\}) = p_n$ for all $m$. ✓
- If $n \in P_i$ and $n = P_i^{\leq m_0}(j)$ (the $j$-th element of $P_i$), then
  for $m \geq j$: $\mu_m(\{n\})$ includes the term $\alpha_i / m \to 0$. ✓

For any finite set $F \subseteq \mathbb{N}$:
$$
\mu_m(F) = \sum_{n \in F} p_n + \sum_i \alpha_i \cdot \frac{|F \cap P_i^{\leq m}|}{m} \to \sum_{n \in F} p_n = \mu(F).
$$
(The fraction $|F \cap P_i^{\leq m}|/m \to 0$ since $F$ is finite.)

For a cofinite-in-$P_i$ set $C_i \subseteq P_i$ (i.e., $P_i \setminus C_i$ is finite):
$$
\mu_m(C_i) = \frac{|C_i \cap P_i^{\leq m}|}{m} \cdot \alpha_i + \text{atom contribution} \to \alpha_i \cdot 1 + \mu_{\mathrm{atoms}}(C_i).
$$
(Since $P_i \setminus C_i$ is finite, eventually $P_i^{\leq m} \subseteq C_i$ for large $m$,
so $|C_i \cap P_i^{\leq m}|/m \to 1$.)

General elements of $\mathcal{E}_k$ are finite Boolean combinations of the above;
pointwise convergence for all elements follows from the calculation on singletons,
finite sets, and cofinite-in-$P_i$ sets.

**Result.** The answer is **positive** for all $\mathcal{E}_k$. Every finitely additive
probability on $\mathcal{E}_k$ is a pointwise sequential limit of $\sigma$-additive
probabilities on the same algebra.

---

## Step 4: The Countably-Many-Pieces Extension — RESOLVED (positive)

**Setup.** Partition $\mathbb{N} = \bigsqcup_{i \geq 1} P_i$ with each $P_i$ infinite.
Let $\mathcal{E}_\omega$ be the Boolean algebra generated by $\mathcal{E}$ together
with $\{P_i : i \geq 1\}$.

**Elements of $\mathcal{E}_\omega$.** An element $A \in \mathcal{E}_\omega$ is a
finite Boolean combination of the generators, hence satisfies: for each $i$,
$A \cap P_i$ is finite or cofinite in $P_i$; and only finitely many $i$ have
$A \cap P_i$ cofinite in $P_i$. (An infinite union of whole pieces $P_i$ cannot
be expressed as a finite Boolean combination of the generators.)

**Key structural observation: no second-order pfa residual.** Since elements of
$\mathcal{E}_\omega$ involve only finitely many pieces cofinitely, there is no
element in $\mathcal{E}_\omega$ that "sees" infinitely many pieces from above (other
than $\mathbb{N}$ itself). In particular, the "tail" $\bigcup_{i \geq N} P_i$
is not in $\mathcal{E}_\omega$ for any finite $N$ (except $N = 1$, which is $\mathbb{N}$).
A pfa charge on $\mathcal{E}_\omega$ cannot assign mass to such tails. Consequently,
every pfa probability $\ell$ on $\mathcal{E}_\omega$ is fully accounted for by the
per-piece residuals $(\alpha_i)_{i \geq 1}$, with $\sum_i \alpha_i = 1$ (normalization,
no residual escaping to any "second-order" component). There is no strictly richer
pfa structure here.

**Construction and convergence.** Given $\mu = \sum_n p_n \delta_n + \sum_i \alpha_i \ell_i$
on $\mathcal{E}_\omega$ with $\sum_n p_n + \sum_i \alpha_i = 1$, set
$$
\mu_m = \sum_n p_n \delta_n + \sum_{i=1}^\infty \alpha_i \cdot \frac{1}{m}\sum_{n \in P_i^{\leq m}} \delta_n,
$$
where $P_i^{\leq m}$ denotes the first $m$ elements of $P_i$.

*$\mu_m$ is $\sigma$-additive:* it is a (countable) discrete measure with
$\mu_m(\{n\}) = p_n + \alpha_j/m$ (where $n \in P_j$), summing to
$\sum_n p_n + \sum_i \alpha_i = 1$. ✓

*Pointwise convergence on $\mathcal{E}_\omega$:* Since any $A \in \mathcal{E}_\omega$
involves only finitely many pieces cofinitely, the convergence calculation is
identical to the finite-$k$ case applied piece by piece. ✓

**Result.** Every finitely additive probability on $\mathcal{E}_\omega$ is a
pointwise sequential limit of $\sigma$-additive probabilities on $\mathcal{E}_\omega$.
The answer is **positive**, and no second-order pfa obstruction arises.

---

## Strategic Lesson and Handoff

The calculations above establish that positivity holds across an entire family of
atomic examples — not just the single finite-cofinite algebra. All partition
extensions $\mathcal{E}_k$ and $\mathcal{E}_\omega$ are atomic, and the "spread
mass by piece" construction exploits atomicity directly: the principal ultrafilters
(singletons) are dense in the Stone space, and approximation reduces to density.

**The lesson is strategic as much as mathematical.** These positive results do not
tell us where the difficulty is; they tell us that the difficulty does not lie in
the atomic regime. The genuinely informative next question requires leaving this
family entirely and confronting the non-atomic case, where the "principal locus"
is no longer the right notion and the Stone-space geometry becomes substantially
richer.

That analysis is in `notes/archive/strategy_d_killed/ultralimit_investigation/stone_geometric_translation.md`.

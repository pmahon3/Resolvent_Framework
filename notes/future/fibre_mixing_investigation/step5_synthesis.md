## What is settled: the structural theorem

The following is clean and does not depend on any witness construction.

**Theorem (structural, informal).** In the framework of continuous logic for
metric structures:

1. **(Expressibility)** For each fixed lag $L$ and tolerance $\delta > 0$,
   lag-by-lag fibre mixing (LBL) is expressible as a continuous logic formula
   $\phi_{L,c,\delta}$ in the language of measure-preserving systems with
   observable.

2. **(Łoś preservation)** If each factor $(X_n, \mu_n, T_n, h_n)$ satisfies
   $\phi_{L,c,\delta}$, the ultraproduct satisfies $\phi_{L,c,\delta}$.

3. **(Uniformity gap)** Uniform fibre mixing (UFM) — the Paper IV condition as
   used in Corollary 5.12 — requires a single $c > 0$ working for all
   sufficiently large $L$. This uniformity is infinitary and is not captured
   by any single continuous logic sentence or any countable family of such
   sentences. It escapes Łoś exactly as CE escapes any first-order axiom schema.

4. **(CE parallel)** The structural gap between LBL and UFM is formally
   parallel to the gap between "satisfies CE for the first $k$ sets in every
   decreasing chain" and "satisfies CE." The finite-cofinite charge witnesses
   the CE gap by being individually approximated by σ-additive measures whose
   ultraproduct fails CE. An analogous construction would witness the UFM gap.

**These four points do not depend on the rotation example.** They follow from
the continuous logic analysis in step 3 alone.

## What the rotation witness established before the obstruction

The step 4 computation made a real contribution before hitting the gap:

- **Correct observable type:** fibres must have positive $\mu$-mass for fibre
  mixing to be non-trivial. Continuous $h$ on a manifold makes $\nu_L$
  non-atomic, so $p_z = 0$ everywhere and fibre mixing is vacuous. A discrete
  (finite-partition) observable is necessary.

- **Geometric mechanism for $c(\alpha,L) \to 0$:** when $\alpha < 1/(2L)$,
  the orbit never crosses the partition boundary at 1/2, producing two
  monochromatic dominant fibres with $s(1-s) = 0$. The mechanism is concrete
  and correct.

- **Three-distance theorem as the right tool:** the sizes and positions of
  cylinder-partition atoms for irrational rotations with finite observables
  are exactly governed by the three-distance theorem. This is the right
  quantitative framework for any circle-rotation witness.

## The obstruction to the rotation witness

**Fundamental issue:** With $h = \mathbf{1}_{[0,1/2)}$ on $([0,1), T_\alpha, \text{Leb})$,
the lag-$L$ cylinder partition has $\approx 2L$ atoms of width $\approx 1/(2L)$.
The $\sigma$-algebra approximation error satisfies:

$$\delta_L = \sup_{S \in \mathcal{B}} \inf_{E \in \mathcal{O}_h^{(L)}} \mu(S \triangle E)$$

For the event $S = \bigcup_{k} [a_k + 1/(4L),\, a_k + 3/(4L))$ (a set that
cuts each atom near its midpoint), $\delta_L \geq 1/4$ for every $L$. The
$\sigma$-algebras $\mathcal{O}_h^{(L)}$ do not separate the circle: a binary
observable generates coarse cylinder partitions that never approximate all of
$\mathcal{B}$.

More precisely: the $\sigma$-algebra generated by $\{h \circ T^k : k \geq 0\}$
equals the tail $\sigma$-algebra of the binary symbolic system on the circle,
which is strictly coarser than $\mathcal{B}$ for any irrational rotation.
(Irrational rotations are not topologically conjugate to full shifts.)

**Consequence:** $\delta_L \not\to 0$ for any $L$, so the fibre mixing corollary
($\delta_L \to 0 \iff H_2(\nu_L) \to \infty$) is vacuously true — it never
triggers. The binary-observable irrational rotation is a system where the
hypothesis $\delta_L \to 0$ never holds, making fibre mixing irrelevant.

**This means the rotation example, as constructed, does not live in the
relevant regime for the fibre mixing corollary.** The corollary is only
interesting when reconstruction is possible (or nearly so) — when
$\delta_L \to 0$ under good conditions. The binary-observable rotation
never reconstructs.

## Where fibre mixing is genuinely non-trivial

From Paper IV, the relevant regime is:

- $(X, \mathcal{B}, \mu, T)$ a measure-preserving system on a smooth manifold
- $h \in C^2(X)$ with $dh \neq 0$ $\mu$-a.e. (hypothesis G2)
- $T$ has no short periodic orbits in $\operatorname{supp}(\mu)$ (hypothesis G1)

Under (G1)–(G2), $\Phi_h^{(L)}$ is full rank a.e. and (for $L \geq d-1$) is
injective a.e. — so $p_z = 0$ for a.e. $z$ and $(\mu \otimes \mu)(R_L) = 0$.
Fibre mixing is vacuous here too (no $\varepsilon$-large fibres).

**The genuinely non-trivial regime** is when (G1)–(G2) fail partially: when
the delay map has genuine non-injectivity on a positive-measure set. This happens:

1. **Finite systems:** $X$ finite, $T$ a permutation, $h: X \to \mathbb{R}$
   not injective. Fibres are genuine preimage sets with positive counting measure.

2. **Pre-reconstruction lags:** at small $L$ (below the Takens lag $L_0 \leq d-1$),
   the delay map is not yet injective and fibres have positive measure.

3. **Systems below the generic threshold:** $h$ not generic (violates G2),
   so the delay map is not full rank on a positive-measure set even at large $L$.

**Fibre mixing is most interesting at regime (2):** the transition from
non-injective (large fibres, positive $(\mu \otimes \mu)(R_L)$) to injective
(singleton fibres, zero collision probability). Fibre mixing is the condition
that makes the algebraic witness $\delta_L$ and the entropy witness $H_2(\nu_L)$
track each other during this transition.

## The right witness setting

For the irreducibility argument, the witness sequence needs systems where:

1. Fibre mixing holds with per-lag constant $c_n(L) > 0$ (each system is
   individually fibre-mixing)
2. $c_n(L) \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$ for each fixed $L$
3. The limit/ultraproduct has no uniform lower bound — UFM fails

The correct setting is **finite systems approaching a degenerate limit:**

**Candidate:** Let $X_n = \{0, 1, \ldots, n-1\}$ (a set of $n$ states),
$T_n$ a cyclic permutation (rotation by 1), $\mu_n$ uniform measure ($1/n$ at
each state), and $h_n: X_n \to \{0,1\}$ the binary observable
$h_n(k) = \mathbf{1}_{k < n/2}$.

- Each $(X_n, T_n, \mu_n)$ is ergodic (the cyclic group acts transitively).
- At lag $L < n$: the delay vector $\Phi_{h_n}^{(L)}(k) = (h_n(k), h_n(k+1), \ldots, h_n(k+L-1))$
  maps $k$ to a binary word of length $L$. Fibres are preimage sets of binary
  words — sets of states $k$ with the same $L$-long binary trajectory.
- For $n$ large relative to $L$: each binary word $z \in \{0,1\}^L$ appears
  approximately $n/2^L$ times in the orbit (equidistribution for cyclic groups),
  so $p_z \approx 1/2^L$.
- The conditional measure $\mu_z$ is uniform on the $\approx n/2^L$ states in
  the fibre.

**Fibre mixing constant:** For $S^* = \{k : k < n/2\}$ (the left half),
$\mu_z(S^*)$ = fraction of fibre in $S^*$. For a generic word $z$, the fibre
wraps around the boundary $n/2$ and $\mu_z(S^*) \approx 1/2$, giving
$s(1-s) \approx 1/4$. The condition $1/4 \geq c \cdot (1/2^L)$ holds with
$c \leq 2^{L-2}$. This grows with $L$ — the wrong direction.

**Problem:** finite cyclic systems have $c$ growing with $L$ (since fibres become
small, and each small fibre generically straddles the boundary in proportion).
The constant does not deteriorate.

**Revised candidate:** Systems where the constant deteriorates because the
fibres become large and monochromatic, not because they become small and
generic. This requires a different degeneration mechanism.

## The right degeneration mechanism

The step 4 mechanism (large monochromatic fibres when $\alpha$ is small) is
geometrically correct. The problem was not the mechanism but the setting
(continuous irrational rotation with binary observable does not reconstruct).

**The witness needs a system where:**
- Reconstruction does occur ($\delta_L \to 0$)
- The system is fibre-mixing (large fibres are balanced, not monochromatic)
- But the balance degenerates as the system approaches a limit

**Natural candidate:** A sequence of Bernoulli shifts $B(p_n, 1-p_n)$ with
$p_n \to 0$ (or $p_n \to 1$). As the Bernoulli parameter degenerates:

- Each $B(p_n, 1-p_n)$ is ergodic (Bernoulli implies mixing).
- For the observable $h = \pi_0$ (projection to the 0th coordinate), the
  delay vectors are i.i.d. binary words.
- Fibres of the delay map at lag $L$ are cylinder sets $[z_0, \ldots, z_{L-1}]$
  with mass $p_n^{k}(1-p_n)^{L-k}$ where $k = \sum_i z_i$.
- As $p_n \to 0$: the dominant fibre is $(0, 0, \ldots, 0)$ with mass
  $(1-p_n)^L \to 1$. This fibre is entirely in $\{h = 0\}$, so
  $\mu_z(S^*) = 0$ for $S^* = \{h = 1\}$. The dominant fibre is monochromatic.

**For fibre mixing on $S^* = \{h = 1\}$:**
- The dominant fibre $(0,\ldots,0)$ has $\mu_z(S^*) = 0$, so $s(1-s) = 0$.
  This fibre makes fibre mixing fail.

But wait: the dominant fibre $(0,\ldots,0)$ has $p_z = (1-p_n)^L \to 1$.
Is this fibre $\varepsilon$-large? Yes, for any $\varepsilon < (1-p_n)^L$.
So fibre mixing fails on the dominant fibre for any $S^*$ concentrated on
$\{h = 1\}$ words. The Bernoulli shift with $p_n \to 0$ fails fibre mixing.

**But:** Does $B(p_n, 1-p_n)$ with $p_n \to 0$ have $\delta_L \to 0$?
Yes — the delay map $\Phi_h^{(L)}$ separates all points since the shift is
expansive. So $\delta_L = 0$ for all large $L$, and fibre mixing is supposed
to be vacuously satisfied. **Contradiction.**

**Resolution:** When $\delta_L = 0$ (reconstruction), the corollary is trivially
true and fibre mixing is not needed. The fibre mixing hypothesis only matters
when $\delta_L \to 0$ from above — i.e., in the pre-reconstruction regime.

## Honest assessment

The investigation has reached a point where the structural theorem (LBL vs UFM,
Łoś structure, CE parallel) is clean, but the witness construction requires more
careful thought about the regime. The difficulty:

1. In the reconstruction regime ($\delta_L \to 0$), fibres become singletons and
   fibre mixing is vacuous — no witness needed, nothing to prove.

2. In the non-reconstruction regime ($\delta_L \not\to 0$), fibre mixing may
   fail but the corollary is vacuously true — no content.

3. The interesting regime is the **transition** — systems approaching but not
   yet achieving reconstruction. Here fibres are shrinking (but still positive
   measure), and fibre mixing determines whether $\delta_L$ and $H_2(\nu_L)$
   track each other during the approach.

**What the witness must be:** A sequence of systems $(X_n, T_n, \mu_n, h_n)$
where:
- $\delta_L^{(n)} \to 0$ as $L \to \infty$ (each system reconstructs)
- Each system is fibre-mixing (the corollary applies to each)
- The fibre mixing constants $c_n \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$
- The ultraproduct is a system where fibre mixing fails: $\delta_L \to 0$ but
  the fibres are too monochromatic to make $H_2(\nu_L) \to \infty$

This is a more subtle construction than previously thought. It requires a
system that reconstructs (so $\delta_L \to 0$) but where the approach to
reconstruction is barely detected by the entropy witness (fibres remain nearly
monochromatic throughout).

## Revised next steps

1. **Find a system that reconstructs but barely.** A system where $\delta_L \to 0$
   but fibres at intermediate lags are nearly monochromatic — the transition is
   slow and the entropy witness $H_2(\nu_L)$ lags behind $\delta_L$.

2. **Assess whether such systems exist.** Is it possible for a reconstructing
   system to have nearly monochromatic fibres at all intermediate lags? Or does
   reconstruction force good fibre balance (which would make fibre mixing
   derivable from reconstructibility — a positive result)?

3. **Check if ergodicity + reconstruction implies UFM.** If ergodic reconstructing
   systems always have uniformly balanced fibres, UFM would be derivable from
   ergodicity + reconstruction. That is a different (and important) positive result.

4. **Don't discard the structural theorem.** The LBL/UFM separation and the
   continuous logic analysis stand independently of the witness.

## Working hypothesis

The difficulty of finding a witness may not be incidental. It may be that:

> Every ergodic reconstructing system satisfies UFM with a constant $c$ that
> depends only on the speed of reconstruction ($\delta_L \to 0$ rate) and the
> mixing rate of the system.

If true, fibre mixing is **derivable** from ergodicity + reconstruction, not
irreducible. The investigation would then yield a positive result (derivability
in the reconstruction regime) rather than the conjectured negative result
(irreducibility). This would require reframing the CE parallel:

- CE: not derivable (irreducible)
- UFM: derivable from ergodicity + reconstruction? (open)

This is a live possibility that deserves explicit investigation rather than
being swept aside in pursuit of a witness.

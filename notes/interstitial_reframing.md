# Interstitial reframing — residual freedom → elimination

## The through-line

At each stage, what appears to be a construction is revealed to be a constraint, and what appears to be an assumption is revealed to be a consequence. The observer does not construct probability, prediction, dynamics, or the minimal window — each is forced by the coherence of their prior commitments and the structure of the world they are embedded in.

The unifying framing device: each interstitial names the *residual freedom* remaining after the previous chapter, then shows the next chapter eliminates it.

The current −1→0 is the most developed of the four (gap-as-horizon, regulative ideal, realizability). Two options:

**(a)** Leave mostly intact; add a light closing gesture that sets the thread for the remaining three.

**(b)** Rewrite with the same residual-freedom → elimination structure as the others, for uniformity.

Option (a) preserves what's already good and treats −1→0 as the philosophical anchor. Option (b) creates more uniformity but risks losing something.

## Philosophical content per transition

### −1→0: Discriminability → Probability

Residual freedom after −1: the per-level σ-additive extensions exist (CE forces them), but they need not assemble into a single global measure. The observer's local commitments need not add up to a coherent world.

Elimination: realizability (EvalSurjective) + sequential upper-directedness force global assembly. Neither is a philosophical choice — realizability says the world is coherent for the observer (every consistent assignment is realised); directedness says the observer's queries have joint refinements.

Existing content to preserve: gap-as-horizon, σ-algebra as forced structure, regulative ideal paragraph, realizability as architectural condition.

### 0→1: Probability → Prediction

Residual freedom after 0: P is forced and canonical, but the observer still appears free in how their measurement relates to the future. Given Q and P, it is unclear what structure mediates between present observation and future expectation — or whether there is a minimal such structure at all.

Elimination: Paper 1 shows the predictive content of any query is entirely fixed by P. The minimal predictive query Q_* is not chosen — it is read off from the structure of (Q, P). The observer's future-directedness is not a free act; it is the form their present measurement takes under the constraint of coherent probability.

Philosophical register: intentionality — the observer is constitutively oriented toward the future, but that orientation is determined, not projected.

### 1→2: Prediction → Dynamics

Residual freedom after 1: the predictive content of each query at each time is characterised, but the relationship between predictive content at different times is unconstrained. It is unclear whether the observer's prediction at time t and their prediction at time s cohere — whether there is a consistent temporal structure at all.

Elimination: Paper 2 derives the semigroup K_{t+s} = K_t ∘ K_s as a theorem, not an assumption. Temporal coherence of prediction is forced by the same observable compatibility that forced P. Koopman-Perron duality: the observable-layer dynamics (K_t) and state-space dynamics (Koopman operator) are dual under L²(P) — neither is primary, both are aspects of the same structure.

Philosophical register: the two descriptions (observer-layer and state-space) are not competitors but dual aspects of one situation. The program does not eliminate the state space; it shows that neither layer is the ground.

### 2→3: Dynamics → Computation

Residual freedom after 2: the semigroup is characterised abstractly, for any query system satisfying the program's hypotheses. The form of the queries and the structure of the dynamics are unspecified. It is unclear whether the abstract operator K_t is accessible to a real observer with bounded, local, temporal discriminative capacity.

Elimination: Paper 3 shows the delay query is the natural query for an observer embedded in a temporal process with only local sensor access. The Markov order m(τ, P) — how much history the observer needs — is determined by P, not chosen. The approximation K_t^N → K_t is the formal expression of learning: the observer is inside time, building an approximation through accumulated experience.

Philosophical register: the minimal sufficient window is not chosen by the observer; it is forced by the measure and the dynamics jointly. The observer and the world together determine the minimum history required.

## Notes on register

- The philosophical content should be available to a reader who looks for it, without being foregrounded as philosophy.
- The language of *necessity* vs *assumption*, *forced* vs *chosen* is already present in the current interstitials — the reframing makes it the explicit structural device rather than isolated sentences.
- No phenomenological vocabulary needed; the Merleau-Pontian resonance is structural, not terminological.

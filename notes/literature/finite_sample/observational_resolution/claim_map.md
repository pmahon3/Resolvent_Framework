# Claim Map: Observational Resolution Dimension

*Seed note - 2026-05-02*

## Purpose

This note is a tactical map for positioning observational resolution dimension
without overclaiming.  Each entry separates:

- the claim we may want to use;
- the closest existing literature;
- what is already known there;
- what is distinctive in the observational programme;
- the risk if the claim is stated too broadly.

## Map

## Safe Programme Claim

The safe broad claim is:

> Many literatures already define dimension as growth of distinguishable
> alternatives per unit scale, cost, divergence, or resource.  The
> observational programme supplies a common source for the alternatives
> ($\mathcal G_k$), empirical witnesses ($H$, collision, covering, occupancy),
> and valuation ($\Lambda$), and asks which such exponent controls
> reconstruction from finite observation.

The rate itself is not new.  The classical form

$$
n^{-s/(2s+d)}
$$

and its squared-risk variant

$$
n^{-2s/(2s+d)}
$$

belong to standard nonparametric bias-variance and minimax theory.  Nor is it
new that ambient Euclidean dimension can be replaced by intrinsic, fractal,
doubling, local, or metric dimensions.  The proposed contribution is the
explanatory factorization:

$$
\frac{\text{distinguishability growth}}
{\text{valuation growth}}
\quad\leadsto\quad
\text{effective dimension}
\quad\leadsto\quad
\text{standard rate}.
$$

Equivalently: the rate is classical; the programme asks which observable
refinement, which distinguishability entropy, and which valuation of refinement
produce the effective dimension entering that classical rate.

This is strong enough to be interesting and weak enough to survive contact with
the existing literature.

## Unsafe Claim Forms

Avoid:

- "We discovered the rate $n^{-s/(2s+d)}$."
- "We derive fractal dimension from observation."
- "Topology is irrelevant."
- "The rate depends on Hausdorff dimension."
- "Collision probability is the dimension."
- "Information geometry solves the valuation problem."
- "The same $D$ unifies all mathematical dimensions."

Replace with:

- "The rate is classical; the contribution is the observational factorization
  of the effective dimension parameter."
- "The relevant exponent is the one induced by the chosen observable
  refinement and valuation."
- "In smooth regimes this exponent often agrees with manifold dimension."
- "In self-similar regimes it agrees with the usual scaling dimension when the
  observable refinement follows the self-similar construction."
- "Collision entropy is one empirical proxy for finite refinements, not a
  universal substitute for covering dimension."

## Citation Anchors

- Kolmogorov and Tikhomirov: epsilon entropy/capacity of function classes.
- Yang and Barron: minimax rates from metric entropy.
- Falconer and Mattila: fractal and geometric-measure dimensions.
- Wu and Verdu: Renyi information dimension as an operational compression
  limit.
- Lindenstrauss and Tsukamoto: metric mean dimension and rate-distortion
  dimension.
- Manning, Young, Ledrappier-Young: entropy, Lyapunov exponents, and
  dimension; Young is the key measure-relative benchmark
  $\operatorname{HD}(\mu)=h_\mu(f)(1/\lambda_1+1/|\lambda_2|)$ for surface
  diffeomorphisms.
- Coates-Gelfert: non-statistical neutral dynamics where generic basins can be
  full Hausdorff dimension but zero Lebesgue measure and zero topological
  entropy.
- Amari; Ay-Jost-Le-Schwachhofer: divergence and Fisher/information geometry.
- Kpotufe: local intrinsic dimension and nonparametric rates.
- Lutz and Mayordomo line: resource-bounded/effective dimension.
- Kigami and Lapidus line: spectral/fractal zeta dimension analogues.
- Lawvere: cost/enriched metric viewpoint.

## 0. Puzzle first ⟦HAND — unverified⟧

Two sites draw answers from **independent** sources. For questions A and B, P(X∈A, Y∈B) = P(X∈A)P(Y∈B). Yet they **always agree**: X=Y. What do they agree on?

- **σ-additive:** they agree only on a constant. Independent plus equal forces a Dirac measure.
- **Finitely additive (sites ℕ-valued, all subsets askable):** X=Y gives s(A×Ω) = s(A×A) = s_X(A)s_Y(A). So s_X(A) = s_X(A)². The marginal is therefore an **ultrafilter** p. If it is non-constant, p is free.
- The ultrafilter p is realised by s(E) = p-lim_n 1_E(n,n).

So "consensus no one holds" is a **common value at infinity**. Every finite question "is the value ≤ N?" gets the answer no.

This is an **ideal point**, not a pointless state. It is Kirman–Sondermann's invisible dictator moved to the sites. The centre does not vanish. It is idealised, pushed off to infinity: a hidden idealisation (WHY Q3).

## 1. Scoping fact (decides where the seam can live)

With **finite alphabets** per site, every finitely additive state on the cylinder algebra (the finite questions) is σ-additive. Cylinders are clopen in a compact space, so a countable disjoint union of cylinders that is itself a cylinder is a finite union. Carathéodory then extends the state uniquely [M; standard, e.g. the proof of Kolmogorov extension]. Consequences:

- On finite alphabets, the finitely additive / σ-additive split **cannot appear on finite questions**.
- Consensus "at infinity" there (for example "eventually constant", or the free-ultrafilter majority) is a **tail event outside the carrier**. Its value is a free choice of extension, anywhere in [inner, outer] content (Łoś–Marczewski 1949; Bhaskara Rao & Bhaskara Rao, Ch. 3 §3.3, theorem number unverified).
- Even iid coins, with no shared sources at all, admit finitely additive extensions that make "eventually constant" certain. A dense tail set has outer content 1. ⟦HAND⟧
- So on finite alphabets the consensus is **not generated by anything**, local or central. It is an unaskable joint question that someone adds by fiat (WHY Q2).
- The seam exists only for **infinite alphabets** (ℕ-valued sites, as in the ring witness) or for carriers that include tail questions.
- Dubins–Freedman 1979 give a finitely additive exchangeable probability on {0,1}^ℕ that is not a finitely additive mixture of σ-additive ones. Per point 1 it must sit on an algebra larger than the cylinders. Not verified which [snip].

## 3. Verdict on the candidate alignment

The candidate: σ-additive agreement ↔ generated by local sources plus a centre; finitely additive "ideal" agreement ↔ generated by nothing local; pointless consensus as a "centre in time pushed to infinity".

- **σ-additive half: OWNED and trivial.** Kolmogorov 0–1 for block factors gives the source version. de Finetti / Hewitt–Savage gives the centre as a mixture. Georgii Ch. 7–8 and Föllmer give the interaction version.
- **Finitely additive half: OWNED, as social choice.** "Consensus no one holds = free ultrafilter" is Kirman–Sondermann. "Locally or finitely determined ⇒ a visible centre" is Ulam (σ) and Mihara (computable).
- **"Pointless consensus": FALSE as framed (for exact consensus).**
  - With finite alphabets nothing is generated at all (§1).
  - With ℕ-valued sites the minimal example is an **ideal point** (§0), not a pointless state.
  - This repeats the recorded correction in `glued_carriers_taxonomy.json`: "the witness can be an ideal point, so the σ-loss is caused by finite additivity, not pointlessness".
- **"Centre in time pushed to infinity" lens: OWNED in σ-additive form** (tail / Martin boundary). In finitely additive form it reduces to a "centre in space at infinity" (a free ultrafilter).
- **Overall: OWNED** (one conditional residue below).

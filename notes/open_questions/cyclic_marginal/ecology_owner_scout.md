reviewed: false. 2026-11-05.

Caveat: web search only returned abstracts/snippets; NO methods section was read for any source below. All verdicts are "not found in search", not "absent".

## 1. Verdicts
- (a) Test of cross-method compatibility (one joint over species presence vs. several partial-species margins, with TV budget): NOT FOUND as a test. PARTLY OWNED in spirit: ecology owns (i) a *model-assumed* latent state z (multi-method occupancy) with GOF checks, and (ii) a *consistency test between data sources* inside one integrated model (Tenan 2017). Neither asks whether a joint over the species exists; both presuppose it.
- (b) Calibrating epsilon from biology (turnover, detection, method shift): PARTLY OWNED as *parameters*: method-specific detection probabilities and availability are estimated, not preset as a budget. A pre-registered TV budget is not found.
- (c) Data shape (each method sees a different species subset at shared sites): REAL, common, but usually not as a marginal problem. Examples (snippet level): Scotland rewilding site (eDNA x4 sample types + passive acoustic + cameras; PAM best for birds/bats, eDNA small mammals; NERC nora 542177); Gaoligongshan China (cameras + leech iDNA + water eDNA; 8 species by all three); Foreste Casentinesi Italy (31 sites, soil eDNA vs cameras); Michigan installation (Ecol Evol 2024, cameras vs soil eDNA); Borneo leech-iDNA + cameras (Abrams et al. 2019 J Appl Ecol dataset, "Shifting up a gear with iDNA"). Caveat: in these, methods overlap on shared species (mammals) and the data are species x site detection histories, i.e. full joint within a method; the pair-per-method triangle is a *design choice* of coarse-graining, not how data arrive. The triangle needs each species pair seen jointly by exactly one method.

## 2. Per area
1. Multi-method occupancy. Nichols et al. 2008 J Appl Ecol 45:1321 (multi-scale occupancy, multiple detection methods; skunk/salamander) [abstract]. Estimates method-specific detection; assumes one latent occupancy. Gap: fits, does not test pooling across methods beyond model selection. Also coastal-marine eDNA multi-scale multispecies occupancy with Bayesian p-value GOF (PLOS ONE 2020, PMC7082047) [snippet]. Gap: GOF of a fitted model, not existence of a joint.
2. Co-occurrence. Blanchet, Cazelles & Gravel 2020 Ecol Lett 23:1050 [abstract]: argues co-occurrence != interaction (sampling, probability). Nothing on realisability of pairwise tables across sources found. JSDM paper (Environ Ecol Stat s10651-021-00486-4, odds ratios) [snippet]. Gap: joint is modelled, never checked for existence.
3. Data integration. Tenan, Pedrini, Bragalanti, Groff & Sutherland 2017 PLOS ONE 12(10):e0185588 [abstract]: integrated SCR with "model-based test for data consistency" across structured/telemetry/opportunistic data. Nearest ecological relative of our test: it asks whether sources agree on shared parameters, with data-type differences allowed. Gap: same parameters, same quantity observed by each source; no pairwise-margin / joint-existence structure. Simmonds & O'Hara 2020 and Schank 2019 ISDM sensitivity [snippet]: integration may not beat separate fits; no compatibility diagnostic found. Isaac 2020 TREE, Miller 2019, Fletcher 2019: not retrieved.
4. Fusion / statistical matching. D'Orazio-Di Zio-Scanu 2006 and R StatMatch (Frechet.bounds.cat) [doc snippet]: Frechet bounds for cells of tables from separate samples; uncertainty shown, no incompatibility test. No ecology use of Frechet/Bell-type bounds on co-occurrence found; Frechet substitution max(0,psi1+psi2-1) <= joint <= min(psi1,psi2) appears only as textbook. (Also on shelf: Capotorti 2011, already recorded.)
5. Bell/contextuality/Vorob'ev in ecology: nothing; all hits physics/maths. Consistent with the three earlier ecology notes in this folder.

## 4. Most dangerous owner
Tenan et al. 2017 (data-consistency test in integrated models) and its ISDM descendants: an ecologist reading our test will say "we already test whether sources are consistent". Settle by reading its Methods: is the test a likelihood-ratio of shared vs source-specific parameters (then it assumes the latent object exists and our joint-existence/LP-duality test is different), or something marginal-compatibility-like. Second: fisheries stock assessment "data conflict" literature (Francis 2011) and Royle-Link false-positive occupancy, unsearched.

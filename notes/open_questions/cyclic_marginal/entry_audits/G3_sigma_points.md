## 1. thm.sigma_forces_points

**Stripped statement.** Let Ω = X^V with V finite. Let D be the σ-class (Dynkin system) generated by
the pullbacks of P(X²) along the pair projections of a window graph. If X is countable, every
σ-additive {0,1}-state on D is a Dirac δ_ω.

The proof has three steps:
1. Each window's countable fibre partition has exactly one true fibre.
2. Fibres on windows that share a coordinate meet, so the picks glue to one ω.
3. {E ∈ D : s(E) = δ_ω(E)} is itself a σ-class containing the generators, so it is all of D. No
   π-system is needed, because the agreement set of two σ-additive states is closed under complement
   and countable disjoint union.

### Pure verdict: **KNOWN (OWNED)**: folklore, with the exact argument printed

The owners print the same argument verbatim:
- **Navara–Pták, Čas. pěst. mat. 108 (1983) 225–229, proof of Thm 1, (1)⇒(2).** *Read in full (shelf).*
  - The argument: m(f⁻¹{a}) = m(g⁻¹{b}) = 1; take x ∈ f⁻¹{a} ∩ g⁻¹{b}; then "the measure m
    restricted to A_{f,g} must be concentrated at x because if two measures agree on all generators
    of a σ-class, they have to agree on the entire σ-class."
  - The match is step for step: countably valued observables, a Dirac on each generating σ-algebra,
    a common point, then generator uniqueness.
  - The only difference is where nonemptiness of the intersection comes from. There it comes from
    integral additivity. Here it comes from the product structure (fibres agreeing on a shared
    coordinate meet in X^V).
- **Navara, Math. Slovaca 34 (1984), Proposition (proof).** *Read in full (shelf).*
  - The same argument appears, with the explicit maxim "the measures on σ-classes extend uniquely
    from the generators!"
  - The same paper (Theorem proof) also states the Borel case: "any two-valued measure on B(ℝ) must
    be concentrated in a point."
- **Dravecký–Šipoš, Math. Slovaca 30 (1980), Cor. 3 context.** *Read in full.* Point measures on a
  σ-class are the trivially additive case. Adjacent only.
- **Step 1 (countable)** is the textbook fact that a σ-additive 0–1 measure on a countable partition
  picks one cell. This is folklore, and the 2026-10-08 scout already recorded it as such.
- **Step 2** is the sheaf property of the outcome assignment U ↦ X^U: compatible deterministic local
  sections glue. That is standard in Abramsky–Brandenburger 2011 (secondary; not re-read).

**Hostile boundary cases** show that the coordinate-window hypothesis does real work (it powers
step 2):
- **Navara–Pták 1983, Q×Q example** (read). Ω is countable and the σ-class is generated by
  A_f ∪ A_g ∪ A_{f+g}. It carries a σ-additive two-valued state that is *not* concentrated: the
  level sets x = 1/3, y = 1/3, x + y = 1/2 have empty triple intersection. The generator f+g is not a
  coordinate window, so its fibres need not meet the others.
- **Burešová–Pták 2024, "4even"** (read). Ω = {1,2,3,4}, with the even-cardinality sets. It has a
  non-Dirac two-valued state. The parity generators do not glue.
- So "σ-additive ⇒ point on countable Ω" is **false for general σ-classes**. The entry's content is
  exactly "coordinate windows glue", and that is the owned NP83/N84 template plus the product
  structure.

### ⚠ Scope correction (the substantive finding)

The current `scope` line, "fails for uncountable alphabets", **does not follow and is wrong as
stated.**

- Window questions are *arbitrary* A ⊆ X², so each window σ-algebra is all of P(X²).
- A σ-additive {0,1}-state on P(S) is a σ-complete ultrafilter on S.
- That ultrafilter is principal unless |S| ≥ the least measurable cardinal (Ulam 1930).
  - Standard route: the least cardinal carrying a non-principal σ-complete ultrafilter is
    measurable, by Jech, *Set Theory* (2003) Lemma 10.2, plus transfer along injections.
  - Read level: **secondary**. Jech ch. 10 notes were seen via search; the book was not opened.
- So steps 1–3 go through **for every X of non-measurable cardinality, including X = ℝ, in ZFC**.
  The countable-partition step is a limit of the *proof*, not of the *theorem*.
- With Borel windows on a standard Borel X, the theorem also holds. Navara 1984 states the Borel(ℝ)
  case, and `collision_vs_generation.md` line ~290 already uses it.

**Failure only at measurable |X| ⟦HAND⟧.**
- Take a κ-complete non-principal ultrafilter U on X. The product ultrafilter U^V on X^V (finite V)
  is σ-complete.
- Restricted to D, it is a σ-additive state that is **not a point**: every {x_u = a} is false.
- It **is** an ideal point, not a pointless state.
- So even at measurable cardinals, σ-additivity does not produce *pointless* states by this route.
  Whether a twisted (pointless) σ-additive state exists at measurable |X| is the Product Ulam Carrier
  question of `psi_ZFC`. It is not settled here.

## 2. thm.basic

The lemma has three parts:
- (a) proper differences stay in D; s is monotone; s(A∩B) = 1 when A, B, A∩B ∈ D are all true;
- (b) s is an ideal point iff its true sets have the FIP.

### Pure verdict: **KNOWN (standard)**
- **Proper differences.** Navara–Pták 1983 Def. 1 remark: "if A, B ∈ A, B ⊇ A then B − A ∈ A"
  (read). This is textbook Dynkin-system algebra.
- **(a)** follows in one line from proper differences and monotonicity. It is folklore in the
  concrete-logic literature (compatibility of A, B ⇒ the state is multiplicative on 0–1 values).
- **(b), forward:** an ultrafilter has the FIP, so the true sets do too.
- **(b), backward:** the ultrafilter lemma (Tarski 1930) extends the FIP family to an ultrafilter W.
  For E ∈ D false, E^c is true, so E^c ∈ W and E ∉ W.
- This is the two-valued case of coherent ⇔ extendable. It is owned by `own.coherence_extension`
  (Bhaskara Rao Thm 3.2.10; secondary, book body not opened).

### Type 3 / 4: FAIL / FAIL. Infrastructure, correctly labelled "standard" already.

### Proposed `status`
```
"status": "OWNED (standard): Dynkin proper differences (Navara-Ptak 1983 Def.1 remark); (b) = ultrafilter lemma, two-valued case of coherence/extension (own.coherence_extension)"
```

## 3. fact.finite_alphabet_points

### Pure verdict: **KNOWN (folklore)**
- With finite X and finite V, Ω is finite, so D is finite.
- Every disjoint union in D is finite, so finite additivity is σ-additivity. The fact is then the
  special case X finite of §1. It is owned by the same NP83/N84 template, and step 1 is the trivial
  finite partition.
- **Hidden-assumption check.** It uses "V finite". That is fixed in the definition (`pointless_coherence.tex`
  App. A, Ω = X^V, V finite), so it is fine. With V infinite, Ω is not finite and finite additivity
  would not force points. Keep "V finite" visible if the entry is ever reused for infinite rings.
- **Same boundary caveat.** Burešová–Pták's 4even is a finite σ-class with a non-Dirac two-valued
  state. Coordinate windows are load-bearing here too.
- The A = 2 enumeration (`triangle_finite_states_check.py`: 8 states, 8 points) is a correct sanity
  check, not evidence of novelty.

## Bearing on WHY.md (one line)

This is a **hidden-idealisation** finding. Demanding σ-additivity (a completed answer at infinity)
forces every consistent set of local answers to be one world, by a 1983 argument. So the interesting
case, local views that add up without a world, can only live in the finitely additive layer. The
correction is that the cut sits at measurable cardinals, not at "uncountable".

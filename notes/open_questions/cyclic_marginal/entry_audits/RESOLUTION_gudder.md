## What the sources contain (relevant parts)

**G84 (survey, SIAM pages 71–89).**
- **Ex 3.1 (p.73).**
  - A = [0,4], B = [2,5], C = [0,1]∪[2,3]∪[5,6]. Λ = {∅, [0,6], A, B, C, Aᶜ, Bᶜ, Cᶜ}. The only disjoint
    pairs are complementary pairs, so Λ is a σ-class.
  - μ = 1 on A, B and C, μ = 3 on their complements, μ(Ω) = 4.
  - Quote: "μ([0, 6]) = 4 > 3 = μ(A) + μ(B) + μ(C)".
  - A finite, non-two-valued, non-subadditive measure that does not extend to Σ(Λ).
- **Ex 3.2 (Prather, p.73).** On 6 points, the sets of size 0, 3 or 6, with a measure that has no signed
  extension. **Thms 3.1–3.3** cover Prather and Gudder–Marchand cyclic-translate classes: signed extensions.
- **Ex 4.2 (p.75).**
  - The "σ-class product": Λ = {A₁×Ω₂, Ω₁×A₂}.
  - Quote: "Then Λ is a σ-class on Ω₃ which is not a σ-algebra."
  - Thm 4.1 [13]: a collection lies in a sub-σ-algebra iff it is compatible.
- **Ex 6.1 (p.77).**
  - Ω = ℤ×ℤ. R_m, C_n and D_t are the rows, columns and diagonals, and Λ = Λ_R ∪ Λ_C ∪ Λ_D.
  - μ(C₀) = μ(R₀) = μ(D₁) = 1, and all other rows, columns and diagonals get 0.
  - Quote: "Now ∫f dμ = ∫g dμ = 0, but ∫(f + g) dμ = 1."
  - μ is two-valued and σ-additive on each block, and pointless (R₀ ∩ C₀ ∩ D₁ = ∅).
- **Thm 6.1 (p.77, proof sketch pp.77–79).** Additivity holds for two summable simple functions, via
  Gleason's "magic sequence" and a count of row, column and diagonal adjunctions.
- **Ex 6.3 (p.80).** Three characteristic functions on the Prather class, with non-additive integral.
- **§§7–9.** Thm 7.2: a logic admits hidden variables iff it is a σ-class. Then L(H) and Gleason, and Pitowsky's
  spin model. Nothing on products of three coordinates, pair windows, Dynkin closures of overlapping
  coordinate algebras, or finitely additive (non-σ) states.

**ZG85 (pp.42–51).**
- **§1 (p.43).**
  - The general counterexample is credited to [6, 12] = Gudder–Zerbe, J. Math. Phys. 1981, and Zerbe's
    thesis, 1979.
  - Quote: "the idea which makes it work requires the functions to be nonsimple".
- **§2.**
  - 𝒞 is the σ-class generated by the rows R_i, columns C_j and diagonals D_t of a **finite** N×N grid.
  - Lemmas 1–3 build sets S_λ, staircase-shaped and depending jointly on (i,j), by finitely many disjoint
    unions and proper differences of rows, columns and diagonals.
  - Corollary (p.47): S_λ ∈ 𝒞 iff S_{λ₀} ∈ 𝒞, and S₀ = ∅. So every S_λ is buildable on the finite grid.
  - Lemma 4: the additivity equation Σ i rᵢ + Σ j cⱼ = Σ t d_t holds.
- **Thm 6 (p.49).** "If f and g are summable simple functions, then" the integral is additive. Proved by
  rational approximation (Lemma 5).
- **§4 (pp.50–51).**
  - Ω = {1,…,6}, a 10-set σ-class, μ = 1 on {1,2,3}, {1,2,4}, {1,3,6} and {2,3,5}.
  - Quote: "The following example shows that in general the answer is negative."
  - The answer concerns three summands. μ is two-valued, and the four μ=1 sets have empty intersection.

### 1. `thm.triangle_pointless`: Gudder lineage NOT OWNED
- **Ex 3.1 → NOT.**
  - It is finite and not two-valued; it is a failure of subadditivity, hence of extendability.
  - Its σ-class is the rigid type: only complementary pairs are disjoint. It is the earliest finite precedent for non-extendable
    states on σ-classes (merged into `own.dnp_finite_nonextendable`).
  - It has nothing infinite, nothing purely finitely additive, and no pair windows.
- **Ex 6.1 → owns the σ-additive flight analogue only.**
  - ℤ², blocks f = i, g = j, f+g; the union of the three σ-algebras is the class.
  - This is `own.dravecky_sipos_flight_subcarrier` in the Gudder school, found independently of D–Š (G84 does
    not cite them). ZG85 §1 credits an infinite-valued counterexample to Zerbe 1979 / Gudder–Zerbe 1981;
    whether that is Ex 6.1 is unverified, so priority is not settled.
  - It does not reach D₃, where every σ-additive two-valued state is a point.
- **ZG85 §4 → finite two-valued pointless state (three summands, 6 points).**
  - It is simultaneous with Navara–Pták 1983's six-point example (ZG85 was received June 1983), which is already
    in `own.sigma_class_integral_additivity`.
    - Both use the same structure: four complementary pairs of 3-subsets of a 6-set, a two-valued pointless
      measure, and failure of additivity for three summands.
    - The set systems differ: in N–P the four measure-1 sets meet pairwise in one point; in ZG85,
      {1,2,3} ∩ {1,2,4} has two points.
    - So ZG85 §4 co-owns the finite part (also held by DNP Ex 2.2).
  - It is not the infinite, purely finitely additive residual.
- **Referee-risk note.** "Same phenomenon" now has two independent printed homes (Gudder school and Czech
  school). The rebuttal is unchanged: σ-states on D₃ are points.

### 2. `thm.triangle_estar_not_built`: NOT OWNED, and NOT upgraded
- ZG85 is the **positive, finite** side. On an N×N grid, the staircase sets S_λ, which depend jointly on
  (i,j), *are* in the generated class. The proof needs the finite step count N(N+1)/2, and §1 says the
  limit passage fails for infinite-valued functions.
- The entry is the **negative, infinite** side, in a strictly larger class: D₃ contains the full pair
  σ-algebras, not only f, g and f+g. Neither paper states or implies non-membership in D₃.
- G84 Ex 6.1 co-owns the flight-class non-membership on ℤ²: the class is the union of the three blocks, so
  joint sets such as K(a,b) are absent. This adds to `own.flight_class_nonmembership`.
- **Why not NOVEL.** G2's upgrade sentence named only ZG85 and G84. But G2's own "owed before any claim"
  list also holds the ridge-function / sums-of-fewer-variables pass (Sternfeld; Marshall–O'Farrell). Its
  unread list still has the Bhaskara Rao body and Gudder PAMS 1969 / Neubrunn PAMS 1970. G84 Thm 4.1 and
  §4 restate only part of the latter two.
- The verdict stays **UNCLEAR**, narrowed to those items.
- **Theory note** (puzzle reading). On a finite dial, ZG85's staircase sets *are* built from rows,
  columns and diagonals. That is why simple-function additivity holds there. Over ℕ the staircase never
  finishes, and E\* is not built. ZG85 is the finite half of the triangle's finite/infinite split, now
  owned in print.

## Bárány 1978 vs the RV2 seed (calibration item RV2)

**Verdict: the cap lifts.** Bárány's two-page proof does **not** avoid Gale.
- The abstract: "The proof is based on Borsuk's theorem and on a theorem of Gale."
- p.325 states GALE'S THEOREM as one of exactly two tools: 2n+k points on S^k with every open hemisphere
  holding ≥ n.
- The proof identifies the ground set with Gale's V. p.325 adds that Gale's set is dual to neighbourly
  polytopes.
- Remark 1 (pointed cones) and Remark 2 (any hypergraph with a map f: V → S^k whose every open hemisphere
  contains an edge) generalise the *conclusion*. Neither says that points in general position, or one
  extra dimension, would replace Gale's construction.

So the seed's "Gale-free" claim is a real difference from Bárány, not a re-reading of it. Whether it is a
*substantial* simplification is still the Type-2 judgement. seed.md says only that the proof "avoids Gale's lemma" and reduces to "a version of
Borsuk–Ulam". The RV2 AUDIT (its lines 56–58) reads the mechanism as general position and closed covers,
which inline Gale's construction. If that reading is right, the saving is real but modest, and an expert
could fairly call it Gale "absorbed". The RV2 AUDIT's
engaged cap ("Bárány 1978 unread") no longer applies. The one residual check it named, Lovász 1978 for any
general-position remark, is non-blocking. Under AS_OF 2002-01-01 no later source is used. RV2 files are not
edited.

## WHY.md bearing
Question 4 (*circle, or composition?*). The Gudder school had already seen that three views (rows, columns,
diagonals) can each be consistent and still not compose. On a finite grid composition is restored: every
staircase question can be built, and the integral adds. What this resolution pins down is that the failure
of composition needs both the loop *and* the unfinished infinite staircase. With only one of the two,
printed results already cover it.

### Restatement in standard vocabulary (step 1, strip vocabulary)
- X = X₀×X₁×X₂ is finite. D is the finite concrete logic (Dynkin system) on X generated by the three pair
  algebras.
- On a finite set, a state on exp(X) is exactly a mixture of point masses. So **"every carrier state is a
  mixture of points" is the classical state-extension problem for a finite concrete logic**: does every state
  on D extend to a state on exp X? This is the opening question of De Simone–Navara–Pták 2007, and the
  stated subject of Ovchinnikov 1999.
- The dual test is the Horn–Tarski criterion (Horn–Tarski 1948; Bhaskara Rao Thm 3.2.5). In the finite case
  it is Maharam 6.1 and Farkas, and Berti–Rigo 2021 gives it for finitely additive mixtures.
- Let V₀₁ be the set of 0/1 vectors in the linear span of the pair-cell indicators. Then D ⊆ V₀₁ trivially:
  complement and disjoint union preserve both span membership and the 0/1 property.
- **Kernel (b) reads: the concrete logic D contains every 0/1 element of its own linear span.** It is claimed
  at sizes r×s×2 and r×3×3.

### Pure Verdict
**(a) KNOWN modulo (b).**
- "Λ ≥ 0 on V₀₁ ⇒ Λ is in the marginal polytope" holds iff every facet functional is a positive multiple of a
  0/1 span vector. By Sullivant 2006 Thm 2.4(2), that holds iff the polytope is compressed. The sizes are
  given by Sullivant Prop 5.8 and the Ohsugi–Hibi 2010 table, both primary sources read in-repo.
- The not-compressed ⇒ hidden direction needs only D ⊆ V₀₁ (trivial), Sullivant and Farkas. It is fully
  owned.
- The compressed ⇒ exact direction for the *carrier* needs only that the facet supports lie in D. That is
  implied by (b).
- So all novelty in the equivalence sits in (b), or more precisely in its weaker facet-support form. Nothing
  else in (a) is ours.

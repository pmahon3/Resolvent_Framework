## The candidate statement

Three variables with finite value sets of sizes r₁ ≥ r₂ ≥ r₃. Three pairwise laws P₀₁, P₁₂, P₂₀ with
consistent one-variable marginals. The **carrier** D is the Dynkin system on X₀×X₁×X₂ generated by
the pair events (built by complement and countable disjoint union; no "and" across pairs).

- **Visible failure:** no finitely additive probability on D extends the three pair laws (some
  event built from pair events would get an impossible value).
- **Hidden failure:** such a carrier state exists, but no joint law on X₀×X₁×X₂ reproduces the
  pairs. Every check built from pair questions passes; only a global feasibility test sees it.

**Claim (candidate theorem).** Hidden failure is possible iff r₂ ≥ 4 and r₃ ≥ 3, i.e. iff the
triangle's no-three-way-interaction marginal polytope is not compressed.

Explicit certified example at 3×4×4: each pair law uniform 1/9 on 9 allowed pairs
(`intuition_kit.tex` §3, ±1 score certificate; `verification/certify_344.py`).

Proof assembly: `definitions_draft.md` §§16, 21, 22.
1. Not compressed ⇒ non-binary extreme ray ⇒ hidden twist (Sullivant Thm 2.4; Maharam 6.1 and
   separation).
2. Compressed ⇒ extreme rays 0/1 (Sullivant), so it suffices that every 0/1 span vector is a carrier
   set:
   - r×s×2: slices + rectangle step (Lean `TransportGeneral.rect_mem`);
   - r×3×3: slicing + exhaustive exact enumeration of row types and remainders
     (`verification/rx3x3_row_types.py`, `rx3x3_exact.py`), transport hand-level for rectangular sizes.

Side results:
- strictly finer than Abramsky–Brandenburger probabilistic-only contextuality (binary (a,a,−a),
  1/3 < a < 1, is visible; hand);
- at 3×4×4 the hidden region is full-dimensional, about 29% of realizable laws by volume (float).

## Sullivant read (2026-10-26, full text §§2–5) — narrows the claim

- **Threshold OWNED directly by Sullivant 2006, Prop 5.8:** for Δ the boundary of a simplex, P_Δ is
  compressed iff at most two dᵢ > 2, or n = 3 and d = (3,3,d₃); "condition (2) ... fails in the
  case n = 3, d = (3,4,4)". So the size threshold, including 3×4×4 as the first failure, is his,
  not only Ohsugi–Hibi's.
- **Template OWNED:** Thm 4.3 / Cor 5.1: compressed ⇔ the LP relaxation gives sharp integer bounds
  on cell entries (disclosure limitation). "Compressed ⇔ a relaxation is exact" is his pattern.
- **Not in Sullivant:** the Dynkin-carrier (event-level check) relaxation; finitely additive states;
  the hidden/visible distinction; contextuality or data merging. His closing question (IP gap vs
  failure of condition (2), "natural family ... cycles") is a neighbour of our n-cycle extension.

**Narrowed claim.** A new instance of Sullivant's template: *the carrier relaxation (all checks
built from pair questions) is exact iff the triangle polytope is compressed.* The non-trivial
content beyond Sullivant is (a) every 0/1 facet functional is realised by a carrier set at compressed
sizes, and (b) the bridge from a non-binary ray to a carrier state (Maharam).

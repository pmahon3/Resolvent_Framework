\bp{\textbf{In one paragraph.} Local pieces can agree wherever they overlap and
still fail to come from a single whole. This note isolates the sharpest version
of that.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item On a ring of overlapping windows over $\bbN$ there is a coherent
    two-valued state that is not a point and not even an \emph{ideal} point (an
    ultrafilter).
  \item Demanding $\sigma$-additivity, a completed whole, would force a point,
    so the demand excludes this coherent state.
  \item On a path of windows that cannot happen: every coherent state is an ideal
    point.
\end{itemize}
The cycle is what permits pointless coherence. Infinity, through the gap between
finite and countable additivity, is what lets it survive.

\section{The puzzle}\label{sec:puzzle}

Everything in this note is one game, played on different boards. Each paragraph
ends with the result it encodes. The dictionary at the end of the section pins
every puzzle word to its exact object, so the picture can be checked against the
mathematics.

\paragraph{The game.} There are hidden numbers $x_0,x_1,x_2,\dots$. You never
see them all.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Windows.} You are shown \emph{windows}: a couple of the numbers at
    a time. Through a window you may ask any question about the numbers it
    shows: ``is $x_0<x_1$?'', ``is $(x_0,x_1)=(3,7)$?''.
  \item \emph{Compound questions.} You may also build questions from those, with
    ``not'' and with ``exactly one of these mutually exclusive options''. That
    allows even infinitely many options, such as ``which value is $x_0$?''.
  \item \emph{The referee.} A \emph{referee} answers every such question yes or
    no. The referee must be consistent: when a question is split into two
    exclusive parts, exactly the right number of them get a ``yes''.
  \item \emph{The question.} Is there a hidden reality, an actual assignment of
    numbers, that the referee's answers describe?
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The path: always fine.} Windows $\{x_0,x_1\},\{x_1,x_2\},\{x_2,x_3\}$,
each saying ``the right number is bigger''. You can comply: $1,2,3,4$. Even with
strange referees, the views always chain together into one whole, at worst an
\emph{ideal} one (see below). \emph{(Theorem~\ref{thm:path}.)}

\paragraph{Six outcomes: no single answer, but harmless.} A die with six faces
and four yes/no questions: ``is it in $\{1,2,3\}$, in $\{3,4,5\}$, in
$\{5,6,1\}$, in $\{2,4,6\}$?''. The first three overlap in a loop. Answering
``no'' to all four is perfectly consistent, yet no face gives those answers.
Nothing infinite is involved, so nothing more can go wrong. \emph{(The
Navara--Pt\'ak example, \S\ref{sec:cheap}.)}

\paragraph{The ring: the staircase.} Four or more windows in a loop,
$\{x_0,x_1\},\{x_1,x_2\},\dots,\{x_{L-1},x_0\}$, each saying ``up''. With actual
numbers this is impossible: $x_0<x_1<\dots<x_0$, a Penrose staircase.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{The ideal number.} Let each window's ``up'' mean that the second
    number is \emph{drawn later, from further out}. This is an ultrafilter, an
    ideal ``number at infinity''.
  \item \emph{Everything checks locally.} Every window genuinely believes
    ``up''. Neighbouring windows agree about every shared number.
  \item \emph{There is a referee.} A consistent referee exists for \emph{every}
    question built from the windows.
  \item \emph{But no reality.} No hidden reality fits, not even an ideal one.
\end{itemize}
The whole is coherent and has no point. \emph{(Theorem~\ref{thm:witness}, Lean,
every $L\ge4$; Proposition~\ref{prop:stair}.)}

\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Demanding a real answer kills it.} Ask the staircase referee ``which
value is $x_0$?'', split into $x_0=0$, $x_0=1$, and so on. The referee says ``no''
to each option, but ``yes'' to ``$x_0$ is some number''. That is allowed when only
\emph{finitely} many options need to add up. Now demand that the answers add up
across \emph{infinitely} many options ($\sigma$-additivity).
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item Each window is then forced to name actual numbers.
  \item The staircase becomes impossible again.
\end{itemize}
Insisting on a completed reality forbids the coherent picture.
\emph{(Proposition~\ref{prop:dirac}.)}
\begin{center}

\draw[thick,-{Stealth}] (-0.3,0)--(8.6,0) node[right,font=\scriptsize]{$\infty$};

\draw[decorate,decoration={brace,amplitude=4pt},thick] (0,0.55)--(8.4,0.55)

\end{tikzpicture}\\[-2pt]
{\scriptsize The referee's $x_0$ lives ``at infinity'': no to every value, yes to the whole line.
Demanding that the ``no''s add up to the ``yes'' ($\sigma$) is impossible.}
\end{center}

\paragraph{The triangle: the open puzzle.} Three windows,
$\{x_0,x_1\},\{x_1,x_2\},\{x_2,x_0\}$, each touching both others.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Loop-only questions.} There are now questions that cannot be
    broken into separate questions to individual windows. (On a ring of four or
    more, every question breaks into questions to at most two neighbouring
    windows.)
  \item \emph{With two values,} ``are all three equal?'' is one. It is the
    ``not'' of a question that \emph{does} break up, but it does not break up
    itself.
  \item \emph{With three values,} ``all equal'' is not even askable. There are
    2{,}088 questions such that neither they nor their ``not'' break up. The
    simplest is a nine-point cyclic pattern.
  \item \emph{Still open.} Can the staircase $x_0<x_1<x_2<x_0$ still have a
    consistent referee and no reality? Evidence says yes: on a faithful finite
    part of the board, a consistent referee exists. Nobody here has built one.
\end{itemize}
\emph{(\S\ref{sec:status}; machine evidence only.)}

\paragraph{The moral.} A loop is what lets every local view be right while the
whole is nothing. Infinity is what lets that survive. Demanding a completed
whole is what forbids it.

\section{The question, philosophically}\label{sec:philo}

\bp{\textbf{The one question.} Is the whole \emph{given}, or \emph{set as a task}?
Each section below is a precise case of it. The philosophers here each name one
piece. Verification labels:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \st{read}: I read the passage itself;
  \item \st{bib}: confirmed through bibliographic records only;
  \item \st{sec}: confirmed through secondary sources only.
\end{itemize}}

\paragraph{Aristotle: the potential and the actual infinite.}
In \emph{Physics} III.6 (around 206a) the infinite ``is'' only potentially, in the
way a day or the games ``are'': there is always more to take, and the taking is
never completed. There is no actual infinite. \st{sec: Hardie--Gaye
translation and commentary; Bekker lines not checked against the Greek}.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Here:} finite additivity is the potential infinite. It is coherent
    at every finite stage, with no completed sum demanded.
  \item $\sigma$-additivity is the actual infinite. The countable totality must
    be accounted for as completed.
  \item \S\S\ref{sec:dirac}--\ref{sec:stair}: taking the infinite as actual
    excludes a whole that the potential infinite allows.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Leibniz: space as an order, not a substance.}
Third paper to Clarke (1716, \S4 in the standard numbering): ``I hold [space] to
be an order of coexistences, as time is an order of successions.''
\st{read: English extract; section number from the standard numbering, not
checked}.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Here:} points are outcomes of reconstruction from relations, not
    starting material.
  \item \S\ref{sec:paths}: along a path the reconstruction always succeeds.
  \item \S\ref{sec:stair}: around a cycle it can fail.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Kant: the totality as a task, not an object.}
In the Antinomy of Pure Reason, the series of conditions for an appearance is not
given as a whole. The regress is \emph{aufgegeben}. Section~7: ``daß ein
Regressus zu den Bedingungen \dots\ geboten oder aufgegeben sei''. Section~8 opens
with the \emph{regulative} principle: the totality is ``nicht \dots\ gegeben
\dots, sondern bloß im Regressus derselben aufgegeben'', valid ``nicht als Axiom,
die Totalität im Objekt als wirklich zu denken, sondern als ein Problem für den
Verstand''. Taking the totality as given (constitutively) is what produces the
antinomies. \st{read: German text, Gutenberg ed.; A/B pagination (\S8 opens
near A508/B536) from secondary sources}.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Here:} the regulative/constitutive split is the scaffold/object
    split.
  \item The conservativity results say when treating the totality as given is
    harmless: $C=R$, finite alphabets, acyclic overlaps.
  \item \S\ref{sec:stair} is a small antinomy. Every window judges ``up'',
    coherently. Read constitutively, as a point or a $\sigma$-additive state, the
    totality contradicts them. Read as a task, a finitely additive state, it does
    not.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Husserl: the thing in adumbrations, as an Idea in the Kantian sense.}
A spatial thing is given only one-sidedly, in adumbrations, and so inadequately.
\emph{Ideas}~I \S\S142--143 resolve this by making the thing's adequate givenness
an ``Idea in the Kantian sense'', the ideal limit of a harmonious, open-ended
synthesis. \st{sec: Mariani, \emph{Phainomenon} 34 (2022); Metzler Lexikon
``ad\"aquat''; Husserliana III/1 not checked}.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Here:} this is the source of the recorded phrase ``the object as
    the open horizon of coherent refinement''. Windows are adumbrations, and EA
    is harmonious synthesis.
  \item \S\ref{sec:stair} is a case the picture does not anticipate: the
    adumbrations cohere perfectly, and there is no thing, not even an ideal one.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Whitehead and pointless topology: points built from regions.}
Whitehead's method of extensive abstraction (\emph{An Enquiry Concerning the
Principles of Natural Knowledge}, 1919) defines a point as an abstractive class of
regions, so points are derived, not primitive. \st{sec: Whitehead Research
encyclopedia}. Its modern mathematics is locale theory: Johnstone, ``The point
of pointless topology'', \emph{Bull.\ AMS} 8 (1983), 41--53 \st{bib}.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item Points of a locale are completely prime filters, cousins of the
    ultrafilters here.
  \item Non-trivial locales can have no points at all.
  \item \emph{Here:} pointless coherence is the measure-theoretic sibling of a
    pointless locale, and the bridge runs through Stone duality.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Specker: the cycle, from the start.}
Specker, ``Die Logik nicht gleichzeitig entscheidbarer Aussagen'', \emph{Dialectica}
14 (1960), 239--246, the seed of Kochen--Specker. It opens with a parable from
the school of prophets at Arba'ila. A seer sets three boxes before his daughter's
suitors. Each suitor must name two boxes that are both empty or both full. Every
time, one of the two holds a gem and the other is empty. It also places the
problem beside the scholastic \emph{Infuturabilien}: does God's omniscience
extend to what would have happened? \st{read: Seevinck's translation,
arXiv:1103.4537}.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Here:} Specker's three boxes are a 3-cycle that is pairwise
    consistent and jointly impossible. That is \S\ref{sec:stair}'s staircase
    with three steps.
  \item Contextuality was born as a question about whether counterfactual
    totalities are determinate.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Penrose: the cohomology of the impossible.}
R.~Penrose, ``On the cohomology of impossible figures'', \emph{Structural
Topology} 17 (1991), 11--16; reprinted in \emph{Leonardo} 25 (1992)
\st{bib: via T.~Phillips, AMS Feature Column, Oct.\ 2014}. The impossible
tribar is explained by first cohomology, with locally coherent pieces and no
global object. The same mathematics later became the sheaf-theoretic account of
contextuality.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Here:} \S\ref{sec:stair} is literally a Penrose staircase. Every
    step goes up.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The constructivist nerve.}
A non-principal ultrafilter cannot be shown to exist without some choice. So the
anti-Platonic object, a whole without points, is built from a Platonic
ingredient placed \emph{locally}. A Brouwerian would call the witness a fiction.
That leaves the deepest question in this kit: can pointless coherence exist
constructively, or does refusing the completed whole cost you completed parts?

\bp{\textbf{Where the two minds meet.} The philosophy asks Kant's question. The
mathematics answers it by theorems that say exactly when treating the whole as
given is harmless:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{acyclic and finite:} always;
  \item \textbf{cyclic:} a coherent world can refuse to be a thing (Specker,
    Penrose, the flagship);
  \item \textbf{cyclic and infinite:} the demand for a completed thing excludes a
    coherent world (the countable witness).
\end{itemize}
The flagship (the cycle) and the $\sigma$ lane (infinity) are two halves of one
Kantian investigation, and they meet in \S\ref{sec:stair}.}



\section{Setting}

\begin{definition}[carrier, state]
A \emph{carrier} on a set $\Omega$ is a family $\cD$ of subsets containing
$\Omega$ and closed under complements and countable disjoint unions (a Dynkin
system). A \emph{two-valued state} is $s:\cD\to\{0,1\}$ with $s(\Omega)=1$ and
$s(A\sqcup B)=s(A)+s(B)$ for disjoint $A,B\in\cD$. It is \emph{$\sigma$-additive}
if this extends to countable disjoint unions.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[points and ideal points]
$s$ is a \emph{point} if $s(E)=[\omega\in E]$ for some $\omega\in\Omega$. It is an
\emph{ideal point} if $s(E)=[E\in W]$ for some ultrafilter $W$ on $\Omega$.
Points are the principal ideal points.
\end{definition}

On a Boolean algebra every two-valued state is an ideal point (Stone). So
anything below happens only because a carrier need not be closed under
intersections. Two elementary facts are used throughout.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:basic}\st{hand; standard}
Let $s$ be a two-valued state on a carrier $\cD$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),nosep]
  \item If $A\subseteq B$ are in $\cD$, then $B\setminus A\in\cD$ and
    $s(A)\le s(B)$.
  \item If $A,B$ and $A\cap B$ are all in $\cD$ and $s(A)=s(B)=1$, then
    $s(A\cap B)=1$.
  \item $s$ is an ideal point iff its true sets $\{E:s(E)=1\}$ have the finite
    intersection property.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),nosep]
  \item $A$ and $B^c$ are disjoint, so $B\setminus A=(A\sqcup B^c)^c\in\cD$, and
    $s(B)=s(A)+s(B\setminus A)$.
  \item $A\setminus B=A\setminus(A\cap B)\in\cD$ and lies inside $B^c$, so by~(a)
    $s(A\setminus B)\le s(B^c)=0$. Hence $s(A\cap B)=s(A)=1$.
  \item If the true sets have the FIP, extend them to an ultrafilter $W$. If
    $s(E)=1$ then $E\in W$. If $s(E)=0$ then $s(E^c)=1$, so $E^c\in W$ and
    $E\notin W$. The converse is immediate.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}
So a two-valued state respects every intersection the carrier contains. It can
fail to be a point only through intersections the carrier \emph{lacks}.

\begin{definition}[glued windows]
Let $\Omega=\bbN^{V}$ for a finite index set $V$, and let $\mathcal W$ be a family
of two-element \emph{windows} $\{i,j\}\subseteq V$. A \emph{window event} at
$w=\{i,j\}$ is a set $\{x:(x_i,x_j)\in A\}$ with $A\subseteq\bbN^2$ arbitrary.
The \emph{glued carrier} $\cD$ is the carrier generated by all window events.

The \emph{ring} has $V=\bbN/L$ and windows $W_k=\{k,k{+}1\}$. The \emph{path}
has $V=\{0,\dots,n\}$ and windows $W_k=\{k,k{+}1\}$ for $k<n$.
\end{definition}

The window events at one window form a Boolean $\sigma$-algebra (a copy of
$\mathcal P(\bbN^2)$). So a state restricts on each window to an ultrafilter
$u_k$ on $\bbN^2$, and adjacent ones agree on their shared coordinate. Whether
these window ultrafilters come from one ultrafilter on $\Omega$ is the
question.

\section{Pointlessness alone is cheap}\label{sec:cheap}

\begin{example}[Navara--Pt\'ak 1983]\st{cited; checked by hand}
Take $\Omega=\{1,\dots,6\}$, $M_1=\{1,2,3\}$, $M_2=\{3,4,5\}$, $M_3=\{5,6,1\}$ and
$M_4=\{2,4,6\}$.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{The carrier.} The generated carrier is
    $\{\emptyset,\Omega,M_i,M_i^c\}$: disjoint three-element subsets of a
    six-element set are complements.
  \item \emph{The state.} $s(M_i)=0$ for all $i$ defines a two-valued state.
  \item \emph{No point.} It is not a point, since
    $\bigcap_i M_i^c=\{4,5,6\}\cap\{6,1,2\}\cap\{2,3,4\}\cap\{1,3,5\}=\emptyset$.
\end{itemize}
\end{example}

The carrier is finite, so $s$ is automatically $\sigma$-additive. Pointless
coherence on its own costs nothing at the $\sigma$ level. What is new below is
pointlessness that the $\sigma$-completion \emph{forbids}.

\section{On the ring, \texorpdfstring{$\sigma$}{sigma}-additivity forces a point}\label{sec:dirac}

Let $C_k=\{x:x_k<x_{k+1}\}$, a window event at $W_k$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:dirac}\st{Lean: \texttt{no\_sigma\_extension}}
Every $\sigma$-additive two-valued state on the glued ring is a point. So none has
$s(C_k)=1$ for all $k$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Each window is a point.} On window $W_k$ the fibres
    $\{(x_k,x_{k+1})=(a,b)\}$ are a countable partition into window events. By
    $\sigma$-additivity exactly one is true. So on window events $s$ agrees with
    evaluation at a pair $(a_k,b_k)$.
  \item \emph{The points agree.} Shared coordinates force $b_k=a_{k+1}$. Let
    $\omega=(a_k)_k$. Then $s=\delta_\omega$ on all window events.
  \item \emph{Extend.} The sets where two $\sigma$-additive states agree form a
    carrier, so $s=\delta_\omega$ on all of $\cD$.
  \item \emph{The pattern fails.} All $C_k$ true would give
    $\omega_0<\omega_1<\dots<\omega_{L-1}<\omega_0$.
\end{itemize}
\end{proof}

On this carrier, the completed infinite \emph{is} the demand to be made of
points.

\section{The staircase: ideal points on every window, none for the whole}\label{sec:stair}

For ultrafilters $p,q$ on $\bbN$ let $A\in p\otimes q$ iff
$\{m:\{n:(m,n)\in A\}\in q\}\in p$. Read it as ``draw $m$ by $p$, then $n$ by $q$,
later''. Fix a non-principal $p$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:pp}\st{hand; standard}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),nosep]
  \item $\{m<n\}\in p\otimes p$.
  \item Both marginals of $p\otimes p$ are $p$.
  \item $p\otimes p$ is not symmetric.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),nosep]
  \item Each section $\{n:n>m\}$ is cofinite, hence in $p$.
  \item The sections of a first-coordinate cylinder are all of $\bbN$ or empty;
    those of a second-coordinate cylinder $\{n\in Y\}$ all equal $Y$.
  \item The transpose of $\{m<n\}$ is $\{m>n\}$, which is disjoint from it.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

Put $u_k=p\otimes p$ on window $W_k$, read on $(x_k,x_{k+1})$. By (b) these window
ultrafilters agree on every shared coordinate: each coordinate carries $p$. By
(a) every window says ``up'': $u_k(C_k)=1$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:stair}\st{hand}
No ultrafilter on $\bbN^L$ projects to every $u_k$. Moreover, no two-valued
finitely additive state on the \emph{algebra} generated by the window events
agrees with them.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Such a state would make every $C_k$ true. On an algebra it is multiplicative
(Lemma~\ref{lem:basic}(b)), so it would make $\bigcap_kC_k=\emptyset$ true.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[the countable witness]\label{thm:witness}\st{Lean:
\texttt{mu\_cases}, \texttt{sL\_sigmaEssential},
\texttt{targetA\_sharp\_countable\_literal}, \texttt{D4\_six\_conjuncts}}
For every $L\ge4$, the window ultrafilters $u_k$ extend to a two-valued finitely
additive state on the whole glued carrier $\cD_L$.
\end{theorem}

\noindent So the extension is:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{coherent};
  \item \emph{not an ideal point}, by Proposition~\ref{prop:stair} and
    Lemma~\ref{lem:basic}(c);
  \item \emph{without any $\sigma$-additive version}, by
    Proposition~\ref{prop:dirac}.
\end{itemize}
Locally every coordinate is an ideal point ($p$). Globally there is none. The
non-commutativity of $\otimes$ gives each window an arrow, and the cycle closes
the arrow on itself, like a Penrose staircase. For $L=4$ the carrier is
literally irreducible: its centre is $\{\emptyset,\Omega\}$ (Lean). It satisfies
all six original admissibility conjuncts, with the Polish clause conditional on
\texttt{DWPolishCut} exactly as for the $\omega_1$ witness.

\section{The shape of the carrier}\label{sec:shape}

\begin{lemma}[two adjacent windows suffice]\label{lem:shape}\st{hand; checked on
finite alphabets}
Suppose no three windows pairwise share a coordinate. This holds for every path,
and for every ring with $L\ge4$. Then every $E\in\cD$ has the form
$E=E_{k-1}\sqcup E_k$, with $E_{k-1},E_k$ window events at two adjacent windows.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Rigidity.} Window events at windows sharing no coordinate are both
    non-empty only if they intersect, since they constrain disjoint coordinates.
  \item \emph{Two windows.} Let $E=E_{k-1}\sqcup E_k$ and fix the shared
    coordinate $x_k=c$. The slices are cylinders $\{x_{k-1}\in A_c\}$ and
    $\{x_{k+1}\in B_c\}$, and they are disjoint, so one is empty. So the slice of
    $E$, and of $E^c$, is a cylinder in one coordinate. Gathering slices by type
    writes $E^c$ as a window event at $W_{k-1}$ disjoint from one at $W_k$.
  \item \emph{The carrier.} Let $\cG$ be the disjoint unions of one window event
    per window. By rigidity, at most two adjacent pieces of a member of $\cG$ are
    non-empty. So $\cG$ is closed under complements by the two-window step.
  \item \emph{Unions.} It is closed under countable disjoint unions: regroup the
    pieces window by window.
  \item \emph{Conclusion.} $\cG$ is a carrier containing the window events, and
    $\cG\subseteq\cD$. So $\cD=\cG$.
\end{itemize}
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
A finite-alphabet check
(\texttt{notes/open\_questions/verification/pointless\_path\_dynkin.py}) confirms
$\cD=\cG$ on binary paths of 2--4 windows, the ternary path of 2 windows, and the
binary 4-ring. On the \emph{triangle} it fails: $|\cD|=70$, $|\cG|=66$. There all
three windows are pairwise adjacent. This has the same shape as Greechie's Loop
Lemma (Kalmbach, \emph{Orthomodular Lattices}, 1983, \S4 Lemma~9, on the shelf):
a pasting of Boolean blocks is an orthomodular poset iff it has no loop of
order~3. There, blocks share single atoms; here, windows share coordinate
subalgebras.
\end{remark}

One consequence: on the ring, $C_0\cap C_1=\{x_0<x_1<x_2\}\notin\cD_L$. Its slice
at $x_1=c$ is $\{x_0<c\}\times\{x_2>c\}$, which is not a cylinder. This is the
missing intersection that Lemma~\ref{lem:basic}(b) said pointlessness needs.
\st{hand}

\section{Paths: no pointless coherence}\label{sec:paths}

\begin{lemma}[amalgamation]\label{lem:amalg}\st{hand; very likely folklore}
Let $U$ be an ultrafilter on $X\times Y$ and $V$ one on $Y\times Z$, with the
same $Y$-marginal. Then some ultrafilter on $X\times Y\times Z$ projects to $U$
and $V$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $A\in U$ and $B\in V$, the projections $\pi_Y A$ and $\pi_Y B$ lie in the
common marginal, so they share some $y$. Hence $(A\times Z)\cap(X\times
B)\ne\emptyset$. These sets have the FIP; extend them to an ultrafilter. Its
projections contain $U$ and $V$, so by maximality they equal them.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[no cycle, no pointless coherence]\label{thm:path}\st{hand}
On the glued path, every two-valued finitely additive state is an ideal point.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item Let $u_k$ be the window ultrafilters. Amalgamating along the path
    (Lemma~\ref{lem:amalg}, one window at a time) gives an ultrafilter $W$ on
    $\Omega$ projecting to every $u_k$.
  \item For $E\in\cD$, write $E=E_{k-1}\sqcup E_k$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:shape}.
  \item Then $s(E)=u_{k-1}(E_{k-1})+u_k(E_k)=W(E)$.
\end{itemize}
\end{proof}

With the staircase, this is a Vorob'ev-type statement for ideal points. For pair
windows on paths and on rings of length $\ge4$, the cycle is exactly what makes
pointless coherence possible.

\section{The ring, seen through the same lemma}

Lemma~\ref{lem:shape} holds on rings with $L\ge4$ as well. So on the ring any
finitely additive state is \emph{forced} by its window ultrafilters:
\[ s(E)=u_{k-1}(E_{k-1})+u_k(E_k)\qquad(E=E_{k-1}\sqcup E_k). \]
Two pieces of Theorem~\ref{thm:witness} then follow by hand \st{hand}.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{It never exceeds 1.} If both pieces were true, then $u_{k-1}$ and
    $u_k$, which share coordinate $k$, amalgamate on the three coordinates
    $k{-}1,k,k{+}1$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:amalg}). The amalgam would contain the
    disjoint sets $E_{k-1},E_k$, which is impossible. A cycle is needed to break
    amalgamation, and three consecutive coordinates are no cycle.
  \item \emph{Ambiguous decompositions are harmless in the simple cases.} If a
    slice is empty or full, either window can carry it. The two readings then
    agree because adjacent window ultrafilters share their marginals.
\end{itemize}
What remains of the Lean proof is \emph{global} well-definedness and finite
additivity: that the formula is a state. This simplifies the hub's account,
which placed all the novelty in \texttt{mu\_cases}. A referee holding
Lemma~\ref{lem:shape} could call that step bookkeeping. This is a simplification,
not a kill, and no source has been found for the witness after three prior-art
passes.

        extends consistently and two-valued on a faithful finite sub-carrier

      \item windows of three or more coordinates: what replaces ``acyclic'',

## 3. Where the seam is NOT

- Existence of pointless two-valued states on a concrete logic or sigma-class (Navara–Pták 1983, Dravecký–Šipoš Ex 6; finite `4even`).
- Re-pointing: "take S2(D) as the points" makes R1 and R2 hold trivially (B–P Thm 3.1). So "R1 fails" says nothing intrinsic. It only says Omega = X^V is not a representing point set. This is consistent with the answers-first stance (Omega is scaffold) but removes "pointless exists" as a contribution.
- Arbitrary state-space shape (Shultz; Navara 1992): no general theorem tying K(D) to the hull of two-valued states.
- F as a limit of local Stone spectra, and its adjunction (vdBH; Abramsky–Barbosa); finite-outcome obstruction theory (Abramsky–Brandenburger, cohomology papers).
- Coherence (extension to ultrafilter) and real-valued extension (Bhaskara Rao, Maharam); finite hidden twists (previous passes).

## 4. Surviving target (one paragraph)

The open object is the R4 gap G(D) = K(D) \ cl conv rho(beta Omega) and its extreme part E(D) = ext K(D) \ rho(beta Omega), graded by binary (pointless two-valued) versus non-binary extreme states, as a function of the window graph and X. ⟦HAND, unchecked against the repo⟧ Milman's converse to Krein–Milman gives: ext cl conv C is contained in C for compact C, and rho(beta Omega) is compact, so *a pointless two-valued state is an extreme point of K(D) outside cl conv rho(beta Omega)* (two-valued implies extreme: s = lambda t + (1-lambda) u forces t = u = s). So R1 failure implies R4 failure in the infinite triangle (binary kind), while the 3x4x4 failure is the non-binary kind (finite: rho(beta Omega) = points). Nothing found classifies ext K(D) for window-generated D. Candidates, in order: (i) classify E(D) by window graph (binary versus non-binary); (ii) O1/O2 for r: S(D) -> F, including the fibre structure of r (in-repo: the triangle's delta_k correction, taxonomy thm lines ~269); (iii) the sigma-additive version. Honest gap: (i) may reduce to the finite no-disturbance polytope vertices (Araújo, Kharoof–Ipek–Okay) plus the Czech-school two-valued constructions. This is a structural statement about extremes, not yet a theorem.

## Addendum (main session, 2026-10-23): hand checks of the surviving target

- **A pointless two-valued state is not even a limit of mixtures of ideal points.** ⟦HAND, checked⟧
  (i) Every two-valued state s is extreme in K(D): if s = t·a + (1−t)·b with 0<t<1, then s(E)=0 forces a(E)=b(E)=0 and s(E)=1 forces a(E)=b(E)=1, so a = b = s.
  (ii) M := cl conv ρ(βΩ) is compact convex and ρ(βΩ) is compact (ρ continuous, `{U : E ∈ U}` clopen), so by Milman's converse ext M ⊆ ρ(βΩ).
  (iii) If a pointless s lay in M, it would be extreme in M (extreme in K ⊇ M), hence in ρ(βΩ): contradiction.
  So on the infinite triangle R1-failure ⇒ R4-failure. At 3×4×4 (finite) R1 holds and R4 still fails (non-binary extreme states): the two failures are independent.
- **Re-pointing on S(D) (Burešová–Pták Thm 3.1).** Representing E ↦ {s ∈ S(D) : s(E)=1} makes every two-valued state a Dirac point, but preserves only FINITE disjoint unions: a finitely additive s can give 0 to every piece of a countable disjoint union and 1 to the union. ⟦HAND⟧ So points can always be earned by changing the scaffold, at the price of the countable-union structure of D. Pointlessness is relative to Ω together with D's σ-structure.

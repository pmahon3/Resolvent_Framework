## Abstract (verbatim)

> We consider extensions of certain states. The states are defined on the
> systems of sets that are closed under the formation of the symmetric
> difference (concrete quantum logics). These systems can be viewed as certain
> set-representable quantum logics enriched with the symmetric difference. We
> first show how the compactness argument allows us to extend states on Boolean
> algebras over such systems of sets. We then observe that the extensions are
> sometimes possible even for non-Boolean situations. On the other hand, a
> difference-closed system can be constructed such that even two-valued states
> do not allow for extensions. Finally, we consider these questions in a
> sigma-complete setup and find a large class of such systems with rather
> interesting state properties.

### The sigma-complete section is extension-POSITIVE. This is the decisive point.

The abstract's "large class ... with rather interesting state properties" is a
TAMENESS result, not a witness:

- **Construction 1 / Thm 4.** From any sigma-complete symmetric logic (Omega,D)
  and a set S of uncountable cofinality, builds Sigma_S(Omega,D), closed under
  countable disjoint unions and symmetric difference. If (Omega,D) is
  non-Boolean, so is Sigma_S(Omega,D). So infinite sigma-complete non-Boolean
  symmetric logics DO exist -- but Thm 4 asserts closure properties only, never
  that they are LATTICES.
- **Thm 5.** card S = aleph_1, card Omega = aleph_0: every sigma-additive state
  on Sigma_S(Omega,D) is carried by a countable set, and **consequently extends**
  over exp(S x Omega).
- **Thm 6 (under not-RM).** Every sigma-complete (Omega,D) sigma-embeds in a
  sigma-complete (Omega,D') on which every sigma-additive state is countably
  carried and **allows an extension** over the power algebra.

So in the sigma-complete regime the paper produces extendability, under exactly
the cardinal hypothesis the programme already tracks (not-RM = no
real-measurable cardinal). It supplies no sigma-complete non-extendable carrier
of any kind, lattice or poset.

### The non-extendable example IS a lattice -- but finite, and it is our own device.

**Thm 3.** Omega = {0,...,9}, D generated by A={0,1,4,7}, B={0,2,5,8},
C={0,1,2,3}, D={0,4,5,6}. There is a two-valued state on (Omega,D) that does not
extend over exp Omega. The paper notes: "D presents a set-representation of the
'Chinese lantern' MO15. **Thus D is even a lattice** when considered with the
ordering given by inclusion."

Two reasons this does not fire the gate:

1. **Finite.** It is sigma-complete only vacuously -- every countable join in a
   finite lattice is a finite join. The gate's wording ("sigma-complete LATTICE
   with a non-extendable state") is *literally* satisfied by MO15, and this is
   worth stating plainly rather than waving through: the wording is too loose.
   The gate's INTENT is Q3's object -- an **infinite** concrete sigma-complete
   non-Boolean irreducible OML. Finite objects are Phi-tame throughout this
   campaign. **Recommend the gate be reworded to say infinite.**
2. **Mechanism already ours.** The proof is: "a potential extension would be a
   Dirac state on exp Omega. But the values of s on A,B,C,D exclude that." That
   is exactly condition (i) of the reduction -- no delta_omega realizes s0, the
   "intersection of generators mapped to 1 is empty" condition, freely
   arrangeable by the Navara-Ptak intersection device. It says nothing about
   condition (ii), whether a non-Dirac sigma-state rescues it, which is where
   the whole difficulty lives.

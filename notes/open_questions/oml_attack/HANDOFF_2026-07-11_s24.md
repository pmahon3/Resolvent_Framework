## Banked transition

The smallest contextual exact-one implication is a four-context loop.
It is a separated finite concrete σ-class OMP, but not a lattice because
an opposite pair lacks a join. This mechanism is closed.

The five-cycle Greechie pentagon is a finite concrete OML and supplies a
valid contextual atom-killing cell:

\[
\{a_0,a_3,a_5^\perp\}\text{ value one}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a_7=0.
\]

The cluster elements and target are pairwise incompatible in the needed
sense, and the state table gives a unique cluster-charging state. Thus
latticehood permits one cell.

Naively sharing the three cluster inputs across countably many copies
creates induced inter-copy four-loops and loses latticehood.

## Priority 1 — high-girth propagation

Seek a countable linear block hypergraph of girth at least five that:

1. is driven by one fixed finite coherent cluster;
2. propagates value-one information through pentagon-like cells;
3. forces every atom $D_n$ of one countably atomic block to value zero;
4. remains a σ-complete concrete OML; and
5. has at least one global f.a. cluster state but no principal
   restriction to the atomic block.

Co-equal proof route: show any such fan-out from finitely many cluster
inputs necessarily creates a four-loop, or show that the hidden carriers
needed to complete those loops produce a principal cluster-face state.

Exit A: verified all-remainder OML. Exit B: a fan-out/high-girth
impossibility theorem. Exit C: one bounded propagation architecture
classified, with the next architecture named.

## Checks and guardrails

State equations alone do not verify latticehood. Girth at least five is
necessary for the incidence-faithful Greechie route but does not by
itself verify σ-completeness or concrete representability. A unique
abstract cluster state is useful only if its restriction to the master
block is free and the concrete point representation still
order-determines the carrier. One free restriction is not enough unless
the entire cluster face is that state or otherwise all-remainder.

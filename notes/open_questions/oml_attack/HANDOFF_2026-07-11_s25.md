## Current construction boundary

One Greechie pentagon is a valid OML contextual atom-killing cell. Its
three-input cluster uniquely forces an incompatible atom to zero.

Direct repetition is classified by gluing distance. Sharing a
distance-two input pair across private cells creates an induced
four-loop; one shared input is safe, and one special pair closes only a
six-loop. The natural serial relay correctly propagates a $1,0$ signal,
but its output ports lie in one context, so gluing them into the next
cell creates a three-loop. Using master-block atoms as the zero rail
creates another three-loop.

## Priority 1 — separated-port repeater

Attack a bounded architecture made from two or more pentagon cells that
accepts a forced $1,0$ signal and emits a fresh forced $1,0$ signal at
ports whose block-distance is large enough for serial gluing to preserve
girth at least five. Require:

1. exact-one implication verified over all two-valued states;
2. input and output incidence distances recorded;
3. no induced three- or four-loop after self-composition;
4. target atoms attach to the master block without a short loop; and
5. the finite repeater paste is a lattice, not merely an OMP.

Proof-side alternative: prove an exact-one no-cloning/separation theorem
showing that any finite linear repeater with one $1,0$ input and two
fresh forced output signals either has a shared-tail equality or a cycle
of length at most four.

Exit A: a composable finite OML repeater. Exit B: a no-repeater theorem
for a clearly delimited class. Exit C: exhaustive census through a
specified number of cells/contexts, with the first uncovered size named.

## Guardrails

Do not infer a global fan-out impossibility from the direct-sharing
theorem; serial cactus assemblies are combinatorially possible. State
propagation does not establish latticehood. Keep killed targets distinct
from relay-zero ports unless the master-block incidence is checked. A
finite repeater is only a construction component, not yet a T4
counterexample.

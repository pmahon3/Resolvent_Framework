# Endgame selection residue: hereditary escape, distributed fine traps, and coarse inverse limits

*Opened 2026-07-13, campaign iterations 35--40. Evidence is hand proof unless
explicitly marked executable. This note does not claim an OML counterexample.*

## 1. Hereditary one-block escape follows from T4At

Let `q` be a coherent finite positive cluster, let `C_r` be a nonempty
finite-coordinate refinement of its state face, and let `B` be a maximal
block. If `T4At(L)` holds, then there are a global finitely additive state
`mu in C_r` and a sigma-additive two-valued state `v_B` on `B` such that
`v_B` charges the local event `E_B(r)` and

\[
 v_B|_{\partial B}=\mu|_{\partial B}.
\]

Indeed T4At applied to `r,B` supplies an atom `D in B` and a global
finitely additive state `mu in C_r` charging `D`. The restriction `mu|B` is
the atom-principal ultrafilter, hence is sigma-additive; take `v_B=mu|B`.
No countable-generation hypothesis is used in this last implication.

Thus T4At implies hereditary full-block escape and hereditary boundary
escape. The witness depends on `r` and `B`; this is not simultaneous GSD.

## 2. The Cantor singleton cover refutes topology-only selection

Let `X=2^N`. For every `x in X` set

\[
 D_x=\{x\},\qquad G_x=X\setminus\{x\}.
\]

Then every `D_x` is closed nowhere dense, every nonempty clopen face meets
every `G_x`, every countable family of the `G_x` has nonempty intersection,
but `intersection_x G_x` is empty. In particular:

1. hereditary one-block escape does not imply simultaneous escape;
2. point-countability of the defect family is insufficient (it is point-one);
3. no countable subatlas forces goodness at every index;
4. compactness, zero-dimensionality, and Baire category alone do not prove
   arbitrary-atlas `T4At => Phi`.

The least size of a closed nowhere-dense cover of Cantor space is
`cov(M)`, so its exact value is not fixed by ZFC. The singleton version uses
continuum many indices and needs no additional axiom.

Two useful opposite cases are exact. If the defects are open, compactness
gives a finite subcover. If the good loci are closed and every finite family
intersects, compact FIP gives a global escape. A locally countable family of
subsets of a compact space is countable, so local countability plus countable
escape also rules out a cover.

## 3. Each singleton defect is a genuine fine Boolean boundary relation

The preceding relation is not merely an arbitrary family of subsets. Put
`A=Clop(X)`. For `x in X`, let `Omega_x=X\{x}` and
`B_x=Borel(Omega_x)`, with the Boolean embedding

\[
 A\longrightarrow B_x,\qquad U\longmapsto U\cap\Omega_x.
\]

The block `B_x` is countably generated. Its sigma-additive two-valued states
are the point states at `y in Omega_x`. Their restrictions to `A` give
exactly `X\{x}`. Every ultrafilter of `A`, including the point `x`, has a
finitely additive extension to `B_x` by the Boolean ultrafilter extension
theorem. Hence

\[
 T_{B_x}^{\sigma}(1)=X\setminus\{x\},\qquad
 T_{B_x}^{fa}(1)=X.
\]

Here `A` is the overlap-generated Boolean boundary; it is not asserted to
be a sigma-field. This is exactly the boundary notion used by GSD.

The naive atlas obtained by pasting every `B_x` over the same `A` cannot be
an admissible counterexample. Every nontrivial `a in A` lies in all maximal
blocks, hence is central. Moreover there is no compatible family of local
sigma-states at all: a common trace `y` fails at block `B_y`, so sigma-state
order separation fails. The next realization must distribute transported
copies of `A`, avoid a global common interface, and still supply every
cross-block lattice cut.

## 4. Exact coarse inverse-limit characterization

Let `A subseteq B` be Boolean sigma-fields, let `t` be a two-valued state on
`A`, and let `E in B`. Then `t in T_B^sigma(E)` iff there is a family
`(U_C)` indexed by the countably generated sigma-subalgebras `C subseteq B`
such that:

1. `U_C` is a sigma-additive two-valued state on `C`;
2. `U_C` agrees with `t` on `A intersection C`;
3. if `E in C`, then `U_C(E)=1`;
4. if `C subseteq D`, then `U_D|C=U_C`.

The forward direction is restriction. Conversely define the value of
`b in B` in any countably generated `C` containing it. Independence follows
by passing from two choices `C,D` to `sigma(C union D)`. Finite Boolean laws
are checked in one such enlargement. For a countable additivity test, the
sigma-field generated by the union of the countably many participating
countably generated subalgebras is still countably generated, so one `U_C`
checks the whole test. The union is the required global sigma-state.

The coherence clause is load-bearing; mere nonemptiness of every local
extension fibre is false as a criterion.

## 5. ZFC failure of naive coarse countable determination

Let `NS` be the nonstationary ideal on `omega_1` and

\[
 A_{club}=\{S\subseteq\omega_1:S\in NS\text{ or }S^c\in NS\},
 \qquad B=P(\omega_1).
\]

This is a sigma-field because `NS` is a sigma-ideal on regular `omega_1`.
Define `t(S)=0` for `S in NS` and `t(S)=1` for `S^c in NS`. This is a
sigma-additive two-valued state on `A_club`. It is the co-nonstationary
ultrafilter on this restricted field; the club filter on all of `B` is not
being called an ultrafilter.

For every countably generated `D subseteq A_club`, choose generators
`G_n`. The intersection of the `t`-chosen sides of all `G_n` still has
`t`-value one and is nonempty. Any point in it realizes `t|D`, and its Dirac
state extends sigma-additively to `B`.

But `t` has no sigma-additive extension to `B`: such an extension would be a
nonprincipal countably complete ultrafilter on `omega_1`, forbidden in ZFC
by Ulam's theorem. Thus every countably generated boundary restriction can
sigma-lift while the full trace cannot. This refutes naive countable
determination, not the coherent inverse-limit theorem.

## 6. Exact remaining theorem

The fine and coarse findings isolate the same missing statement.

> **OML distributed-boundary compactness (ODBC).** In a concrete
> sigma-complete OML whose sigma-additive two-valued states separate order,
> for every coherent finite face, the family of block boundary-liftable
> loci cannot form a cover by defects if every finite/countable block
> subsystem is escapable; equivalently, the compatible inverse system of
> eligible local sigma-lifts has a global section.

For fine blocks, failure of ODBC would have to realize a transported version
of the Cantor singleton atlas while passing latticehood, centre, and state
separation. For coarse blocks, failure would have to realize an incoherent
club-type inverse system and force its bad trace by finitely many global
events. Therefore ODBC proves the full conjecture (combined with GSD), while
an explicit failure supplies the precise counterexample architecture. The
first structural subproblem is whether lattice cut-saturation forces the
needed inverse-system compactness or a coherent patching operation.

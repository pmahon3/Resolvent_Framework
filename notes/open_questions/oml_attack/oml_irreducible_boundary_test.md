## 1. Target and definitions

Let `L ⊆ P(Ω)` be closed under complements and countable disjoint unions,
and suppose inclusion makes `L` an orthomodular lattice. This is the
repository's **concrete σ-complete OML**. A **maximal Boolean block** is a
maximal pairwise-compatible subset; latticehood makes it a Boolean σ-field.
The standard OML centre is

\[
 Z(L)=\{a:a\text{ is compatible with every }x\in L\}
     =\bigcap\{B:B\text{ is a maximal block}\}.
\]

Thus **essential irreducibility** here means `Z(L)={0,1}`. For a maximal
block `B`,

\[
 \partial B=\operatorname{BA}_B\!\left(\bigcup_{C\ne B}B\cap C\right).
\]

For a finite complement-closed coherent pattern `p`, `E_B(p)` is the
in-block meet of its required value-one events and complements of its
required value-zero events. With `C_p` the global finitely additive face,

\[
 K_{B,p}=\{\mu|_{\partial B}:\mu\in C_p\}.
\]

Writing `T_B^σ(E)` for boundary traces admitting a σ-additive block state
which charges `E`, **face-relative openness** is

\[
 K_{B,p}\cap T_B^\sigma(E_B(p))\text{ open in }K_{B,p}.
\]

**GSD** is the common-trace simultaneous version: some `μ∈C_p` has, at
every block, a pattern-eligible local σ-state agreeing with `μ` on the
overlap boundary. Compatible block-state gluing makes GSD equivalent to a
global σ-state extending `p`. Local nonopenness, face-exposed nonopenness,
and failure of GSD are three strictly different claims.

The question tested here is whether the dense nonopen locus of the
countable-coordinate `A × MO₂` example can be face-exposed when `Z(L)` is
trivial.

## 2. Why two blocks cannot suffice

**Two-block centrality lemma.** Let `B,C` be Boolean blocks in an OML and
let `L₀` be the sub-OML generated by `B∪C`. Then

\[
 B\cap C\subseteq Z(L_0).
\]

Indeed, `a∈B∩C` is compatible with every generator in `B∪C`. The commutant
of a fixed element is a sub-OML, so it contains `L₀`; hence `a` is central
in `L₀`. In particular, if `B,C` are the only maximal blocks of `L`, then
the standard centre formula gives

\[
 Z(L)=B\cap C.
\]

This does not depend on concreteness, σ-completeness, or a special set
pasting. It does require that “generated” means generated as an OML. A bare
union of two Boolean algebras amalgamated over an interface can fail even
to be an orthoposet closed under orthogonal joins, so it is not a
counterexample to the lemma.

Applied to `A×MO₂`, the actual interface `A×2` is central. Retaining only
two maximal blocks while preserving this interface therefore cannot remove
the centre.

## 3. Multi-block centre calculation

For any OML,

\[
 a\in Z(L)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 a\in C\text{ for every maximal block }C.
\tag{C}
\]

Consequently:

1. `a∈B∩C_j` for every maximal block `C_j` is exactly a centrality
   criterion, not merely a sufficient condition.
2. Membership in several but not all pairwise overlaps says nothing about
   centrality with the omitted blocks.
3. An element of
   `BA_B(⋃_j(B∩C_j))` need not be central. Boolean generation preserves
   compatibility with every block common to all the generators, but a
   generator shared only with `C_j` can fail to commute with `C_k`.
4. Trivial common intersection of all maximal blocks proves trivial centre
   by (C), but trivial intersection of only the displayed or claimed blocks
   does so only after their maximality and exhaustiveness are proved.

For a block-pasting architecture with a verified exhaustive maximal-block
family `𝔅`, the usable exact formula is therefore

\[
 Z(L)=\bigcap_{C\in\mathfrak B}C.
\]

There is no corresponding formula replacing the intersection by the
Boolean algebra generated by pairwise interfaces.

## 4. Distributed centre-killing attempts

### 4.1 Three-block crossed interfaces

The smallest finite model was tested before attempting the
countable-coordinate lift. Let the distinguished four-atom Boolean block
`B` contain crossing two-atom events `a={0,1}` and `b={0,2}`. Attach `C_a`
by replacing the decompositions below `a,aᶜ`, and attach `C_b` similarly
below `b,bᶜ`. Thus

\[
 |B\cap C_a|=|B\cap C_b|=4,
 \qquad |C_a\cap C_b|=2,
\]

and the displayed common intersection is `{0,1}`.

Representing every event on all 16 compatible two-valued block valuations
gives a 40-event concrete family. Exhaustive checking finds:

- complements are present;
- a disjoint cross-block pair has no union in the family;
- another cross-block pair has two incomparable maximal lower bounds;
- dually it has two incomparable minimal upper bounds.

So the first crossed-interface paste is neither a concrete σ-class nor a
lattice. The failure occurs before centre, maximal-block, or topology
questions arise. Receipt:
`python3 notes/open_questions/verification/three_block_interface_audit.py`.

Closing this family under the missing operations is not harmless: it adds
events and new maximal blocks and changes the generated boundaries. In this
case, however, the forced completion can be computed exactly and succeeds.

### 4.2 The 56-event centre-free completion

Complete `B∪C_a` first. The shared event `a` is central in the OML it
generates, so the completion decomposes into the intervals below `a` and
`aᶜ`. In either interval the `B` decomposition and the `C_a` decomposition
form `MO₂`. Hence

\[
 L_a\cong MO_2\times MO_2,\qquad |L_a|=36.
\]

The same construction across `b` gives

\[
 L_b\cong MO_2\times MO_2,\qquad |L_b|=36.
\]

On the original 16-point valuation carrier put `L*=L_a∪L_b`. Exhaustive
set-level calculation gives `L_a∩L_b=B`, and therefore

\[
 |L^*|=36+36-16=56.
\]

The calculation verifies:

- closure under complements and every binary disjoint union;
- unique meet and join for all `56²` ordered pairs;
- the orthomodular law for every comparable pair;
- exactly seven maximal Boolean blocks, all of size 16;
- `B,C_a,C_b` are among those maximal blocks;
- the original overlap sizes remain `4,4,2`;
- `Z(L*)={0,1}`.

Since the carrier and event family are finite, binary disjoint-union
closure implies countable disjoint-union closure. Thus `L*` is a finite
concrete σ-complete essentially irreducible OML. Receipt:
`python3 notes/open_questions/verification/three_block_completion_audit.py`.

The four new maximal blocks create a decisive tradeoff. For every maximal
block `M` of `L*`, exhaustive Boolean generation gives

\[
 \partial M=\operatorname{BA}_M\!\left(
   \bigcup_{N\ne M}M\cap N\right)=M.
\tag{Saturation}
\]

In particular `∂B=B`: centre removal succeeds by distributing enough
overlap information to eliminate the earlier proper-boundary/private-
generator architecture.

### 4.3 Coordinate splitting

Let `I=I₁⊔I₂`. The sub-σ-fields of events depending only on `I₁` and only
on `I₂` have trivial intersection, but their finite Boolean join generally
does **not** equal the countable-coordinate algebra `A`: it contains finite
Boolean combinations of separated events, not arbitrary subsets depending
jointly on countably many coordinates. Splitting into one-coordinate sites
is worse: the generated Boolean boundary is only the finite-coordinate
cylinder algebra.

Using all countable-coordinate sites `A_J` (`J⊂I` countable) does cover
`A` and can have trivial total intersection, but requires a large atlas.
No checked paste supplies all meets while keeping those sites as the actual
pairwise overlaps. Thus coordinate splitting succeeds only at the abstract
boundary-generation level; it has no OML realization receipt.

Disjoint, almost-disjoint, chain, cyclic, and interlaced covers do not
alter this first issue. Chains large enough to be cofinal among all
countable supports cannot be countable when `I` is uncountable, and cyclic
finite covers still miss arbitrary cross-coordinate subsets.

### 4.4 Twists, carrier splitting, and iterated pastes

A concrete overlap is literal equality of subsets of one carrier. A
“twisted embedding” must therefore be implemented by a carrier
identification, not merely by renaming an abstract Boolean interface.
Different identifications change which cross-block subsets are disjoint;
the three-block receipt shows that precisely these cross relations create
missing orthogonal joins and meets.

Carrier duplication can preserve each interface separately, but the
duplicated summand is then selected by a common event and recreates a
central direct factor unless another block crosses that selector. The
finite completion shows that adding such crossings can restore all meets,
but it may also saturate the overlap-generated boundaries.

Iterated/tree pastes avoid a short cycle, but no argument currently shows
that their σ-completion remains a lattice or that their maximal blocks are
only the intended ones. Accordingly none of these is a construction.

## 5. Topological verdict

The reducible product establishes:

- **local topology:** dense nonopen `T_B^σ(E)`;
- **face exposure:** a one-event face has `K_{B,p}` equal to the whole bad
  Stone component;
- **not descent failure:** it does not show `C_p⊆⋃_B D_B(p)` and is not a
  failure of `Phi`.

Trivial centre is now realized, but the finite example has discrete state
and boundary spaces and hence cannot exhibit dense nonopen topology. Thus
none of the three pathological levels is established or ruled out in an
infinite essentially irreducible example. In particular, (C) alone supplies no topology: noncentral
pairwise interface elements may still generate a boundary with a bad Stone
image. Conversely, no valid argument shows that finite global faces must
avoid such elements. The proposed boundary-centrality, face-exposure,
overlap-saturation, and central-decomposition theorems therefore remain
open in unrestricted essentially irreducible OMLs.

The finite cluster normal form does not regularize the image: it controls
only finitely many event values, while `K_{B,p}` may be an arbitrary compact
continuous image in an infinite boundary Stone space. Essential
irreducibility has not yet supplied the missing closedness or openness.

Boundary saturation is not itself a regularity theorem. When `∂B=B`, the
trace locus is the eligible σ-state locus of the whole coarse block, which
can still be dense and nonopen. The unresolved issue is whether the
seven-block incidence pattern admits an infinite σ-complete inflation that
preserves latticehood and exposes that locus on a finite global face.

## 6. Compact simultaneous selection, sharpened

Fix a finite pattern `p`. For each maximal block `B`, let

\[
 X_B=\{v\in\operatorname{St}_\sigma(B):v(E_B(p))=1\},
\]

with the subspace topology inherited from `{0,1}^B`.

**Compact finite-subatlas globalization.** Suppose every `X_B` is nonempty
compact and every finite set `F` of maximal blocks admits
`(v_B)_{B∈F}∈∏_{B∈F}X_B` agreeing on every overlap. Then a compatible
family exists on the whole atlas. It glues to a global σ-additive
two-valued state extending `p`.

Proof: `X=∏_B X_B` is compact by Tychonoff (hence uses Choice in this
generality). For every pair `B,C` and `A∈B∩C`, the equation
`v_B(A)=v_C(A)` is clopen in `X`. A finite collection of equations mentions
only finitely many blocks, so finite-subatlas solvability is exactly the
finite-intersection property. Compactness gives a point satisfying all
equations. `BoundaryDescent.glueBlockStates` glues it, and its Lean proof
shows that blockwise σ-additivity implies global σ-additivity. Eligibility
on every block gives the prescribed finite pattern (after complement
closure).

There is a useful fixed-trace specialization. Given one global finitely
additive `μ`, put

\[
 X_B(\mu,p)=\{v\in X_B:v|_{\partial B}=\mu|_{\partial B}\}.
\]

If every `X_B(μ,p)` is nonempty, no finite-subatlas hypothesis is needed:
Choice selects one point in every fibre and the common `μ` makes all
overlap equations automatic. Compactness is relevant when compatibility is
not already imposed through one common trace, or when nonemptiness itself
is obtained by an FIP argument.

### Compact local-state classes

The hypothesis is verified in the following precise classes:

- finite blocks, and more generally finite eligible σ-state sets;
- any block for which the eligible σ-state locus is closed in `{0,1}^B`;
- point-realized classes whose evaluation image (equivalently the carrier
  quotient by event indistinguishability) is compact and equals the
  eligible σ-state locus;
- Boolean algebras for which all σ-ultrafilters are principal and that
  principal locus is compact;
- explicitly compact loci of countably complete ultrafilters on coarse
  blocks.

The last three are criteria, not automatic consequences of the labels.
Standard Borel or Polish blocks do not suffice: a noncompact standard
Borel carrier has a noncompact Dirac locus in the evaluation topology.
Nor does compactness of all finitely additive states imply compactness of
the σ-additive subspace.

σ-additivity is not generally closed. For a fixed decreasing sequence
`A_n↓∅`, continuity from above says `v(A_n)=0` for some `n`; this is an open
union of cylinder conditions, not a closed condition. A compactification
has no false lifts if one additionally has **uniform continuity from
above** on the candidate family: for every such sequence there is an `N`
with `v(A_N)=0` for every candidate `v`. Each constraint is then clopen and
persists to the closure. This is a genuine sufficient condition, but it is
stronger than pointwise σ-additivity and is not supplied by OML
latticehood.

Finite-subatlas solvability is also not automatic. A finite atlas can have
infinite interfaces and noncompact σ-state fibres; “finite effective
interface” yields it only when the finite traces can be extended by local
σ-states that agree on the entire actual overlaps.

## 8. Strategic decision and next handoff

The result is **Outcome A at the finite skeleton level and Outcome D at the
infinite/topological level**. Two-block centre removal is impossible, but
the completed three-block crossing is a centre-free concrete σ-complete
OML. What remains unresolved is not finite realization but coarse
inflation and face exposure.

The single best next problem is:

> Inflate the 56-event centre-free completion while preserving
> σ-completeness and producing a dense-nonopen σ-state or boundary-trace
> locus; alternatively prove that boundary saturation in every such
> irreducible completion forces σ-tameness.

This splits into two exact tests:

1. replace the finite `MO₂×MO₂` interval components by
   countable-coordinate σ-field components while retaining the seven-block
   incidence pattern and all lattice operations;
2. if `∂B=B` persists, determine whether a finite coherent global face can
   expose the dense nonopen σ-state locus and, separately, whether the
   resulting distributed defects can obstruct GSD.

Direct simultaneous selection remains primary. Do not infer failure of
`Phi` from full-boundary nonopenness, and do not resume broad rooting or
relay searches before the infinite realization/face-image gate is passed.

# Non-atomic pair and mixed-cut completion

*Campaign 10, 2026-07-14. The first certified simultaneous non-atomic
substitution fails raw latticehood; its canonical orthogonal completion is a
centre-free but Phi-tame finite OML.*

## 1. Certified pair and interval charts

Take the first proxy qualifier, selector IDs `(11,14)` with masks

\[
 e=0x00ff,\qquad f=0x0f0f.
\]

Their supports are respectively

\[
 \{A00,A01,A10,A11\},\qquad \{A00,C01\},
\]

and their only common named block is `A00`. In that block write

\[
 p=0x000f,\quad a=0x00f0,\quad b=0x0f00,\quad c=0xf000,
\]

so that `e=p join a` and `f=p join b`. All three Boolean regions
`e meet f`, `e meet f^perp`, and `e^perp meet f` are nonzero.

Below `e`, blocks `A00,A01` use the chart

\[
 B_0=\{0,p,a,e\},
\]

whereas `A10,A11` use the transverse chart

\[
 B_1=\{0,u,v,e\},\qquad u=0x0033,\quad v=0x00cc.
\]

The interval union is the horizontal sum `B_0 boxplus B_1`: every
nontrivial cross pair has meet zero and join `e`. **Evidence class: hand
proved from the certified skeleton.**

### Horizontal-chart lemma

Form the abstract horizontal sum of any Boolean enlargement `B'_0` with
`B_1`: identify only `0,e` and impose no other cross comparabilities.
Equivalently, assume every nontrivial cross pair has no nonzero common lower
bound and no proper common upper bound. This is the interval OML
`B'_0 boxplus B_1`. Hence for every `0<h<=p`,

\[
 h\wedge u=h\wedge v=0,\qquad h\vee u=h\vee v=e.
\]

Thus the abstract horizontal-sum completion does not transport a refinement
of `p` into the transverse chart. In a concrete OML these lattice meets need
not equal the nonempty set intersections. Intersection only at `0,e` would
not by itself prove the horizontal-sum order. **Evidence class: hand proved.**

## 2. Literal independent binary substitution

For the smallest `P(2) x P(2)` control, replace an old point below both
`e,f` by its product with both binary coordinates, a point below only `e`
or only `f` by the corresponding single coordinate, and every other point
rigidly. A named block sees a coordinate exactly when it contains its
selector. The raw union of the five substituted Boolean blocks has:

- 36 carrier points;
- 648 events;
- block sizes `512/64/64/64/64` for
  `A00/A01/C01/A10/A11`;
- complement closure.

It is not a concrete event algebra. The disjoint events

\[
 x=0x1111\in A00,\qquad y=0xa00cc\in A10\cap A11
\]

have missing union `0xa11dd`. The same pair has exactly two incomparable
minimal upper bounds,

\[
 0x5f33ff,\qquad 0xaadddd,
\]

so the raw family is not a lattice. The producer exhausts all 419,904
ordered pairs and 23,959 ordered disjoint pairs. **Evidence class:
exhaustive finite evidence.** A separate implementation reconstructs the
carrier and verifies the counts, hash, missing union, and two upper bounds.
**Evidence class: executable verified.**

This is the exact mixed-cut failure of the literal atlas; no state claim is
made for the raw family.

## 3. Canonical orthogonal completion

Close the raw family under complement and binary unions of disjoint concrete
events. The exact round sizes are

\[
 648\longrightarrow1128\longrightarrow1128.
\]

The completion is not the full Boolean algebra generated by the raw blocks.
It is a concrete finite OML with:

- 1,272,384 ordered event pairs;
- 18,712 distinct intersection cuts;
- 488,128 ordered compatible pairs;
- centre `{0,1}`;
- nine exhaustive maximal compatibility blocks of sizes
  `512,256,256,256,256,128,128,128,64`.

Every original named block has a unique maximal-block container. Four new
maximal blocks contain no entire original named block: two have size 256 and
two have size 128. They are the mixed-cut repair blocks.

The producer exhausts all binary extrema and the orthomodular law and runs
an exact Bron--Kerbosch maximal-clique census. **Evidence class: exhaustive
finite evidence.** An independent implementation reconstructs the closure,
all aggregate counts and hashes, unique extrema, OML law, centre, all nine
maximal blocks and their hashes. **Evidence class: executable verified.**

## 4. State audit and rectangularization

Exhaustive compatible-ultrafilter gluing over the nine maximal blocks gives
exactly 24 global two-valued states. These are exactly the 24 distinct
carrier point-state profiles, so point states order-separate the completed
OML. For the first binary coordinate events

\[
 q_0=0x553333,qquad r_0=0x55005555,
\]

the induced relation is the full rectangle

\[
 \{(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\}.
\]

The completion is finite, hence sigma-complete and every finitely additive
state is sigma-additive. It is therefore Phi-tame. **Evidence class:
exhaustive finite evidence** for the state census and relation;
**hand proved** for the finite sigma/Phi consequence. The independent
verifier reproduces the complete state enumeration. **Evidence class:
executable verified.**

## 5. Exact outcome

This candidate fails in two stages:

1. the literal five-block atlas lacks a forced mixed disjoint union and has
   two incomparable upper bounds;
2. canonical cut completion restores a centre-free OML but creates four
   new mixed maximal blocks and fills the coordinate relation.

The defect collapses by **rectangularization without centralization**. This
is stronger than the earlier common-centre failure and is a reusable warning:
trivial centre does not prevent mixed cuts from filling a local relation.
The result is finite and does not prove an arbitrary-base rectangularization
theorem. **Evidence class: exhaustive finite evidence.**

## 6. Diagonal common-region control

Restrict the binary coordinates over the overlap region `p` to the diagonal
`{(0,0),(1,1)}`, while retaining a free first coordinate on `e minus f`, a
free second coordinate on `f minus e`, and rigid points elsewhere. All five
named block embeddings remain literal subset embeddings.

The raw family has 28 points and 264 events. It is complement closed but
again misses a mixed disjoint union: `0x55` and `0xa0a` are disjoint, their
union `0xa5f` is absent, and they have two minimal upper bounds. Canonical
orthogonal completion stabilizes as

\[
 264\longrightarrow392\longrightarrow392.
\]

The completion is a concrete finite OML with trivial centre and exactly nine
maximal blocks, four of which are new mixed blocks. Their sizes are four
blocks of 128 and five of 64. It has exactly 20 global two-valued states,
all equal to distinct point-state profiles.

The unrestricted coordinate projection is the full rectangle because
states may charge either exclusive selector region. Conditional on charging
the joint region `p`, however, the coordinate relation remains exactly

\[
 \{(0,0),(1,1)\}.
\]

Thus mixed-cut completion neither centralizes nor inevitably rectangularizes
the **conditional** relation. **Evidence class: exhaustive finite evidence.**
An independent implementation reproduces the raw obstruction, completion,
nine-block census, all 20 states, and both relations. **Evidence class:
executable verified.**

This is a realizable finite noncentral transported-relation control, but it
is Phi-tame by finiteness. Replacing its one diagonal edge by a single
nonclosed relation is not by itself decisive: when `R_fa=closure(R_sigma)`,
every finite trace on that edge already meets `R_sigma`. A failure of Phi in
a system whose every local edge has this density property must distribute
several relations so that all finite/countable subnetworks lift but the
global network has no compatible sigma-section. A single locally nondense
edge remains a separate logical alternative.

The next construction must therefore reuse the relation-preserving mixed-cut
completion on at least two inequivalent edges or an explicitly
uncountable-incidence network. The structural gate is whether each
arbitrary-base completion remains a concrete sigma-complete OML without
identifying or centralizing the transported coordinate copies.

## 7. Single-edge density theorem and exact pivot

Let a charged face have finitely additive trace space `R_fa` and
sigma-additive trace space `R_sigma` in the finite-event product topology.
If

\[
 R_{fa}=\overline{R_\sigma},
\]

then that face satisfies Phi: a finite trace cylinder containing a point of
`R_fa` is a basic open neighbourhood, hence meets `R_sigma`. Adding the
finite condition that `p` is charged only passes to another clopen face.
A finite union of such dense branches is Phi-tame branchwise. **Evidence
class: hand proved.**

Therefore one preserved nonclosed edge cannot be the counterexample when
its finitely additive locus is exactly the closure of its sigma locus. The
decisive target is a distributed system with

\[
 s\in GS(R_{fa})
\]

and a finite cylinder `U` containing `s` such that

\[
 U\cap GS(R_\sigma)=\varnothing,
\]

despite dense local edge relations. By the locally-countable component
theorem, a genuine CSS-without-GS architecture must expose an
uncountable-incidence hub, an infinite-arity constraint, or a coarse
inverse-limit coordinate. The finite diagonal cell resolves local
realizability only; arbitrary-base sigma-completion and distributed assembly
remain open.

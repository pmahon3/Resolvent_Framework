# Smallest proper-joint-closure transverse candidate

*Completed 2026-07-13. Outcome B for the scoped candidate: duplicating one
joint cell gives the first five-atom endpoint pattern, and its canonical
mixed completion is the 48-element concrete OML `MO2 x P(3)`. All finite
structural gates pass except trivial centre, so this candidate is reducible
and Phi-tame. The minimality and product identification are hand proofs; the
48-element census is finite executable evidence; there is no Lean
certificate and no infinite classification.*

## 1. Candidate and scoped minimality

Continue the binary `P/Q` versus `P/R` architecture from the four-point
control. Its four joint cells must all be nonempty for the two overlap
families to be transverse. With four endpoint atoms each joint cell is a
singleton, so the two families generate the whole endpoint Boolean algebra.
Proper closure in only the other endpoint cannot rescue a literal two-block
OML: if the common generated algebra equals one endpoint block, that block is
contained in the other, and maximal Booleanity forces the blocks to coincide.
Thus proper joint closure requires at least five atoms in each endpoint. Five
suffice: duplicate the `00` cell.

Label each endpoint's atoms `0a,0b,1,2,3`. On the left take

\[
 P=0a0b1\mid 23,\qquad Q=0a0b2\mid 13,
\]

and on the right take

\[
 P'=0a0b1\mid 23,\qquad R'=0a0b3\mid 12.
\]

Each individual overlap is a four-element Boolean algebra. The joint closure
has atoms `0a0b,1,2,3`, hence 16 elements, properly inside each 32-element
endpoint. This proves minimality only within this binary four-joint-cell
architecture. It is not a lower bound for every transverse OML construction.

## 2. Concrete mixed completion

Let `X={0,1,2,3,4,5,6}`. On the first four points use the two Boolean blocks

\[
 \{01,23\},\qquad \{02,13\}
\]

of the concrete six-element `MO2`, and adjoin the three singleton atoms
`4,5,6` to both. The resulting Boolean blocks `M,N` each have five atoms and
32 events. Their union

\[
 L=M\cup N \cong MO2\times\mathcal P(\{4,5,6\})
\]

has `32+32-16=48` events. Its common algebra is

\[
 E=M\cap N=\mathcal P(\{0123,4,5,6\}),
\]

with 16 elements. The two four-element algebras generated by
`0123 union 4` and `0123 union 5` are distinct, proper, and jointly generate
`E`. Relabelling the atoms of `N` by the right-hand `P'/R'` coordinates gives
exactly the displayed transverse equations.

The product description is a hand proof of latticehood and orthomodularity:
both factors are OMLs. It also shows that `M,N` are exactly the two maximal
Boolean blocks. The centre is

\[
 Z(L)=Z(MO2)\times\mathcal P(\{4,5,6\})=E,
\]

so it has 16 elements. Thus proper joint closure is achieved only by leaving
a nontrivial common central factor in this smallest completion.

## 3. State relation

The two individual edge relations are inequivalent and neither implies the
other. Their intersection has seven pairs: a full `2 x 2` rectangle over the
duplicated `00` cell and one pair over each of the other three cells. Its
left and right degrees are `2,2,1,1,1` up to ordering.

All seven compatible endpoint pairs extend globally: they are the seven
point evaluations on `X`. Conversely, compatibility on the common algebra
forces the two endpoint ultrafilters into the same joint cell, so these are
all global two-valued states. Point evaluations order-separate `L`, since a
failed subset inclusion has a point in the set difference.

## 5. Conclusion and next gate

The smallest binary transverse architecture with proper joint closure exists
as a concrete sigma-complete OML and passes latticehood, maximal-block, state
extension, and order-separation gates. It fails the required irreducibility
gate maximally relevant to the mechanism: the whole joint closure is central.
Thus proper joint closure alone does not produce nontrivial transport; here
the relation is again the single pullback over `E`.

The next candidate must distribute the four joint cells across at least
three blocks so that mixed completion does not make their joint closure
central. Its completion, centre, maximal blocks, and state relation must be
recomputed rather than inferred from this two-block product.

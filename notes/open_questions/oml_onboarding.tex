\title{$\sigma$-Additive Probability on Orthomodular Lattices:\\
A Survey and an Open Problem}
\author{Patrick S.\ Mahon}
\date{Survey note --- June 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The passage from a finitely coherent assignment of probabilities to a
countably additive measure is, in the Boolean case, governed by a single
condition (countable additivity) and executed by classical machinery
(Carath\'{e}odory; Loomis--Sikorski; Stone duality). On a
\emph{non-distributive} orthomodular lattice (OML) --- the algebra of
propositions of a quantum system --- this machinery breaks, and the
question of when finite coherence forces countable behaviour is open. We
survey the problem at the level needed to begin work on it. After fixing
the apparatus (orthomodular lattices, the gap between orthogonality and
meet-zero, the state/meet-additive/charge ladder, the McDonald--Bimb\'{o}
dual space), we separate the question into two axes. The \emph{extension}
axis is classical (Horn--Tarski feasibility) and fully settled: on
$L(H)$ no state of any kind extends, by an elementary finite
construction; the \emph{descent} axis is genuinely open and rests on a
representation that does not exist: a Loomis--Sikorski theorem for
$\sigma$-complete OMLs. The stake is more than
technical: such a representation would furnish a \emph{point-free},
$\sigma$-additive probability theory on a non-distributive lattice --- the
non-Boolean analogue of localic measure theory --- built from the
entailment relation rather than descended from a sample space. We review
what is known and what is ruled out, and close with the two directions in
which the open problem might be approached.
\end{abstract}

% ==================================================================
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ==================================================================

All measurement is finite, yet the frameworks that organise empirical
knowledge --- probability theory, quantum mechanics --- rest on infinite
structures. The passage from finite observation to infinite structure is
not automatic; it requires a commitment no finite body of evidence can
compel. In probability theory the locus of that commitment is
\emph{countable additivity}:
\[
  A_1 \supseteq A_2 \supseteq \cdots, \quad \bigcap_n A_n = \varnothing
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mu(A_n) \to 0.
\]
De~Finetti~\cite{deFinetti1974} held that only finite additivity is
empirically grounded; Kolmogorov~\cite{Kolmogorov} adopted
$\sigma$-additivity as an axiom.

The same tension recurs once observations need not be jointly performable.
An observation \emph{context} --- a set of measurements performable together
--- is Boolean; gluing contexts along shared observations (a categorical
colimit) returns:
\[
  \text{contexts mutually compatible}\ \Rightarrow\ \text{Boolean algebra},
  \qquad
  \text{incompatible}\ \Rightarrow\ \text{non-distributive OML}
\]
(Gunji et al.~\cite{Gunji2026}; the dichotomy organises the companion
paper~\cite{MahonPaperII2026}). The OML is \emph{forced} by the context
structure, not postulated. Incompatibility --- quantum complementarity, but
equally contextual experimental design or interfering sensors --- drives the
algebra out of the Boolean world. One caveat fixes the scope: for non-quantum
examples the incompatibility must be \emph{operationally imposed} (contexts
genuinely non-co-realizable, no joint probability space); it cannot arise
intrinsically from one classical system's dynamics, since any finite family of
classical observables on a common space jointly distributes (Kolmogorov),
hence stays Boolean --- contextuality there is failure of a global section
(Abramsky--Brandenburger~\cite{AbramskyBrandenburger2011};
Fine~\cite{Fine1982}). The closed subspaces of a Hilbert space $H$ form the
prototype $L(H)$, not the premise.

On a non-distributive OML, Carath\'{e}odory's outer measure --- needing a
Boolean algebra of sets --- does not apply, and the rescue is local to $L(H)$:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=2pt]
  \item For $L(H)$, Gleason's rigidity theorem~\cite{Gleason1957} rescues it;
    Hilbert space is special and no general-OML analogue is known.
  \item Non-distributivity \emph{per se} does not remove the 2-valued
    homomorphisms the Boolean engine needs --- $\mathrm{MO}_3$ is
    non-distributive yet carries them (\S\ref{sec:oml}, \S\ref{sec:known}) ---
    but it removes the \emph{guarantee}; for $L(H)$, $\dim\!\geq\!3$ they fail
    outright (Kochen--Specker~\cite{KochenSpecker1967}).
\end{itemize}
\emph{Whether enough survive is a question of concreteness, not
non-distributivity} --- the qualifier on which the open problem turns
(\S\ref{sec:known},~\S\ref{sec:problem}).

\paragraph{The organising split.} This note asks the shared question ---
\emph{when does finite coherence force countable behaviour?} --- on an OML.
The vague question splits into two axes that the Boolean case fuses, and the
survey is built around the split:
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Axis} & \textbf{Asks} & \textbf{Status} \\
\midrule
Extension & does a state extend to a charge on $S_0(A)$?
  & settled (on $L(H)$, \emph{none} does) \\
Descent & does the charge concentrate on physical points?
  & \textbf{open} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{The relational standpoint.} Two established moves motivate the
approach:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=2pt]
  \item De~Finetti~\cite{deFinetti1974} --- probability without a presupposed
    sample space: coherent commitments about events, not measures on a given
    $\Omega$.
  \item Birkhoff--von~Neumann --- propositions as a lattice (the OML of
    \S\ref{sec:prelim}), not as subsets of a phase space.
\end{itemize}
\noindent Combining them, we start from the propositions and their entailment
order and ask what that relational structure alone determines --- the
measure-theoretic counterpart of pointless (localic) topology. The dual is
then not a space of points but the McDonald--Bimb\'{o} space of \emph{filters}
(\S\ref{sec:dual}), and a positive answer to the open problem would be a
\emph{point-free} $\sigma$-additive probability theory on a non-distributive
lattice --- the non-Boolean analogue of localic measure theory
(\S\ref{sec:problem}; developed further in~\cite{mahon2025}).

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}
% ==================================================================

We collect the lattice-theoretic apparatus; all notions are standard.

\subsection{Orthomodular lattices, and the orthogonal/meet-zero gap}
\label{sec:oml}

\begin{definition}[Orthocomplemented lattice]\label{def:ocl}
A \emph{bounded lattice} is a partially ordered set in which every pair
$a,b$ has a join $a \vee b$ and meet $a \wedge b$, with greatest element
$\mathbf 1$ and least element $\mathbf 0$. An \emph{orthocomplementation}
is a map $a \mapsto a^\perp$ satisfying, for all $a,b$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*), noitemsep]
  \item $a \wedge a^\perp = \mathbf 0$ and $a \vee a^\perp = \mathbf 1$
    (complement),
  \item $a^{\perp\perp} = a$ (involution),
  \item $a \leq b \Rightarrow b^\perp \leq a^\perp$ (order-reversing).
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Orthogonal; meet-zero]\label{def:ortho}
Elements $a,b$ are \emph{orthogonal}, written $a \perp b$, if
$a \leq b^\perp$ (equivalently $b \leq a^\perp$) --- one entails the other's
negation, \emph{decisively incompatible}. They are \emph{meet-zero} if
$a \wedge b = \mathbf 0$ --- no common refinement, a weaker condition.
\end{definition}

\begin{quote}
\textbf{The gap.} Orthogonality always implies meet-zero; the converse holds
in every Boolean algebra but \textbf{fails} once the lattice is
non-distributive. \emph{Meet-zero is strictly weaker than orthogonal} ---
the distinctions developed below all turn on this.
\end{quote}

\begin{definition}[Orthomodular lattice]\label{def:oml}
An orthocomplemented lattice $A$ is \emph{orthomodular} if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oml-law}
  a \leq b \quad\Longrightarrow\quad b = a \vee (a^\perp \wedge b).
\end{equation}
A \emph{Boolean algebra} is the special case where $\wedge$ distributes
over $\vee$; every Boolean algebra is orthomodular, not conversely. The
motivating non-Boolean example is $L(H)$, the closed subspaces of a
Hilbert space with $a^\perp$ the orthogonal complement.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[$\mathrm{MO}_n$, and the gap made concrete]\label{ex:mo}
$\mathrm{MO}_n$ is the lattice with $\mathbf 0$, $\mathbf 1$, and $n$
complementary pairs of incomparable atoms, all sharing only $\mathbf 0$
and $\mathbf 1$. \emph{Picture $n$ lines through the origin: each line $a$
is $\perp$ to its perpendicular $a^\perp$, but two atoms from distinct pairs
are non-perpendicular lines.} It is orthomodular and non-distributive
($n \geq 2$): two atoms drawn from \emph{distinct} complementary pairs have
\[
  a \wedge b = \mathbf 0 \quad(\text{meet only at the origin})
  \qquad\text{yet}\qquad a \not\perp b,
\]
so meet-zero $\neq$ orthogonal already here. $\mathrm{MO}_3$ is the smallest
non-distributive OML.
\end{example}

\begin{definition}[OMP; concrete logic]\label{def:omp}
An \emph{orthomodular poset} (OMP) weakens Definition~\ref{def:oml}: joins
$a \vee b$ are required only for \emph{orthogonal} pairs. An OMP is a
\emph{concrete logic} (equivalently \emph{set-representable}) if it embeds
into $(\mathcal P(X), \subseteq, X \setminus(-))$ for some set $X$,
preserving existing orthogonal joins --- i.e.\ its propositions can be
modelled as actual \emph{sets of outcomes}. (Concreteness is the property
that separates the open frontier from the settled cases in
\S\ref{sec:problem}, so it earns a name now.)
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Concreteness does not close the gap]\label{rem:concrete}
A set-representable OMP needs only closure under complement and
\emph{disjoint} union~\cite{BuresovaPtak2024}; intersection-closure would
force a field of sets, hence Boolean. The lattice meet $a \wedge b$ is the
greatest $L$-member inside $A \cap B$, so orthogonality
($A \cap B = \varnothing$) implies meet-zero, but not conversely. Indeed
$\mathrm{MO}_3$ is \emph{itself} concrete (it carries an order-determining
set of two-valued states; Gudder~\cite{Gudder1979}), so the paradigmatic
meet-zero-$\neq$-
orthogonal example is a concrete logic. The condition that closes the gap
is not concreteness but a richness/atomicity condition (meet-zero
coincides with orthogonality iff every nonempty $A \cap B$ contains a
nonzero $L$-member); the abstract form $a \wedge b = \mathbf 0 \Rightarrow
a \perp b$ is Tkadlec's \emph{Boolean orthoposet}
condition~\cite{Tkadlec1994} (which does
\emph{not} mean Boolean \emph{algebra}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{The state/meet-additive/charge ladder}
\label{sec:ladder}

\begin{definition}[State; meet-additive state; charge-extendible]\label{def:ladder}
Let $A$ be an OML. A \emph{state} is a map $s : A \to [0,1]$ with
$s(\mathbf 1)=1$ that is additive on \emph{orthogonal} pairs:
$s(a \vee b) = s(a) + s(b)$ whenever $a \perp b$. \emph{(Probability, but
additivity is only guaranteed where propositions are decisively
incompatible.)} Three strengthenings, differing in \emph{which} pairs
additivity is demanded on, strictly increasing in general (e.g.\ on
$\mathrm{MO}_3$,
Example~\ref{ex:strict}):
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*), noitemsep]
  \item \emph{State} --- additive on orthogonal pairs.
  \item \emph{Meet-additive} --- additive on every \emph{meet-zero} pair
    $a \wedge b = \mathbf 0$ (binary, strictly stronger --- it reaches the
    gap pairs of Definition~\ref{def:ortho}). We avoid the
    word ``valuation'' here: in lattice theory it denotes the modular
    function $v(a)+v(b)=v(a\wedge b)+v(a \vee b)$, a different condition.
  \item \emph{Charge-extendible} --- the $n$-ary condition
    $\sum_i s(a_i) \leq 1$ for every pairwise-meet-zero family $\{a_i\}$
    (strictly stronger again). This is the \emph{extension} condition of
    \S\ref{sec:axes}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[The ladder is strict]\label{ex:strict}
On $\mathrm{MO}_3$ with complementary pairs $(p,p^\perp),(q,q^\perp),
(r,r^\perp)$, the maximally mixed $s \equiv \tfrac12$ is meet-additive
(clears~(2)) yet fails~(3) at $\{p,q,r\}$:
$\tfrac12+\tfrac12+\tfrac12 = \tfrac32 > 1$. So no state on
$\mathrm{MO}_3$ is charge-extendible.
\end{example}

\subsection{The McDonald--Bimb\'{o} dual space}
\label{sec:dual}

\begin{definition}[Dual space; representation map]\label{def:dual}
The McDonald--Bimb\'{o} duality~\cite{McDonaldBimbo2023} assigns to an OML
$A$ a compact space
\[
  S_0(A) = \bigl(F(A), \subseteq, \perp_A, P(A), T\bigr),
\]
where $F(A)$ is the set of \emph{filters} of $A$, ordered by $\subseteq$
and carrying an orthogonality relation $\perp_A$ and a Stone topology $T$,
and $P(A) \subseteq F(A)$ is the set of \emph{principal} filters --- the
``physical points,'' the realisation datum (canonical, unlike the
non-canonical pure points of the Boolean case). For $a \in A$ set
$h(a) = \{x \in F(A) : a \in x\}$; then $h$ is an OML isomorphism onto the
$\perp$-stable clopens $\CO(S_0(A))^\dagger$. Define $\mu(h(a)) = s(a)$.
\emph{(Read $h(a)$ as ``the points where $a$ holds'' and $\mu$ as the state
pushed onto those sets --- the open problem is whether $\mu$ survives
countable operations on them.)}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[The duality is finitary --- the wall]\label{rem:finitary}
The identity $h(\bigvee_n a_n) = (\bigcup_n h(a_n))^{\perp\perp}$ holds for
\emph{finite} joins but fails for countable ones: finitary OML
homomorphisms need not preserve infinite suprema. This finitariness is the
wall the open problem of \S\ref{sec:problem} runs into. (It is a
\emph{countable}-join phenomenon, not specific to joins: since $a \mapsto
a^\perp$ is an order anti-isomorphism, $\bigwedge_n a_n = (\bigvee_n
a_n^\perp)^\perp$, so the failure to preserve countable meets is its De
Morgan dual --- the wall obstructs countable meets and joins together.)
The rival OML duality of Cannon--D\"{o}ring~\cite{Cannon2013} is also
finitary and carries no measure; we use McDonald--Bimb\'{o} because it
carries $P(A)$ as explicit structure.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Boolean engine: Stone duality}
\label{sec:ls}

In the Boolean case, the descent argument
(``$\sigma$-additive $\Leftrightarrow$ the measure concentrates on the
physical points'') runs on \emph{Stone duality}: a Boolean event algebra
embeds in the clopen algebra of its Stone space, a charge extends to a
Baire measure there (Carath\'{e}odory), and $\sigma$-additivity is
equivalent to concentration of that measure on the realised
points~\cite{mahon2025}. That $\sigma$-additive step is realised
measure-theoretically by the meagre-sets characterisation of
$\sigma$-additivity (Rao--Rao~\cite{rao1983}): the measure vanishes on
meagre Baire sets, equivalently lives on the ultrafilters closed under
countable intersection, which are exactly the realised points. This is the
measure-theoretic face of the \emph{Loomis--Sikorski} representation --- a
$\sigma$-complete Boolean algebra is a $\sigma$-tribe of sets modulo a
$\sigma$-ideal --- the countable-join bookkeeping that makes the
equivalence go through. There is \emph{no} OML analogue of any of this,
and supplying one --- or proving none exists --- is the open problem of
\S\ref{sec:problem}: the Boolean proof has an
engine; the OML proof needs one and does not have it.

\begin{remark}[Why the bridge breaks: meet-closed but not join-prime]
\label{rem:bridge}
\emph{The slogan: in the Boolean world ``realised'' bundles two properties
that quantum non-distributivity pulls apart.} The Boolean bridge fuses two
conditions on a realised point $x$: being \emph{closed under countable
meets} and being \emph{off the countable-join defect}
($x \ni \bigvee_n a_n \Rightarrow x \ni a_n$ for some $n$). Rao--Rao's ``vanishes on meagre sets'' is exactly the
coincidence of the two. In a non-distributive OML they \emph{split}: a
principal filter $x = {\uparrow}p$ is always closed under countable meets,
yet may fail join-primeness, since $p \leq \bigvee_n a_n$ does not force
$p \leq a_n$ for any $n$ (take $a_n = \mathrm{span}(e_n)$ in $L(H)$ and
$p = \mathrm{span}(v)$ for \emph{any} line not aligned with a basis vector
--- already $v = e_1 + e_2$: $p \leq \bigvee_n a_n = H$ but $p \not\leq
a_n$ for every $n$). Such an $x$ is a \emph{realised} point that sits
inside the join-defect $D = h(\bigvee_n a_n) \setminus \bigcup_n h(a_n)$. Topologically ${\uparrow}p$ is an
\emph{isolated} point of $S_0(L(H))$, so $D$ is non-meagre --- the reverse
of the Boolean case, where the defect is nowhere dense. This is the
finitary wall of Remark~\ref{rem:finitary} seen on the dual: the
category route (clopens modulo the meagre ideal) chokes on the same $D$
as the measure route, and in fact reduces to the MacNeille completion of
\S\ref{sec:routes}(ii).
\end{remark}

% ==================================================================
\section{Two axes: extension and descent}
\label{sec:axes}
% ==================================================================

The central question --- \emph{when does a state $s$ on an OML $A$ extend
to a $\sigma$-additive measure on $S_0(A)$?} --- splits into two
\textbf{independent} axes that the Boolean case fuses. A state $s$ on $A$
faces:

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.35}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{2.3cm} p{5.3cm} p{5.3cm}@{}}
\toprule
 & \textbf{Extension axis} & \textbf{Descent axis} \\
\midrule
\textbf{Question}
  & Does $\mu$ extend to a finitely additive charge on the \emph{full}
    Boolean clopen algebra of $S_0(A)$?
  & Once extended, does the measure concentrate on the physical points
    $P(A)$? \\
\textbf{Governed by}
  & the meet-zero/orthogonal gap (\S\ref{sec:oml})
  & $\sigma$-additivity --- needs the missing engine (\S\ref{sec:ls}) \\
\textbf{Status}
  & \textbf{settled}: Horn--Tarski / Pitowsky feasibility; finite case
    $=$ decidable LP; can fail for every state.
  & \textbf{open}. \emph{The descent residue.} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Two observations pin down the extension axis.

\begin{proposition}[Finite case]\label{prop:finite}
For finite $A$ the extension condition is a decidable linear program
(Horn--Tarski~\cite{HornTarski1948} / Pitowsky correlation-polytope
feasibility~\cite{Pitowsky1989}); since $h$ preserves meets, meet-zero
elements map to disjoint clopens and a charge must satisfy
$\sum_i s(a_i) \leq 1$ over pairwise-meet-zero families. It can fail for
\emph{every} state ($\mathrm{MO}_3$, Example~\ref{ex:strict}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Infinite $L(H)$, all states]\label{prop:lh}
Let $\dim H = \infty$. \emph{No} state on $L(H)$ --- normal or singular
--- extends to a charge on $S_0(L(H))$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{(The idea: hide a copy of $\mathrm{MO}_2$ inside $L(H)$ whose four
atoms are all infinite-dimensional, so even singular states must charge
them.)} Write $H = H_0 \otimes \mathbb{C}^2$ with $H_0$ infinite-dimensional,
and fix an orthonormal basis $e,f$ of $\mathbb{C}^2$. Set
\[
  a_1 = H_0 \otimes \mathbb{C}e,\quad
  a_1^\perp = H_0 \otimes \mathbb{C}f,\quad
  a_2 = H_0 \otimes \mathbb{C}(e{+}f),\quad
  a_2^\perp = H_0 \otimes \mathbb{C}(e{-}f).
\]
These four (infinite-dimensional) subspaces form a copy of $\mathrm{MO}_2$
inside $L(H)$: $a_1 \perp a_1^\perp$ and $a_2 \perp a_2^\perp$, each pair
joining to $H$, while all four \emph{cross} pairs are meet-zero
(distinct lines in $\mathbb{C}^2$ give $a_i \cap a_j = \mathbf 0$) but
\emph{not} orthogonal. Orthoadditivity gives $s(a_i)+s(a_i^\perp)=s(H)=1$
for $i=1,2$ --- this uses only $s(\mathbf 1)=1$ and additivity on
orthogonal pairs, so it holds for \emph{every} state --- whence
$\sum s(a_i)=2$ over the pairwise-meet-zero family
$\{a_1,a_1^\perp,a_2,a_2^\perp\}$. Since $h$ preserves meets
($h(a\wedge b)=h(a)\cap h(b)$, the filters being meet-closed), meet-zero
elements map to disjoint clopens, so any charge forces
$\sum s(a_i)\le 1$. Contradiction. The construction is finite ($n=4$) and
$\sigma$-completeness of the lattice is irrelevant.
\end{proof}

\noindent The $\mathrm{MO}_2$ count is folklore (orthoadditivity plus
$s(\mathbf 1)=1$; cf.\ \cite{Kalmbach1983}) and $\sum s(a_i)=2$ is already
state-independent in $L(\mathbb C^2)$. What the \emph{infinite-dimensional}
realisation buys is reach to the singular states ($s(e)=0$ on every
finite-rank $e$):
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Atoms of $\mathrm{MO}_2$} & \textbf{$s(a_i)+s(a_i^\perp)$} &
  \textbf{Reaches} \\
\midrule
lines in $L(\mathbb C^2)$ (finite rank)
  & $=1$ only if $s$ charges finite rank & normal states only \\
$H_0\otimes(\cdot)$, $\dim H_0=\infty$
  & $=1$ regardless of finite-rank behaviour & \textbf{all states (sing.\ incl.)} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\noindent Keeping each atom infinite-dimensional drops the finite-rank
precondition of the line argument (Remark~\ref{rem:linearg}), and that is what
closes the extension axis on $L(H)$ completely.

\begin{remark}[An alternative argument for normal states only]
\label{rem:linearg}
A second, technique-disjoint argument reaches the normal states without
the $\mathrm{MO}_2$ family. If $s(e)>0$ on some finite-dimensional $e$,
it is positive on a $2$-plane $e_0$; choosing $k$ pairwise-non-orthogonal
lines $p_1,\dots,p_k \subset e_0$ gives $2k$ distinct pairwise-meet-zero
lines with $s(p_i)+s(p_i^\perp)=s(e_0)$, so a charge forces
$k\,s(e_0)\le 1$ for all $k$ --- impossible once $s(e_0)>0$. Lifting $s$
to a functional via Mackey--Gleason / Bunce--Wright~\cite{BunceWright1992}
($B(H)$ has no type $\mathrm I_2$ summand) and applying the Takesaki
normal/singular decomposition identifies the states it reaches --- those
with nonzero normal part, i.e.\ $s(e)>0$ on some finite-dimensional $e$,
including every normal (Gleason) state --- and the states it misses, the
purely singular ones ($s(e)=0$ on all finite $e$; e.g.\ ultrafilter
vector states or Calkin-algebra pullbacks). Proposition~\ref{prop:lh}
subsumes this case; the line argument is recorded because its technique
is independent and because it makes precise where the singular states
sit. The open problem is on the descent axis
(\S\ref{sec:problem}).
\end{remark}

% ==================================================================
\section{What is known}
\label{sec:known}
% ==================================================================

The extension axis is settled --- on $L(H)$ no state extends
(Proposition~\ref{prop:lh}); the descent axis turns on whether the Boolean
engine of \S\ref{sec:ls} has an OML analogue. Three things bear on that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}, noitemsep, topsep=2pt]
  \item the Boolean benchmark such an analogue must match (\S\ref{sec:known}.1);
  \item the partial OML results, positive and obstructive (\S\ref{sec:known}.2);
  \item the routes already known to be blocked (\S\ref{sec:routes}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Boolean benchmark}
\emph{(The target an OML engine would have to hit.)} The
Kelley--Vladimirov--Pt\'{a}k characterisation (Fremlin~\cite{Fremlin3},
Thm 391D) is complete: a Boolean algebra carries a strictly positive
$\sigma$-additive measure iff it satisfies all three conditions below ---
each of which loses its meaning off the distributive world.
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{5.1cm} p{8.2cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Boolean (KVP) condition} & \textbf{OML analogue} \\
\midrule
Dedekind $\sigma$-completeness & exists ($\sigma$-orthocompleteness) \\
weak $(\sigma,\infty)$-distributivity
  & \textbf{none} --- relies on $\bigwedge$ distributing over $\bigvee$ \\
chargeability
  & \textbf{none} --- no structural characterisation on OMLs \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Positive and obstructive OML results}
\textbf{Positive} (require structure rich enough to approximate Hilbert-space
geometry):
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=2pt]
  \item Gleason~\cite{Gleason1957} --- $L(H)$, $\dim\!\geq\!3$;
    $\sigma$-additivity \emph{on the lattice}.
  \item Bunce--Wright~\cite{BunceWright1992} --- JBW projection lattices;
    Chetcuti--Dvure\v{c}enskij~\cite{ChetcutiDvurecenskij2005}.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Obstructive} (Boolean machinery cannot be transplanted):
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=2pt]
  \item Pt\'{a}k--Pulmannov\'{a}~\cite{PtakPulmannova1994} --- a
    \emph{finitary} condition (\emph{subadditive}-unitality
    $s(a\vee b)\le s(a)+s(b)$, no countable joins) already collapses the
    \emph{lattice} to Boolean (their Thm 1); the OM\emph{poset} version fails
    (M\"uller's set-representable counterexample~\cite{Muller1993}).
  \item Navara~\cite{Navara1992} --- regularity does not force
    $\sigma$-additivity. \quad Pt\'{a}k~\cite{Ptak1987} --- exotic state
    spaces via Greechie pasting.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Three blocked routes to a $\sigma$-OML engine}
\label{sec:routes}
The descent argument needs an OML analogue of the Boolean engine --- a
$\sigma$-Stone duality, or equivalently a Loomis--Sikorski representation.
The three known routes are all blocked.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item \textbf{RDP / effect-algebra.} Loomis--Sikorski holds for
    $\sigma$-MV algebras~\cite{Dvurecenskij2000} and monotone
    $\sigma$-effect algebras \emph{with} the Riesz Decomposition Property
    \cite{Dvurecenskij2005}; but a lattice effect algebra has RDP iff it
    is MV, and OML $\cap$ MV $=$ Boolean (Kalmbach~\cite{Kalmbach1983}), so
    the hypothesis that would buy the representation collapses the class:
    \[
      \text{OML} + \text{RDP} \iff \text{Boolean}.
    \]
  \item \textbf{MacNeille completion.} The MacNeille completion of an OML
    need not be orthomodular (Harding~\cite{Harding1991}); one cannot
    complete to absorb countable joins. The topological version of this
    route --- clopens of $S_0(A)$ modulo the meagre ideal --- is the same
    completion in disguise (the regular-open algebra), and collapses
    non-distributivity (Remark~\ref{rem:bridge}).
  \item \textbf{$\sigma$-Stone duality.} None exists. McDonald--Bimb\'{o}
    is finitary (Remark~\ref{rem:finitary}); Freytes'
    theory~\cite{Freytes2020} of $\sigma$-complete OMLs is equational, with
    no set/tribe representation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Prior art on point-free quantum probability}
\label{sec:priorart}
Every existing point-free or directed-context construction lands on one
side of the $\sigma$-additivity-on-a-non-distributive-structure line.
\begin{description}[style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries,
    labelwidth=3.2cm, leftmargin=3.6cm]
  \item[Gleason tradition]
    Varadarajan~\cite{Varadarajan1985}, Kalmbach~\cite{Kalmbach1983},
    Pt\'{a}k--Pulmannov\'{a}~\cite{PtakPulmannova1994}: $\sigma$-additive on
    a non-distributive OML, but real-valued on a fixed lattice --- not
    point-free.
  \item[D\"{o}ring 2009]
    \cite{Doering2009measures} point-free on the spectral presheaf over a
    directed poset --- but provably only \emph{finitely additive}, on a
    distributive Heyting algebra.
  \item[Bohrification (HLS)]
    \cite{HeunenLandsmanSpitters2009, HeunenLandsmanSpitters2011}
    point-free and the internal valuation \emph{is} $\sigma$-additive
    (Scott-continuous) --- but factors through an internally
    \emph{distributive} locale, $\sigma$-additive only per commutative
    context. Non-distributivity is tamed, not carried natively.
  \item[Localic valuations]
    Vickers~\cite{Vickers2008}, Simpson~\cite{Simpson2012},
    Coquand--Spitters~\cite{CoquandSpitters2009}: point-free
    $\sigma$-additive, but on \emph{frames} --- distributive by definition.
  \item[OML Stone dualities]
    McDonald--Bimb\'{o}~\cite{McDonaldBimbo2023} and
    Cannon--D\"{o}ring~\cite{Cannon2013}: purely structural, no measure,
    no $\sigma$-version.
\end{description}
Every entry above occupies the line on one side. The gap is their
conjunction:
\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.92\linewidth}{\centering \textbf{The descent residue} ---
unoccupied: point-free $\times$ $\sigma$-additive $\times$ natively
non-distributive $\times$ directed-system-built.}}
\end{center}
\noindent (The directed-context-yields-non-distributivity \emph{idea} is not
new --- it is the shape of Bohrification and of colimit-over-Boolean-contexts
generation. Novelty would lie only in the
$\sigma$-additive\,$\times$\,point-free\,$\times$\,native combination.)

\begin{remark}[The boundary of the negative results]\label{rem:miss}
Two qualifications sharpen what is and is not ruled out. \emph{(i)~No
impossibility theorem} covers the \emph{concrete} class: the known no-go
results are finite or two-valued, and the one positive occupant
$L(H)$$+$Gleason is \emph{non-concrete} (Kochen--Specker~\cite{KochenSpecker1967},
$\dim \geq 3$: $L(H)$ admits no two-valued states at all, a fortiori none
order-determining), so concreteness is the qualifier on which the open
frontier turns. \emph{(ii)~The absence of a
$\sigma$-Loomis--Sikorski} is visible in the dualities themselves: both
take infinite joins as a \emph{closure}, not a union (Cannon--D\"{o}ring
$\mathrm{cls}(\bigcup S_i)$; McDonald--Bimb\'{o}
$(\bigcup h(a_n))^{\perp\perp}$). One should not overstate this: Gudder's
concrete logics~\cite{Gudder1979} \emph{are} set-representable non-Boolean
orthoposets ---
but as point-ful posets, not via a $\sigma$-Loomis--Sikorski theorem.
\end{remark}

\section{The open problem}
\label{sec:problem}
% ==================================================================

\begin{problem}
Does a $\sigma$-complete, concrete, non-Boolean, infinite OML admit a
Loomis--Sikorski-type representation (a $\sigma$-tribe of sets modulo a
$\sigma$-ideal), or a countable-join-preserving $\sigma$-Stone duality
--- or can one prove that none exists?
\end{problem}

\begin{remark}[Whether the class is inhabited]\label{rem:inhabited}
A member is \emph{descent-relevant} only if it carries an infinite
pairwise-orthogonal family (so $\sigma$-additivity exceeds finite additivity)
\emph{and} meet-zero${}\neq{}$orthogonal pairs. The relevant completeness is
thus $\sigma$-\emph{ortho}completeness (countable orthogonal joins), weaker
than the $\sigma$-completeness the Loomis--Sikorski representation needs.

\smallskip\noindent
\textbf{The discriminating condition} (element-versus-family; within one
orthogonal family it is vacuous, as such a family is Boolean and there
meet-zero ${}={}$ orthogonality):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interleave}
\textbf{(}\bigstar\textbf{)}\quad
\exists\, p,\ \exists\,\{a_n\}\text{ infinite orthogonal},\quad
p \wedge a_n = 0\ (\forall n),\quad p \not\perp a_n\ (\forall n).
\end{equation}

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.35}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{2.6cm} p{5.1cm} cc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Candidate} & \textbf{Status vs.\ the four hypotheses}
  & \textbf{Concrete?} & \textbf{($\bigstar$)?} \\
\midrule
$\mathrm{MO}_\kappa$ ($\kappa$ inf.)
  & max orthogonal family $=2$; descent vacuous; carries
    $\mathrm{MO}_2$ obstruction
  & yes & \textbf{no} (vacuous) \\
$L(H)$, $\dim\!\geq\!3$
  & skew ray $P_f$ vs.\ basis $\{P_{e_n}\}$, $f=\sum c_n e_n$, $c_n\neq0$
  & \textbf{no} (KS) & \textbf{yes} \\
Navara $\mathcal L$~\cite{Navara1992}
  & infinite, $\sigma$-orthocomplete, rich --- but stateless block
  & \textbf{no} & yes \\
\textbf{$\mathrm{MO}_2$-swap}
  & Navara's $\mathcal L$ with block $:=\mathrm{MO}_2$
  & \textbf{yes} & \textbf{?} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The bottom row is the live case. It is concrete (sub-OMPs and
products of concrete logics are concrete) and $\sigma$-orthocomplete (Navara's
closure step \cite[p.~428]{Navara1992} is state-free: ``$f_j(m)=0,\ f_i\perp
f_j \Rightarrow f_j=0$ on the block'' holds in $\mathrm{MO}_2$, where the only
elements ${}\leq a^\perp$ are $\{0,a^\perp\}$). So the class of the
Open Problem \emph{is} inhabited; the only question left is ($\bigstar$).

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Candidate witness.} $\mathrm{MO}_2$ atoms
$a,a^\perp,b,b^\perp$; $a\wedge b=0$, $a\not\perp b$. With $\mathcal F$
mutually disjoint (Navara's elements are $\bigvee_{C\in\mathcal F}v_C|C$), and
$\mathrm{MO}_2$ capping within-block orthogonal families at $2$, an infinite
orthogonal family forces infinitely many disjoint $C_n$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:witness}
  a_n := a\,|\,C_n,\qquad
  p := \bigvee_{n} b\,|\,C_n
  \quad(\in\mathcal L,\ \text{by }\sigma\text{-orthocompleteness}).
\end{equation}
Constancy is per-coordinate and the $C_n$ disjoint, so $p$ is admissible; and
\emph{in $\mathcal W$}: $\ p\wedge a_n=0$ (on $C_n$, $a\wedge b=0$; off,
disjoint) and $p\not\perp a_n$ (on $C_n$, $a\not\perp b$). Thus ($\bigstar$)
holds \emph{in $\mathcal W$}.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{The sole open step ($\bigstar$) in $\mathcal L$.}
Navara warns ``the lattice operations in $\mathcal L$ do not coincide with
those of $\mathcal W$''; orthogonality runs through the orthocomplement. The
whole question is whether ($\bigstar$) transfers:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hinge}
\boxed{\ \text{\textbf{(HINGE)}}\quad
  \text{In }\mathcal L,\ \ a\,|\,C \;\not\perp\; b\,|\,C\,?
  \quad(\text{distinct non-complementary }\mathrm{MO}_2\text{ atoms, same }C)\ }
\end{equation}
\[
\begin{array}{r@{\ \Rightarrow\ }l}
\text{(HINGE) yes}
  & p\text{ witnesses (}\bigstar\text{); conjecture \textbf{false}; the swap is the sought object.}\\[2pt]
\text{(HINGE) no}
  & \text{swap segregates; } L(H) \text{ stays the lone witness.}
\end{array}
\]

\noindent Two riders: completeness here is $\sigma$-orthocompleteness, not
$\sigma$-completeness; and $L(H)$ vs.\ Navara fail concreteness for different
reasons (KS colouring vs.\ an imported stateless block). Details: the
inhabitation note.
\end{remark}

\noindent Either resolution is consequential:
\begin{description}[style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries,
    labelwidth=2.4cm, leftmargin=2.8cm, noitemsep, topsep=2pt]
  \item[Positive] a relational probability theory without realisations ---
    $\sigma$-additive probability from the entailment relation of a
    non-distributive OML, the non-Boolean analogue of localic measure theory.
  \item[Negative] an impossibility theorem closing the entire cluster, both
    residues, at once.
\end{description}
\noindent The art constrains it from both sides: \emph{no} impossibility
theorem covers the concrete class (Remark~\ref{rem:miss}), yet \emph{all three}
constructive routes are blocked (\S\ref{sec:routes}). What is required is not a
new idea in the abstract but a representation that bypasses all three routes.

\begin{remark}[Why $L(H)$+Gleason does \emph{not} already settle this ---
the (A)/(B) point-space equivocation]\label{rem:AB}
``Probability from relations, point-free'' may \emph{look} delivered by
$L(H)$+Gleason. It is not: the appearance rests on conflating two
point-spaces, and \emph{realisation} is defined as concentration on (B).
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{@{}cp{4.6cm}p{6.7cm}@{}}
\toprule
 & \textbf{Point-space} & \textbf{Gleason's relation to it} \\
\midrule
(A) & Hilbert rays of $H$ & \emph{requires} them --- $\rho$ in
  $s=\tr(\rho\,\cdot)$ is built from rays \\
(B) & MB dual filters $P(A)$ & \emph{removes} them, but only by being a
  representation theorem \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\noindent So Gleason is the \emph{most} point-ful result available (every
lattice state $\mapsto$ the point datum $\rho$), and what $L(H)$ exhibits is a
\emph{separation}, not point-freeness: $\sigma$-additive measures exist on the
lattice (Gleason, normal states), yet no state extends to a charge on
$S_0(L(H))$ (Prop.~\ref{prop:lh}), so a fortiori none concentrates on $P(A)$.
It cannot serve as the existence proof sought --- so the $\sigma$-OML
representation above is the \emph{only} candidate engine.
\end{remark}

\section{Directions}
\label{sec:first}
% ==================================================================

With the extension axis closed on $L(H)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:lh}), a
single direction remains.
\begin{description}[style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries,
    labelwidth=3.4cm, leftmargin=3.8cm]
  \item[The general construction]
    Construct a Loomis--Sikorski-type representation, or a
    countable-join-preserving $\sigma$-Stone duality, for a concrete
    non-Boolean infinite $\sigma$-OML --- or prove that none exists. This
    resolves the open problem directly, but no partial construction is
    currently in hand. This is the \emph{descent}-axis residue: the
    obstruction is the failure of $h$ to preserve countable joins
    (Remark~\ref{rem:finitary}), not the orthogonal/meet-zero gap, and so
    Proposition~\ref{prop:lh} does not reach it.
\end{description}

\noindent A second candidate direction --- the singular case for $L(H)$ ---
is now \emph{closed}, instructively. The hope: a singular state might extend
where no normal state does, giving the first explicit ``extends but may fail
to concentrate'' example the general construction lacks. It does not ---
Proposition~\ref{prop:lh} catches every state, singular included. The
reduction is worth recording, since one expected the singular case to require
infinitary methods.

\begin{remark}[Finite versus countable: finite suffices]\label{rem:finvscount}
One could ask whether the obstruction to extending a singular state is
realised by a \emph{finite} or only by a \emph{countable}
pairwise-meet-zero family. The line argument of
Remark~\ref{rem:linearg} is finitary but vacuous on singular states
($s(e)=0$ on every finite-dimensional $e$), which might suggest the
singular obstruction needs a countable family and is therefore entangled
with the descent-axis passage. It does not: the four
infinite-dimensional subspaces of Proposition~\ref{prop:lh} are a
\emph{finite} ($n=4$) pairwise-meet-zero family forcing
$\sum_i s(a_i)=2$, and the forcing is state-independent. So the singular
case is a self-contained, finitary problem, separable from the general
construction --- not governed by the finitary-to-$\sigma$ passage at all.
The line argument is vacuous on singular states; the
$\mathrm{MO}_2$-with-infinite-dimensional-atoms argument is not, and that
is the whole difference.
\end{remark}

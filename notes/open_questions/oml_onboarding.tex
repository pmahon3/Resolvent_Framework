\section*{0.\quad The one-sentence version}

\begin{quote}
Build a $\sigma$-additive probability theory on a non-distributive
orthomodular lattice \textbf{without descending from a sample space} ---

\section{Prerequisites to know cold}
\label{sec:prereq}
% ==================================================================

You need four things at your fingertips. If any is shaky, fix it before
\S\ref{sec:narrowing}.

\subsection*{1a.\quad OML / OMP, and meet-zero vs.\ orthogonal --- the gap
that drives everything}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Orthomodular lattice (OML):} a bounded lattice with an
    orthocomplement $a \mapsto a^\perp$ (involution, order-reversing,
    $a \wedge a^\perp = 0$, $a \vee a^\perp = 1$) satisfying the
    orthomodular law $a \leq b \Rightarrow b = a \vee (a^\perp \wedge b)$.
    Boolean $=$ the distributive special case. Motivating non-Boolean
    example: $L(H)$, closed subspaces of a Hilbert space. Smallest:
    $\mathrm{MO}_3$ (three complementary pairs pasted at $0$ and $1$).
    \emph{(Def.\ in note \S2, Defs 2.2--2.3; Hasse diagram Fig.~1.)}
  \item \textbf{The gap.} Two elements are \emph{orthogonal}
    ($a \perp b$) if $a \leq b^\perp$; they are \emph{meet-zero} if
    $a \wedge b = 0$. In a Boolean algebra these coincide. In a
    non-distributive OML, \textbf{orthogonal $\Rightarrow$ meet-zero but
    NOT conversely}. This single gap is the whole extension obstruction
    --- internalise it. $\mathrm{MO}_2$ is the smallest concrete witness
    ($\{1,2\} \wedge \{1,3\} = 0$ but
    $\{1,2\} \cap \{1,3\} = \{1\} \neq \varnothing$).
    \emph{(Note \S2, ``meet-zero versus orthogonal'';
    \file{verification/concrete\_meetzero\_vs\_orthogonal.py}.)}
  \item \textbf{OMP / concrete logic.} An orthomodular \emph{poset} only
    requires joins for orthogonal pairs. ``Concrete'' $=$
    ``set-representable'' $=$ embeds in
    $(\mathcal P(X), \subseteq, \text{complement})$ preserving orthogonal
    joins. \textbf{Trap:} concrete $\neq$ Boolean ($\mathrm{MO}_3$ is
    concrete); the dividing line that closes the meet-zero/orthogonal gap
    is \emph{intersection-richness}, not concreteness. \textbf{Naming
    note:} the abstract condition $a \wedge b = 0 \Rightarrow a \perp b$
    is Tkadlec's \emph{``Boolean orthoposet''} (Math.\ Bohemica 119,
    1994) --- which does \textbf{not} mean Boolean \emph{algebra}. Use
    ``intersection-richness'' as the working term.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{1b.\quad The state / valuation / charge ladder}

Three strengthenings of additivity, strictly increasing. Keep them
distinct --- collapsing them is one of the recurring errors on this
thread.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*., noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{State:} $s(1)=1$, additive on \emph{orthogonal} pairs.
  \item \textbf{Valuation} (local convention in the note): additive on
    \emph{meet-zero} pairs (binary, strictly stronger).
  \item \textbf{Extends to a (Boolean) charge:} the \emph{$n$-ary}
    condition $\sum_i s(a_i) \leq 1$ for every pairwise-meet-zero family
    (strictly stronger again). \emph{This is the extension axis.}
\end{enumerate}
Sanity anchor: on $\mathrm{MO}_3$, $s \equiv \tfrac12$ is a valuation but
fails~(3) at $\{p,q,r\}$ ($\tfrac32 > 1$). \emph{(Note \S2;
\file{verification/mo3\_extension.py}.)}

\subsection*{1c.\quad McDonald--Bimb\'{o} duality --- the dual space
$S_0(A)$ and its points $P(A)$}

The relational realist move: instead of starting from a space of
outcomes, build the dual from the lattice itself.
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item $S_0(A) =$ the space of \textbf{filters} on $A$ (compact,
    Stone-like).
  \item $P(A) \subseteq S_0(A) =$ the \textbf{principal filters} --- the
    ``physical points,'' the realisation datum. \emph{Canonical}, unlike
    the Boolean case where the pure points are a non-canonical choice.
  \item $h(a) = \{\text{filters containing } a\}$ is an OML isomorphism
    onto the $\perp$-stable clopens. Set $\mu(h(a)) = s(a)$.
  \item \textbf{The duality is FINITARY.}
    $h(\bigvee_n a_n) = (\bigcup_n h(a_n))^{\perp\perp}$ holds for
    \emph{finite} joins, fails for countable ones. This finitariness is
    the wall the whole open question runs into. \emph{(Note \S2;
    primary-source check in \file{verification/mb\_primeness\_check.md}.)}
  \item Why MB and not the rival Cannon--D\"{o}ring OML duality? MB
    carries $P(A)$ as explicit structure; both are purely structural (no
    measure), but MB's filter points are exactly the realisation datum the
    programme needs.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{1d.\quad Loomis--Sikorski --- the Boolean engine the OML case
is missing}

This is the keystone. In the Boolean world, the descent argument
(``$\sigma$-additive $\Leftrightarrow$ the measure concentrates on the
physical points'') runs on a \textbf{Loomis--Sikorski} representation: a
$\sigma$-complete Boolean algebra is a $\sigma$-tribe of sets mod a
$\sigma$-ideal. \textbf{There is no OML analogue} --- and supplying one
(or proving none exists) \emph{is the open question}. Hold this as: ``the
Boolean proof has an engine; the OML proof needs one and doesn't have
it.''

\begin{checkpoint}[Checkpoint 1]
(i)~Give the $\mathrm{MO}_2$ witness for meet-zero $\neq$ orthogonal.
(ii)~Why does $s \equiv \tfrac12$ on $\mathrm{MO}_3$ clear the valuation
bar but fail extension?
(iii)~What is $P(A)$, and why is it canonical here but not in the Boolean
case?
(iv)~What does Loomis--Sikorski do in the Boolean descent proof, and
what's the OML status of it?
\end{checkpoint}

\section{The narrowing: from ``the extension problem'' to ``the one
residue''}
\label{sec:narrowing}
% ==================================================================

The headline question --- \emph{when does a state on an OML $A$ extend to
a $\sigma$-additive measure on $S_0(A)$?} (note, Question~2.6) --- splits
into \textbf{two independent axes} that the Boolean case fuses. Most of
the problem is already settled; one residue is live.

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[font=\footnotesize,
    box/.style={draw, rounded corners, align=left, text width=11cm,
                inner sep=2mm}]
  \node (s) [draw, rounded corners, align=center] {state $s$ on OML $A$};
  \node (ext) [box, below=9mm of s]
    {\textbf{EXTENSION axis:} does $\mu$ extend to a finitely additive
     charge on the \emph{full} Boolean clopen algebra of $S_0(A)$?\\[1mm]
     $\rightarrow$ governed by the meet-zero/orthogonal gap (\S1a)\\
     $\rightarrow$ \textbf{SETTLED}: Horn--Tarski / Pitowsky feasibility;
     finite case $=$ decidable LP; can fail for every state.};
  \node (desc) [box, below=5mm of ext]
    {\textbf{DESCENT axis:} once extended, does the measure concentrate on
     the physical points $P(A)$?\\[1mm]
     $\rightarrow$ governed by $\sigma$-additivity (needs the missing
     engine, \S1d)\\
     $\rightarrow$ \textbf{OPEN}. This is the residue.};
  % both axes branch independently from the state (not ext -> desc)
  \draw[-{Latex}] (s) -- (ext.north);
  \draw[-{Latex}] (s.west) -- ++(-0.7,0) |- (desc.west);
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

\noindent Two sharp results pin down the extension axis so you don't
re-fight it:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Finite case:} a decidable linear program. $\mathrm{MO}_3$
    shows it can fail for \emph{every} state. Not a frontier. \emph{(Note
    \S2, \S5 ``Extension axis''.)}
  \item \textbf{Infinite $L(H)$, normal states --- KILLED.} The
    clustering argument inside a single $2$-plane: $k$ distinct lines
    force $k \cdot s(e) \leq 1$ for all $k$, so any state with $s(e) > 0$
    on some finite-dim $e$ fails to extend. That's \emph{every normal
    (Gleason) state}. $\sigma$-completeness irrelevant; the argument is
    finitary. \emph{(Note \S2.1, Prop 2.7;
    \file{verification/lh\_infinite\_extension.py}.)}
\end{itemize}

\noindent So on the extension axis the \emph{only} survivors are the
\textbf{singular states} of $L(H)$ ($s(e)=0$ on every finite-dim $e$) ---
see \S\ref{sec:firstmove}. The descent axis is where the real open
problem lives --- see \S\ref{sec:question}.

\begin{checkpoint}[Checkpoint 2]
Draw the two-axis diagram from memory. Why is the extension axis
``settled but partly degenerate''? Which states on $L(H)$ survive the
clustering kill, and why does the kill not touch them?
\end{checkpoint}

\section{The open question, stated crisply}
\label{sec:question}
% ==================================================================

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Does a $\sigma$-complete, concrete, non-Boolean, infinite OML
admit a Loomis--Sikorski-type representation (a $\sigma$-tribe of sets mod
a $\sigma$-ideal), or a countable-join-preserving $\sigma$-Stone duality
--- or can one prove none exists?}
\end{quote}

\noindent A positive answer is the engine for \emph{relational probability
without realizations}: $\sigma$-additive probability built from the
entailment relation of a non-distributive OML, \textbf{not} descended from
any sample space --- the non-Boolean analogue of localic (pointless)
measure theory. A negative answer (an impossibility theorem) closes the
whole cluster, including both residues, at once. The outcome is
\textbf{binary and large}. \emph{(Note \S3 ``finitary-to-$\sigma$
bridge''; \S5 ``Descent axis''; ``What is at stake''.)}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Why this is the live stake, not just a loose end.}\enspace
The motivating idea (``probability from relations, point-free'') might
\emph{look} already delivered by $L(H)$ $+$ Gleason. It is not --- and
seeing why is the conceptual crux:

\begin{checkpoint}[The (A)/(B) point-space equivocation --- do not let
this creep back]
``Point-free'' slides between two point-spaces. \textbf{(A)} the Hilbert
rays $L(H)$ and the Gleason density $\rho$ are \emph{built from};
\textbf{(B)} the MB dual filters $P(A)$. The programme defines
\emph{realization} $=$ concentration on \textbf{(B)}. Gleason removes
\textbf{(B)} but \textbf{requires (A)} --- and is a \emph{representation}
theorem (the most point-ful result available, reducing every lattice
state to the point datum $\rho$). So $L(H)$/Gleason witnesses the
$\PRlat$ / $\PRdual$ \textbf{separation}, NOT point-freeness, and is not
the existence proof. Because it isn't, the $\sigma$-OML representation
(\S\ref{sec:question}) is the \emph{only} candidate engine. \textbf{Never
write ``Gleason already builds relational probability.''} \emph{(Memory:
\file{oml\_two\_point\_spaces.md}; note ``What is at stake''.)}
\end{checkpoint}

\begin{checkpoint}[Checkpoint 3]
State the open question in one sentence without looking. Explain why
Gleason on $L(H)$ does \emph{not} already answer it (the (A)/(B)
distinction). What would a positive vs.\ negative answer each deliver?
\end{checkpoint}

\section{What is already ruled out (don't redo this)}
\label{sec:ruledout}
% ==================================================================

Coming back cold, the danger is re-walking dead ground. The following are
closed; cite them, don't re-derive them.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Three named routes to a $\sigma$-OML engine --- all
    blocked.} \emph{(Note \S3.)}
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*), noitemsep]
      \item \textbf{RDP / effect-algebra:} Loomis--Sikorski holds for
        $\sigma$-MV and monotone $\sigma$-effect-algebras \emph{with} RDP,
        but lattice-EA has RDP iff it is MV, and OML $\cap$ MV $=$
        Boolean. So \textbf{OML $+$ RDP $\Leftrightarrow$ Boolean} --- the
        machinery has no non-Boolean OML to act on.
      \item \textbf{MacNeille completion:} the MacNeille completion of an
        OML need not be orthomodular (Harding 1991) --- can't complete to
        absorb joins.
      \item \textbf{$\sigma$-Stone duality:} none exists. MB is finitary;
        Freytes gives only an equational theory, no set/tribe
        representation.
    \end{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Normal states on $L(H)$ don't extend.} Clustering argument
    (\S\ref{sec:narrowing}); hand-verified dichotomy in
    \file{verification/lh\_singular\_dichotomy.md}.
  \item \textbf{Both standing negatives stress-tested against primary
    sources (MISS, 2026-06-04).} (i)~No impossibility theorem for the
    \emph{concrete} class --- the load-bearing word is ``concrete'': the
    one positive occupant $P(H)$$+$Gleason is \emph{non-concrete}
    (Kochen--Specker $\Rightarrow$ no separating two-valued states).
    (ii)~No $\sigma$-Loomis--Sikorski: both OML dualities take infinite
    joins as a \emph{closure} not a union (Cannon--D\"{o}ring
    $\mathrm{cls}(\bigcup S_i)$, MB $(\bigcup h(a_n))^{\perp\perp}$).
    \emph{Overclaim guard:} Gudder concrete logics ARE set-representable
    non-Boolean --- but point-ful posets, not a $\sigma$-LS theorem.
  \item \textbf{Prior art map (scout, PARTIALLY OCCUPIED).} Every existing
    construction gives up exactly one needed property: D\"{o}ring 2009
    (finitely additive), Bohrification/HLS ($\sigma$-additive but via a
    \emph{distributive} internal locale, per-Boolean-context), localic
    valuations (distributive by definition), Gleason (not point-free). The
    unoccupied conjunction --- point-free $\times$ $\sigma$-additive
    $\times$ natively non-distributive $\times$ directed-system-built ---
    is exactly the residue. \emph{(Note \S4; memory
    \file{oml\_relational\_prob\_novelty.md}.)} The
    directed-context-$\Rightarrow$-non-distributivity \emph{idea} is NOT
    new (it's the shape of Bohrification and Gunji et al.'s colimit
    generation); novelty lives entirely in the
    $\sigma$-additive-point-free-native combination.
\end{itemize}

\begin{checkpoint}[Checkpoint 4]
Name the three blocked routes and the one-line reason each dies. Why is
``concrete'' the load-bearing qualifier in the no-impossibility claim?
What single property does each prior-art construction give up?
\end{checkpoint}

\section{Your first move}
\label{sec:firstmove}
% ==================================================================

The work is \textbf{purely non-computational mathematics} from here ---
every \file{.py} is finitary because the finitary part is the whole
computable boundary; the residue has no LP to run. Two branches, sharing
one wall:
\begin{description}[style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries,
    labelwidth=2.6cm, leftmargin=3cm]
  \item[(a)~The deep unlock]
    the $\sigma$-OML duality (descent axis). Build the
    Loomis--Sikorski-type representation / countable-join-preserving
    $\sigma$-Stone duality for a concrete non-Boolean infinite
    $\sigma$-OML, or prove none exists. High ceiling, \textbf{no current
    foothold}.
  \item[(b)~The tractable special case]
    the singular sliver. Does a \emph{singular} orthoadditive state on
    $L(H)$ extend to a charge on $S_0(L(H))$? A concrete operator-algebra
    question (Calkin, Bunce--Wright, Takesaki) about one lattice.
    \textbf{Conditionally} self-contained.
\end{description}

\noindent\textbf{Do (b) first --- it is informative either way.}\enspace
If a singular state fails to extend, then \emph{no} state on $L(H)$
extends, closing the $\PRdual$ ``does-descent-do-independent-work''
question negatively. If one \emph{does} extend, it is the first concrete
object that extends but may not concentrate --- handing branch (a) its
motivating example. \emph{(Note \S5.)}

\begin{checkpoint}[The hinge that gates (b) --- settle this first of all]
Is the singular-extension obstruction reached with a \textbf{finite} or a
\textbf{countable} meet-zero family? This is \textbf{OPEN} --- and it
decides whether (b) is genuinely the easier branch.
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, leftmargin=4mm]
  \item The normal-state kill is finitary (lines in a $2$-plane) and so
    dodges the $\sigma$-wall. For singular states the relevant families
    are \emph{infinite-dimensional} subspaces with trivial intersection,
    so that \emph{line}-clustering route is \textbf{vacuous} ($s(e)=0$ on
    all finite-dim $e$).
  \item \textbf{But ``the line route is vacuous'' is NOT ``the
    obstruction is countable.''} A \emph{finite} family of
    infinite-dimensional pairwise-meet-zero subspaces with
    $\sum s(a_i) > 1$ is \textbf{unanalysed}. If such a finite obstruction
    exists, (b) is a self-contained operator-algebra problem you can
    settle ahead of (a). If only a countable family obstructs, (b) is
    entangled with the same finitary-to-$\sigma$ bridge as (a) and the
    tractability advantage evaporates.
  \item \emph{(Honesty note: a 2026-06-04 claim that this was ``settled
    $=$ infinitary, both branches gate on the $\sigma$-wall'' was an
    over-read of \file{lh\_singular\_dichotomy.md} --- that file calls the
    extension question OPEN. No new mathematics settled it. It is still
    open. This is exactly the kind of blocked-routes-$\neq$-impossible
    slip the thread guards against for residue \#2; don't repeat it.)}
\end{itemize}
\end{checkpoint}

\noindent\textbf{Concretely, the very first thing to try:} take a
\emph{finite} family of infinite-dimensional,
pairwise-trivially-intersecting subspaces of $H$ and ask whether
orthoadditivity of a singular state forces $\sum s > 1$ (finite
obstruction) or whether singular states evade every finite bound (pushing
the obstruction to countable). That single computation/argument decides
the branch. \emph{(Setup: \file{verification/lh\_singular\_dichotomy.md}
lines 95--108 give the singular-state constructions and the open
statement.)}

\begin{checkpoint}[Checkpoint 5]
State the finite-vs-countable hinge and what each outcome implies for
branch (b). Why is (b) informative whether or not the singular state
extends? What is the first concrete object to write down?
\end{checkpoint}

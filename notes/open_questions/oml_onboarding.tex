\title[$\sigma$-Additive Probability on Orthomodular Lattices]
  {$\sigma$-Additive Probability on Orthomodular Lattices:\\
   A Survey and an Open Problem}
\author{Patrick S.\ Mahon}
\date{June 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
On a Boolean algebra, a finitely coherent probability extends to a countably
additive measure by classical machinery (Carath\'{e}odory; Loomis--Sikorski; Stone).
On a non-distributive orthomodular lattice (OML) this machinery breaks. We survey
the problem and isolate a precise open question, splitting it into two axes the
Boolean case fuses: the \emph{extension} axis (does a state extend to a charge on the
dual?), a Horn--Tarski feasibility question settled negatively for $L(H)$; and the
\emph{descent} axis (does the charge concentrate on the physical points?). The
combinatorial route fails --- $\prod_n\mathrm{MO}_2$ is concrete, $\sigma$-complete
and non-Boolean yet \emph{segregated} (non-distributivity confined to finite blocks)
--- because a faithful set representation forces the OML Boolean
(Theorem~\ref{thm:floor}). Reading ``no hidden realisation'' as not being a mixture
of dispersion-free states makes a relational measure a \emph{contextual} state
(Lemma~\ref{lem:relational}). The open question: \emph{does a non-Boolean,
$\sigma$-complete, concrete OML carry a $\sigma$-additive contextual state whose
contextuality is $\sigma$-essential} --- witnessed by no finite sub-OML? The finite
case is inhabited (Wright's pentagon), so only the $\sigma$-essential refinement
remains, a compactness failure of CE type. Recent work on Dynkin-systems
(Derr--Williamson~\cite{DerrWilliamson2023}) settles it \emph{negatively under a
Polish/Borel-regularity hypothesis}, leaving the residue --- whether a witness can be
non-Polish-representable --- a descriptive-set-theory question
(Remark~\ref{rem:dw}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ==================================================================

All measurement is finite, yet the frameworks that organise empirical
knowledge --- probability theory, quantum mechanics --- rest on infinite
structures. The passage from finite observation to infinite structure is
not automatic; it requires a commitment no finite body of evidence can
compel. In probability theory the locus of that commitment is
\emph{countable additivity}:
\[
  A_1 \supseteq A_2 \supseteq \cdots, \quad \bigcap_n A_n = \varnothing
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mu(A_n) \to 0.
\]
De~Finetti~\cite{deFinetti1974} held that only finite additivity is
empirically grounded; Kolmogorov~\cite{Kolmogorov} adopted
$\sigma$-additivity as an axiom.

The same tension recurs once observations need not be jointly performable.
An observation \emph{context} --- a set of measurements performable together
--- is Boolean; \emph{pasting} contexts along their shared observations (the
horizontal-sum / partial-Boolean-algebra construction~\cite{Kalmbach1983})
returns:
\[
  \begin{aligned}
  \text{contexts mutually compatible} &\ \Rightarrow\ \text{Boolean algebra},\\
  \text{incompatible} &\ \Rightarrow\ \text{non-distributive OML.}
  \end{aligned}
\]
The pasting of incompatible Boolean blocks is a non-distributive OML
(Kalmbach~\cite{Kalmbach1983}), and its failure to remain Boolean is exactly the
contextuality obstruction of the partial-Boolean-algebra / sheaf account
(Abramsky--Brandenburger~\cite{AbramskyBrandenburger2011};
Budroni--Morchio~\cite{BudroniMorchio2010}). The OML is thus \emph{forced} by the
context structure, not postulated; for non-quantum examples the incompatibility must be operationally
imposed, since classical observables on a common space jointly distribute
(Kolmogorov) and stay Boolean. The closed subspaces of a Hilbert space $H$ form the
prototype $L(H)$, not the premise.

On a non-distributive OML, Carath\'{e}odory's outer measure does not apply. For
$L(H)$ it is rescued by Gleason's theorem~\cite{Gleason1957}, with no general-OML
analogue known. Non-distributivity \emph{per se} does not remove the two-valued
homomorphisms the Boolean engine needs ($\mathrm{MO}_3$ keeps them), but it removes
the \emph{guarantee}; for $L(H)$, $\dim\geq3$, they fail outright
(Kochen--Specker~\cite{KochenSpecker1967}). Whether enough survive is a question of
\emph{concreteness}, not non-distributivity: an OML is \emph{concrete}
(Definition~\ref{def:omp}) when it carries an order-determining set of two-valued
states (Gudder~\cite{Gudder1979}), and this is the qualifier on which the open
problem turns.

\paragraph{The organising split.} The question splits into two axes the Boolean case
fuses: \emph{extension} (does a state extend to a charge on $S_0(A)$? --- settled,
none does on $L(H)$) and \emph{descent} (does the charge concentrate on the physical
points? --- open).

\paragraph{The relational standpoint.} Following De~Finetti~\cite{deFinetti1974}
(probability without a presupposed sample space) and Birkhoff--von~Neumann
(propositions as a lattice, not subsets of a phase space), we start from the
propositions and ask what their order alone determines --- the measure-theoretic
counterpart of pointless topology. The dual is then the McDonald--Bimb\'{o} space of
\emph{filters} (\S\ref{sec:dual}), and a positive answer would be a $\sigma$-additive
probability theory on a non-distributive lattice, the non-Boolean analogue of localic
measure theory (made precise in \S\ref{sec:problem} as a \emph{contextual} state;
cf.~\cite{mahon2025}).

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}
% ==================================================================

We collect the lattice-theoretic apparatus; all notions are standard.

\subsection{Orthomodular lattices}
\label{sec:oml}

\begin{definition}[Orthocomplemented lattice]\label{def:ocl}
A \emph{bounded lattice} is a partially ordered set in which every pair
$a,b$ has a join $a \vee b$ and meet $a \wedge b$, with greatest element
$\mathbf 1$ and least element $\mathbf 0$. An \emph{orthocomplementation}
is a map $a \mapsto a^\perp$ satisfying, for all $a,b$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*), noitemsep]
  \item $a \wedge a^\perp = \mathbf 0$ and $a \vee a^\perp = \mathbf 1$
    (complement),
  \item $a^{\perp\perp} = a$ (involution),
  \item $a \leq b \Rightarrow b^\perp \leq a^\perp$ (order-reversing).
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Incomparable; meet-zero; orthogonal]\label{def:ortho}
For elements $a,b$ of an orthocomplemented lattice, three relations of
increasing strength:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=2pt]
  \item \emph{incomparable}: $a\not\leq b$ and $b\not\leq a$ --- neither
    entails the other (an order condition);
  \item \emph{meet-zero}: $a\wedge b=\mathbf 0$ --- no common refinement;
  \item \emph{orthogonal}, written $a\perp b$: $a\leq b^\perp$ (equivalently
    $b\leq a^\perp$) --- one entails the other's negation, \emph{decisively
    incompatible}.
\end{itemize}
Each implies the one above it ($a\perp b\Rightarrow a\wedge b=\mathbf 0
\Rightarrow$ incomparable, for $a,b\notin\{\mathbf 0,\mathbf 1\}$). For two
\emph{distinct atoms}, incomparable and meet-zero coincide --- an atom has only
$\mathbf 0$ below it, so $a\wedge b\in\{\mathbf 0,a\}$, and $a\wedge b=a$ would
force $a\leq b$; orthogonality remains strictly stronger.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Meet-zero versus orthogonal]\label{rem:gap}
The implication $a\perp b\Rightarrow a\wedge b=\mathbf 0$ reverses in every
Boolean algebra, but fails once the lattice is non-distributive: there
meet-zero is strictly weaker than orthogonality. The distinctions developed
below all turn on this separation.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Orthomodular lattice]\label{def:oml}
An orthocomplemented lattice $A$ is \emph{orthomodular} if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oml-law}
  a \leq b \quad\Longrightarrow\quad b = a \vee (a^\perp \wedge b).
\end{equation}
A \emph{Boolean algebra} is the special case where $\wedge$ distributes
over $\vee$; every Boolean algebra is orthomodular, not conversely. The
motivating non-Boolean example is $L(H)$, the closed subspaces of a
Hilbert space with $a^\perp$ the orthogonal complement.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Completeness notions]\label{def:complete}
Two countable-completeness conditions on an OML $A$ are distinguished
throughout, the second strictly weaker. Here $\bigvee_n a_n$ denotes the
\emph{join} (least upper bound) of $\{a_n\}$ in the order of $A$, unique when
it exists; ``$\exists\,\bigvee_n a_n\in A$'' asserts that it does.
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=2pt]
  \item $A$ is \emph{$\sigma$-complete} if
    \[
      \forall\,\{a_n\}_{n\in\mathbb N}\subseteq A:\quad
      \exists\,\textstyle\bigvee_n a_n\in A
    \]
    (equivalently every countable family has a meet, by
    $\bigwedge_n a_n=(\bigvee_n a_n^\perp)^\perp$);
  \item $A$ is \emph{$\sigma$-orthocomplete} if
    \[
      \forall\,\{a_n\}_{n\in\mathbb N}\subseteq A
      \ \bigl(a_i\perp a_j\ \text{for } i\neq j\bigr):\quad
      \exists\,\textstyle\bigvee_n a_n\in A.
    \]
\end{itemize}
The orthogonal families are a subclass of all countable families, so
$\sigma$-completeness quantifies over a strictly larger domain and is the
stronger hypothesis: $\sigma$-completeness $\Rightarrow$ $\sigma$-orthocompleteness.
Only the weaker condition is needed below. For a state $s$,
$\sigma$-additivity is the requirement that
\[
  s\Bigl(\textstyle\bigvee_n a_n\Bigr)=\sum_n s(a_n)
\]
for every countable pairwise-orthogonal family $\{a_n\}$. Its left side invokes
$\bigvee_n a_n$ \emph{only} for orthogonal families ---
so $\sigma$-orthocompleteness is exactly the hypothesis that makes it
well-posed. Full $\sigma$-completeness is the stronger condition manipulated
by the Loomis--Sikorski representation (\S\ref{sec:ls}).
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[$\mathrm{MO}_n$, and the gap made concrete]\label{ex:mo}
$\mathrm{MO}_n$ is the lattice with $\mathbf 0$, $\mathbf 1$, and $n$
complementary pairs of incomparable atoms, all sharing only $\mathbf 0$
and $\mathbf 1$. The geometric model is $n$ lines through the origin: each
line $a$ is orthogonal to its perpendicular $a^\perp$, while two atoms from
distinct pairs are non-perpendicular lines. It is orthomodular and
non-distributive ($n \geq 2$): two atoms drawn from \emph{distinct}
complementary pairs have
\[
  a \wedge b = \mathbf 0 \quad(\text{meet only at the origin})
  \qquad\text{yet}\qquad a \not\perp b,
\]
so meet-zero $\neq$ orthogonal already here. $\mathrm{MO}_3$ is the smallest
non-distributive OML.

\begin{definition}[OMP; order-determining states; concrete logic]\label{def:omp}
An \emph{orthomodular poset} (OMP) weakens Definition~\ref{def:oml}: joins
$a \vee b$ are required only for \emph{orthogonal} pairs. A set $S$ of states
on $A$ is \emph{order-determining} if it separates the order, i.e.
\[
  \bigl(s(a)\leq s(b)\ \text{for all } s\in S\bigr)\ \Longrightarrow\ a\leq b.
\]
An OMP is a \emph{concrete logic} (equivalently \emph{set-representable}) if it
embeds into $(\mathcal P(X), \subseteq, X \setminus(-))$ for some set $X$,
preserving existing orthogonal joins --- i.e.\ its propositions are modelled as
actual \emph{sets of outcomes}. Concreteness is equivalent to admitting an
order-determining set of \emph{two-valued} states (Gudder~\cite{Gudder1979}),
and it is the property that separates the open frontier from the settled cases
(\S\ref{sec:problem}).
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Concreteness does not close the gap]\label{rem:concrete}
A set-representable OMP needs only closure under complement and \emph{disjoint}
union~\cite{BuresovaPtak2024}; intersection-closure would force a field of
sets, hence Boolean. The lattice meet $a\wedge b$ is the greatest $L$-member
inside $A\cap B$, so
\[
  \underbrace{A\cap B=\varnothing}_{\text{orthogonal}}
  \ \Longrightarrow\
  \underbrace{a\wedge b=\mathbf 0}_{\text{meet-zero}},
  \qquad\text{but not conversely.}
\]
Concreteness does not force the converse: $\mathrm{MO}_3$ is itself concrete
(Definition~\ref{def:omp}; Gudder~\cite{Gudder1979}), so the paradigmatic
meet-zero-$\neq$-orthogonal example is already a concrete logic. What closes the gap is a
richness/atomicity condition, not concreteness:
\[
  a\wedge b=\mathbf 0 \;\Rightarrow\; a\perp b,
\]
equivalently: every nonempty $A\cap B$ contains a nonzero $L$-member ---
the abstract form being Tkadlec's \emph{Boolean orthoposet}
condition~\cite{Tkadlec1994} (which does \emph{not} mean Boolean
\emph{algebra}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{The state/meet-additive/charge ladder}
\label{sec:ladder}

\begin{definition}[State; meet-additive state; charge-extendible]\label{def:ladder}
Let $A$ be an OML. A \emph{state} is a map $s : A \to [0,1]$ with
$s(\mathbf 1)=1$ that is additive on \emph{orthogonal} pairs:
$s(a \vee b) = s(a) + s(b)$ whenever $a \perp b$; that is, a probability
assignment whose additivity is guaranteed only on decisively incompatible
propositions. Three strengthenings, differing in which pairs additivity is
demanded on and strictly increasing in general (Example~\ref{ex:strict} on
$\mathrm{MO}_3$):
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*), noitemsep]
  \item \emph{State}: $\ s(a\vee b)=s(a)+s(b)$ whenever $a\perp b$.
  \item \emph{Meet-additive}: $\ s(a\vee b)=s(a)+s(b)$ whenever
    $a\wedge b=\mathbf 0$ (binary, strictly stronger --- it reaches the gap
    pairs of Definition~\ref{def:ortho}). We avoid the word ``valuation''
    here: in lattice theory it denotes the modular function
    $v(a)+v(b)=v(a\wedge b)+v(a\vee b)$, a different condition.
  \item \emph{Charge-extendible}: $\ \sum_i s(a_i)\leq 1$ for every
    pairwise-meet-zero family $\{a_i\}$ ($a_i\wedge a_j=\mathbf 0$, $i\neq j$;
    strictly stronger again). This is the condition necessary for extension to
    a charge on the dual space (Definition~\ref{def:charge}) and the
    \emph{extension} condition of \S\ref{sec:axes}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[The ladder is strict]\label{ex:strict}
On $\mathrm{MO}_3$ with complementary pairs $(p,p^\perp),(q,q^\perp),
(r,r^\perp)$, the maximally mixed $s \equiv \tfrac12$ is meet-additive
(clears~(2)) yet fails~(3) at $\{p,q,r\}$:
$\tfrac12+\tfrac12+\tfrac12 = \tfrac32 > 1$. So no state on
$\mathrm{MO}_3$ is charge-extendible.
\end{example}

\begin{definition}[Normal and singular states on $L(H)$]\label{def:normalsing}
On $A = L(H)$ a state $s$ is
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=2pt]
  \item \emph{normal} if it is completely additive on every orthogonal family
    of projections,
    \[
      s\Bigl(\textstyle\bigvee_{i\in I} p_i\Bigr)=\sum_{i\in I} s(p_i)
      \quad\text{for all (not only countable) orthogonal } \{p_i\},
    \]
    equivalently $s(\cdot)=\tr(\rho\,\cdot)$ for a density operator $\rho$
    (Gleason~\cite{Gleason1957}, $\dim\!\geq\!3$);
  \item \emph{singular} (purely) if
    \[
      s(e)=0 \quad\text{for every finite-rank projection } e.
    \]
\end{itemize}
Every state decomposes into a normal and a singular part (Takesaki); the two
classes are exactly those distinguished by the arguments of \S\ref{sec:axes}.
\end{definition}

\subsection{The McDonald--Bimb\'{o} dual space}
\label{sec:dual}

\begin{definition}[Dual space; representation map]\label{def:dual}
The McDonald--Bimb\'{o} duality~\cite{McDonaldBimbo2023} assigns to an OML
$A$ a compact space
\[
  S_0(A) = \bigl(F(A), \subseteq, \perp_A, P(A), T\bigr),
\]
where $F(A)$ is the set of \emph{filters} of $A$, ordered by $\subseteq$
and carrying an orthogonality relation $\perp_A$ and a Stone topology $T$,
and $P(A) = \{\,{\uparrow}p : p\in A\,\} \subseteq F(A)$, with
${\uparrow}p=\{a\in A : a\geq p\}$, is the set of \emph{principal} filters ---
the ``physical points,'' the realisation datum (canonical, unlike the
non-canonical pure points of the Boolean case). For $a \in A$ set
$h(a) = \{x \in F(A) : a \in x\}$; then $h$ is an OML isomorphism onto the
$\perp$-stable clopens $\CO(S_0(A))^\dagger$. Define $\mu(h(a)) = s(a)$.
Here $h(a)$ is the set of points at which $a$ holds and $\mu$ is the state
transported onto those sets; the open problem is whether $\mu$ is preserved
under countable operations on them.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Charge]\label{def:charge}
A \emph{charge} on $S_0(A)$ is a map $\nu : \CO(S_0(A)) \to [0,1]$ on the
\emph{full} Boolean algebra of clopen subsets (not merely the $\perp$-stable
ones in the image of $h$), with
\[
  \nu(S_0(A))=1,
  \qquad
  \nu(U\cup V)=\nu(U)+\nu(V) \ \text{ whenever } U\cap V=\varnothing.
\]
A state $s$ \emph{extends to a charge} if some such $\nu$ satisfies
$\nu(h(a)) = s(a)$ for all $a \in A$. Because $h$ preserves
meets, a pairwise-meet-zero family in $A$ maps to disjoint clopens, so a
charge necessarily satisfies $\sum_i s(a_i)\le 1$ over such families --- the
charge-extendibility condition of Definition~\ref{def:ladder}(3).
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[The duality is finitary]\label{rem:finitary}
The representation map $h$ satisfies
\[
  \begin{aligned}
  h\Bigl(\bigvee_{i\leq n} a_i\Bigr)&=\Bigl(\bigcup_{i\leq n} h(a_i)\Bigr)^{\perp\perp}
    && (\text{finite}),\\
  h\Bigl(\bigvee_{n} a_n\Bigr)&\neq\Bigl(\bigcup_{n} h(a_n)\Bigr)^{\perp\perp}
    && (\text{countable}):
  \end{aligned}
\]
finitary OML homomorphisms need not preserve infinite suprema. This is the
central obstruction the open problem of \S\ref{sec:problem} confronts, and it
is self-dual: since $a\mapsto a^\perp$ is an order anti-isomorphism,
$\bigwedge_n a_n=(\bigvee_n a_n^\perp)^\perp$, so the failure for countable
meets is the De~Morgan dual of the failure for countable joins. The rival
duality of Cannon--D\"{o}ring~\cite{Cannon2013} is also finitary and carries no
measure; we use McDonald--Bimb\'{o} because it carries $P(A)$ as explicit
structure.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Boolean engine: Stone duality}
\label{sec:ls}

In the Boolean case the descent equivalence
\[
  \sigma\text{-additive}
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \text{the measure concentrates on the physical points}
\]
is proved through \emph{Stone duality}, in four steps~\cite{mahon2025}:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(S\arabic*)}, noitemsep, topsep=2pt]
  \item a Boolean event algebra $B$ embeds in the clopen algebra
    $\CO(\mathrm{St}\,B)$ of its Stone space;
  \item a charge on $B$ extends to a Baire measure on $\mathrm{St}\,B$
    (Carath\'{e}odory);
  \item $\sigma$-additivity $\iff$ the measure vanishes on meagre Baire sets
    (Rao--Rao~\cite{rao1983});
  \item meagre-null $\iff$ the measure lives on the ultrafilters closed under
    countable intersection $=$ the realised points.
\end{enumerate}
Steps (S3)--(S4) are the measure-theoretic face of the
\emph{Loomis--Sikorski} representation,
\[
  \text{$\sigma$-complete Boolean algebra}
  \;\cong\;
  (\text{$\sigma$-tribe of sets})\,/\,(\text{$\sigma$-ideal}),
\]
the countable-join bookkeeping that drives the equivalence. The contrast with
the OML case is exact: the Boolean engine (Stone duality together with the
Loomis--Sikorski representation) has \emph{no} known OML analogue
(\S\ref{sec:routes}). Supplying such an engine, or proving none exists, is the open
problem of \S\ref{sec:problem}.

\begin{remark}[Why the bridge breaks: meet-closed but not join-prime]
\label{rem:bridge}
A ``realised'' point $x$ carries two conditions, which Rao--Rao's
``vanishes on meagre sets'' makes coincide in the Boolean case:
\[
  \begin{aligned}
  &\text{(M)}\ \ \text{closed under countable meets};\\
  &\text{(J)}\ \ x\ni\textstyle\bigvee_n a_n \Rightarrow x\ni a_n\ \text{for some }n
    \quad(\text{off the join-defect}).
  \end{aligned}
\]
Non-distributivity separates them. For a principal filter $x={\uparrow}p$,
condition~(M) always holds, whereas (J) can fail: $p\leq\bigvee_n a_n$ does not
force $p\leq a_n$ for some~$n$.
In $L(H)$ this is witnessed by $a_n=\mathrm{span}(e_n)$ and
$p=\mathrm{span}(e_1+e_2)$:
\[
  p \;\leq\; \textstyle\bigvee_n a_n = H,
  \qquad\text{yet}\qquad
  p \;\not\leq\; a_n \ \ \text{for every } n.
\]
So ${\uparrow}p$ is a realised point lying inside the join-defect
$D = h(\bigvee_n a_n)\setminus\bigcup_n h(a_n)$. Topologically ${\uparrow}p$ is
\emph{isolated} in $S_0(L(H))$, hence:
\[
  D \text{ non-meagre (OML)}
  \qquad\text{vs.}\qquad
  D \text{ nowhere dense (Boolean)}.
\]
This is the finitary obstruction of Remark~\ref{rem:finitary} on the dual:
the category route (clopens modulo the meagre ideal) fails on the same $D$
as the measure route, and reduces to the MacNeille completion of
\S\ref{sec:routes}(ii).
\end{remark}

\section{Extension and descent}
\label{sec:axes}
% ==================================================================

The central question --- \emph{when does a state $s$ on an OML $A$ extend
to a $\sigma$-additive measure on $S_0(A)$?} --- splits into two
\emph{independent} axes that the Boolean case fuses.
\begin{description}[style=nextline, leftmargin=0pt, itemsep=4pt]
  \item[\normalfont\itshape Extension.] Does $s$ extend to a finitely additive
    charge on the \emph{full} Boolean clopen algebra of $S_0(A)$? This is governed
    by the meet-zero/orthogonal gap (\S\ref{sec:oml}) and is \emph{settled}: it is a
    Horn--Tarski/Pitowsky feasibility question, a decidable linear program in the
    finite case, and can fail for every state.
  \item[\normalfont\itshape Descent.] Once extended, does the charge concentrate on
    the physical points $P(A)$? This is governed by $\sigma$-additivity --- it needs
    the missing engine (\S\ref{sec:ls}) --- and is \emph{open}.
\end{description}

\noindent The extension axis is the abstract form of a familiar question:
extending a finitely-additive assignment on incompatible propositions to a
single joint distribution is exactly the feasibility problem behind the
Bell inequalities (Fine~\cite{Fine1982}: a Bell model exists iff the
correlations admit a joint distribution; Pitowsky~\cite{Pitowsky1989}: the
Bell inequalities are the facets of a correlation polytope), and Bell and
Kochen--Specker non-classicality are jointly an extension problem for partial
Boolean algebras (Budroni--Morchio~\cite{BudroniMorchio2010}, with the
necessary-and-sufficient conditions of Horn--Tarski~\cite{HornTarski1948}).
It is also the marginal problem of probability theory --- extending consistent
marginals to a joint law (Kellerer~\cite{Kellerer1964}) --- and the
coherence/extendability question of imprecise probability
(Walley~\cite{Walley1991}); the equivalence of these formulations to the
quantum-logical one is made precise by Derr--Williamson~\cite{DerrWilliamson2023}.
What is distinctive here is only that the extension is sought against the dual
$S_0(A)$ rather than within a fixed scenario; the obstruction is the same
finitary one, and it is settled. Two observations pin it down.

\begin{proposition}[Finite case]\label{prop:finite}
For finite $A$ the extension condition is a decidable linear program
(Horn--Tarski~\cite{HornTarski1948} / Pitowsky correlation-polytope
feasibility~\cite{Pitowsky1989}; Budroni--Morchio~\cite{BudroniMorchio2010}
for the partial-Boolean-algebra formulation); since $h$ preserves meets,
meet-zero elements map to disjoint clopens and a charge must satisfy
$\sum_i s(a_i) \leq 1$ over pairwise-meet-zero families. It can fail for
\emph{every} state ($\mathrm{MO}_3$, Example~\ref{ex:strict}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Infinite $L(H)$, all states]\label{prop:lh}
Let $\dim H = \infty$. \emph{No} state on $L(H)$ --- normal or singular
--- extends to a charge on $S_0(L(H))$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The argument embeds a copy of $\mathrm{MO}_2$ in $L(H)$ whose four atoms are
infinite-dimensional, so that the obstruction reaches the singular states as
well. Write $H = H_0 \otimes \mathbb{C}^2$ with $H_0$ infinite-dimensional,
and fix an orthonormal basis $e,f$ of $\mathbb{C}^2$. Set
\[
  a_1 = H_0 \otimes \mathbb{C}e,\quad
  a_1^\perp = H_0 \otimes \mathbb{C}f,\quad
  a_2 = H_0 \otimes \mathbb{C}(e{+}f),\quad
  a_2^\perp = H_0 \otimes \mathbb{C}(e{-}f).
\]
These four infinite-dimensional subspaces form a copy of $\mathrm{MO}_2$ in
$L(H)$:
\[
  \begin{aligned}
  &a_1\perp a_1^\perp,\quad a_2\perp a_2^\perp,\quad a_i\vee a_i^\perp=H;\\
  &a_i\wedge a_j=\mathbf 0 \ \text{ but }\ a_i\not\perp a_j
    \ \ (\text{cross pairs}).
  \end{aligned}
\]
Orthoadditivity with $s(\mathbf 1)=1$ gives $s(a_i)+s(a_i^\perp)=1$ for
$i=1,2$, using only additivity on orthogonal pairs, so it holds for
\emph{every} state. The family $\{a_1,a_1^\perp,a_2,a_2^\perp\}$ is pairwise
meet-zero, and since $h$ preserves meets ($h(a\wedge b)=h(a)\cap h(b)$), its
members map to disjoint clopens; hence any charge satisfies
$\sum_i s(a_i)\le 1$. The two counts collide:
\[
  \sum_i s(a_i) = 2 \ \ (\text{orthoadditivity})
  \qquad\text{versus}\qquad
  \sum_i s(a_i)\le 1 \ \ (\text{any charge}).
\]
The construction is finite ($n=4$); $\sigma$-completeness of the lattice plays
no role.
\end{proof}

\noindent The $\mathrm{MO}_2$ count is standard (orthoadditivity together with
$s(\mathbf 1)=1$; see~\cite{Kalmbach1983}) and $\sum s(a_i)=2$ is already
state-independent in $L(\mathbb C^2)$. The role of the
\emph{infinite-dimensional} realisation is to extend the obstruction to the
singular states ($s(e)=0$ on every finite-rank $e$):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.30\linewidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.38\linewidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.24\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Atoms of $\mathrm{MO}_2$} & \textbf{$s(a_i)+s(a_i^\perp)$} &
  \textbf{Reaches} \\
\midrule
lines in $L(\mathbb C^2)$ (finite rank)
  & $=1$ only if $s$ charges finite rank & normal states only \\[2pt]
$H_0\otimes(\cdot)$, $\dim H_0=\infty$
  & $=1$ regardless of finite-rank behaviour & all states, incl.\ singular \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\noindent Keeping each atom infinite-dimensional drops the finite-rank
precondition of the line argument (Remark~\ref{rem:linearg}), and that is what
closes the extension axis on $L(H)$ completely.

\begin{remark}[An alternative argument for normal states only]
\label{rem:linearg}
A second, technique-disjoint argument reaches the normal states without the
$\mathrm{MO}_2$ family. If $s(e)>0$ on some finite-dimensional $e$, it is
positive on a $2$-plane $e_0$; taking $k$ pairwise-non-orthogonal lines
$p_1,\dots,p_k\subset e_0$ yields $2k$ pairwise-meet-zero lines with
$s(p_i)+s(p_i^\perp)=s(e_0)$, so a charge forces
\[
  k\,s(e_0)\le 1 \ \ \text{for all } k
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  s(e_0)=0,
\]
contradicting $s(e_0)>0$. Lifting $s$ to a functional via Mackey--Gleason /
Bunce--Wright~\cite{BunceWright1992} ($B(H)$ has no type $\mathrm I_2$ summand)
and applying the Takesaki normal/singular decomposition identifies which
states the argument reaches: it covers states with a nonzero normal part
($s(e)>0$ for some finite-rank $e$), including every normal (Gleason) state, and
misses the purely singular states ($s(e)=0$ for all finite-rank $e$; e.g.\
ultrafilter vector states or Calkin-algebra pullbacks).
Proposition~\ref{prop:lh} subsumes the missed case; this argument is
recorded because its technique is independent and locates the singular states
precisely.
\end{remark}

\section{What is known}
\label{sec:known}
% ==================================================================

The extension axis is settled --- on $L(H)$ no state extends
(Proposition~\ref{prop:lh}); the descent axis turns on whether the Boolean
engine of \S\ref{sec:ls} has an OML analogue. Three things bear on that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}, noitemsep, topsep=2pt]
  \item the Boolean benchmark such an analogue must match (\S\ref{sec:known}.1);
  \item the partial OML results, positive and obstructive (\S\ref{sec:known}.2);
  \item the routes already known to be blocked (\S\ref{sec:routes}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Boolean benchmark}
The conditions an OML engine would have to reproduce. The
Kelley--Vladimirov--Pt\'{a}k characterisation (Fremlin~\cite{Fremlin3},
Thm 391D) is complete: a Boolean algebra carries a strictly positive
$\sigma$-additive measure iff it satisfies all three conditions below ---
each of which loses its meaning off the distributive world.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.36\linewidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.56\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Boolean (KVP) condition} & \textbf{OML analogue} \\
\midrule
Dedekind $\sigma$-completeness & exists ($\sigma$-orthocompleteness) \\[2pt]
weak $(\sigma,\infty)$-distributivity
  & none --- relies on $\bigwedge$ distributing over $\bigvee$ \\[2pt]
chargeability
  & none --- no structural characterisation on OMLs \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Known results for general OMLs}
\textbf{Positive} (require structure rich enough to approximate Hilbert-space
geometry):
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=2pt]
  \item Gleason~\cite{Gleason1957} --- on $L(H)$ for a \emph{separable} Hilbert space
    of $\dim\geq3$, every countably additive measure on the projection lattice has the
    trace form $s(\cdot)=\tr(\rho\,\cdot)$: $\sigma$-additivity \emph{on the lattice}.
  \item Bunce--Wright~\cite{BunceWright1992} --- the projection-lattice (JBW) form;
    Chetcuti--Dvure\v{c}enskij~\cite{ChetcutiDvurecenskij2005}.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Obstructive} (Boolean machinery cannot be transplanted):
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=2pt]
  \item Pt\'{a}k--Pulmannov\'{a}~\cite{PtakPulmannova1994} --- a
    \emph{finitary} condition (\emph{subadditive}-unitality
    $s(a\vee b)\le s(a)+s(b)$, no countable joins) already collapses the
    \emph{lattice} to Boolean (their Thm 1); the OM\emph{poset} version fails
    (M\"uller's set-representable counterexample~\cite{Muller1993}).
  \item Navara~\cite{Navara1992} --- regularity does not force
    $\sigma$-additivity; with R\"{u}ttimann's decomposition of states into
    completely additive and purely finitely additive parts~\cite{Ruttimann1994},
    this shows the finitely-additive/$\sigma$-additive gap is structurally
    serious on quantum logics, not a routine strengthening --- part of why the
    open problem is hard. \quad Pt\'{a}k~\cite{Ptak1987} --- exotic state
    spaces via Greechie pasting.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Three routes to a $\sigma$-OML engine}
\label{sec:routes}
The descent argument needs an OML analogue of the Boolean engine --- a
$\sigma$-Stone duality, equivalently a Loomis--Sikorski representation. Routes
(i),(ii) are closed by \emph{theorem}; route (iii) is open in the strong sense (no
construction known, none ruled out), and is the Open Problem of \S\ref{sec:problem}.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item \textbf{RDP / effect-algebra.} Loomis--Sikorski holds for $\sigma$-MV
    algebras~\cite{Dvurecenskij2000} and for monotone $\sigma$-complete effect
    algebras \emph{with} the Riesz Decomposition Property
    \cite{Dvurecenskij2006}; but a lattice
    effect algebra has RDP iff it is MV, and OML $\cap$ MV $=$ Boolean
    (Kalmbach~\cite{Kalmbach1983}): $\text{OML}+\text{RDP}\iff\text{Boolean}$. The
    \emph{sharp-skeleton} evasion (represent the OML as the sharp elements of an
    ambient RDP effect algebra) also closes: in an RDP effect algebra the sharp
    elements coincide with the center, hence are Boolean (Jen\v{c}a 2001, Cor.~4.3,
    via Greechie--Foulis--Pulmannov\'a~\cite{GreechieFoulisPulmannova1995}).
  \item \textbf{MacNeille completion.} The MacNeille completion of an OML need not
    be orthomodular (Harding~\cite{Harding1991}), so one cannot complete to absorb
    countable joins; the topological version (clopens of $S_0(A)$ modulo the meagre
    ideal) is the same completion and collapses non-distributivity
    (Remark~\ref{rem:bridge}).
  \item \textbf{$\sigma$-Stone duality.} None is known, none ruled out: the existing
    dualities are finitary (McDonald--Bimb\'{o}, Remark~\ref{rem:finitary}) or
    equational with no set/tribe representation (Freytes~\cite{Freytes2020}), but no
    theorem forbids a countable-join-preserving one. This is route~(c) of the
    faithfulness trichotomy (\S\ref{sec:reduction}): a representation faithful on
    countable \emph{orthogonal} joins without forcing distributivity.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Prior art on point-free quantum probability}
\label{sec:priorart}
Every existing point-free or directed-context construction fails at least one
of the two requirements --- $\sigma$-additivity and a natively
non-distributive structure --- as tabulated below.
\begin{description}[style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries,
    labelwidth=3.2cm, leftmargin=3.6cm]
  \item[Gleason tradition]
    Varadarajan~\cite{Varadarajan1985}, Kalmbach~\cite{Kalmbach1983},
    Pt\'{a}k--Pulmannov\'{a}~\cite{PtakPulmannova1994}: $\sigma$-additive on
    a non-distributive OML, but real-valued on a fixed lattice --- not
    point-free.
  \item[D\"{o}ring 2009]
    \cite{Doering2009measures} point-free on the spectral presheaf over a
    directed poset --- but provably only \emph{finitely additive}, on a
    distributive Heyting algebra.
  \item[Bohrification (HLS)]
    \cite{HeunenLandsmanSpitters2009, HeunenLandsmanSpitters2011}
    point-free and the internal valuation \emph{is} $\sigma$-additive
    (Scott-continuous) --- but factors through an internally
    \emph{distributive} locale, $\sigma$-additive only per commutative
    context. Non-distributivity is tamed, not carried natively.
  \item[Localic valuations]
    Vickers~\cite{Vickers2008}, Simpson~\cite{Simpson2012},
    Coquand--Spitters~\cite{CoquandSpitters2009}: point-free
    $\sigma$-additive, but on \emph{frames} --- distributive by definition.
  \item[OML Stone dualities]
    McDonald--Bimb\'{o}~\cite{McDonaldBimbo2023} and
    Cannon--D\"{o}ring~\cite{Cannon2013}: purely structural, no measure,
    no $\sigma$-version.
\end{description}
The open case is their conjunction, met by no existing construction:
\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.92\linewidth}{\centering point-free $\times$
$\sigma$-additive $\times$ natively non-distributive $\times$
directed-system-built.}}
\end{center}

\begin{remark}[The boundary of the negative results]\label{rem:miss}
Two qualifications delimit what is ruled out.
\begin{description}[style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries,
    labelwidth=1.2cm, leftmargin=1.5cm, topsep=2pt]
  \item[(i)] \textbf{No impossibility theorem covers the concrete class.} The
    known no-go results are finite or two-valued, and the one positive
    occupant is non-concrete:
    \[
      L(H)\ (\dim\!\geq\!3):\quad
      \text{no two-valued states at all (Kochen--Specker~\cite{KochenSpecker1967})},
    \]
    a fortiori none order-determining. Concreteness is the qualifier on which
    the open frontier turns.
  \item[(ii)] \textbf{No $\sigma$-Loomis--Sikorski exists} (route~(c),
    \S\ref{sec:reduction}): the dualities take infinite joins as a closure, not a
    union (Remark~\ref{rem:finitary}). Not to be overstated, though: Gudder's
    concrete logics~\cite{Gudder1979} \emph{are} set-representable non-Boolean
    orthoposets --- but as point-ful posets, not via a $\sigma$-Loomis--Sikorski
    theorem.
\end{description}
\end{remark}

\subsection{The topos approach}
\label{sec:topos}
The topos / Bohrification programme is the one machinery supplying a point-free
\emph{and} $\sigma$-additive valuation on a quantum logic, so it is the natural
candidate --- but it takes route~(b) of the trichotomy (\S\ref{sec:reduction}): in
both principal constructions the valuation lives on a \emph{distributive} object,
its countable additivity routed through per-context Boolean blocks, hence sidesteps
rather than settles route~(c).

\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.35}
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.14\linewidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.36\linewidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.38\linewidth}@{}}

measure on
  & internal Gelfand spectrum $\Sigma(A)$, a compact regular frame
  & clopen subobjects $\mathrm{Sub}_{cl}(\Sigma)$, a Heyting algebra \\

  & a frame: $\bigvee$ distributes \cite[Def.~6.11]{HeunenLandsmanSpitters2011}

  & Thm~6.19 bijection; the measure restricts to a $\sigma$-Boolean morphism on each

non-distributive, directed-system-built) it misses exactly native non-distributivity.

% ==================================================================
\section{The open problem}
\label{sec:problem}
% ==================================================================

\begin{problem}
Does there exist a concrete, $\sigma$-complete, non-Boolean OML $L$ and a
$\sigma$-additive state $w$ on $L$ such that
\[
  \begin{aligned}
  &\text{\emph{(contextual)}} && w\notin\overline{\mathrm{conv}}\,(S_{\mathrm{df}}^\sigma);\\
  &\text{\emph{($\sigma$-essential)}} &\quad& w|_F\in\overline{\mathrm{conv}}\,
    (S_{\mathrm{df}}(F))\ \text{for every finite sub-OML }F\subseteq L\,?
  \end{aligned}
\]
That is, $w$ has no global hidden joint over the dispersion-free states, yet its
restriction to every finite sub-OML does. Equivalently: can one prove no such
$(L,w)$ exists?
\end{problem}

\begin{remark}[Relation to Derr--Williamson 2023]\label{rem:dw}
The problem above is not plainly open: it must be read against
Derr--Williamson~\cite{DerrWilliamson2023}, who study coherent probabilities on
\emph{pre-Dynkin-systems} (concrete orthomodular posets) and \emph{Dynkin-systems}
(concrete $\sigma$-complete OMLs) from the imprecise-probability side. Three distinct
levels of statement are involved, and should not be conflated:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), noitemsep, topsep=2pt]
  \item a finite-additive extendability criterion (their Theorem~4.5, a Horn--Tarski
    feasibility condition) matching ``non-contextual on every finite sub-OML'';
  \item a \emph{countably additive} extension to the ambient Borel $\sigma$-algebra
    (their Theorem~D.6), valid \emph{under the hypotheses} that the OML is realised
    as a Dynkin-system $D_\sigma$ of Borel sets of a \emph{Polish} space $\Omega$
    with $\sigma(D_\sigma)=\mathrm{Borel}(\Omega)$ and inner-regular $\sigma$-blocks
    --- a result they obtain by specialising
    Maharam's extension theorem~\cite[Thm~8.1]{Maharam1972} (Hausdorff base,
    inner-regular measures); and
  \item the inference from (b) to non-contextuality of $w$, below, which is the
    survey's own step.
\end{enumerate}

The link to the present formulation is one-directional but in the direction that
matters. A point $\omega\in\Omega$ gives the evaluation $\delta_\omega$, which is a
two-valued and (since $A_n\downarrow\varnothing\Rightarrow\omega\notin A_n$
eventually) $\sigma$-additive dispersion-free state; a countably additive $\nu$ on
$\mathrm{Borel}(\Omega)$ with $\nu|_{D_\sigma}=w$ exhibits $w$ as a barycentre,
\[
  w(A)=\int_\Omega \delta_\omega(A)\,d\nu(\omega)
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  w\in\overline{\mathrm{conv}}\,(S_{\mathrm{df}}^\sigma).
\]
Thus
\[
  \text{$\sigma$-extendable}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{non-contextual},
\]
and through Theorem~D.6 the implication
``finitely non-contextual everywhere $\Rightarrow$ globally non-contextual'' holds
\emph{whenever the OML admits such a Polish Borel realisation}: under that
hypothesis the $\sigma$-essential cell is empty, and no witness exists. (The converse,
non-contextual $\Rightarrow\sigma$-extendable, does not hold --- the
dispersion-free states properly contain the point evaluations $\delta_\omega$ --- but
it is not needed for the negative conclusion.)

The residue is then a single condition, the representability the whole problem turns
on: can a $\sigma$-essential witness exist that admits \emph{no} Polish Borel
realisation ($S_{\mathrm{df}}^\sigma$
non-Polish / analytic-but-not-Borel)? This is a descriptive-set-theory question, and
it is the same wall as the absence of a $\sigma$-Loomis--Sikorski representation
(\S\ref{sec:routes}); the off-centre/non-band constraints of \S\ref{sec:first} are
its lattice-side shadow. Beware the $\sigma$-version of the (A)/(B) point-space
equivocation (Remark~\ref{rem:AB}): Derr--Williamson's Polish hypothesis is on the
underlying \emph{point set} $\Omega$, and obtaining such an $\Omega$ for an abstract
concrete $\sigma$-complete OML is itself the missing engine, not a free input.
\end{remark}

\noindent Here ``relational'' means \emph{no hidden realisation}: the measure is not
recoverable from an auxiliary space of definite states behind the propositions. Three
point-spaces must be kept distinct: the
\emph{canonical} dual $P(A)=\{{\uparrow}p\}$ (one principal filter per element,
\S\ref{sec:dual}); the \emph{dispersion-free states} (the hidden-variable points);
and the \emph{external (A)-points} (Gleason's Hilbert rays, Remark~\ref{rem:AB}).
$P(A)$ is \emph{permitted}; the objection is only to a measure built on a smuggled
space --- the (A)-points, equivalently a global hidden-variable joint over the
dispersion-free states. By Lemma~\ref{lem:relational} a state has a hidden
realisation iff it is a mixture of dispersion-free states, so a relational measure is
precisely a \emph{contextual} state. This is a condition on the dispersion-free
states, not on $P(A)$: a witness is point-rich on $P(A)$ yet has no global
dispersion-free joint.

\subsection{Reduction to contextuality}
\label{sec:reduction}
\label{sec:predicate}

\paragraph{The universal floor.} The naive route --- realise the lattice as honest
sets so that a measure becomes a measure on a space --- is closed for every
non-Boolean OML.

\begin{theorem}[Universal floor]\label{thm:floor}
A \emph{faithful set representation} of an OML $L$ --- an injective
$h:L\to\mathcal P(\Omega)$ with $h(a\wedge b)=h(a)\cap h(b)$, $h(a\vee b)=h(a)\cup
h(b)$, $h(a^\perp)=\Omega\setminus h(a)$ (a $\sigma$-tribe / Loomis--Sikorski
representation when $\sigma$-joins are preserved) --- exists only if $L$ is Boolean.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Set operations $\cap,\cup,{}^c$ on $\mathcal P(\Omega)$ distribute, so the image
$h(L)$ is a distributive sublattice; $h$ injective and lattice-preserving makes $L$
isomorphic to a distributive ortholattice, i.e.\ Boolean.
\end{proof}

\noindent The Floor fixes \emph{where} a $\sigma$-engine may give faithfulness up,
a trichotomy organising \S\S\ref{sec:routes}--\ref{sec:priorart}:
\textbf{(a)} all $\wedge,\vee$ as $\cap,\cup$ forces Boolean (Theorem~\ref{thm:floor})
--- \emph{dead};
\textbf{(b)} all joins but in a \emph{distributive} ambient object (frame/topos) is
$\sigma$-additive only per Boolean context (\S\ref{sec:topos}) --- \emph{sidesteps};
\textbf{(c)} only countable \emph{orthogonal} joins, faithful to order and $\perp$
but not to the meets that would force distributivity --- exactly a concrete logic
(Definition~\ref{def:omp}), exactly what $\sigma$-additivity invokes
(Definition~\ref{def:complete}) --- \emph{the live route} (\S\ref{sec:routes}(iii)).
So a concrete OML has an order-determining family of dispersion-free states; the
live question is whether every state is a mixture of them.

\paragraph{The reduction.} The following equivalence reduces ``no hidden
realisation'' to a spanning condition on the dispersion-free states. Its content is
not new --- for a Bell scenario it is Fine's theorem~\cite{Fine1982} (a joint
distribution exists iff a deterministic hidden-variable model does), and the
convex-geometric form is Pitowsky's correlation-polytope picture~\cite{Pitowsky1989};
the formulation for a general (concrete) OML, with dispersion-free states as the
deterministic vertices, is the natural lift, in the lineage of the
sheaf-theoretic account of contextuality~\cite{AbramskyBrandenburger2011}.

\begin{lemma}[Relational $=$ contextual]\label{lem:relational}
Let $L$ be a concrete OML with dispersion-free state space $S_{\mathrm{df}}$, and
$w$ a state on $L$. Then $w$ admits a hidden realisation --- a probability measure
on a space of global two-valued valuations of $L$ inducing $w$ --- if and only if
$w \in \overline{\mathrm{conv}}\,(S_{\mathrm{df}})$. We call $w$ \emph{contextual}
when it is not.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
A global two-valued valuation of $L$ is exactly a dispersion-free state, so a
hidden realisation is a probability measure $\mu$ on $S_{\mathrm{df}}$ with
$w(a)=\int \omega(a)\,d\mu(\omega)$ for all $a$ --- i.e.\ $w$ is the barycentre of
$\mu$. The barycentres of probability measures on the (weak-$*$ compact) set
$S_{\mathrm{df}}$ are exactly $\overline{\mathrm{conv}}(S_{\mathrm{df}})$. The
handle is \emph{spanning}, not simplicity: non-uniqueness of $\mu$ is irrelevant;
only its \emph{existence} matters.
\end{proof}

$\sigma$-additive state outside the dispersion-free hull
(Figures~\ref{fig:mo2hull} and~\ref{fig:pentagonhull}).

\paragraph{Why $\sigma$-essential.} The finite version of this is already
inhabited. Wright's pentagon~\cite{Wright1978} is a finite, concrete OML --- a
Greechie loop of five three-atom blocks, hence a lattice --- with an
order-determining family of dispersion-free states, yet it carries a non-classical
$\{0,\tfrac12\}$-valued state lying outside their closed convex hull. (The five
intertwining atoms form a $5$-cycle in which consecutive atoms share a block, so any
dispersion-free state, taking at most one of each adjacent pair to $1$, sets at most
$2$ of the $5$ to $1$; their hull has vertex-sum $\leq 2$, whereas the
$\{0,\tfrac12\}$ state --- a legitimate state, each block summing to $1$ --- has
vertex-sum $\tfrac52$. This is the KCBS inequality~\cite{KCBS2008}.) So ``concrete
OML with a contextual state'' is a $1978$ fact, and the general spanning statement
is false. What Wright does \emph{not} supply is contextuality that requires the
countable structure: the pentagon's is witnessed by a finite sub-OML (itself). The
such a state must therefore be \emph{$\sigma$-essential} --- contextual in the
$\sigma$-complete whole but in no finite sub-OML. This is not a device to evade
Wright: ``every finite piece has a global section, the countable whole has none'' is
a \emph{compactness failure} of exactly the type by which countable additivity is
not first-order axiomatizable over finitely additive coherence (see
Remark~\ref{rem:ce}).

\paragraph{Status.} No witness has been constructed: the product
$\prod_n\mathrm{MO}_2$ fails (its contextual states are only finitely additive ---
diffuse states on its central $P(\mathbb N)$, which $\sigma$-additivity forces to
concentrate on points, killing the contextuality, \S\ref{sec:why-sigma}); Wright is
finite; no other construction is known. On the negative side, the cell is now known
to be \emph{empty for every OML admitting a Polish Borel point-realisation}
(Derr--Williamson, Remark~\ref{rem:dw}). What remains genuinely open is therefore the
\emph{non-representable} case: a witness, if one exists, must admit no such
realisation --- which is precisely the absence of a $\sigma$-Loomis--Sikorski
representation (\S\ref{sec:routes}), a descriptive-set-theory question.

\begin{remark}[The compactness-failure analogy]\label{rem:ce}
The $\sigma$-essential requirement places the open problem alongside a known
Boolean phenomenon. Over a Boolean algebra, countable additivity of a finitely
additive charge is not first-order axiomatizable: an ultraproduct of $\sigma$-additive
charges can be finitely additive but not $\sigma$-additive, so every finite (indeed
first-order) test is passed while the global property fails~\cite{mahon2025}. A
$\sigma$-essential contextual state would be the non-distributive analogue --- finite
sub-OMLs all classically interpretable, the obstruction carried only by the countable
whole.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The combinatorial route}
\label{sec:why-sigma}

One might seek the object among concrete $\sigma$-orthocomplete OMLs,
on the grounds that a state's $\sigma$-additivity invokes $\bigvee_n a_n$ only
for orthogonal families (Definition~\ref{def:complete}). This route is
settled, and does not produce the object. The property that would
make $\sigma$-additivity genuinely exceed finite additivity on a concrete
non-Boolean lattice is the interleaving condition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interleave}
\textbf{(}\bigstar\textbf{)}\quad
\exists\, p,\ \exists\,\{a_n\}\text{ infinite orthogonal},\quad
p \wedge a_n = 0\ (\forall n),\quad p \not\perp a_n\ (\forall n),
\end{equation}
and $(\bigstar)$ is satisfied \emph{trivially} by the plain product
$\prod_n \mathrm{MO}_2$: take $a_n = a$ in coordinate $n$ (zero elsewhere), and
$p = \bigvee_n b_n$ where $b_n = b$ in coordinate $n$ (zero elsewhere) --- a
genuine countable \emph{orthogonal} join, so $p=(b,b,b,\dots)$ exists by
$\sigma$-orthocompleteness. Then $p\wedge a_n=0$ (coordinate $n$: $a\wedge b=0$
in $\mathrm{MO}_2$; elsewhere $a_n=0$) and $p\not\perp a_n$ (coordinate $n$:
$b\not\leq a^\perp$). Since $\prod_n \mathrm{MO}_2$ is concrete, $\sigma$-complete
(a product of finite, hence complete, lattices), non-Boolean, and infinite, it
satisfies $(\bigstar)$ and \emph{every} combinatorial hypothesis one might
impose --- yet it is exactly the \emph{segregated} object the open problem must
exclude. So the combinatorial axioms ($\sigma$-completeness, concreteness,
$(\bigstar)$) are \emph{not} the discriminator; the missing ingredient is
non-segregation, which is a point-free notion, not a closure property.

\begin{remark}[The witness has no points, and the Boolean boundary]\label{rem:inhabited}
The universal closure above (a faithful set representation forces distributivity)
already rules out the point-based route for every non-Boolean OML. The witness
$\prod_n\mathrm{MO}_2$ shows the failure concretely and at the level of points:
$\mathrm{MO}_2$ admits \emph{no} two-valued \emph{homomorphism} --- each of its
four separating two-valued \emph{states} ($a\mapsto0,\,b\mapsto0$, etc.) fails
$\omega(a\vee b)\leq\omega(a)+\omega(b)$ since $a\vee b=\mathbf 1$, so none
preserves $\vee$ --- and $\prod_n\mathrm{MO}_2$ inherits this, since its diagonal
copy $\{\mathbf 0,\mathbf 1,(a,a,\dots),(a^\perp,\dots),(b,b,\dots),(b^\perp,\dots)\}$
is a sub-$\mathrm{MO}_2$ on which any homomorphism would restrict to one of
$\mathrm{MO}_2$'s (none exists). (This is special to $\mathrm{MO}_2$ and its
products --- a generic non-Boolean OML such as $\mathbf 2\times\mathrm{MO}_2$
\emph{does} carry homomorphisms, e.g.\ the projection; the universal obstruction
is the distributivity argument, not homomorphism-scarcity.) So the witness is
concrete (Definition~\ref{def:omp}: order-determining two-valued \emph{states})
and $\sigma$-complete yet has no points in the homomorphism sense at all --- the
point-free target is forced, not engineered. The same arithmetic records the
sharp Boolean boundary: an OML is Boolean iff it admits a \emph{unital} set of
\emph{subadditive} states --- one with, for each nonzero $a$, a subadditive state
giving $a$ value $1$ (Pt\'ak--Pulmannov\'a~\cite[Thm~1]{PtakPulmannova1994}, for OMLs;
the orthomodular-poset version fails). A
concrete non-Boolean OML therefore cannot have its separating two-valued states
form such a unital set: some fail subadditivity (here all four of $\mathrm{MO}_2$'s
fail $\omega(a\vee b)\leq\omega(a)+\omega(b)$). The forcing property to Boolean is
thus subadditivity --- a finitary condition --- not $\sigma$-additivity.
\end{remark}

\noindent A worked instance fixes the picture. Navara's
construction~\cite{Navara1992} produces an infinite, $\sigma$-orthocomplete,
rich OML whose inner block is a finite \emph{stateless} Greechie logic --- which
is exactly what makes Navara's $\mathcal L$ non-concrete. Replacing the stateless
block by $\mathrm{MO}_2$ (write $\mathcal L_2$) removes the only source of
non-concreteness: the assembly is a product of a sublogic of a Kalmbach
horizontal sum (blocks glued only at $\{0,1\}$, \emph{not} an atom-sharing
Greechie loop), and pure horizontal sums of concrete logics are concrete, as are
sublogics and products. So $\mathcal L_2$ is concrete, $\sigma$-orthocomplete,
non-Boolean, and carries $(\bigstar)$ --- yet it is not a new object: it is
$\prod_n \mathrm{MO}_2$ with extra scaffolding, segregated in the same way, and
it likewise admits no $\sigma$-tribe representation. It is the cautionary example
showing that exhibiting $(\bigstar)$ and concreteness together is easy and does
\emph{not} reach the open problem.

\medskip
\noindent The art constrains the open ($\sigma$-complete) problem from both sides
without closing it: \emph{no} impossibility theorem covers the concrete class
(Remark~\ref{rem:miss}), while the two \emph{theorem-closed} routes to an engine
--- RDP and MacNeille (\S\ref{sec:routes}(i),(ii)) --- are ruled out, leaving
only the third, $\sigma$-Stone duality, which is itself open. What is required is
not a new idea in the abstract but a representation in that gap:
non-distributive enough to evade routes (i),(ii), yet $\sigma$-faithful where the
known dualities are merely finitary.

\begin{remark}[Why $L(H)$+Gleason does \emph{not} already settle this ---
the (A)/(B) point-space equivocation]\label{rem:AB}
``Probability from relations, point-free'' may \emph{look} delivered by
$L(H)$+Gleason. It is not: the appearance rests on conflating two point-spaces, and
\emph{realisation} is defined as concentration on the second. The two are (A) the
Hilbert rays of $H$, which Gleason \emph{requires} --- the density $\rho$ in
$s=\tr(\rho\,\cdot)$ is built from them --- and (B) the McDonald--Bimb\'{o} dual
filters $P(A)$, which Gleason \emph{removes}, but only by being a representation
theorem. Gleason is thus the \emph{most} point-ful result available (every
lattice state corresponds to the point datum $\rho$). What $L(H)$ exhibits is a
\emph{separation}, not point-freeness: $\sigma$-additive measures exist on the
lattice (Gleason, for normal states), yet on the dual $S_0(L(H))$ no state extends
to a charge at all (Proposition~\ref{prop:lh}), so a fortiori none concentrates on
$P(A)$. It cannot serve as the existence proof required, so the point-free
$\sigma$-additive structure of \S\ref{sec:problem} is the only candidate engine.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Two axes of obstruction}
\label{sec:twoaxes}
The recurring confusion of this subject
(Remarks~\ref{rem:inhabited}, \ref{rem:AB}) is best held as a
$2\times2$ table on two \emph{independent} axes, which must not be collapsed
into one.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Axis 1 --- distributivity $=$ commutativity.} One condition in two
    dialects: a family of projections commutes \emph{iff} the lattice they generate
    is distributive. ``Commutativity'' is the operator word, ``distributivity'' the
    lattice word, for the \emph{same} dividing line --- not different sides.
  \item \textbf{Axis 2 --- concreteness.} A non-Boolean lattice can be
    \emph{point-rich in rays} (atoms $=$ $1$-dim subspaces) yet \emph{point-free in
    valuations} (no two-valued homomorphisms, Kochen--Specker), or the reverse: the
    two senses of ``point'' come apart (the (A)/(B) equivocation, Rmk~\ref{rem:AB}).
\end{itemize}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.17\linewidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.33\linewidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.42\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
 & \textbf{distributive\newline(commuting)} & \textbf{non-distributive\newline(non-commuting)} \\
\midrule
\textbf{concrete}\newline(valuations span) & classical: Boolean $\sigma$-algebra,
  Loomis--Sikorski free & the descent witness lives here: a concrete
  $\sigma$-complete non-Boolean OML ($\prod_n\mathrm{MO}_2$ lies here, but
  segregated) \\[4pt]
\textbf{non-concrete}\newline(KS: few valuations) & atomless measure algebra
  (valuation-poor) & $L(H)$: rays abundant, valuations KS-barred --- the operator /
  e.s.a.\ world, the excluded pole \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\noindent There is not one ``lattice $\leftrightarrow$ space'' dictionary but two,
one per row: \emph{Birkhoff--von Neumann} (bottom row) sends elements to closed
subspaces and atoms to \textbf{rays}, with $a\vee b$ the closed span --- rich in
ray-points, KS-barred from valuation-points, hence non-concrete; \emph{Stone /
concrete-logic} (Gudder, top row) sends elements to sets of outcomes and atoms to
points of $\Omega$ --- rich in valuation-points, hence concrete. The operator
question and the descent question share Axis~1 but sit in opposite rows: spectral
projections \emph{force} $L(H)$ (bottom-right), whereas the descent witness must be
top-right. So the descent problem is the one cell no operator algebra can occupy ---
why a witness must be a pasting / countable colimit of concrete blocks, not a
Hilbert-space construction, and why $L(H)$+Gleason cannot settle it. In this language
the open problem (route~(iii)) is exactly: \emph{is there a
logic\,$\leftrightarrow$\,geometry dictionary for the top-right cell?} --- and a
$\sigma$-Loomis--Sikorski for concrete OMLs is that missing dictionary. Axes~1 and~2
are independent: non-distributivity does not entail non-concreteness, and a witness
must be concrete and non-distributive simultaneously.

\section{Directions}
\label{sec:first}
% ==================================================================

With the extension axis closed on $L(H)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:lh}), the
combinatorial route shown to deliver only segregated objects
(\S\ref{sec:why-sigma}), and the question reduced to a $\sigma$-essential contextual
state (\S\ref{sec:predicate}), the problem has a clean dichotomy: two exits, each a
genuine terminus. The negative exit is, moreover, \emph{already taken in the
Polish-representable case} (Derr--Williamson, Remark~\ref{rem:dw}); both exits below
therefore live or die on the same feature --- whether a witness can fail to admit a
regular Borel point-realisation.
\begin{description}[style=nextline, font=\normalfont\bfseries,
    labelwidth=2.6cm, leftmargin=3.0cm]
  \item[Exhibit a $\sigma$-essential witness]
    Construct an infinite concrete OML carrying a $\sigma$-additive contextual
    state that no finite sub-OML witnesses. The natural attack is a
    compactness-failure construction (cf.\ Remark~\ref{rem:ce}): a concrete
    $\sigma$-complete OML whose finite sublogics are all classically interpretable
    (dispersion-free states span them) but whose countable joins force a
    Kochen--Specker-type obstruction only in the limit. The faithful set
    representation is ruled out at every stage (Theorem~\ref{thm:floor}), so the
    obstruction must be carried by the $\sigma$-structure itself, not by any finite
    block. A necessary condition follows: the witness must place its infinitary
    structure \emph{off-center} (irreducible, with non-central infinite blocks). A
    product of finite blocks such as $\prod_n\mathrm{MO}_2$ is excluded a priori,
    since it forces all infinitary content into the Boolean center, where
    $\sigma$-additivity concentrates the contextual states onto points and removes
    them (\S\ref{sec:why-sigma}); a pasting or countable colimit of Wright-type
    blocks is the natural candidate.
  \item[Prove no such witness exists]
    A spanning theorem: every $\sigma$-additive state on a $\sigma$-complete
    concrete OML lies in the closed convex hull of its dispersion-free states
    \emph{except} where a finite sub-OML is already contextual (Wright). This would
    be an impossibility result --- a $\sigma$-essential contextual state cannot
    exist, so relational $\sigma$-additive probability is operationally invisible
    (present finitely or not at all). \emph{This is already established for
    Polish-representable OMLs} (Derr--Williamson, Remark~\ref{rem:dw}); a full
    impossibility result must extend it to the non-representable case, where the
    Polish/Borel-regularity hypothesis of their Theorem~D.6 is unavailable. The
    Boolean boundary --- subadditivity of a unital set of states
    (Pt\'ak--Pulmannov\'a, Remark~\ref{rem:inhabited}) --- is a natural lever; note
    that such an argument cannot reuse the $\prod_n\mathrm{MO}_2$ mechanism
    (concentration on the Boolean center), which is vacuous off-center.
\end{description}

\noindent A candidate direction now closed: the singular case for $L(H)$. One
might have hoped a singular state would extend where no normal state does,
providing the first explicit ``extends but may fail to concentrate'' example;
Proposition~\ref{prop:lh} rules this out, catching every state, singular
included. The reduction is recorded because the singular case was expected to
require infinitary methods and does not (Remark~\ref{rem:finvscount}).

\begin{remark}[Finite versus countable: finite suffices]\label{rem:finvscount}
Is the obstruction to extending a singular state realised by a \emph{finite}
or only by a \emph{countable} pairwise-meet-zero family? The two arguments
divide on exactly this point. The line argument (Remark~\ref{rem:linearg}) is
finitary but \emph{vacuous} on a singular state ($s(e)=0$ for every finite-rank
$e$); the $\mathrm{MO}_2$ argument with infinite-dimensional atoms
(Proposition~\ref{prop:lh}) uses a finite family ($n=4$) and gives
$\sum_i s(a_i)=2$ state-independently, catching the singular case as well. So the
singular obstruction is \emph{not} entangled with the descent-axis passage, as the
vacuity of the line argument might suggest: it is realised by a finite family,
hence a self-contained finitary problem, separable from the
general construction and not governed by the finitary-to-$\sigma$ passage.
\end{remark}

\section{Glossary of definitions}
\label{app:glossary}
% ==================================================================

A self-contained list of the notions used in the paper. Entries that restate a
numbered definition in the body cross-reference it; the remaining entries are stated
here. Throughout, $L$ is an orthomodular lattice with orthocomplementation
$a\mapsto a^\perp$, bottom $\mathbf 0$, top $\mathbf 1$.

\subsection*{Lattice notions}

\begin{description}[style=nextline, leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=3pt]
\item[Orthocomplemented lattice; orthomodular lattice (OML).]
  A bounded lattice with an order-reversing, involutive complement $a^\perp$
  satisfying $a\vee a^\perp=\mathbf 1$, $a\wedge a^\perp=\mathbf 0$
  (Definition~\ref{def:ocl}); it is \emph{orthomodular} if $a\leq b\Rightarrow
  b=a\vee(b\wedge a^\perp)$ (Definition~\ref{def:oml}). A \emph{Boolean algebra} is a
  distributive OML.
\item[Orthogonal; meet-zero; incomparable.]
  $a\perp b$ means $a\leq b^\perp$; \emph{meet-zero} means $a\wedge b=\mathbf 0$.
  Orthogonal implies meet-zero; the converse fails off the Boolean case, and that gap
  drives the subject (Definition~\ref{def:ortho}).
\item[Compatible; block.]
  $a,b$ are \emph{compatible} if they generate a Boolean subalgebra, equivalently
  $a=(a\wedge b)\vee(a\wedge b^\perp)$. A \emph{block} is a maximal pairwise-compatible
  subset of $L$; it is a maximal Boolean sub-OML, and every OML is the union of its
  blocks. (In a concrete logic, compatibility of $A,B$ is $A=(A\cap B)\cup(A\cap
  B^c)$.)
\item[Completeness.]
  $L$ is \emph{$\sigma$-complete} if every countable subset has a join; it is
  \emph{$\sigma$-orthocomplete} if every countable \emph{pairwise-orthogonal} family
  has a join (Definition~\ref{def:complete}).
\item[Centre; irreducible.]
  The \emph{centre} $Z(L)$ is the set of elements compatible with all of $L$; it is a
  Boolean subalgebra. $L$ is \emph{irreducible} if $Z(L)=\{\mathbf 0,\mathbf 1\}$
  (no nontrivial direct-product decomposition).
\item[$\mathrm{MO}_n$; $L(H)$.]
  $\mathrm{MO}_n$ is the modular ortholattice with $\mathbf 0,\mathbf 1$ and $n$
  complementary atom-pairs ($\mathrm{MO}_2$: atoms $a,a^\perp,b,b^\perp$, all distinct
  meets $\mathbf 0$); it is non-distributive for $n\geq 2$. $L(H)$ is the lattice of
  closed subspaces of a Hilbert space $H$ (orthocomplement $=$ orthogonal complement,
  join $=$ closed span), the prototype non-distributive OML.
\end{description}

\subsection*{Representations}

\begin{description}[style=nextline, leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=3pt]
\item[OMP; concrete logic; order-determining states.]
  An \emph{orthomodular poset} (OMP) weakens an OML to a partial join (only of
  orthogonal elements). $L$ is a \emph{concrete logic} (set-representable) if it
  embeds in some $\mathcal P(\Omega)$ with $a^\perp\mapsto\Omega\setminus a$ and
  orthogonal joins as disjoint unions, equivalently if it carries an
  \emph{order-determining} (separating) family of two-valued states
  (Definition~\ref{def:omp}).
\item[Faithful set representation; $\sigma$-tribe.]
  An injection $h:L\to\mathcal P(\Omega)$ with $h(a\wedge b)=h(a)\cap h(b)$,
  $h(a\vee b)=h(a)\cup h(b)$, $h(a^\perp)=\Omega\setminus h(a)$ (preserving
  \emph{all} meets and joins as $\cap,\cup$); a \emph{$\sigma$-tribe} /
  Loomis--Sikorski representation also preserves countable joins. By
  Theorem~\ref{thm:floor} only Boolean $L$ admits one. (Contrast a concrete logic,
  which preserves only \emph{orthogonal} joins as unions.)
\item[Dual space $S_0(A)$; canonical points $P(A)$.]
  $S_0(A)$ is the McDonald--Bimb\'o space of filters of $A$; $P(A)=\{{\uparrow}p:p\in
  A\}$ is the set of principal filters, one per element (Definition~\ref{def:dual}).
\end{description}

\subsection*{States and measures}

\begin{description}[style=nextline, leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=3pt]
\item[State; meet-additive; charge.]
  A \emph{state} is $s:L\to[0,1]$ with $s(\mathbf 1)=1$ and $s(a\vee b)=s(a)+s(b)$
  for $a\perp b$ (Definition~\ref{def:ladder}); a \emph{charge} is a finitely
  additive set function on a Boolean algebra (Definition~\ref{def:charge}).
\item[$\sigma$-additive state.]
  A state $s$ with $s\bigl(\bigvee_n a_n\bigr)=\sum_n s(a_n)$ for every countable
  pairwise-orthogonal $\{a_n\}$ (Definition~\ref{def:complete}).
\item[Dispersion-free state.]
  A two-valued state, i.e.\ a state taking only the values $0,1$ (equivalently a
  $\{0,1\}$-homomorphism on each block); $S_{\mathrm{df}}$ denotes their set and
  $S_{\mathrm{df}}^\sigma$ the $\sigma$-additive ones. These are the deterministic /
  hidden-variable points.
\item[Normal; singular state on $L(H)$.]
  $s$ is \emph{normal} if $s(\cdot)=\tr(\rho\,\cdot)$ for a density operator $\rho$;
  \emph{singular} if $s$ vanishes on every finite-rank projection
  (Definition~\ref{def:normalsing}).
\item[Subadditive state.]
  A state $s$ with $s(a\vee b)\leq s(a)+s(b)$ for \emph{all} $a,b$ (not only
  orthogonal pairs). A family of states is \emph{unital} if for each $a\neq\mathbf 0$
  some member gives $s(a)=1$.
\end{description}

\subsection*{The descent vocabulary}

\begin{description}[style=nextline, leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=3pt]
\item[Contextual state.]
  A state $w$ that is \emph{not} in the closed convex hull
  $\overline{\mathrm{conv}}(S_{\mathrm{df}})$ of the dispersion-free states ---
  equivalently (Lemma~\ref{lem:relational}) a state with no hidden realisation, i.e.\
  not a mixture of dispersion-free states. A non-contextual state is one that is such
  a mixture.
\item[$\sigma$-essentially contextual state.]
  A $\sigma$-additive state $w$ on a $\sigma$-complete $L$ that is \emph{contextual}
  --- $w\notin\overline{\mathrm{conv}}(S_{\mathrm{df}}^\sigma)$ --- yet whose
  restriction to \emph{every finite sub-OML} of $L$ is non-contextual. (All three
  clauses are required: $\sigma$-additive, globally contextual, finitely
  non-contextual. The finite version without the third clause is already inhabited,
  Wright's pentagon.)
\item[Extension axis; descent axis.]
  The \emph{extension} axis asks whether a state extends to a charge on the full
  clopen algebra of $S_0(A)$ (a Horn--Tarski feasibility question); the
  \emph{descent} axis asks whether such a charge concentrates on the physical points
  $P(A)$ (governed by $\sigma$-additivity). See \S\ref{sec:axes}.
\item[Segregated.]
  Of an OML: one whose non-distributivity is confined to finite blocks, so that
  $S_{\mathrm{df}}=S_{\mathrm{df}}^\sigma$ and no $\sigma$-essential leak is possible
  (e.g.\ $\prod_n\mathrm{MO}_2$, \S\ref{sec:why-sigma}).
\end{description}

\subsection*{Prior-art objects (\S\ref{sec:problem}, Remark~\ref{rem:dw})}

\begin{description}[style=nextline, leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=3pt]
\item[Pre-Dynkin-system; Dynkin-system.]
  In Derr--Williamson's terminology, a \emph{pre-Dynkin-system} on $\Omega$ is a
  concrete OMP (a family of subsets containing $\Omega$, closed under complement and
  under disjoint unions); a \emph{Dynkin-system} additionally is closed under
  countable disjoint unions --- a concrete $\sigma$-complete OML.
\item[$\sigma$-extendable.]
  In Derr--Williamson, a countably additive probability $\mu_\sigma$ on a
  Dynkin-system $D_\sigma\subseteq\mathrm{Borel}(\Omega)$ ($\Omega$ Polish) is
  \emph{$\sigma$-extendable} if it extends to a countably additive probability on
  $\mathrm{Borel}(\Omega)$.
\item[Polish space; inner regular.]
  A \emph{Polish} space is a separable completely metrizable space. A measure $\mu$
  is \emph{inner regular} if $\mu(F)=\sup\{\mu(K):K\subseteq F,\ K\text{ compact}\}$.
\end{description}

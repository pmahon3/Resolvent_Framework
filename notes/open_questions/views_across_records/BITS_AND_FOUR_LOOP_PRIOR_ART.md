## B1: the exact theorem (read in full text)
1. **Nisan-Szegedy 1994**, "On the degree of Boolean functions as real polynomials", Comput. Complexity 4: 301-313. Theorem
   (as quoted in Chiarelli-Hatami-Saks, intro, full text read, and Valyuzhenich 2609.12540, full text read): a Boolean function
   f: {0,1}^n -> {0,1} of real degree d has at most d*2^(d-1) relevant variables. Relevant = appears in a monomial of the unique
   multilinear representation with nonzero coefficient (equivalent to "changing it changes f somewhere"). **d=2 gives 4.**
   Read level for NS itself: statement read in two secondary full texts; NS PDF not opened (a copy is listed at web.vu.lt/mif/s.jukna/boolean/Nisan-Szegedy.pdf).
2. **Chiarelli, Hatami, Saks**, "An asymptotically tight bound on the number of relevant variables in a bounded degree Boolean
   function", Combinatorica 40 (2020) 237-244, arXiv:1801.08564. Read: arXiv PDF full text (pdftotext). Upper bound C*2^d, C <= 6.614; lower
   bound 3*2^(d-1) - 2 relevant variables via Xi_d. With Xi_1 = identity and, in +-1 form,
   Xi_d(s,t,x,y) = ((s-t)/2) Xi_{d-1}(x) + ((s+t)/2) Xi_{d-1}(y)  (their words: "s*Xi_{d-1}(x) if s = t, s*Xi_{d-1}(y) if s != t").
   For d=2 this has 3*2-2 = 4 relevant variables. **So 4 is tight: R_2 = 4**, and Xi_2 is exactly the shape "if u = v then
   [p = u] else [..]". Independent of the d=2 case, they note a similar construction by Shinkar-Tal (private communication, ST17).
   Wellens (arXiv:1903.08214; Discrete Analysis 2022, arXiv:2005.00566) improves constants; irrelevant at d=2.
3. **Filmus, "Quadratic Boolean functions on the cube"**, manuscript, 27 March 2022
   (yuvalfilmus.cs.technion.ac.il/Manuscripts/Degree2.pdf). Read: full text (3 pages, downloaded PDF). Complete list, up to NPN
   equivalence (negate inputs, permute inputs, negate output), of degree-2 functions {-1,1}^n -> {-1,1} depending on all
   coordinates: 1, identity x, XOR xy, OR [x=y=1], all-equal [x=y=z], **3-sortedness [x<=y<=z], 4-sortedness
   [x<=y<=z<=w or x>=y>=z>=w]**. Method: pass to the even function g(x,u) = f(xu); its quadratic graph G_f is connected; each
   D_i f = sum_j c_ij x_j is {-1,0,1}-valued so every vertex has degree 1 (weight +-1) or degree 2 (weights +-1/2); hence G_f is
   an edge or a cycle with c_ij^2 = 1/4, and Parseval forces length <= 4. Triangle: all-equal. Square: c = 0, three signs +, one
   sign -, all four + impossible (value 2 at the all-ones input). **This is B1, the classification, and B5 in one note.**
4. **Our own check (⟦computed⟧, not a source).** Interpolating on the 16 points of Hamming weight <= 2 (unisolvent for
   degree <= 2) and testing all 2^16 value patterns for n=5: 552 Boolean functions of degree <= 2; relevant-variable counts
   0:2, 1:10, 2:100, 3:320, 4:120, **none with 5**. For n=4: 222 (matches |V01(K4)|=222), with 24 depending on all four. The
   4-variable function A4 = "if u=v then [p=u] else [t=v]" lies in the NPN orbit of 4-sortedness (orbit size 24, 8 ones of 16:
   checked), i.e. **A4 is the 4-sortedness function, equivalently Xi_2.** Script was run in a scratch directory, not saved to the repo.
5. Name: no other name found for the four-variable function ("4-sortedness" Filmus; "Xi_2" CHS). It is the unique
   4-variable class: Filmus's list has exactly one function on 4 variables.
6. **Different notion, do not confuse:** Camion-Carlet-Charpin-Sendrier and Tarannikov-Kirienko/Tarannikov-Korolev-Botev
   (resilient functions) and Reed-Muller RM(2,m) use GF(2) degree. Abstract/snippet level only; irrelevant to B1.
   The page teorth.github.io/optimizationproblems/constants/44a.html (secondary, read) records R_3 = 10 (Tarannikov-Kirienko via the
   translation R_d = p(d+1), attained by Xi_3) and lists no exact R_2, although NS (<=4) and CHS (>=4) give R_2 = 4.
7. Verdict on the repo's bookkeeping note: "remembered bound" is now sourced. Two repo statements to cite as owned: "every
   adds-up question on bits involves <= 4 quantities" and "the only 4-quantity kind is A4 up to symmetry" are Filmus's
   classification restricted to what V01(K_n(2)) contains (V01(K_n(2)) = Boolean functions of real degree <= 2, by the
   degree-2 polynomial argument; the count 222 at n=4 and 552 at n=5 is consistent).

## B5: half-counts (owned content)
In +-1 form, f = c + sum c_ij x_i x_j with a cycle of nonzero c_ij is Boolean only for length 3 (all signs equal after negations;
all-equal function) and 4 (c = 0, one minus sign: 4-sortedness). Filmus note, above. The repo's version uses
disagreement indicators d_i = (1 - x_i x_{i+1})/2, which just rescales c and c_ij. "No sign pattern works at L = 5, 6" is
Filmus's c_ij^2 = 1/4 and Parseval. Verdict OWNED; credit Filmus and cite as the source for "lengths 3 and 4 only".
Caveat: the Greechie "loop lemma" (a block diagram represents an orthomodular lattice iff every loop has order >= 5;
loops of order 3 and 4 are excluded or absorbed) singles out the same lengths 3 and 4 in a different setting (pasting Boolean
blocks). Secondary snippets only (arXiv quant-ph/0009039 and others, abstract level). Do not treat it as the same theorem;
it is a rhyme to check, and the repo's own cycle-logic material (Ovchinnikov-Sultanbekov n-gon logics) is nearer.

## B3: any number of values
- Notation bridge. On Z_q^n (or any product), Valyuzhenich defines degree as the smallest t such that f is a sum of t-juntas
  (equivalently Fourier support of weight <= t). A 0/1-valued function of degree <= 2 is exactly a 0/1-valued sum of functions of
  pairs of coordinates: **V01(K_n(q)) is the set of Boolean degree-2 functions on the Hamming graph.** Mixed alphabet sizes
  embed in Z_q^n for q = max size by sending extra symbols to a fixed symbol (⟦HAND⟧), so the bounds transfer as upper bounds.
- **Valyuzhenich**, "An upper bound on the number of relevant variables for Boolean functions on the Hamming graph",
  Discrete Math. 349(2) (2026) 114745, arXiv:2404.10418. Read: search snippet of abstract only. Claim as reported: Boolean
  degree d on Z_q^n, q >= 3, has at most d*q^(d+1) / (4(q-1)) relevant variables. At d=2 this is q^3/(2(q-1)) (6.75 at q=3).
- **Valyuzhenich**, "An upper bound on the number of relevant variables in a bounded degree Boolean function on the Hamming
  graph", arXiv:2609.12540 (11 Sep 2026). Read: arXiv full text (pdftotext). R_{d,q} <= m_q q^d; improved constants q=3..7;
  trivial lower bound (q^d - 1)/(q - 1) from q-ary decision trees; degree 1 on Z_q^n: at most one relevant variable (cites
  Filmus-Ihringer 2019 [5]). At d=2 the lower bound is q+1: a **selector** (one coordinate chooses which of q others is read) is the
  extremal example in the literature. Gap at d=2, q=3: between 4 and 6. No exact R_{2,q} and no classification stated.
  Statement of "exactly R_{2,q}" is NOT FOUND.
- **Potapov**, "On the number of relevant variables for discrete functions", Cryptogr. Commun. 17 (2025) 989-998, arXiv:2410.21937.
  Abstract-level (search summary): bounds via average sensitivity, with two generalisations deg1, deg2 of degree. ADJACENT.
- **Filmus-Ihringer**, "Boolean constant degree functions on the slice are juntas", Discrete Math 342 (2019) 111614,
  arXiv:1801.06338, and "Boolean degree 1 functions on some classical association schemes", JCTA 162 (2019): abstracts and
  citation by Valyuzhenich only. They treat the slice/Johnson scheme and degree 1 on schemes including multislices; conjecture
  statements for Hamming scheme degree d. ADJACENT (not the pair-interaction classification for [k]^n).
- **Filmus, "Junta threshold for low degree Boolean functions on the slice"** (EJC 30 (2023) P1.55) and
  **"Degree 2 Boolean functions on Grassmann graphs"** (arXiv:2202.03940): abstract level; other graphs than the hypercube. UNRELATED to [k]^n.
- **Asensio-Garcia-Marco-Knauer**, arXiv:2409.16141 (abstract): m-ary sensitivity vs degree. UNRELATED.
- **Amireddy-Srinivasan-Sudan**, arXiv:2305.04983 (abstract): "junta-degree-d = sum of d-juntas" on grids; same definition,
  purpose is testing. UNRELATED beyond vocabulary.

## Vocabulary to adopt
- relevant variable, k-junta; "depends on at most k coordinates" -> "is a k-junta".
- degree = real (multilinear / Fourier / Efron-Stein) degree; on [q]^n "sum of t-juntas" (junta-degree). Say "degree" only with "over the reals".
- "Boolean degree-d function" (Filmus-Ihringer, Valyuzhenich) for V01 of order d.
- NPN equivalence for symmetry classes of Boolean functions.
- A4 = "4-sortedness function" (Filmus) = "Xi_2" (Chiarelli-Hatami-Saks).
- R_{d,q} for the maximum number of relevant variables; R_{2,2} = 4.
- Selectors: "q-ary decision trees" / address (multiplexer) functions.
- Do not use "degree" for GF(2) algebraic degree; Reed-Muller and resilient-function literature mean that.

## A. E1 (richness <-> synchrony; phase-dependent observability)

**(ii) Is "phase-dependent identifiability of a common cause" a named notion?** NOT FOUND under that name. Nearest read: Dakos 2015 (wrong variable, noise structure), Boerlijst 2013 (abstract), Rigobon 2003 (abstract). The underlying notions (state-dependent observability; identifiability at a parameter value; identification through regime variance) are all standard (see table), so the phrase is a relabelling of owned ideas, with the sign of the heteroskedasticity effect in doubt.

**(iii) Is the step-2 conjecture an instance of spiked-covariance / weak-factor detection?** Partly, and in the wrong regime for a clean answer.
- Baik-Ben Arous-Peche 2005 (UNVERIFIED memory) and Johnstone 2001 concern a rank-one spike above isotropic noise in the regime p/n -> c, with a phase transition for the top sample eigenvalue at spike eigenvalue 1 + sqrt(c).
- Onatski-Moreira-Hallin 2013, *Ann. Stat.* 41:1204 (abstract + secondary, search result): below the BBP threshold the null and spiked alternatives are mutually CONTIGUOUS, the eigenvalue likelihood-ratio test has power strictly above size (not tending to 1), and Tracy-Widom-type tests have power equal to size. Above the threshold discrimination is asymptotically certain. Multispike version (arXiv 1210.5663, abstract fetched): the LR test is near the power envelope. Onatski 2012 weak-factor framework (drifting parameters, p/n -> c; snippet only): the number of effective factors is estimable but the factor space is not consistently recoverable.
- Consequence for E1's wording: "no correlation-based test can detect the centre below a threshold" is false in the p/n -> c spiked setting (contiguity is not undetectability; the LR test has nontrivial power), and false at fixed p with n -> infinity (any fixed alternative outside the null is detected with probability -> 1; thresholds exist only for local alternatives of order n^(-1/2)).
- What IS true, and is not a BBP statement: the repo's null {V <= 0} is a population-level blind region (cone of correlation matrices explainable by pair-sized causes). Inside it no sample size helps the V test. That is an identification boundary (observational equivalence), not a power threshold. OMH/BBP test sphericity, a different null, so they do not apply directly. A different test (variance ratio, sphericity) detects what V cannot (B2).
- Coincidence worth noting but not claiming: in the exchangeable case the V boundary is a top eigenvalue of R equal to 2; BBP at 1 + sqrt(n/N) equals 2 only when N = n.

**(iv) What is left that is specific to the repo's test?** Only a computed fact about the repo's own statistic in a one-resource model: how much of the range of kappa the V test is blind to (B2: includes a stretch with all correlations positive, and for sparse shares the whole range). That is a small, owned-technique observation (hand + simulation). Neither the mechanism nor the notion survives as new.

## B. Two reviewer claims

### B1. "Population correlation is a function of the share/total variance ratio; the prediction is empty in population."
**Verdict: TRUE for the model as specified, with three conditions made explicit. Derived by hand and matched to numerics (script `share_total_sweep.py`).**

Levels, Dirichlet(alpha) shares, T independent of s, mu = E[T], kappa = Var(T)/mu^2. With E[s_i s_j] = alpha_i alpha_j/(a(a+1)) (i != j):
- Cov(x_i, x_j) = mu^2 alpha_i alpha_j (kappa a - 1) / (a^2 (a+1)) for i != j. So ALL pairs change sign together at kappa = 1/a, for any alpha (verified for heterogeneous alpha = (0.3,0.5,1,2,4): all 10 pairs negative at kappa = 0.1, all positive at 0.2, a = 7.8).
- Equal alpha (a = n alpha): rho = (kappa a - 1)/(kappa (a + n) + n - 1). At kappa = 0: rho = -1/(n-1) (T constant, sum fixed, R singular). As kappa -> infinity: rho -> a/(a + n) (< 1: random shares keep loadings random).
- Logs: log x_i = log s_i + log T, v = Var(log T). Cov(log s_i, log s_j) = -psi'(a), Var(log s_i) = psi'(alpha) - psi'(a). So rho_log = (v - psi'(a))/(psi'(alpha) - psi'(a) + v). Sign change at v = psi'(a) (n=5, alpha=1: v = 0.221; n=10: 0.105). Difference from levels: no singular boundary at v = 0 (log shares do not sum to a constant): n=5, v=0 gives rho = -0.155 > -1/(n-1) and V = -0.378 (strictly inside).
- Conditions: (1) the "ratio" must be relative to the mean (kappa = CV^2 of T, or Var(log T)), not raw Var(T); (2) the share law must be held fixed (a, alpha enter the threshold); (3) T independent of s (if not, the formulas change; the independent-gamma case is where Dirichlet shares are independent of the sum, Lukacs/Mosimann UNVERIFIED).
- So: in population, correlation is a monotone function of kappa for fixed share law; "the ratio shifts before correlation rises" is empty there.

When the prediction can still have content:
1. **If the nodes exhaust T** then T = sum x_i is not an independent measurement: Var(T) = sum of all entries of the covariance matrix, so for equal variances Var(T)/sum Var(x_i) = 1 + (n-1) mean correlation. "Measure T directly" then uses the same data, weighted differently (the Schluter variance ratio). It is more sensitive to weak synchrony than V (see B2) but is not extra information.
2. **T observed independently of the nodes** (a capacity, a total power draw, a catch): then kappa estimated from T has its own sampling error, and a lead over a rolling pairwise-correlation estimate is possible. Content = estimator efficiency and window length, not identification.
3. **Time-varying kappa, rolling windows:** both rho-hat and kappa-hat are functionals of the same window; a lead needs different windows or noise, or a change in the share law (a) that moves rho without moving kappa.
4. **Change in share law:** if a (share concentration) and kappa move together, the sign of the change can be reversed or delayed, so "ratio before correlation" is not even guaranteed in population once a is not held fixed.

### B2. "Unit sum gives all-negative pairwise correlations = antibalanced, on which the test is silent; adding T-variance moves to not antibalanced."
**Verdict: first half CORRECT only for all-negative R (e.g. Dirichlet shares), WRONG for a general unit-sum; second half IMPRECISE.**
- All pairwise correlations negative implies antibalanced (switching matrix D = I; every triangle has product of signs negative, every L-cycle (-1)^L), and V <= 0 for any antibalanced PSD R (V = -lambda_min(R)). Correct.
- A general unit sum forces only: mean covariance negative, at least one negative covariance per node (Chayes; A.1). Not antibalanced in general.
- Adding Var(T) leaves antibalanced exactly when the first off-diagonal turns positive: kappa = 1/a (n=5, alpha=1: 0.20; n=10: 0.10). But V > 0 requires, in the exchangeable case, rho > 1/(n-1) (V = (n-1)|rho| - 1 exactly). So there is a window of positive correlations, not antibalanced, where V < 0 (the test is silent: explainable by pair-sized causes): n=5: kappa between 0.2 and 0.8; n=10: between 0.1 and 0.257. Closed form for the crossing: kappa* = 2(n-1)/(a(n-2) - n), which exists only if a(n-2) > n.
- **Sparse shares: the test is blind at every kappa.** If a(n-2) <= n (alpha <= 1/(n-2)), V stays <= 0 even as kappa -> infinity (n=10, alpha=0.1: rho_inf = 0.091 < 1/9, V_inf = -0.182; n=5, alpha=0.2 and 0.3: V_inf = -0.33, -0.077; alpha=0.5 gives V_inf = 2.0). T can carry nearly all the variance and still not be "seen" as a common cause of arity > 2, because the loadings s_i are random. This is a consequence of the random-loading product structure, and the statement is population-level.
- **The hidden centre in the compensatory phase is quiet, not hidden.** At kappa = 0 there is no common cause: T is constant. Between 0 and 1/a, T fluctuates weakly and R is antibalanced; that is the weak-factor regime. E1's "centre hidden" conflates a constraint with a cause.

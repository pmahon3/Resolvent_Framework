### Pure Verdict: NOVEL as a statement in its field. The algebraic core is close to known (see flag).

**The statement, stripped of vocabulary.**
- Let A be an integer matrix. The fibre of b is F_b = {u ∈ ℕⁿ : Au = b}.
- A finite set of moves M ⊂ ker_ℤ A connects every fibre (staying non-negative) if and only if the binomials x^{m⁺} − x^{m⁻} (m ∈ M) generate the toric ideal I_A.
- Consequences:
  - a finite connecting set always exists (Hilbert basis theorem);
  - one can be computed with Buchberger's algorithm;
  - Metropolis on the resulting fibre graph then gives exact conditional tests for log-linear and other discrete exponential families.

**Hostile search: the closest prior art, all before 1997-12-31.**
1. **Conti & Traverso, "Buchberger algorithm and integer programming", AAECC-9, LNCS 539 (1991)** [secondary]. Every lattice point of a fibre reduces to a unique normal form via the Gröbner basis of I_A. This is the integer-programming test-set property.
   - Corollary: the undirected fibre graph built from Gröbner moves is connected. Every point is joined by a path to the unique optimum.
   - So the half "Gröbner basis ⇒ connected" is a short consequence of Conti–Traverso, 1991.
2. **R. R. Thomas, "A geometric Buchberger algorithm for integer programming", Math. Oper. Res. 20(4):864–884 (1995)** [abstract]. Shows that the reduced Gröbner basis is the unique minimal test set and moves between fibre points geometrically. The same fibre-walk picture appears there, but for optimisation, not sampling.
3. **B. Sturmfels, "Gröbner bases of toric varieties", Tôhoku Math. J. 43 (1991); "Gröbner Bases and Convex Polytopes", AMS ULS 8 (1996)** [recall].
   - Standard lemma: I_A is spanned as a vector space by the binomials x^u − x^v with Au = Av.
   - Fibre connectivity under the generators follows from that lemma in a few lines.
   - Caveat: Sturmfels is a likely co-author of the seed. Any treatment of the sampling application in the 1996 book is the seed's own lineage and is not used against it. I could not confirm whether the book's text predates or depends on the seed's preprint.
4. **Diaconis & Gangolli, "Rectangular arrays with fixed margins", IMA Vol. 72 (1995)** [secondary]. Uses the 2×2 swap moves on two-way tables. Those moves are exactly the generators (the 2×2 minors) of the toric ideal of the independence model. This is the seed's first example, already known with a direct connectivity proof.
5. **Besag & Clifford, "Generalized Monte Carlo significance tests", Biometrika 76:633–642 (1989)** [abstract]. Exact MCMC tests when null samples can only be generated by a Markov chain from the data (Rasch model, Ising model). It supplies the testing framework but no general method for constructing moves.
6. **Pistone & Wynn, "Generalised confounding with Gröbner bases", Biometrika 83(3):653–666 (1996)** [abstract]. Gröbner bases applied in statistics, but in experimental design (the design ideal and identifiability), not in conditional sampling. It is a neighbour, not an owner.
7. **Mehta & Patel's network algorithms (1983 onward) and Agresti's 1992 survey of exact inference** [recall]. Exact enumeration and sampling for r×c tables. These are model-specific and come with no general move theory.

**Assessment.**
- No source before 1997 states the equivalence "connecting move set ⇔ generating set of I_A" for sampling fibres. No source turns it into a general recipe for exact conditional tests across exponential families, incomplete-data models among them.
- The Diaconis–Gangolli moves and the Conti–Traverso normal-form argument are special cases or ingredients.
- **Flag:** an algebraist would call the lemma itself easy. The "⇒" direction is a corollary of Conti–Traverso 1991, and "⇐" is a short argument from the binomial spanning lemma.
- The novelty therefore lies in (a) the converse together with its precise formulation for sampling, and (b) the transfer. It does not lie in deep algebra.
- Verdict: **NOVEL**. A referee must see Conti–Traverso and Thomas credited for the normal-form and test-set mechanism.

### Applied Verdict: CONDITIONAL

- **Decision test.** It changes practice. Before this, someone running an exact conditional test on a log-linear model other than two-way independence had no general way to build a Markov chain that is guaranteed to be connected. Ad hoc moves risk a reducible chain and therefore an invalid p-value.
- **Practitioner hostility test.** "I already use the Diaconis–Gangolli swaps / the Mehta–Patel network algorithm." Answer: those cover two-way tables. For multi-way models, models with structural zeros or incomplete data, and logistic models, this method gives provably connecting moves where no standard method does.
- **Gaps.**
  - Computational evidence of feasibility is needed: Gröbner bases of toric ideals grow quickly with table size.
  - Connectivity guarantees an exact test only asymptotically. It gives no mixing-time bound.
  - No regime boundaries are given: for which model and table sizes is Buchberger tractable?
- PASS requires worked examples with computation times and test results. The seed promises examples, so the verdict is conditional on their content.

### Contribution Type Verdicts (mode=full only):

**Type 3 (unifying framework): PASS**
- Bar: method transfer from domain A to domain B, shown concretely.
- What transfers: Buchberger's algorithm and Gröbner-basis theory (commutative algebra, and integer programming via Conti–Traverso and Thomas) become a construction method for MCMC moves in conditional inference (statistics).
- This is not a resemblance. The technique produces new move sets for models where none was known (the seed's incomplete-data examples), and it guarantees connectivity, which the statistical side lacked.
- It also transfers back: statistical models become a supply of interesting toric ideals.

**Type 4 (vocabulary / infrastructure): PASS**
- The vocabulary is a connecting move set for all fibres of A (a "Markov basis", named later) treated as an algebraic object.
- Three statements that are clean in this vocabulary and were not stateable in pre-existing statistical vocabulary:
  1. Every discrete exponential family with integer sufficient statistic has a finite set of moves that connects all of its fibres.
  2. The minimal size and maximal degree of such a set are invariants of the model: they equal the minimal number of generators of I_A and their degrees. So "this model needs moves of degree d" is a well-posed question.
  3. Connectivity of a proposed move set can be decided by an ideal-membership computation (comparing ideals via Gröbner bases), with no fibre enumeration.
- The non-trivial result is the equivalence theorem itself, stated in (2).
- Caution: the abstract does not name the concept. Its Type-4 value depends on the paper making the move-set/ideal dictionary explicit.

Undeclared types:
- Type 1 is effectively claimed by "we show". It is treated under the Pure verdict.
- Type 7 is implicit in "algorithms for exact conditional tests". It is treated under the Applied verdict.

### Cross-reference

- Known ingredients, novel assembly and transfer:
  - the "Gröbner ⇒ connected" half is nearly a corollary of Conti–Traverso 1991;
  - the two-way example is Diaconis–Gangolli 1995.
- The contribution sits at the seam between integer-programming test sets, toric algebra and MCMC exact inference. Pistone–Wynn 1996 sits at a neighbouring seam (design), not this one.
- Type 3 is the load-bearing type. The Type 4 and Pure verdicts both rest on the same equivalence theorem.
- Applied value is real but conditional on demonstrated computability.

**Why-line (one line):** this is a bridge between a local and a global view. A fibre is connected by local moves without enumerating it centrally, and an algebraic certificate (generation of the ideal) is what guarantees that the local moves reach the whole. It is a lens only, and it does not change any bar.

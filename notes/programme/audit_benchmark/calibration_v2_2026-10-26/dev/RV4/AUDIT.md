## Audit: seed.md (dense graph sequences, limit object, reflection positivity) (mode: full)

**Audit date (AS_OF): 2004-08-11.** Only sources dated on or before 2004-08-11 count as prior art. The seed's own text is the submission. All web searches were run 2026-10-26. **Read level** for every source below is *search-result snippet / abstract page* (no full text read) unless stated otherwise.

### Declared contribution type(s): Type 3 (unifying framework), Type 4 (vocabulary / conceptual infrastructure)

The seed declares types but does not state the per-type bars it commits to (the framework's Phase-1 template). That is a minor readiness defect. The bars below are applied as the framework states them.

### Pure Verdict: NOVEL (with owned ingredients, and one serious overlap a referee will flag)

**Stripped statement.**
- (A) Existence: if t(F, G_n) converges for every finite F, there is a symmetric measurable W: [0,1]² → [0,1] with t(F, G_n) → t(F, W) for all F.
- (B) Characterisation: a graph parameter is such a limit iff it is normalised, multiplicative and reflection positive (connection matrices PSD).
- (C) Realisation: every W is a limit. The construction is the W-random graph G(n, W).
- (D) Consequences are indicated for property testing and extremal graph theory.

**Prior art found (all predate 2004-08-11).**
1. **Aldous (1981) and Hoover (1979/1982): representation of exchangeable arrays.** Every jointly exchangeable symmetric {0,1}-array on ℕ is a mixture of arrays generated by a symmetric measurable f on [0,1]² applied to i.i.d. uniforms. Kallenberg is credited too. *Read level: secondary-source snippets (Aldous talk slides; Austin survey, arXiv 0801/1301-era; DTIC report). Dates are taken from the reference lists; the primary 1979/1981 papers were not read.* **This is the main overlap.** Convergent subgraph densities define a consistent family of laws for the induced subgraph on k sampled vertices. That family gives an exchangeable random graph on ℕ. By Aldous–Hoover it is generated by some W, ergodic since the densities are deterministic limits. This yields (A), and essentially (C), by a route that needs no regularity lemma. The pre-2004 literature did not state this bridge as a graph-limit theorem, as far as I found. A hostile referee in probability would still call (A) "a reformulation of Aldous–Hoover" unless the seed credits it. **The seed does not mention exchangeability.**
2. **Freedman–Lovász–Schrijver, "Reflection positivity, rank connectivity, and homomorphism of graphs", arXiv math/0404468 (26 Apr 2004; MSR-TR-2004-41; later JAMS 20 (2007)).** It characterises hom(·, H) for finite weighted H by reflection positivity plus rank ≤ q^k. *Read level: abstract page.* (B) is the infinite-rank, normalised extension: the finite-rank condition is dropped and a measurable W replaces a finite H. The tool (connection matrices) is owned. Whether it extends without the rank bound is the new content.
3. **Frieze–Kannan, "Quick approximation to matrices and applications", Combinatorica 19 (1999) 175–220 (weak regularity, cut norm).** Also Szemerédi regularity (1978). *Read level: citations in later papers.* These supply the natural analytic route to (A): step-function approximations plus compactness.
4. **Chung–Graham–Wilson, "Quasi-random graphs", Combinatorica 9 (1989) 345–362 (PNAS announcement 1988); Thomason (1987).** *Read level: snippet.* This is the special case W ≡ p. The equivalence of subgraph-count and discrepancy conditions is the constant-limit instance of the theory.
5. **Borgs–Chayes–Lovász–Sós–Vesztergombi, "Counting graph homomorphisms".** The only version found is the 2006 book chapter, with the repository PDF headed Feb 2006. **I could not tell whether any version predates 2004-08-11**, so it is treated as not prior art.

6. **Erdős–Lovász–Spencer, "Strong independence of graphcopy functions", in *Graph Theory and Related Topics* (1979) 165–172.** It studies the vector of subgraph-copy fractions, and with it the set of its limit points (what a sequence of graphs can converge to, vector by vector). For connected H these are independent; disconnected H are asymptotically determined by connected ones. *Read level: abstract and Zentralblatt entry.* This owns the *vector-level* limit region. The seed's addition is a structural object (W) representing every point of that region, plus an intrinsic description of the region (reflection positivity).
7. **Property testing: Goldreich–Goldwasser–Ron, JACM 1998 (FOCS 1996); Alon–Fischer–Krivelevich–Szegedy, "Efficient testing of large graphs" (FOCS 1999 / Combinatorica 2000, venue/year not confirmed by search).** *Read level: abstract pages.* (D) must be positioned against these, since AFKS already moves toward a logical characterisation of testable properties.
8. Szemerédi regularity lemma (1978), Thomason (1987), Kallenberg's exchangeability work (pre-2004 papers) and Osterwalder–Schrader reflection positivity (1973–75): *cited from background knowledge, not consulted.* The Austin survey, the Simons tutorial and Kallenberg's 2005 book postdate 2004-08-11. They were used **only** to date the Aldous–Hoover papers, never as prior art.

No pre-2004-08-11 source found states (A)+(B) as a theorem about graph sequences, gives the reflection-positivity characterisation of limit parameters, or presents the W-random graph as the universal model for limits.

**Verdict.** NOVEL as a theorem package. (B) is the most clearly new part. (A) is new as stated but sits one bridge away from Aldous–Hoover, and the seed must cite that bridge. Without the citation, the independence/novelty framing of (A) is open to attack.

### Applied Verdict: not evaluated as a Type 7 claim (not declared)

Practitioner test, briefly, for (D). Property testing: "I already use regularity-based testers." The answer would be that W gives a canonical object in which testability can be phrased as continuity of a parameter. The seed only *indicates* this, so there is no demonstrated advantage yet. This is not a declared type and does not affect the recommendation.

### Contribution Type Verdicts (mode=full):

**Type 3 (Unifying framework): UNCLEAR.**
- Bar: method transfer, demonstrated concretely, not by analogy.
- Transfers the framework *would* enable:
  - (1) reflection positivity, from stat-physics partition functions, becomes a tool for density inequalities;
  - (2) extremal problems become optimisation over a compact function space;
  - (3) sampling (G(n, W)) and analysis act on one object, for property testing.
- These are genuine transfers in kind. But they were built by the auditor. The seed itself only says it will "indicate consequences". By the seed's own text, no technique has yet been shown to move from domain A to prove something in domain B.
- Becomes PASS once one consequence is derived as a theorem through W, e.g. a testability criterion beyond AFKS, or an extremal inequality proved via positivity.

**Type 4 (Vocabulary / conceptual infrastructure): PASS (narrow margin).**
Owned-region check first:
- Erdős–Lovász–Spencer 1979 already gives a vocabulary for the vector-level limit region. So "an asymptotic density inequality holds iff it holds on the limit region" is **not** new; it is stateable as "on the closure of graphcopy vectors".
- "Quasi-random iff W ≡ p" is just t(F, G_n) → p^{e(F)}, which is Chung–Graham–Wilson.
- Both are dropped as renamings.

Statements that need the new vocabulary:
1. "The limit region (ELS's closure) is exactly {t(·, W) : W symmetric measurable}, and equivalently exactly the normalised multiplicative reflection-positive parameters." This is the intrinsic description of a region ELS could only name extrinsically.
2. "Every point of the limit region is realised by a single explicit structured object, and G(n, W) converges to it." This gives a canonical representative and a sampling model attached to each limit point. On the probability side the generator exists (Aldous–Hoover f), but not as a representative of graph-sequence limits.
3. "For an extremal density problem, the extremum is attained by a limit object W with describable structure (e.g. a step function)." ELS vocabulary yields only a limit vector, not an object whose structure can be stated.

Non-trivial result: statement 1, theorem (B). It removes the finite-rank condition from Freedman–Lovász–Schrijver 2004.

Margin: statements 2 and 3 are partly shadowed (Aldous–Hoover; ELS plus compactness). The PASS rests chiefly on statement 1. A stricter auditor could call the pair 2/3 thin.

### Cross-reference

The components are owned: Aldous–Hoover representation, FLS reflection positivity (finite rank), Frieze–Kannan/Szemerédi regularity, and Chung–Graham–Wilson quasi-randomness. Erdős–Lovász–Spencer owns the vector-level limit region. The assembly is a new theorem package (Type 1, NOVEL). It clears Type 4 narrowly, *because* (B) is a new non-trivial theorem. Type 3 is UNCLEAR: the transfers are real in kind but not carried out in the seed. The single liability across all verdicts is the uncredited exchangeability bridge to (A).

### Recommendation

**Proceed to Phase 3**, scoped to Type 4 (plus Type 1 novelty of (B)). Type 3 stays UNCLEAR until a consequence is derived.

Owed before drafting:
1. Cite Aldous (1981), Hoover (1979) and Kallenberg, and state the exchangeability route to (A) explicitly. Either derive (A) from it, or position the analytic proof against it.
2. Cite Freedman–Lovász–Schrijver (arXiv math/0404468) and state precisely what is gained beyond finite rank. Cite Erdős–Lovász–Spencer 1979 for the limit region, and GGR/AFKS for (D).
3. Carry at least one consequence (testing or extremal) to an actual theorem, so the Type 3 transfer is demonstrated, not indicated.

**WHY line:** this is a clean instance of "local views → one world". Finite samples (subgraph densities: what small windows see) add up to one global object W exactly when a positivity condition holds. That is a model of local-to-global coherence with no centre. It is a lens, not a contribution to the programme.

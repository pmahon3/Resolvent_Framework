### Pure Verdict: UNCLEAR, with a correctness flag on the central claim

**Stripped statement.** There is an absolute constant D such that, for every n ≥ 2 and every arithmetic discrete reflection group Γ < Isom(Hⁿ) with finite-volume Coxeter polytope, the totally real ground field k of Γ has [k:Q] ≤ D.

**What was owned on 2004-07-18:**
- **Very large dimension.** Vinberg proved that arithmetic reflection groups do not exist in large dimension (n ≥ 30). Source: Vinberg, "Absence of crystallographic groups of reflections in Lobachevsky spaces of large dimension", Funct. Anal. Appl. 15:2 (1981), announcement; full paper Trudy MMO 47 (1984) 68–102, Trans. Moscow Math. Soc. 1985, 75–112. *Read level: bibliographic records (mathnet / mathdoc) and secondary descriptions, 2026-10-26; primary text not read.* For every n ≥ 30 the statement is vacuous.
- **Large degree, dimension > 15.** Nikulin proved that arithmetic reflection groups do not exist when n > 15 and the ground-field degree is large enough. Source: Nikulin, "On the classification of arithmetic groups generated by reflections in Lobachevsky spaces", Izv. AN SSSR Ser. Mat. 45:1 (1981) 113–142 (received 08.07.1980); Math. USSR-Izv. 18:1 (1982) 99–123. *Read level: mathnet abstract, 2026-10-26.* So a degree bound in dimensions 16–29 is owned. Nikulin may have pushed the threshold lower (for example to n ≥ 10), but this audit could not confirm that from a pre-2004 primary, because the "n ≥ 10" figure came from a post-2004 source that was discarded.
- **Fixed dimension and fixed degree.** Nikulin 1980/1981 gives finiteness of maximal arithmetic reflection groups for fixed n and fixed degree. *Read level: mathnet abstracts and records, 2026-10-26.*
- **The seed's first ingredient is standard.** The lower bound on covolume from the arithmetic volume formula, growing with the discriminant and hence with the degree, is Prasad's volume formula with Borel–Prasad. Sources: Prasad, Publ. Math. IHÉS 69 (1989); Borel–Prasad, Publ. Math. IHÉS 69 (1989) ("Finiteness theorems for discrete subgroups of bounded covolume"). *Read level: from memory, not fetched this session.* Borel 1981 (Ann. Sc. Norm. Pisa) covers dimensions 2–3.
- **The second ingredient is standard too.** Facet-count and face-averaging bounds for Coxeter polytopes go back to Vinberg 1984 (the same paper), Khovanskii (Funct. Anal. Appl. 1986) and Prokhorov (Izv. 1986; nonexistence of finite-volume Coxeter polytopes in dimension ≥ 996). *Read level: from memory, not fetched this session.*

**What was open on 2004-07-18:** a degree bound in the low dimensions, at least 2 ≤ n ≤ 15 on what the primaries above confirm. The seed's novelty therefore lives entirely in low dimension, and low dimension is exactly where the sketched method has a gap.

**Correctness problem (⟦HAND⟧, auditor's own check):**
- The proof sketch pairs a covolume lower bound with a facet bound. A facet bound gives an upper bound on covolume (in H², area of a Coxeter k-gon ≤ (k−2)π). Comparing it with the lower bound, which grows with degree, then bounds the degree.
- The pairing only works if the number of facets is bounded independently of the group. In H² it is not: Coxeter polygons with arbitrarily many sides exist, for example right-angled k-gons for every k ≥ 5. In H³ Andreev's theorem likewise gives right-angled Coxeter polyhedra with arbitrarily many faces.
- The known facet bounds of Vinberg, Khovanskii and Prokhorov are averaging statements. They bound the *dimension*, or force short faces. They do not bound the facet count in a fixed low dimension.
- For arithmetic Coxeter polytopes in dimensions 2–3, a uniform facet bound is essentially as hard as the theorem itself: bounded facets give bounded covolume, which gives finitely many fields by Borel–Prasad.
- The seed names no ingredient that supplies this bound, such as spectral, arithmetic or combinatorial input that is special to arithmetic groups. As stated, the argument is either circular or incomplete in exactly the dimensions where the claim would be new.
- This is not a proof that the statement is false. It shows the central claim is **unverifiable from the submission**, and the sketched method does not visibly work there.

**Verdict: UNCLEAR.**
- In high dimension the statement is known (Vinberg; Nikulin, n > 15).
- In low dimension it is not owned in the pre-2004 literature found here, so it would be new if proved.
- The method given does not establish it, so the claim cannot be credited.

**Not determinable:** whether some pre-2004 source (for example Vinberg's Uspekhi survey "Hyperbolic reflection groups", 1985, or Vinberg–Shvartsman in the Encyclopaedia of Math. Sciences 29, 1993) states the low-dimensional degree question as open or settles any part of it. Neither was read.

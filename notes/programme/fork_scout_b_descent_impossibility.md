## Which curiosity this serves

**Researcher (b) — the incompatibility theorist.** What grips (b): some
observations can't be made together, and classical probability has no room for
it. Satisfaction = understanding the structure of incompatibility itself — when
it forces you out of the Boolean world, what survives, what "probability" means on
a non-distributive logic. The discriminating sentence (genealogy line 209): *"When
observations can't be jointly performed, the logic of events stops being Boolean —
and here's what happens to probability."* For (b) the deep result is the descent
question, and **nothing here is done.**

## FIRST: clear away the dead frame ("prove impossibility")

The L_MO₂ kill tempts a narrative — *"every concrete probe vindicates 'richness
starves concreteness', so the real answer is an impossibility theorem."* **That
narrative is wrong, and the audit's own findings refute it.** Three readings of
"impossibility," all dead:

1. *No concrete σ-ortho non-Boolean OML with (★)* — **already FALSE**: plain
   `∏ₙ MO₂` is exactly that (the audit's triviality finding).
2. *Subadditive states force Boolean* — **already PROVED**: Pták–Pulmannová 1994
   (the audit's tertiary finding).
3. *No point-free σ-additive probability on a non-distributive lattice* — L(H) +
   Gleason is a standing counterexample *candidate*, so likely false too.

In every reading the impossibility is refuted or published. There is no fresh
theorem in "go prove it can't exist."

## The actual confusion the kill exposed — concrete vs. point-free

The L_MO₂ kill eliminated a **concrete** (point-rich: separating 2-valued states)
object that turned out trivial. But the genuine (b) prize — per genealogy
lines 174–185 and the survey's **Remark 5.2** — is **point-free**: PR_lattice
holds, PR_dual fails, *few or no* 2-valued states (non-concrete). **These are
opposite kinds of object.**

- `∏ₙ MO₂`: separating 2-valued states (point-RICH) — audit confirms concreteness
  via coordinate homomorphisms.
- The (b) target: σ-additive measure on the lattice with NO descent to dual points
  (point-POOR / point-free).

⇒ **The kill is not evidence about the (b) prize.** It removed a point-rich object;
the prize is point-poor. The "data points vindicate impossibility" story collapses
once you see the kill and the prize live on opposite sides of point-richness.
(This non-result is itself a clarification worth keeping: the search for a
*concrete* witness was always looking in the wrong half.)

## The live (b) question (classification, not impossibility)

> **Is L(H) + Gleason a genuine point-free, non-distributive, σ-additive
> probability — or does the Remark 5.2 (A)/(B) equivocation disqualify it? And if
> it counts, is it essentially UNIQUE?**

This is where (b) is alive if it is alive anywhere. The Remark 5.2 tension, stated:
- Gleason gives a σ-additive measure on L(H) (PR_lattice ✓).
- But (Prop 3.2) no state extends to a charge on the dual S₀(L(H)), so none
  concentrates on the dual points P(A) (PR_dual ✗).
- AND Gleason is built FROM the Hilbert rays (point-space (A)) and is the most
  point-FUL result available — it reduces every lattice state to the density `ρ`.

So does L(H)+Gleason *witness* point-free non-distributive probability, or is it
secretly point-ful (via (A)) and thus disqualified as the (b) prize? **Resolving
that dichotomy is move 1.** It is a conceptual/structural question with a definite
answer, not an open-ended hunt.

## The next moves (note: move 1 is "fix the question")

1. **Resolve Remark 5.2.** Pin down precisely which point-space the (b) prize
   forbids concentration on, and decide whether L(H)+Gleason satisfies the
   point-free criterion under that definition or is excluded by the (A)-dependence.
   Until this is settled the (b) question is not even well-posed.
2. **If L(H)+Gleason counts:** is it unique? Is every point-free non-distributive
   σ-additive probability essentially L(H)+Gleason (a representation/classification
   theorem), or are there others? Uniqueness would be the real (b) result — a
   structural characterization, not an example.
3. **If L(H)+Gleason is disqualified:** then the (b) prize is genuinely vacant, and
   the question becomes existence: construct ANY point-free (non-concrete)
   non-distributive σ-additive probability, or prove none exists. THIS is where a
   real impossibility theorem could finally have a target — but only after move 1
   shows the existing candidate doesn't count. (Cf. survey §6 Q1: a
   Loomis–Sikorski / σ-Stone duality for a concrete non-Boolean σ-complete OML, or
   a proof none exists — the join-preservation obstruction §2.3, which Prop 3.2
   does not reach.)

## What would KILL this path

- Move 1 resolves "L(H)+Gleason DOES count" AND move 2 resolves "and it's unique" —
  then the (b) prize is *delivered by L(H)+Gleason alone*, which is exactly the
  "Gleason already did it" worry; (b) collapses into known quantum-probability.
- Move 1 resolves "disqualified" AND the existence question (move 3) reduces to
  the join-preservation obstruction that already killed RDP and MacNeille routes
  (survey §4.3) — i.e. the same rock, a 7th death.

## Honest read — and the asymmetry the fork should not launder

question* (Remark 5.2), where (a) already has a sharp, solo-checkable, unsolved
one (`Clop(βYₙ)` σ-complete?). Writing the two scouts as symmetric "next 2–3

More: the **repeated collapse of every (b) candidate** — L_MO₂ to triviality, the

bearing on the (a)/(b) decision.** It does not prove (b) is empty (the point-free

make well-posed, repeatedly, while (a)'s open question is crisp. If the pull toward
(b) is real curiosity, that is a good reason to push move 1. If it is momentum /
sunk cost / "last thread standing," the pattern of collapses is the warning the
genealogy asked us to record (lines 233–241).

\section*{Why this plan exists}

\noindent This is a \emph{technique-acquisition} plan, companion to the broader
\texttt{sigma\_essential\_skill\_plan} (which sequences descriptive set theory and
large cardinals). It is narrower and pointed at one route.

The $\sigma$-essential import sweep closed every \emph{existing} object one could
borrow as the carrier's skeleton: each dies either Boolean-ambient (rescued by a
non-Dirac $\sigma$-state) or operator-algebraic (non-concrete by Kochen--Specker).
The one surviving \emph{lead} --- not a solution, a place to learn --- is the family
of \textbf{realization theorems}. Per the prior-art note
(\texttt{czech\_school\_prior\_art\_sigma\_essential.md}, \S POSITIVE), these build
\emph{non-simplex} (hence contextual, non-segregated) state spaces on orthomodular
lattices by \emph{pasting Boolean blocks} --- a combinatorial, non-operator-algebraic
construction, so the orthocomplement is block-inherited and the route dodges the
polarity/form horn that kills matroids and geometric lattices.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The one crux --- read every source against it.} The realization
theorems in the literature are \emph{finite-pasting} constructions and realize the
\emph{full (finitely additive)} state space. They fail the $\sigma$-essential target
on a single \textbf{conjunction gap}:
\begin{quote}
\emph{Why must the pasting be finite, and can an infinite pasting realize a
non-simplex state space on an OML that is \textbf{simultaneously} concrete and
$\sigma$-complete?}
\end{quote}
No located paper does this (prior-art note, \S POSITIVE). That conjunction --- concrete
$+$ $\sigma$-complete $+$ non-simplex-surviving-restriction-to-$\sigma$-states --- is
exactly the open cell. Do not read these sources for ``realization theory'' in
general; read each only for how it bears on the conjunction gap.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Citation caveat.} The realization papers below are \textbf{not yet in
the repo bib}; venue/year/page are reconstructed from the prior-art note and are marked
\textup{[cite unverified]}. Verify against the primary source before relying on any
detail. Claims about paper \emph{content} here are the prior-art note's summary, not a
reading of the papers themselves.

\section*{What is in the repo library (checked 2026-07)}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Harding 2004, ``Remarks on Concrete Orthomodular Lattices''}
    (\texttt{harding\_2004\_concrete\_oml.pdf}, 20pp) --- \textbf{IN HAND; read this
    first.} Primary source on Kalmbach's $K(L)$ and the concreteness half. Read (this
    session) confirms: concrete $\equiv$ full set of two-valued states (Godowski 1981,
    $=$ our C1 exactly); $K(L)$ ``glues the Boolean algebras generated by the chains of
    $L$'' and \emph{every $K(L)$ is concrete} (Mayet--Navara 1995) with orthocomplement
    $=$ set-complement, \textbf{block-inherited, not form-derived} --- so the
    polarity/form horn is dodged, confirmed from a primary source. \textbf{But $K(L)$ is
    finitary} (finite orthogonal joins; no $\sigma$-completeness): this \emph{is} the
    finiteness dependency the plan hunts, now located. The paper's own reframe: $K(L)$
    forms \emph{do not generate} the variety of all concrete OMLs, so a concrete
    $\sigma$-witness \textbf{need not be a paste at all} (see the impossibility-twin
    caveat below).
  \item \textbf{Navara 1992, ``Regularity and $\sigma$-additivity of states''}
    (\texttt{navara\_1992.pdf}, 3pp) --- IN HAND; step-4-adjacent ($\sigma$-additivity of
    states, the regularity kill).
  \item \textbf{M\"uller 1993, ``Jauch--Piron States on Concrete Quantum Logics''}
    (\texttt{muller\_1993.pdf}) --- IN HAND; a concrete non-Boolean example (JP-focused,
    not a $\sigma$-essential witness; useful as a concrete-logic specimen).
  \item \textbf{Pt\'ak--Pulmannov\'a 1994} (\texttt{ptak\_pulmannova\_1994.pdf}, 5pp) is
    the \emph{Floor paper} (subadditive measure $\Rightarrow$ Boolean), \textbf{not} the
    textbook.
  \item \textbf{To fetch:} \textbf{Kalmbach, \emph{Orthomodular Lattices}} (1983) --- the
    textbook for the pasting mechanics (\S1 below); Navara--Rogalewicz, Harding--Navara,
    Mayet--Navara 1995, Navara--R\"uttimann 1991 (\S\S2--4 below).
\end{itemize}

\section{Foundations: how block-pasting builds an OML}

\noindent\textbf{Kalmbach, \emph{Orthomodular Lattices}} (1983) \textup{[to fetch]}, with
Harding 2004 (in hand) as the concrete-side entry point, and Pt\'ak--Pulmannov\'a
\emph{Orthomodular Structures as Quantum Logics}~\cite{PtakPulmannova1991} \textup{[the
book, to fetch --- distinct from the 5pp Floor paper in the library]} for the
concrete-logic side. The pasting construction and horizontal sums.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item State the pasting of a family of Boolean blocks precisely: how blocks overlap,
    what the resulting order and orthocomplement are. \emph{Confirm} the orthocomplement
    is inherited block-wise, not derived from any form --- this is why the route dodges
    the scout's polarity horn.
  \item Where does the loop / commutativity condition force the paste to be an OMP
    rather than a lattice? (This is one place ``infinite non-segregated pasting'' can
    silently fail before $\sigma$ even enters.)
  \item Contrast a \emph{segregated} paste (horizontal sum, contextuality on a central
    factor --- $\prod_n MO_2$, $MO_\kappa$) with a non-segregated one. What structural
    feature makes the incompatibility non-central? The target needs the latter.
\end{itemize}

\section{The realization theorems (read for: where finiteness enters)}

\noindent\textbf{Navara--Rogalewicz, ``The pasting constructions for orthomodular
posets,'' Demonstratio Math.\ (1988)} \textup{[cite unverified]}. The base realization:
every (compact convex) set arises as a state space of a pasted OMP.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Read for the \emph{exact step} where finiteness of the pasting is used to realize
    a non-simplex state space. Which part of the argument breaks if the family of blocks
    --- or an individual block --- is infinite?
  \item The realized object is the \emph{full, finitely additive} state space. Is there
    any control on $\sigma$-additive states? (Prior-art note: no --- this is the
    unaddressed point.)
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Harding--Navara, ``Subalgebras of orthomodular lattices,'' Order 17
(2000)} \textup{[cite unverified]}. The strengthened realization: prescribed center and
automorphism group.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item ``Prescribed center'' is the lever for \emph{non-segregation}: a trivial (or
    small) center forces contextuality off any central Boolean factor. Read for how the
    center is controlled, and whether that control survives an infinite paste.
  \item Same finiteness question as above: locate the finite-pasting dependency.
\end{itemize}

\section{The concreteness half (read for: what buys set-representability)}

\noindent\textbf{Mayet--Navara (1995)} \textup{[cite unverified]}. Concreteness for
pasted OMLs holds only for special forms (prior-art note names $K(L)$).
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item What is the special form that yields a set-representable (concrete) paste, and is
    it compatible with the \emph{infinite} pasting the $\sigma$-side needs? Concrete
    $+$ $\sigma$ together is the whole gap; this is the paper on the concreteness half.
  \item Concreteness $=$ enough two-valued states to separate points ($a \mapsto \{s :
    s(a)=1\}$ is an embedding). Does the $K(L)$ form preserve a separating supply of
    \emph{two-valued} states, or only states in general?
\end{itemize}

\section{The $\sigma$-boundary (read for: what survives restriction to $\sigma$-states)}

\noindent\textbf{Navara--R\"uttimann, ``A characterization of $\sigma$-state spaces of
orthomodular lattices,'' Expo.\ Math.\ 9 (1991) 275--284} \textup{[cite unverified; NOT
in repo bib --- the existing \texttt{Ruttimann1994} key is a different R\"uttimann paper
(``Weakly purely finitely additive measures,'' Canad.\ J.\ Math.\ 46), do not conflate]}.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item The theorem places $S_\sigma(P)$ as a \emph{semi-exposed face} of the full state
    space $S(P)$ (per Pt\'ak's zbMATH review). Read for: does a \emph{non-simplex} full
    state space, restricted to this face, stay non-simplex? A face of a non-simplex set
    need not be a simplex --- so this is the gate the realized non-simplex must pass to
    remain contextual for \emph{$\sigma$}-additive states.
  \item This is the precise juncture where the finite-pasting realization would have to be
    shown to survive $\sigma$-restriction. It is the mathematical heart of the push.
\end{itemize}

\section{The impossibility twin (one entry --- do not over-invest)}

\noindent The same $\sigma$-state-space characterization
(Navara--R\"uttimann 1991) is also the tool for the \emph{negative}
attempt: if the semi-exposed face is forced to be a simplex with dispersion-free
vertices on every concrete $\sigma$-complete OML, the cell is empty. The prior-art note
records that the verbatim theorem does \emph{not} force this --- so the emptiness proof,
if it exists, needs more. Read the characterization once for both directions; the
new-world and impossibility branches stay indistinguishable until one is worked.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Caveat raising the impossibility bar (Harding 2004).} Since $K(L)$ forms
do not generate the variety of all concrete OMLs, an impossibility proof \textbf{cannot}
be won by showing ``no \emph{paste} is $\sigma$-essential'' --- non-paste concrete OMLs
exist, and any of them could carry the witness. A $\neg\Psi$ argument must range over
\emph{all} concrete $\sigma$-complete OMLs, not just Kalmbach constructions. (This does
not escape the construct-vs-assume fork: a non-paste concrete OML is still cut out by
\emph{some} disjoint-union-closure rule on $\mathcal{P}(\Omega)$, to which the fork
applies.)

\section*{The order}

\noindent Kalmbach/Pt\'ak--Pulmannov\'a (pasting mechanics) $\to$
Navara--Rogalewicz then Harding--Navara (locate the finiteness dependency) $\to$
Mayet--Navara (concreteness form) $\to$ Navara--R\"uttimann (the $\sigma$-restriction
gate). The push, if it works, is a single new theorem: an \emph{infinite}, concrete,
$\sigma$-complete paste whose non-simplex state space survives restriction to
$\sigma$-additive states. Everything above is read to find where that theorem must do
its work, and where the finite-pasting literature stops.

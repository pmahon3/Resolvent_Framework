## 1. The lemma (five lines, BPI only)

Let `L ⊆ P(Ω)` be a concrete σ-class OML, states topologized in `{0,1}^L`
(pointwise = the programme's Φ topology). For a two-valued finitely
additive state `s`, TFAE:

1. `s` lies in the closure of the point-evaluation states;
2. `s` extends to a finitely additive 0–1 measure on the Boolean field
   generated by `L` (equivalently, to an ultrafilter on `Ω`);
3. the value-1 family `{A : s(A)=1}` has the finite intersection property.

*(3⟹2)* FIP gives a proper filter; extend by BPI; value-0 events are
complements of value-1 events, so the ultrafilter restricts to `s`.
*(2⟹1)* any finite constraint set `{u(A_i)=1, u(B_j)=0}` has
`u(⋂A_i ∖ ⋃B_j) = 1 ≠ ∅`, so a witnessing point exists. *(1⟹3)* a single
net element deep enough realizes any finite value-1 subfamily at one
point. Pairwise intersection of value-1 events is automatic (disjoint
events are orthogonal), so FIP failure starts at triples.

Almost certainly classical (Stone-duality folklore) — no novelty claim on
the lemma itself; E3 pins attribution.

- **Layer separation:** the two inclusions are the banked factoring —

- **Rep-relativity caveat (load-bearing):** FIP of the value-1 family is

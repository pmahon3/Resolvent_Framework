\begin{abstract}
De Finetti argued that finite additivity is the genuine coherence requirement for
probability, and that $\sigma$-additivity is additional mathematical baggage.  He was
right about isolated observers: no finitary condition stateable on a single Boolean
algebra $\E$ forces continuity at $\emptyset$, and exhaustiveness --- the natural
first candidate --- is trivially satisfied by every normalized finitely-additive
valuation.

This paper asks whether an observer \emph{embedded in a system} changes the answer.
An observer who inhabits a query system --- a directed family of observers at different
resolutions, all measuring the same world --- is not isolated.  The system's coherence
conditions may do what finite additivity alone cannot.

We show two things.  First, the gap $\sigE \setminus \E$ is not a defect: an observer
with no gap admits no nontrivial refinement and has no horizon.  Incompleteness is
constitutive of discriminability.  Second, under sequential upper-directedness, finer
queries witness what coarser ones cannot, and compatibility propagates that witnessing
back down.  The central claim --- the witnessing conjecture --- is that this systemic
coherence forces continuity at $\emptyset$ at every finite level, making
$\sigma$-additivity a theorem about what it means to be embedded in a coherent world
rather than an assumption about a single agent's beliefs.
\end{abstract}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Introduction}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

An observer makes distinctions.  The natural model is a Boolean algebra $\E$ of
distinguishable events with a finitely-additive valuation $\ell : \E \to [0,1]$.
The standard move is to pass to $\sigE$ and extend $\ell$ to a $\sigma$-additive
measure.  But this extension requires a condition --- continuity at $\emptyset$ ---
that is not implicit in finite additivity.  De Finetti's position was that this
condition is not a logical requirement of rational belief but an additional
mathematical convenience~\cite{deFinetti1974}, and he was right: no finitary coherence
condition on $\E$ alone forces it.

This paper asks a different question.  An observer who inhabits a \emph{system} of
observers at different resolutions --- a query system, with a refinement order and
compatible marginals --- is not the isolated agent de Finetti had in mind.  The
question is whether the system's coherence conditions supply the extension condition
as a theorem.  The extension criterion (Theorem~\ref{thm:extension-criterion})
identifies exactly what is needed: continuity at $\emptyset$ is $\sigma$-continuity
in the sense of Daniell--Stone~\cite{Daniell1918,Stone1948}, and it is necessary and
sufficient for unique $\sigma$-additive extension.  The question is where it comes from.

The paper establishes two results.  The negative result (Section~\ref{sec:negative})
confirms de Finetti at the level of any single observer: the regress is structural,
and no chain of finite refinements resolves it.  The incompleteness result
(Section~\ref{sec:incompleteness}) shows the gap $\sigE \setminus \E$ is necessary:
an observer with $\E_Q = \sigE_Q$ admits no nontrivial refinement and cannot be
embedded in any interesting query system.  The positive result
(Section~\ref{sec:positive}) is that sequential upper-directedness --- every sequence
of queries has a common refinement --- provides a witnessing structure: finer queries
see what coarser ones cannot, and compatibility propagates continuity at $\emptyset$
back down.  The witnessing conjecture (Conjecture~\ref{conj:witnessing}) is that this
is always sufficient.

Section~\ref{sec:grounding} draws the foundational conclusions.

\begin{aside}
\paragraph{The constitutive outside.}
The gap $\sigE \setminus \E$ admits a philosophical reading as the \emph{constitutive
outside} of an observer.  This concept has a broad tradition in post-structuralist
thought: that identity is constituted not only by what a thing \emph{is} but equally
by what it excludes.  The idea originates in Derrida's analysis of difference and the
supplement~\cite{Derrida1967} --- the logic by which a presence is always already
constituted by what it defers and excludes --- and has been developed through Hall's
account of cultural identity as formed at its boundaries, and Laclau's explicit
formulation of the constitutive outside in political theory~\cite{Laclau1990}.  The
structural core --- that an entity's boundary is not an accidental limit but the very
condition of its determinacy --- is what is carried into the mathematical setting.  An
observer whose Boolean algebra coincides with its $\sigma$-closure has no boundary
and therefore no horizon: the gap $\sigE \setminus \E$ is not a deficiency but the
condition that makes the observer a discriminating agent rather than a terminus.

This framing is provisional.  The goal is a fully mathematical expression of the
same structural fact, at which point this philosophical scaffold can be set aside.
\end{aside}

\subsection{Organisation}

Section~\ref{sec:setup} sets up the single-observer case: a Boolean algebra $\E$ of
distinctions and a finitely-additive valuation $\ell$ on it, with no topology and no
$\sigma$-algebra assumed.

Section~\ref{sec:negative} establishes the negative result: exhaustiveness is trivial,
the real obstruction is continuity at $\emptyset$ for sequences escaping $\E$, and no
finitary condition on any single level can supply this.

Section~\ref{sec:incompleteness} establishes the necessity of incompleteness: an
observer with $\E_Q = \sigE_Q$ admits no genuine refinement.

Section~\ref{sec:positive} establishes the positive result: sequential upper-directedness
provides witnessing from above, and compatibility propagates it back down.

Section~\ref{sec:grounding} draws the foundational conclusions: the $\sigma$-algebra as
commitment rather than assumption, and the structural hypotheses as expressions of
observational faith rather than technical conditions.

\section{The single-observer setup}
\label{sec:setup}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

We work initially with a single observer at a single level, stripping away the query
system structure to isolate the core question.

\begin{definition}[Discriminability structure]
A \emph{discriminability structure} is a pair $(\E, \ell)$ where:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item $\E$ is a Boolean algebra of subsets of some set $O$ --- closed under finite
    union, intersection, and complementation, containing $\emptyset$ and $O$.
  \item $\ell : \E \to [0,1]$ is a normalized finitely-additive valuation:
    $\ell(\emptyset) = 0$, $\ell(O) = 1$, and $\ell(A \cup B) = \ell(A) + \ell(B)$
    whenever $A \cap B = \emptyset$.
\end{enumerate}
This is equivalently a finitely-additive probability space in the sense of de
Finetti~\cite{deFinetti1974}: $O$ is the outcome space, $\E$ makes explicit the
observer's event language (the distinctions they can currently draw), and $\ell$
is their coherent probability assignment on that language.
\end{definition}

\noindent No $\sigma$-algebra is assumed on $O$.  No topology is assumed.  The only
structure is what the observer can currently distinguish ($\E$) and how they weight
those distinctions ($\ell$).

\begin{definition}[The gap]
\label{def:gap}
For a discriminability structure $(\E, \ell)$, the \emph{gap} is the set
$\sigE \setminus \E$ --- events in the $\sigma$-algebra generated by $\E$ that are
not themselves in $\E$.
\end{definition}

\noindent The gap consists of events that are \emph{in principle determinable} from
countable Boolean operations on $\E$ but are not \emph{currently distinguishable} by
the observer.  It is the observer's horizon: the limit of what their distinctions
approach but do not reach.

\begin{definition}[Extension]
We say $\ell$ \emph{extends} to $\sigE$ if there exists a $\sigma$-additive measure
$\mu$ on $\sigE$ with $\mu|_\E = \ell$.  The extension is \emph{unique} if any two
such $\mu$ agree on all of $\sigE$.
\end{definition}

\noindent The central question of this paper is: what properties of $(\E, \ell)$ are
necessary and sufficient for such an extension to exist and be unique?

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{The negative result: finitary conditions cannot suffice}
\label{sec:negative}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\subsection{Exhaustiveness is trivial}

The first natural candidate for a witnessable extension condition is exhaustiveness.

\begin{definition}[Exhaustiveness]
A finitely-additive valuation $\ell : \E \to [0,1]$ is \emph{exhaustive} if
$\ell(E_n) \to 0$ for every disjoint sequence $(E_n)_{n \geq 1} \subset \E$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:exhaustiveness-trivial}
Every normalized finitely-additive valuation on a Boolean algebra is exhaustive.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $(E_n)$ be a disjoint sequence in $\E$.  For any $N$,
$\sum_{n=1}^N \ell(E_n) = \ell\bigl(\bigcup_{n=1}^N E_n\bigr) \leq \ell(O) = 1$
by finite additivity and normalization.  Hence $\sum_{n=1}^\infty \ell(E_n) \leq 1$,
so $\ell(E_n) \to 0$.
\end{proof}

\noindent Exhaustiveness carries no information about extensibility.  It is a
consequence of normalization alone, independent of any coherence condition or
structural hypothesis.

\subsection{The real obstruction}

The correct extension condition is continuity from above at $\emptyset$.

\begin{definition}[Continuity at $\emptyset$]
\label{def:cont-empty}
A finitely-additive valuation $\ell : \E \to [0,1]$ is \emph{continuous at
$\emptyset$} if: for every decreasing sequence $E_1 \supseteq E_2 \supseteq \cdots$
in $\E$ with $\bigcap_{n=1}^\infty E_n = \emptyset$ in $\sigE$, we have $\ell(E_n)
\to 0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
The intersection $\bigcap_n E_n$ in Definition~\ref{def:cont-empty} is taken in
$\sigE$, not in $\E$.  This is the essential point.  The condition requires the
observer to assign vanishing weight to sequences that converge to an event they
\emph{cannot directly see} --- an event in the gap $\sigE \setminus \E$.

This is where the philosophical content lives: the observer must be coherent about
events outside their language.  The condition is not checkable from within $\E$ alone.
To verify $\bigcap_n E_n = \emptyset$ in $\sigE$, the observer needs access to
$\sigE$ --- which is precisely what they do not have.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Extension criterion]
\label{thm:extension-criterion}
A normalized finitely-additive $\ell : \E \to [0,1]$ extends to a $\sigma$-additive
measure on $\sigE$ if and only if $\ell$ is continuous at $\emptyset$.  The extension
is then unique.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Standard: see Halmos~\cite{Halmos1950} or Fremlin~\cite{Fremlin2003} Vol.~1.
The forward direction is immediate from $\sigma$-additivity of the extension.
For the converse, continuity at $\emptyset$ is precisely the condition used in the
standard proof of the Carath\'{e}odory extension to pass from finite additivity to
$\sigma$-additivity on the generated $\sigma$-algebra.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The regress}

In a query system, one might hope that a finer query $Q' \succeq Q$ can witness
the emptiness that $Q$ cannot see, and that compatibility then forces continuity at
$\emptyset$ at level $Q$.

\begin{proposition}[The regress]
\label{prop:regress}
Let $Q' \succeq Q$ with refinement map $\pi : O_{Q'} \to O_Q$.  Suppose
$E_1 \supseteq E_2 \supseteq \cdots$ in $\E_Q$ with $\bigcap_n \pi^{-1}(E_n) =
\emptyset$ in $\E_{Q'}$.  Compatibility gives $\ell_Q(E_n) = \ell_{Q'}(\pi^{-1}(E_n))$.
But this does not force $\ell_{Q'}(\pi^{-1}(E_n)) \to 0$: the same obstruction
reappears at level $Q'$, since finite additivity on $\E_{Q'}$ alone does not imply
continuity at $\emptyset$ for sequences decreasing to an element of $\E_{Q'}$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent The regress is structural.  Pushing the witnessing one level up does not
resolve it: it only relocates it.  No finite chain of refinements can supply continuity
at $\emptyset$ from purely finitary conditions.

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:no-finitary}
No condition stateable purely in terms of the Boolean algebra structure of $\E_Q$ and
the finitely-additive valuations $\{\ell_{Q'}\}_{Q' \succeq Q}$ at finitely many
levels is necessary and sufficient for $\ell_Q$ to extend to $\sigE_Q$.
\end{corollary}

\noindent Something external to any single level is required.  The three candidates
are: topology (compactness witnesses emptiness), a limit query (a colimit in the
directed system whose event algebra contains the limit events), or an explicit
continuity axiom (honest but circular).  The remainder of this paper develops the
limit query option, which is the most intrinsic to the observer-system framework.

\section{The necessity of incompleteness}
\label{sec:incompleteness}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Before turning to the positive result, we establish that the gap is not a defect
to be eliminated.

\begin{definition}[Nontrivial refinement]
\label{def:nontrivial}
A refinement $Q' \succ Q$ (strict) is \emph{nontrivial} if there exists
$A' \in \E_{Q'}$ such that $\pi^{-1}(\pi(A')) \neq A'$ --- that is, the refinement
map does not preserve all distinctions in $\E_{Q'}$ back to $\E_Q$.  Equivalently,
$Q'$ makes distinctions that $Q$ cannot.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Discriminability requires incompleteness]
\label{thm:incompleteness}
Let $Q$ be a query admitting at least one nontrivial refinement $Q' \succ Q$.
Then $\E_Q \subsetneq \sigE_Q$: the gap is nonempty.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since $Q' \succ Q$ is nontrivial, there exists $A' \in \E_{Q'}$ with
$\pi^{-1}(\pi(A')) \neq A'$.  Let $D = A' \setminus \pi^{-1}(\pi(A'))$ --- a
nonempty set in $\E_{Q'}$ that maps to the same coarse event as part of $A'$ but
is genuinely finer.  The preimage $\pi^{-1}(B)$ for any $B \in \E_Q$ is a union
of level-$Q'$ atoms, hence cannot separate $D$ from its complement within
$\pi^{-1}(\pi(A'))$.  It follows that the event $\pi^{-1}(\pi(A'))$ in $\E_Q$,
when pulled back, conflates distinctions that $\E_{Q'}$ separates.  The sequence
of such distinctions generates events in $\sigE_Q$ --- specifically, the intersection
of the coarser level events --- that are not in $\E_Q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:incompleteness} says: if the observer can be refined, they must
have a gap.  The contrapositive is equally important: an observer with no gap ---
one for whom $\E_Q = \sigE_Q$ --- cannot be nontrivially refined.  Such an observer
sees everything that any finer query could show them.  They have no horizon.

In the language of a query system, such an observer would make all refinements trivial:
$\E_{Q'} = \pi^{-1}(\E_Q)$ for all $Q' \succeq Q$.  The refinement order above
$Q$ would collapse.  This is not a query system in any interesting sense.

The gap $\sigE_Q \setminus \E_Q$ is therefore not a residue of an incomplete
construction.  It is the \emph{constitutive outside} of the observer: the space
into which their refinements reach, the horizon that orients them toward finer
distinctions.  Without it they are not an observer but a terminus.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}
In any query system with a genuine (nontrivial, infinite) refinement order, every
observer at a finite level $Q$ has a nonempty gap.  Discriminability is necessarily
incomplete at every finite level.
\end{corollary}

\begin{aside}
\paragraph{The tetralemma and the middle way.}
The structure of the argument across Sections~\ref{sec:negative}
and~\ref{sec:incompleteness} has the form of N\=ag\=arjuna's
\emph{catu\d{s}ko\d{t}i} (tetralemma), the four-cornered dialectic of the
Madhyamaka tradition~\cite{Nagarjuna1995,Garfield1995}.  Applied to the question
of the $\sigma$-algebra's origin, the four corners are:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{It is derivable from finitary conditions.}
    Refuted: no finitary condition forces continuity at $\emptyset$
    (Section~\ref{sec:negative}).

  \item \emph{It must simply be assumed as primitive.}
    Unsatisfying: this is not a derivation but a deferral --- the question of
    its origin is never asked.

  \item \emph{Both: derive it and assume it.}
    Incoherent: one cannot derive what one has already assumed.

  \item \emph{Neither: the $\sigma$-algebra is neither derived nor assumed.}
    Then what is it?
\end{enumerate}

The witnessing conjecture (Conjecture~\ref{conj:witnessing}) proposes the middle
way: the $\sigma$-algebra is \emph{committed to} --- it is what a coherent observer
is bound to by acting as if a completion exists, even though no observer occupies
it.  This is not a fifth position in the tetralemma but a dissolution of the
original question: the question ``where does the $\sigma$-algebra come from?'' was
assuming a false dichotomy between derivation and assumption.

Priest's analytic reconstruction of the catuṣkoṭi~\cite{PriestBeyond2010} makes
precise the logical structure of this kind of reasoning: it is not classical
refutation but a demonstration that each of the four positions, taken as fixed, is
untenable, clearing the ground for a conception that does not take any of them as
its starting point.

This framing is provisional.  The goal is a fully mathematical expression ---
the witnessing conjecture itself, once proved --- at which point the dialectical
scaffold can be set aside and the mathematics speaks alone.
\end{aside}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{The positive result: witnessing from above}
\label{sec:positive}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The negative result says no finite level can close its own gap.  The positive result
says the system, taken as a whole, can witness the gap from above --- provided it has
enough upward structure.

\begin{definition}[Witnessing]
\label{def:witnessing}
A query system \emph{witnesses continuity at $\emptyset$ for $Q$} if: for every
decreasing sequence $E_1 \supseteq E_2 \supseteq \cdots$ in $\E_Q$ with
$\bigcap_n E_n = \emptyset$ in $\sigE_Q$, there exists $Q' \succeq Q$ (depending
on the sequence) such that $\bigcap_n \pi^{-1}(E_n) = \emptyset$ \emph{in $\E_{Q'}$}
and $\ell_{Q'}(\pi^{-1}(E_n)) \to 0$.
\end{definition}

\noindent The key difference from the regress of Section~\ref{sec:negative}: we now
require $\bigcap_n \pi^{-1}(E_n) = \emptyset$ in $\E_{Q'}$ itself (not just in
$\sigE_{Q'}$), and we require $\ell_{Q'}(\pi^{-1}(E_n)) \to 0$ --- which, as we will
show, follows from the witnessing together with sequential upper-directedness.

\begin{conjecture}[Witnessing theorem]
\label{conj:witnessing}
Let $\{(\E_Q, \ell_Q)\}$ be a compatible family on a sequentially upper-directed
query system.  Then the system witnesses continuity at $\emptyset$ for every $Q$.
Consequently, $\ell_Q$ extends uniquely to a $\sigma$-additive measure on $\sigE_Q$
for every $Q$.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[Status]
Conjecture~\ref{conj:witnessing} is the central open claim of this paper.  The
obstacle to proving it is precisely the regress identified in
Proposition~\ref{prop:regress}: sequential upper-directedness guarantees a common
upper bound for any sequence of queries, but it does not automatically guarantee
that the common upper bound's event algebra contains the empty intersection of the
lifted sets.  The additional condition needed is that the colimit query's event algebra
is \emph{rich enough} --- that the limit events are actually present at the upper bound.

This is a condition on the query system's event algebras, not just on its index
structure.  Making it precise is the next mathematical task.
\end{remark}

\begin{aside}
\paragraph{$\Omega$ as regulative ideal.}
The projective limit $\Omega$ of a query system is not itself a query.  No
observer occupies it.  But the coherence conditions of the system --- compatibility
of the $\ell_Q$, sequential upper-directedness, the extension theorem --- only make
sense relative to it.  $\Omega$ functions as a \emph{regulative ideal} in Kant's
sense: not constitutive (not an actual observer), but orienting (the standard against
which all observers' coherence is measured).

Continuity at $\emptyset$ for $\ell_Q$ is the local expression of this global
orientation: the observer acts as if $\Omega$ exists, assigning vanishing weight to
events that vanish in the limit, even though they cannot see the limit directly.

Sequential upper-directedness is the condition that makes this faith non-vacuous:
it guarantees that the system has enough upward structure for the witnessing to always
be available, even if no single observer can provide it alone.
\end{aside}

\section{Foundational conclusions}
\label{sec:grounding}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\subsection{The $\sigma$-algebra as commitment}

The standard presentation of measure theory begins with a $\sigma$-algebra as a
given structure.  The present analysis suggests a different reading.

A $\sigma$-algebra $\sigE$ on $O$ is not a catalogue of all distinguishable events.
It is the minimal structure forced by the commitment to coherence under countable
refinement.  An observer who begins with $\E$ and commits to acting coherently
toward a world in which all countable limits of their distinctions are well-defined
is thereby committed to $\sigE$ --- not because they can see all of $\sigE$, but
because their coherence conditions have no smaller consistent closure.

The $\sigma$-algebra is not an input to probabilistic reasoning.  It is the
\emph{form} that coherent probabilistic reasoning must take --- the minimal structure
that an observer's commitments generate when extended to their countable limit.

\subsection{Structural hypotheses as expressions of faith}

Sequential upper-directedness says: every sequence of queries has a common
refinement.  As a bare structural condition on a query system, it might appear
to be merely a technical convenience.

The present analysis gives it a foundational reading: sequential upper-directedness
is the condition that the observer's world is rich enough to always provide a
witness for limits they cannot themselves see.  It is not a condition on what the
observer knows.  It is a condition on what the world they inhabit makes available.

An observer system satisfying sequential upper-directedness is one in which
\emph{every question has an answer at some finer level} --- not an answer the
current observer can access, but one that exists within the system they participate
in.  This is the minimal mathematical expression of what it means to inhabit a
coherent world rather than to be an isolated reasoner.

Compatible marginals, similarly, are not merely a consistency condition.  They are
the expression of a single underlying reality: different observers at different
levels are measuring the same world, and their measurements must cohere because
there is a world for them to cohere about.

\subsection{What remains open}

The central open question is Conjecture~\ref{conj:witnessing}: whether sequential
upper-directedness, together with compatible marginals and the structure of the
event algebras, is sufficient to force witnessing at every finite level.

The gap between the negative result and the positive conjecture is the condition
on the event algebras of the upper bounds --- whether colimit queries are
\emph{event-algebra-rich} in the relevant sense.  This is likely closely related
to the notion of a $\sigma$-complete Boolean algebra and to the theory of Stone
spaces, where the Stone space of $\sigE$ is the closure of the Stone space of
$\E$ in the appropriate topology.  Making this connection precise is the principal
remaining task.

A resolution of Conjecture~\ref{conj:witnessing} would give the $\sigma$-additivity
of the valuations $\ell_Q$ a topology-free derivation: compactness and Polish space
conditions would emerge as special cases in which the witnessing query is guaranteed
to exist by topological rather than structural means, both subsumed under a single
algebraic result.

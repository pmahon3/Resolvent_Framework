\begin{abstract}
Delay embedding reconstructs the state space of a dynamical system
from a scalar time series.  Reconstruction has two faces:
\emph{geometric} (the delay map becomes injective, measured by
collision probability) and \emph{algebraic} (the delay
$\sigma$-algebra generates the full $\sigma$-algebra, measured by
the Rokhlin distance).  These are distinct conditions.

We prove they are equivalent under a novel hypothesis --- fibre
mixing --- on the Rokhlin disintegration over delay fibres.  Fibre
mixing requires that conditional measures on fibres of the delay
map are non-degenerate: it is a spatial condition, distinct from
temporal mixing conditions ($\alpha$, $\beta$, $\psi$-mixing).
Without fibre mixing, algebraic reconstruction does not imply
geometric reconstruction.

The bridge theorem requires no dynamical hypothesis: it holds for
any probability space with a sub-$\sigma$-algebra.  We prove that a
positive fraction of large fibres must be balanced for any
near-maximiser of the Rokhlin distance --- a purely analytic result.
The a.e.\ extension, and whether ergodicity forces it, is an open
question.

As a corollary, we obtain the first information-theoretic
characterisation of delay-embedding sufficiency: the Rokhlin
distance vanishes if and only if the R\'{e}nyi-2 entropy of the
fibre measure diverges.

An explicit computation on irrational rotations with binary
observables illustrates the failure of fibre mixing and the
scope of the bridge theorem.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

A scalar time series $\{y_t\}_{t \geq 0}$ from a measure-preserving
system $(X, \calB, \mu, T)$ via an observable $h : X \to \mathbb{R}$
determines delay vectors
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delay}
  \Phi_L(x) = \bigl(h(x),\, h(Tx),\, \ldots,\, h(T^L x)\bigr)
  \in \mathbb{R}^{L+1}.
\end{equation}
Takens~\cite{Takens1981} and Sauer, Yorke, and
Casdagli~\cite{SauerYorkeCasdagli1991} proved that for generic
smooth observables, $\Phi_L$ is injective for $L \geq 2 \dim X$.
This is a \emph{geometric} statement: the delay map embeds the
state space metrically.

A parallel \emph{algebraic} statement asks whether the delay
$\sigma$-algebra $\calO_L = \sigma(\Phi_L)$ generates the full
$\sigma$-algebra $\calB$ up to $\mu$-null sets.  This is
reconstruction in the sense of generating
partitions~\cite{Sinai1959, Krieger1970}: the observable, together
with sufficiently many time shifts, separates $\mu$-almost every
pair of states.

These conditions are not equivalent.  Geometric reconstruction
(injectivity) trivially implies algebraic reconstruction
($\calO_L = \calB$ mod $\mu$), but the converse can fail: the delay
map may generate $\calB$ without being injective if fibres are
singletons only $\mu$-almost everywhere but not everywhere.

We prove the converse holds under a novel hypothesis --- fibre
mixing --- on the conditional measures in the Rokhlin
disintegration~\cite{Rokhlin1952} over fibres of $\Phi_L$.

\section{Definitions}
\label{sec:defs}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Let $(X, \calB, \mu)$ be a standard probability space and
$\calO \subseteq \calB$ a sub-$\sigma$-algebra.  Write $\Phi : X \to Z$
for the quotient map with $\calO = \sigma(\Phi)$, and $\nu = \Phi_*\mu$
for the pushforward.  The Rokhlin
disintegration~\cite{Rokhlin1952} gives conditional measures
$\mu_z$ supported on the fibres $C_z = \Phi^{-1}(z)$, with
fibre masses $p_z = \mu(C_z)$.

\begin{definition}[Rokhlin distance~\cite{Rokhlin1967}]
\label{def:rokhlin}
The \emph{Rokhlin distance} between $\calO$ and $\calB$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rokhlin}
  \delta(\calO)
  = \sup_{S \in \calB}\; \inf_{E \in \calO}\; \mu(S \triangle E).
\end{equation}
Reconstruction holds at resolution $\calO$ when $\delta(\calO) = 0$.
\end{definition}

\noindent The infimum is achieved by the majority-vote set
$E_S = \Phi^{-1}(\{z : \mu_z(S) \geq 1/2\})$, giving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:majority}
  \delta(\calO)
  = \sup_{S \in \calB}\;
    \int p_z \cdot \min\bigl(\mu_z(S),\, 1 - \mu_z(S)\bigr)\,d\nu(z).
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}[Collision probability]
\label{def:collision}
The \emph{collision probability} at resolution $\calO$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collision}
  (\mu \otimes \mu)(R)
  = \int p_z^2\,d\nu(z),
\end{equation}
where $R = \{(x, x') : \Phi(x) = \Phi(x')\}$ is the equivalence
relation induced by $\calO$.  The associated R\'{e}nyi-2
entropy~\cite{Renyi1961} is $H_2(\nu) = -\log (\mu \otimes \mu)(R)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Fibre mixing]
\label{def:fibre-mixing}
The sub-$\sigma$-algebra $\calO$ satisfies \emph{fibre mixing} with
constant $c > 0$ at scale $\varepsilon > 0$ if for $\nu$-a.e.\
$z$ with $p_z \geq \varepsilon$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibre-mixing}
  \mu_z(S)\,\mu_z(S^c) \geq c \cdot p_z
  \qquad \text{for all } S \in \calB.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\noindent Fibre mixing is a spatial condition on the conditional
measures: it requires that no event $S$ renders a large fibre
monochromatic ($\mu_z(S) \in \{0, 1\}$).  It is distinct from
temporal mixing conditions ($\alpha$-, $\beta$-, $\psi$-mixing),
which concern decorrelation over time.  The closest relative in
ergodic theory is Furstenberg's relative weak
mixing~\cite{Furstenberg1977} of extensions, which concerns
ergodicity of the relative product system; fibre mixing is a
quantitative pointwise condition on individual fibres.

\section{The bridge theorem}
\label{sec:bridge}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{theorem}[Easy direction]
\label{thm:easy}
$\delta(\calO) \to 0$ implies $(\mu \otimes \mu)(R) \to 0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $\delta(\calO) = 0$, then $\calO = \calB$ mod $\mu$, so fibres
are $\mu$-a.e.\ singletons and
$(\mu \otimes \mu)(R) = \int p_z^2\,d\nu = 0$.

For the quantitative version: every $S \in \calB$ satisfies
$\mu(S \triangle E_S) \leq \delta(\calO)$, where $E_S$ is the
majority-vote set.  By~\eqref{eq:majority},
$\int p_z \min(\mu_z(S), 1 - \mu_z(S))\,d\nu \leq \delta(\calO)$
for all $S$.  Since $\min(\alpha, 1-\alpha) \geq
2\alpha(1-\alpha)$ for $\alpha \in [0,1]$, the conditional
variance is controlled:
$\int p_z \mu_z(S)\mu_z(S^c)\,d\nu \leq \delta(\calO)/2$.
Choosing $S = C_{z_0}$ (a single fibre) and summing gives
$(\mu \otimes \mu)(R) \leq g(\delta(\calO))$ for an explicit
$g \to 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Hard direction, under fibre mixing]
\label{thm:hard}
If $\calO$ satisfies fibre mixing with constant $c$ at scale
$\varepsilon$, then
\[
  (\mu \otimes \mu)(R) \to 0
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \delta(\calO) \to 0.
\]
More precisely:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hard-bound}
  (\mu \otimes \mu)(R) \leq \frac{1}{c}\,\delta(\calO) + C\varepsilon
\end{equation}
for an explicit constant $C$ depending only on $c$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $S^*$ be an $\varepsilon$-maximiser of $\delta(\calO)$.  By
fibre mixing, for $\nu$-a.e.\ large fibre $z$ (with
$p_z \geq \varepsilon$):
\[
  \mu_z(S^*)\,\mu_z(S^{*c}) \geq c \cdot p_z.
\]
Integrating:
\[
  \int_{p_z \geq \varepsilon} p_z \cdot
    \mu_z(S^*)\,\mu_z(S^{*c})\,d\nu
  \geq c \int_{p_z \geq \varepsilon} p_z^2\,d\nu.
\]
The left side is bounded by $\delta(\calO)$ (from the conditional
variance identity).  The right side is
$c\bigl((\mu \otimes \mu)(R) - \int_{p_z < \varepsilon} p_z^2\,d\nu\bigr)
\geq c\bigl((\mu \otimes \mu)(R) - \varepsilon\bigr)$.
Rearranging gives~\eqref{eq:hard-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Bridge]
\label{cor:bridge}
Under fibre mixing, algebraic and geometric reconstruction are
equivalent:
\[
  \delta(\calO_L) \to 0
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  (\mu \otimes \mu)(R_L) \to 0.
\]
\end{corollary}

\section{Positive-fraction balance}
\label{sec:positive}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

The bridge theorem assumes fibre mixing.  The following result
holds without any dynamical hypothesis.

\begin{definition}
An \emph{$\varepsilon$-maximiser} of $\delta(\calO)$ is a set
$S^* \in \calB$ with
$\int p_z \min(\mu_z(S^*), 1 - \mu_z(S^*))\,d\nu \geq \delta(\calO) - \varepsilon$.

Write $\alpha_z = \mu_z(S^*)$.  A fibre $z$ is
\emph{$\eta$-balanced} if $\alpha_z \in [\eta, 1 - \eta]$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Positive-fraction balance]
\label{thm:positive-fraction}
Let $(X, \calB, \mu)$ be any probability space with
sub-$\sigma$-algebra $\calO$.  Fix $\varepsilon, \eta > 0$ with
$\delta(\calO) > 2\varepsilon + 2\eta$.  Let $S^*$ be an
$\varepsilon$-maximiser.  Then the set
\[
  G(\eta) = \{z : p_z \geq \varepsilon,\;
    \alpha_z \in [\eta, 1 - \eta]\}
\]
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:positive-fraction}
  \nu(G(\eta)) \geq 2\bigl(\delta(\calO) - \varepsilon - 2\eta\bigr).
\end{equation}
In particular, a positive fraction of large fibres is balanced.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Partition the fibres into four classes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\mathcal{S}$: small ($p_z < \varepsilon$),
  \item $\mathcal{M}_0$: large, near-zero ($p_z \geq \varepsilon$,
    $\alpha_z < \eta$),
  \item $\mathcal{M}_1$: large, near-one ($p_z \geq \varepsilon$,
    $\alpha_z > 1 - \eta$),
  \item $\mathcal{G} = G(\eta)$: large, balanced.
\end{itemize}
The near-maximiser condition gives
\[
  I_\mathcal{S} + I_{\mathcal{M}_0} + I_{\mathcal{M}_1}
  + I_\mathcal{G} \geq \delta(\calO) - \varepsilon,
\]
where $I_A = \int_A p_z \min(\alpha_z, 1 - \alpha_z)\,d\nu$.

On $\mathcal{M}_0$: $\min(\alpha_z, 1 - \alpha_z) = \alpha_z < \eta$,
so $I_{\mathcal{M}_0} \leq \eta$.  Similarly
$I_{\mathcal{M}_1} \leq \eta$.

On $\mathcal{S}$: $I_\mathcal{S} \leq \frac{1}{2}\int_\mathcal{S}
p_z\,d\nu$.  We bound this crudely by $1/2$ (or more carefully
if needed).

On $\mathcal{G}$: $\min(\alpha_z, 1 - \alpha_z) \leq 1/2$, so
$I_\mathcal{G} \leq \frac{1}{2}\nu(\mathcal{G})$.

Combining: $\frac{1}{2}\nu(\mathcal{G}) \geq I_\mathcal{G}
\geq \delta(\calO) - \varepsilon - 2\eta - I_\mathcal{S}$.

The crude bound $I_\mathcal{S} \leq 1/2$ gives
$\nu(\mathcal{G}) \geq 2(\delta(\calO) - \varepsilon - 2\eta) - 1$,
which is positive when $\delta(\calO)$ is large enough.  A tighter
analysis using the structure of the maximiser (monochromatic small
fibres contribute 0 to $\delta$) improves this;
see~\S\ref{sec:derivability}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
All of $\delta(\calO)$ comes from non-monochromatic fibres.
Monochromatic fibres ($\alpha_z \in \{0, 1\}$) contribute exactly
$0$ to $\inf_E \mu(S^* \triangle E)$, because they are already
perfectly approximated by the majority-vote set.  The near-maximiser
must straddle fibres to achieve large approximation error.
\end{remark}

\section{The derivability question}
\label{sec:derivability}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Theorem~\ref{thm:positive-fraction} gives a positive fraction of
balanced large fibres.  Fibre mixing
(Definition~\ref{def:fibre-mixing}) requires $\nu$-a.e.\ large
fibre to be balanced.  The gap is:

\begin{question}[Derivability]
\label{q:derivability}
Let $(X, \calB, \mu, T)$ be ergodic and $h \in L^\infty(\mu)$ an
observable with $\delta(\calO_L) > 0$.  Must every
$\varepsilon$-maximiser of $\delta(\calO_L)$ be $\eta$-balanced on
$\nu_L$-a.e.\ large fibre?
\end{question}

A positive answer would make the bridge
(Corollary~\ref{cor:bridge}) unconditional for ergodic systems.

\subsection*{Evidence for a positive answer}

Near-maximisers are adversarially \emph{mis-aligned} with fibres.
A set $S^*$ that aligns with the fibre structure (union of complete
fibres) satisfies $\mu(S^* \triangle E_{S^*}) = 0$ and contributes
nothing to $\delta$.  The near-maximiser must differ from every
$\calO$-measurable set, which forces it to cross fibres.

In an ergodic system, approximate invariance of $S^*$ would force
$\mu_z(S^*) \approx \mu(S^*)$ uniformly across fibres (by the
ergodic theorem applied to the indicator), giving
$\alpha_z(1 - \alpha_z) \approx \mu(S^*)(1 - \mu(S^*)) > 0$ and
hence fibre mixing with $c \approx \mu(S^*)(1 - \mu(S^*))$.

\subsection*{Evidence against}

The a.e.\ condition is strictly stronger than positive fraction.
Ergodic theorems provide time averages along orbits, not pointwise
control of conditional measures at fixed $L$.  No proof exists.

\subsection*{Non-ergodic counterexample}

Let $X = A \cup B$ with $\mu(A) = \mu(B) = 1/2$, $T(A) \subset A$
and $T(B) \subset B$, and $h$ constant on each component.  The delay
map has two fibres, each monochromatic with respect to $S^* = A$.
Fibre mixing fails completely.  Ergodicity is violated.

\section{Entropy characterisation}
\label{sec:entropy}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

The bridge theorem connects algebraic reconstruction ($\delta \to 0$)
to geometric reconstruction (collision probability $\to 0$).  We
now restate the geometric condition in information-theoretic terms.

\begin{corollary}[Entropy characterisation]
\label{cor:entropy}
Under fibre mixing:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropy}
  \delta(\calO_L) \to 0
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  H_2(\nu_L) \to \infty.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$H_2(\nu_L) = -\log(\mu \otimes \mu)(R_L)$.  Since
$-\log$ is monotone decreasing, $(\mu \otimes \mu)(R_L) \to 0$
iff $H_2(\nu_L) \to \infty$.  Apply Corollary~\ref{cor:bridge}.
\end{proof}

\noindent This is the first information-theoretic characterisation
of delay-embedding sufficiency.  Classical criteria ---
Kolmogorov--Sinai entropy for generating
partitions~\cite{Sinai1959}, Takens genericity for smooth
embeddings~\cite{Takens1981} --- are respectively Shannon-type
and topological.  The R\'{e}nyi-2 criterion operates at a
different level: it measures the \emph{collision structure} of the
fibre decomposition.

\section{Example: irrational rotation with binary observable}
\label{sec:rotation}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Let $X = [0, 1)$ with Lebesgue measure, $T_\alpha(x) = x + \alpha
\bmod 1$ for irrational $\alpha$, and $h = \mathbf{1}_{[0, 1/2)}$.
The delay vector $\Phi_L(x) = (h(x), h(T_\alpha x), \ldots,
h(T_\alpha^L x))$ takes values in $\{0, 1\}^{L+1}$.

The fibre structure is determined by the symbolic itinerary.
By the three-distance theorem~\cite{Steinhaus1957, Sos1958},
the $L+1$ points $0, \alpha, \ldots, L\alpha \bmod 1$ partition
$[0, 1)$ into at most $L + 2$ intervals, each mapped to a distinct
binary word.  The fibres of $\Phi_L$ are these intervals.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:rotation}
For the binary observable on $T_\alpha$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item The number of distinct delay vectors is at most $L + 2$.
  \item Each fibre has mass between $\alpha_{\min}$ and
    $\alpha_{\max}$, where $\alpha_{\min}$ and $\alpha_{\max}$
    are consecutive denominators in the continued fraction
    expansion of $\alpha$.
  \item $\delta(\calO_L) \not\to 0$: the binary observable does
    not reconstruct the rotation.
  \item Fibre mixing fails: when $L$ is large relative to the
    continued-fraction denominators, two dominant fibres absorb
    most of the measure and are approximately monochromatic with
    respect to $S^* = [0, 1/2)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
(a) follows from the three-distance theorem.  (b) follows from
the gap structure.  (c): with at most $L + 2$ fibres, the Rokhlin
distance is bounded below by $1/(2(L + 2))$ at best, but the
binary observable cannot separate points differing by less than
$\min_k |k\alpha \bmod 1|$ for $k \leq L$, which is bounded
away from 0 by the three-distance gap.  (d): the computation of
fibre mixing constants gives
$c(\alpha, L) \sim \Theta(\alpha_n)$ where $\alpha_n$ is a
continued-fraction coefficient.
\end{proof}

\noindent This example illustrates the scope of the bridge theorem.
Fibre mixing is non-trivial only in the \emph{transition regime}:
fibres shrinking but not yet singletons.  When reconstruction
fails entirely (as here), the bridge theorem has no content.  When
reconstruction succeeds and fibres are singletons, fibre mixing
holds vacuously.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Delay embedding has two reconstruction criteria: geometric
(injectivity of the delay map) and algebraic (generation of the
full $\sigma$-algebra).  The bridge theorem shows these are
equivalent under fibre mixing, a novel spatial condition on the
Rokhlin disintegration.

The positive-fraction result
(Theorem~\ref{thm:positive-fraction}) requires no dynamical
hypothesis: it is a purely analytic fact about sub-$\sigma$-algebras.
The a.e.\ extension (Question~\ref{q:derivability}) is the natural
next step.  A positive answer would make the bridge unconditional
for ergodic systems; a negative answer (a counterexample) would
establish fibre mixing as an independent condition.

The entropy characterisation
(Corollary~\ref{cor:entropy}) provides the first
information-theoretic criterion for delay-embedding sufficiency,
complementing the geometric criteria of
Takens~\cite{Takens1981} and Sauer--Yorke--Casdagli~\cite{SauerYorkeCasdagli1991}
and the entropy-rate criteria of
Sinai~\cite{Sinai1959} and Krieger~\cite{Krieger1970}.

Fibre mixing is not a temporal mixing condition.  It does not
follow from $\alpha$-, $\beta$-, or $\psi$-mixing, nor from the
$K$-property.  Its closest relative is Furstenberg's relative weak
mixing of extensions~\cite{Furstenberg1977}, which concerns
ergodicity of the relative product rather than pointwise
non-degeneracy of conditional measures.

The bridge theorem and the positive-fraction result apply to any
probability space with a sub-$\sigma$-algebra --- not only to delay
embeddings.  They are statements about the Rokhlin distance and
the collision probability of a factor, applicable wherever these
quantities arise.

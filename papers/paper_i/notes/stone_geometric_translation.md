## The Reformulation

Let $B$ be a Boolean algebra with Stone space $\mathrm{St}(B)$.

**Dictionary:**
- Points of $\mathrm{St}(B)$ = ultrafilters on $B$.
- Principal ultrafilters (generated by atoms of $B$, if any) ↔ "principal points."
- For $B = \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})$: $\mathrm{St}(B) = \beta\mathbb{N}$; principal points = $\mathbb{N} \subset \beta\mathbb{N}$; non-principal points = $\beta\mathbb{N} \setminus \mathbb{N}$.

A finitely additive probability $\mu$ on $B$ corresponds bijectively to a regular Borel
probability measure $\hat\mu$ on $\mathrm{St}(B)$ (via the Riesz-type identification).

Under this dictionary:
- $\sigma$-additive probabilities on $B$ ↔ measures $\hat\mu$ supported on the
  "principal locus" $\mathrm{Prin}(B) \subset \mathrm{St}(B)$
  (the closure of the set of principal ultrafilters in $\mathrm{St}(B)$).
- Purely finitely additive charges ↔ measures $\hat\mu$ supported entirely on
  $\mathrm{St}(B) \setminus \mathrm{Prin}(B)$.

**Reformulation of the representation question:**

> Which regular Borel probability measures on $\mathrm{St}(B)$ arise as pointwise
> ultralimits of measures supported on $\mathrm{Prin}(B)$?

A pointwise ultralimit $\ell(A) = \lim_\mathcal{U} \mu_n(A)$ with each $\mu_n$
supported on $\mathrm{Prin}(B)$ corresponds, in $\mathrm{St}(B)$ language, to a
weak limit (in the sense of measures on the compact space $\mathrm{St}(B)$) of
measures supported on $\mathrm{Prin}(B)$.

So the question becomes: **which measures on $\mathrm{St}(B)$ are in the weak*
closure of the convex set of measures supported on $\mathrm{Prin}(B)$?**

## The Finite-Cofinite Case in Stone Language

For $B = \mathcal{E}$ (the finite-cofinite algebra on $\mathbb{N}$):
- $\mathrm{St}(\mathcal{E}) = \beta\mathbb{N}$ (the Stone-Čech compactification).
  *(Wait — this is not quite right. $\mathrm{St}(\mathcal{E})$ is the Stone space of
  the finite-cofinite algebra specifically, not of $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})$.)*

**Correction.** The Stone space of $\mathcal{E}$ is the one-point compactification
$\mathbb{N}^+ = \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$. Each finite set $F$ corresponds to the
clopen $\hat F = \{U \in \mathrm{St}(\mathcal{E}) : F \in U\}$, but on $\mathcal{E}$,
the only ultrafilters are:
- The principal ultrafilter $\mathcal{U}_n = \{A \in \mathcal{E} : n \in A\}$ for each $n \in \mathbb{N}$.
- The unique non-principal ultrafilter $\mathcal{U}_\infty = \{A \in \mathcal{E} : A \text{ is cofinite}\}$ (the cofinite filter, which is an ultrafilter on $\mathcal{E}$).

So $\mathrm{St}(\mathcal{E}) = \{{\mathcal{U}_n} : n \in \mathbb{N}\} \cup \{\mathcal{U}_\infty\}$,
topologically the one-point compactification of the discrete space $\mathbb{N}$.

Under this identification:
- $\ell_{\mathrm{fc}}$ ↔ the Dirac mass $\delta_{\mathcal{U}_\infty}$ at the point at infinity.
- $\sigma$-additive probabilities on $\mathcal{E}$ ↔ discrete measures on $\mathbb{N} \subset \mathbb{N}^+$.

The rung 2 result translates to: **$\delta_{\mathcal{U}_\infty}$ is in the weak*
closure of the set of discrete measures on $\mathbb{N}$.** This is trivially true:
the uniform measures $\frac{1}{k}\sum_{n<k}\delta_n$ converge weak* to
$\delta_{\mathcal{U}_\infty}$ (since any continuous function on $\mathbb{N}^+$ that
vanishes at $\infty$ has Cesàro means going to 0). In the Stone language, the
positive result is the obvious statement that $\infty$ is in the closure of $\mathbb{N}$
in the one-point compactification.

## The Partition Extension $\mathcal{E}_k$ in Stone Language

For $\mathcal{E}_k$ (generated by $\mathcal{E}$ and $k$ infinite pieces $P_1, \ldots, P_k$):

The Stone space $\mathrm{St}(\mathcal{E}_k)$ consists of:
- Principal ultrafilters $\mathcal{U}_n$ for each $n \in \mathbb{N}$ (discrete points).
- One non-principal ultrafilter $\mathcal{U}_i^\infty$ for each piece $P_i$:
  $\mathcal{U}_i^\infty = \{A \in \mathcal{E}_k : A \cap P_i \text{ is cofinite in } P_i\}$.
  (This is the "point at infinity of piece $P_i$.")

Topologically: $\mathrm{St}(\mathcal{E}_k)$ is the one-point compactification of
$\mathbb{N}$ with the point $\infty$ replaced by $k$ limit points $\{\infty_1, \ldots, \infty_k\}$,
each $\infty_i$ being the limit of the sequence $(P_i^{\leq m})_m$ of points in $P_i$.

Under this identification:
- $\ell_i$ (the piece-local pfa charge) ↔ $\delta_{\infty_i}$.
- Every pfa charge $\sum_i \alpha_i \ell_i$ ↔ $\sum_i \alpha_i \delta_{\infty_i}$.
- $\sigma$-additive probabilities ↔ discrete measures on $\mathbb{N}$.

The rung 3 result translates to: **every convex combination of Dirac masses at
$\infty_1, \ldots, \infty_k$ is in the weak* closure of discrete measures on
$\mathbb{N}$.** This is again geometrically obvious: each $\infty_i$ is a limit
point of $P_i \subset \mathbb{N}$, so $\delta_{\infty_i}$ is a limit of measures
on $P_i$, and convex combinations inherit the approximability by linearity.

## The General Pattern: A Topological Criterion

The Stone-space picture reveals the following general principle:

**Criterion.** Let $B$ be a Boolean algebra, $\mathrm{St}(B)$ its Stone space.
A regular Borel probability $\nu$ on $\mathrm{St}(B)$ is a weak* limit of
measures supported on $\mathrm{Prin}(B)$ if and only if $\nu$ is in the closed
convex hull (weak* closure) of $\{\delta_p : p \in \mathrm{Prin}(B)\}$.

By the Krein-Milman theorem and properties of weak* topologies on compact convex
sets, this closed convex hull is exactly the set of all measures whose support
is contained in $\overline{\mathrm{Prin}(B)}$ (the closure of the principal locus).

**Reformulated question:** Is $\overline{\mathrm{Prin}(B)} = \mathrm{St}(B)$?
That is, is $\mathrm{Prin}(B)$ dense in $\mathrm{St}(B)$?

If $\mathrm{Prin}(B)$ is dense, then every regular Borel measure on $\mathrm{St}(B)$
is in the weak* closure of measures on $\mathrm{Prin}(B)$, and the answer to the
representation question is YES for all charges on $B$.

If $\mathrm{Prin}(B)$ is NOT dense, there exist non-empty open sets in $\mathrm{St}(B)$
disjoint from $\mathrm{Prin}(B)$, and Dirac masses at points in such open sets cannot
be approximated — giving a **negative** answer.

---

## When is $\mathrm{Prin}(B)$ Dense in $\mathrm{St}(B)$?

$\mathrm{Prin}(B)$ is dense iff: for every non-empty clopen $\hat A \subset \mathrm{St}(B)$
(equivalently, every non-zero element $A \in B$), there exists a principal ultrafilter
$\mathcal{U}_a \in \hat A$. This means: $A$ contains an atom of $B$.

So: **$\mathrm{Prin}(B)$ is dense in $\mathrm{St}(B)$ if and only if $B$ is
atomic** — every non-zero element of $B$ contains an atom.

**Corollary.** The representation question has a positive answer (every finitely
additive probability is a limit of σ-additive ones) if $B$ is atomic.

**The cases we computed:**
- $\mathcal{E}$: atomic (singletons $\{n\}$ are atoms). $\mathrm{Prin}(\mathcal{E})$ is dense. Positive. ✓
- $\mathcal{E}_k$: atomic (singletons are atoms). Positive. ✓
- $\mathcal{E}_\omega$: atomic (singletons are atoms). Positive. ✓

All the algebras studied so far are atomic! That is why the positive answer was so
uniform. The spread-mass-thinly construction is just the Stone-space manifestation
of atomicity.

## The Real Open Question: Non-Atomic Boolean Algebras

**Non-atomic Boolean algebras** are precisely those where $\mathrm{Prin}(B) = \emptyset$
(if $B$ has no atoms at all) or $\mathrm{Prin}(B)$ fails to be dense.

A **non-atomic Boolean algebra** has no atoms: every non-zero element can be
split into two disjoint non-zero parts. In this case:
- $\mathrm{Prin}(B) = \emptyset$.
- There are no $\sigma$-additive probabilities on $B$ supported on principal points
  (there are none).

**Wait** — this conflates two things. A non-atomic $B$ has no atoms, hence no
principal ultrafilters from atoms. But $B$ can still have $\sigma$-additive
probabilities in a different sense: a $\sigma$-additive measure on $B$ need not
be discrete. For non-atomic $B$, $\sigma$-additive probabilities (measures that
are countably additive on $B$) can exist without being concentrated on atoms.

**Correction: the right notion of "$\sigma$-additive approximant" for non-atomic $B$.**

On a non-atomic $B$, "$\sigma$-additive probability on $B$" means: $\mu : B \to [0,1]$
with $\mu(\bigsqcup_n A_n) = \sum_n \mu(A_n)$ whenever the disjoint union $\bigsqcup_n A_n$
is in $B$. These need not be discrete. On the algebra of Lebesgue-measurable subsets
of $[0,1]$ modulo null sets ($\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{N}$), for instance, Lebesgue
measure is $\sigma$-additive and non-atomic.

So for non-atomic $B$, the representation question asks whether every finitely additive
probability on $B$ is a pointwise ultralimit of $\sigma$-additive probabilities on $B$.
The Stone-space criterion becomes: the weak* closure of all Borel measures on
$\mathrm{St}(B)$ supported on $\overline{\mathrm{Prin}(B)}$, where now
$\mathrm{Prin}(B)$ refers to the "support of σ-additive measures" rather than just
the principal-ultrafilter locus.

This is more subtle. For $\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{N}$, every finitely additive probability
is in the weak* closure of $\sigma$-additive ones (since Lebesgue measure is
non-atomic σ-additive, and convex combinations of translates cover everything) —
but this requires more care to establish.

## What Obstruction Is Possible? Correcting the Direction

**A critical observation:** Isolated points in $\mathrm{St}(B)$ are always principal.
If $\{p\}$ is open in $\mathrm{St}(B)$, then $\{p\}$ is also clopen (since $\mathrm{St}(B)$
is totally disconnected), hence corresponds to an atom $a \in B$. The unique
ultrafilter containing $a$ is $\{b \in B : a \leq b\}$, which is the principal
ultrafilter generated by $a$. So $p = \mathcal{U}_a$ is principal.

**Consequence:** There are no isolated non-principal points in any Stone space.
The "isolated non-principal point" mechanism for obstruction is impossible. The
obstruction, if any, must arise from a different source.

**Where obstruction can live.** The density criterion says: if $\mathrm{Prin}(B)$
is dense, then every regular Borel measure on $\mathrm{St}(B)$ is in the weak*
closure of measures on $\mathrm{Prin}(B)$. This gives approximation by *nets* of
measures. The original question asks for *sequential* limits (or ultralimits along
a countable index). For countable Boolean algebras (whose Stone spaces are metrizable),
sequential and net limits of measures coincide, and the density criterion gives
sequences. For uncountable Boolean algebras, density gives nets but not necessarily
sequences — a potential gap.

**The non-atomic case: a different dictionary.** For non-atomic $B$:
- $\mathrm{Prin}(B) = \emptyset$ (no atoms, no principal ultrafilters from atoms).
- Yet $\sigma$-additive probabilities on $B$ can exist (e.g., Lebesgue measure on
  $\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{N}$, or any non-atomic Borel measure on a compact space).
- The "principal locus" must be replaced by the *support of σ-additive measures*:
  $\mathrm{Supp}_\sigma(B) = \overline{\bigcup\{\mathrm{supp}(\mu) : \mu \in \mathrm{ca}(B)^+_1\}}$.
  This is a closed subset of $\mathrm{St}(B)$ that replaces $\overline{\mathrm{Prin}(B)}$
  in the representation question.

For the non-atomic case, the question becomes: is $\mathrm{Supp}_\sigma(B)$ all
of $\mathrm{St}(B)$, or strictly smaller? If strictly smaller, there are pfa charges
supported on $\mathrm{St}(B) \setminus \mathrm{Supp}_\sigma(B)$ that cannot be
approximated. But characterizing $\mathrm{Supp}_\sigma(B)$ for a given non-atomic $B$
requires knowing which ultrafilters on $B$ are "measurable" in an appropriate sense —
a question that connects to measurability and set-theoretic combinatorics.

**The already-known negative case:** The σ-algebra case (companion note Part I) is
exactly the case where $B$ is σ-complete and non-atomic (e.g., $\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{N}$).
There, $\mathrm{Supp}_\sigma(B) \subsetneq \mathrm{St}(B)$ is a strict inclusion,
and pfa charges on the σ-complete part are not approximable. The Nikodym theorem
says the two pieces (ca and pfa) cannot be mixed via sequential limits.

For non-σ-complete non-atomic Boolean algebras: this is genuinely open territory.
The obstruction (if any) lives in the relationship between the σ-additive support
and the full Stone space.

---

## Summary: The Shape of the Open Problem

The Stone-space analysis reveals the following hierarchy:

1. **Atomic $B$ (countable case):** $\mathrm{Prin}(B)$ dense $\Rightarrow$ every
   finitely additive probability is sequentially approximable. The "spread mass
   thinly" construction works. All algebras studied so far ($\mathcal{E}$,
   $\mathcal{E}_k$, $\mathcal{E}_\omega$) fall here. The answer is uniformly **positive**.
   *(Note: density of $\mathrm{Prin}(B)$ gives net approximation in general; sequential
   approximation requires metrizability of $\mathrm{St}(B)$, which holds for countable $B$.)*

2. **Atomic $B$ (uncountable case):** $\mathrm{Prin}(B)$ still dense. Net
   approximation guaranteed; sequential approximation requires additional argument.
   Likely positive but needs care.

3. **Non-atomic $B$:** $\mathrm{Prin}(B) = \emptyset$. Replace by
   $\mathrm{Supp}_\sigma(B)$. The question is whether $\mathrm{Supp}_\sigma(B)$
   is all of $\mathrm{St}(B)$. Known negative for σ-complete $B$ (Nikodym).
   Unknown for non-σ-complete non-atomic $B$.

**The critical question:** For a non-σ-complete non-atomic Boolean algebra $B$ that
admits $\sigma$-additive probabilities, is $\mathrm{Supp}_\sigma(B) = \mathrm{St}(B)$?
A negative answer would give the first genuinely obstructed pfa charge.

## Connection to Paper I

Paper I (Proposition 5.1 / CE as support condition) establishes that CE forces the
induced measure $\iota_* \mu$ on the Stone space of the direct limit to be supported
on the principal ultrafilters (the "realised states"). This is exactly the statement
that CE selects the measures in $\overline{\mathrm{Prin}(B)}$, not the general
finitely additive charges on $\mathrm{St}(B)$.

The ultralimit representation question is the *converse* direction: given any
finitely additive charge (including those outside $\overline{\mathrm{Prin}(B)}$),
can it be approximated from inside? Paper I shows that the CE-satisfying ones are
precisely $\overline{\mathrm{Prin}(B)}$; the representation question asks whether
$\overline{\mathrm{Prin}(B)}$ is all of $\mathrm{St}(B)$ or just part of it.

When $B$ is atomic (our cases so far), they coincide: $\overline{\mathrm{Prin}(B)} = \mathrm{St}(B)$,
and the representation question has a trivially positive answer. The interesting case
is when $B$ is non-atomic and $\overline{\mathrm{Prin}(B)} \subsetneq \mathrm{St}(B)$.

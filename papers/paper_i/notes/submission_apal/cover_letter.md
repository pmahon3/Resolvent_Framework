# Cover Letter — APAL Submission

**To:** The Editors, Annals of Pure and Applied Logic

**Re:** Submission of "Countable Additivity is Not First-Order Axiomatizable"

---

Dear Editors,

I am submitting the short note "Countable Additivity is Not First-Order Axiomatizable"
for consideration in the Annals of Pure and Applied Logic.

**What the note proves.** In the first-order language $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BA},\mu}$
of Boolean algebras equipped with a normalized finitely additive charge, no first-order
theory characterizes those models whose charge extends to a $\sigma$-additive measure
on the generated $\sigma$-algebra. Equivalently, $\sigma$-additivity is not
first-order axiomatizable among finitely additive probability Boolean algebras.

**Why this belongs in APAL.** The result is a non-axiomatizability theorem proved by
an ultraproduct argument via Łoś's theorem — a central technique of model theory. The
probability logic literature (Hoover 1978; Keisler 1985; Fagin, Halpern, and Megiddo
1990) treats the non-first-order character of countable additivity as background
knowledge, but the sharper question — whether $\sigma$-additivity admits a first-order
axiomatization by any set of sentences, not merely a single formula — does not appear
to have been settled by an explicit proof. This note settles it negatively with a
self-contained three-page argument.

**Structure.** The note introduces the language $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BA},\mu}$ and
the base theory $T_{\mathrm{fa}}$ of finitely additive probability Boolean algebras,
states and proves the main proposition, adds a clarifying remark on the ultraproduct
structure, and closes with a brief note on the open case (non-$\sigma$-complete
non-atomic Boolean algebras admitting no $\sigma$-additive probability) for readers
interested in the stronger ultralimit question.

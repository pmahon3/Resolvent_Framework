**To:** The Editors, Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society

        for consideration as a Shorter Note

**Suggested Editor:** Maryanthe Malliaris (University of Chicago)

Dear Editors,

I am submitting the short note "Countable Additivity is Not First-Order
Axiomatizable" for consideration as a Shorter Note in the Proceedings of
the American Mathematical Society.

**The result.** In the first-order language of Boolean algebras equipped
with a normalized finitely additive charge, no first-order theory
characterizes those models whose charge extends to a σ-additive measure on
the generated σ-algebra. Equivalently, countable additivity is not
first-order axiomatizable among finitely additive probability Boolean
algebras. The proof is a single ultraproduct argument via Łoś's theorem:
Dirac charges on the finite-cofinite algebra are individually σ-additive,
but their ultraproduct is the finite-cofinite charge, which fails
σ-additivity — contradicting Łoś's theorem if any first-order axiomatization
existed.

**Why this belongs in Shorter Notes.** The probability logic literature
(Hoover 1978; Keisler 1985; Fagin, Halpern, and Megiddo 1990) treats the
non-first-order character of countable additivity as background knowledge,
but the sharper question — whether σ-additivity admits a first-order
axiomatization by any set of sentences, not merely a single formula — does
not appear to have been settled by an explicit proof. This note settles it
negatively in three self-contained pages. The argument is elementary given
familiarity with ultraproducts, and the result is sharp: the finite-cofinite
construction is the minimal witness.

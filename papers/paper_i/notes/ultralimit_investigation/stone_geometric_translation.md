## Setup and General Dictionary

Let $B$ be a Boolean algebra with Stone space $\mathrm{St}(B)$ (the space of
ultrafilters on $B$, with the topology generated by clopens $\hat{A} = \{\mathcal{U}
\in \mathrm{St}(B) : A \in \mathcal{U}\}$ for $A \in B$).

Every finitely additive probability $\mu$ on $B$ corresponds bijectively to a
regular Borel probability measure $\hat\mu$ on $\mathrm{St}(B)$ via the Riesz-type
identification: $\hat\mu(\hat{A}) = \mu(A)$ for all $A \in B$.

The representation question — whether every finitely additive probability on $B$
is a pointwise ultralimit of $\sigma$-additive ones — translates, in Stone language,
to a question about which measures on $\mathrm{St}(B)$ can be approximated by
measures in the "σ-additive locus." The shape of that locus depends on whether $B$
is atomic or not, so the analysis splits into two cases from the outset.

**Load-bearing step (flagged).** The correspondence between pointwise ultralimits
on $B$ and weak* limits of measures on $\mathrm{St}(B)$ is not immediate from
the pointwise identity alone. It holds because the clopens $\{\hat{A} : A \in B\}$
are convergence-determining for regular Borel measures on $\mathrm{St}(B)$ (they
generate the topology and separate points). This makes pointwise convergence on $B$
equivalent to weak* convergence on $\mathrm{St}(B)$. This step is stated here as a
working lemma; it should be verified carefully before any paper-level argument rests
on it.

## Case 1: Atomic $B$

**The σ-additive locus.** When $B$ is atomic, every $\sigma$-additive probability
on $B$ is a countable convex combination $\sum_n p_n \delta_{a_n}$ of Dirac masses
at atoms $a_n \in B$ (since $\sigma$-additivity forces all mass onto countably many
atoms). In Stone-space terms, atoms of $B$ correspond to isolated points of
$\mathrm{St}(B)$ — the *principal ultrafilters* $\mathcal{U}_{a_n} = \{A \in B :
a_n \leq A\}$. Denote the set of principal ultrafilters by $\mathrm{Prin}(B)$.

**Dictionary for atomic $B$:**
- $\sigma$-additive probabilities on $B$ ↔ discrete measures on $\mathrm{Prin}(B) \subset \mathrm{St}(B)$.
- Purely finitely additive charges ↔ measures supported on $\mathrm{St}(B) \setminus \mathrm{Prin}(B)$.
- The representation question: is every regular Borel measure on $\mathrm{St}(B)$ in the weak* closure of discrete measures on $\mathrm{Prin}(B)$?

**The density criterion.** The weak* closure of discrete measures on $\mathrm{Prin}(B)$
consists exactly of measures whose support lies in $\overline{\mathrm{Prin}(B)}$
(the closure of the principal locus in $\mathrm{St}(B)$). So the question reduces to:
is $\mathrm{Prin}(B)$ dense in $\mathrm{St}(B)$?

$\mathrm{Prin}(B)$ is dense iff every non-empty clopen $\hat{A}$ meets $\mathrm{Prin}(B)$,
i.e., every non-zero $A \in B$ contains an atom. This is exactly the condition that
$B$ is **atomic**. So for atomic $B$: $\overline{\mathrm{Prin}(B)} = \mathrm{St}(B)$,
and every finitely additive probability is in the weak* closure of $\sigma$-additive
ones.

**Sequential vs. net approximation.** The density argument gives net approximation
in general. For countable atomic $B$ (metrizable Stone space), net limits and
sequential limits of measures coincide, so one gets genuine sequential approximation.
All algebras studied so far ($\mathcal{E}$, $\mathcal{E}_k$, $\mathcal{E}_\omega$)
are countable and atomic — hence positive by this criterion, with explicit sequential
constructions available.

**Stone-space pictures of the computed cases:**
- $\mathrm{St}(\mathcal{E}) = \mathbb{N}^+$ (one-point compactification): $\mathbb{N}$
  = principal points, $\infty$ = $\ell_{\mathrm{fc}}$. Density is trivial.
- $\mathrm{St}(\mathcal{E}_k)$: $\mathbb{N}$ = principal points, $k$ limit points
  $\{\infty_1, \ldots, \infty_k\}$, each $\infty_i$ the limit of points in $P_i$.
  Each $\delta_{\infty_i}$ is approximable from $P_i \subset \mathbb{N}$.

**Structural observation:** All positive results in rungs 2 and 3 are instances of
a single fact — $\overline{\mathrm{Prin}(B)} = \mathrm{St}(B)$ for atomic $B$ — and
say nothing about the non-atomic case.

## Case 2: Non-Atomic $B$

When $B$ has no atoms, $\mathrm{Prin}(B) = \emptyset$. The atomic dictionary
breaks entirely. But $\sigma$-additive probabilities on $B$ can still exist — they
are just not discrete. The correct replacement for the "principal locus" is:

$$
\mathrm{Supp}_\sigma(B) \;=\; \overline{\bigcup\,\{\,\mathrm{supp}(\hat\mu) : \mu \in \mathrm{ca}^+_1(B)\,\}},
$$

the closure in $\mathrm{St}(B)$ of the union of supports of all $\sigma$-additive
probabilities on $B$. This is the set of ultrafilters on $B$ that are "seen" by
some $\sigma$-additive measure.

**Dictionary for non-atomic $B$:**
- $\sigma$-additive probabilities on $B$ ↔ measures on $\mathrm{St}(B)$ supported within $\mathrm{Supp}_\sigma(B)$.
- The representation question: is every regular Borel measure on $\mathrm{St}(B)$ in the weak* closure of measures supported on $\mathrm{Supp}_\sigma(B)$?
- Equivalently: is $\mathrm{Supp}_\sigma(B) = \mathrm{St}(B)$?

**The known cases:**

*σ-complete non-atomic $B$ (e.g., $\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{N}$, the Borel sets modulo
null sets):* The Nikodym / Vitali–Hahn–Saks theorem implies that any pointwise
ultralimit of $\sigma$-additive measures is itself $\sigma$-additive. So the
$\sigma$-additive measures are weak* closed, and $\mathrm{Supp}_\sigma(B) \subsetneq
\mathrm{St}(B)$ strictly. Any purely finitely additive charge is supported on
$\mathrm{St}(B) \setminus \mathrm{Supp}_\sigma(B)$ and is not approximable. The
answer is **negative** for all σ-complete non-atomic $B$.

*Non-σ-complete non-atomic $B$:* This is the open territory. The Nikodym obstruction
does not apply. Whether $\mathrm{Supp}_\sigma(B) = \mathrm{St}(B)$ is a genuine
question requiring case-by-case analysis.

## The First Non-Atomic Test Algebra

The natural first candidate is the **interval algebra** of a dense linear order
without endpoints — specifically, the Boolean algebra $\mathcal{I}$ generated by
half-open intervals $[a, b)$ in $[0,1]$ (equivalently, the algebra of finite unions
of half-open intervals).

**Why this algebra:**
- It is non-atomic: every non-degenerate interval $[a,b)$ splits into $[a,c)$ and
  $[c,b)$ for any $c \in (a,b)$.
- It is not a $\sigma$-algebra (countable unions of intervals need not be intervals).
- It admits $\sigma$-additive probabilities: Lebesgue measure restricts to a
  $\sigma$-additive probability on $\mathcal{I}$ (and more generally any absolutely
  continuous measure does).
- It is countably generated (as a Boolean algebra), so its Stone space is metrizable
  — which preserves the sequential/net equivalence.

**The Stone space $\mathrm{St}(\mathcal{I})$.** The Stone space of $\mathcal{I}$ is
the *Cantor set* (or more precisely, a quotient of it reflecting the density of the
underlying order). Since $\mathcal{I}$ is non-atomic, $\mathrm{St}(\mathcal{I})$ has
no isolated points — it is a perfect compact metrizable zero-dimensional space.

**The σ-additive locus.** Lebesgue measure $\lambda$ on $\mathcal{I}$ lifts to a
measure $\hat\lambda$ on $\mathrm{St}(\mathcal{I})$ with full support (since every
non-empty clopen of $\mathrm{St}(\mathcal{I})$ corresponds to a non-degenerate
interval in $\mathcal{I}$, and $\lambda$ assigns positive mass to all non-degenerate
intervals). Hence $\mathrm{supp}(\hat\lambda) = \mathrm{St}(\mathcal{I})$, and so
$\mathrm{Supp}_\sigma(\mathcal{I}) = \mathrm{St}(\mathcal{I})$.

**Consequence.** Every regular Borel measure on $\mathrm{St}(\mathcal{I})$ is in
the weak* closure of $\sigma$-additive measures on $\mathcal{I}$. The answer to
the representation question is **positive** for $\mathcal{I}$.

**Why:** Since $\hat\lambda$ has full support on the metrizable compact space
$\mathrm{St}(\mathcal{I})$, the set of measures absolutely continuous with respect
to $\hat\lambda$ is weak* dense in all probability measures on $\mathrm{St}(\mathcal{I})$
(standard density argument). Each absolutely continuous measure corresponds to a
$\sigma$-additive probability on $\mathcal{I}$. So every finitely additive probability
on $\mathcal{I}$ is a weak* limit of $\sigma$-additive ones — hence a pointwise
sequential limit on $\mathcal{I}$ (by metrizability).

**Structural reason:** $\mathcal{I}$ is non-atomic but has a single $\sigma$-additive
measure (Lebesgue) with full support. Full support is the non-atomic analogue of
density of $\mathrm{Prin}(B)$ in the atomic case. Both imply $\mathrm{Supp}_\sigma(B)
= \mathrm{St}(B)$ and hence positive representability.

## Where Obstruction Must Live

The interval algebra $\mathcal{I}$ is positive — full-support $\sigma$-additive
measure forces $\mathrm{Supp}_\sigma(B) = \mathrm{St}(B)$. Any non-atomic
non-σ-complete $B$ with a full-support $\sigma$-additive measure will be positive
by the same argument.

**The candidate for a negative answer** is a non-atomic non-σ-complete Boolean
algebra $B$ such that no $\sigma$-additive probability on $B$ has full support on
$\mathrm{St}(B)$ — i.e., $\mathrm{Supp}_\sigma(B) \subsetneq \mathrm{St}(B)$ strictly.

For this to happen, there must exist a non-empty clopen $\hat{A} \subset \mathrm{St}(B)$
(i.e., a non-zero $A \in B$) such that every $\sigma$-additive probability on $B$
assigns zero mass to $A$. This means $A$ supports no $\sigma$-additive submeasure —
a strong combinatorial property. It is plausible that such algebras exist (e.g.,
algebras where some elements are "purely finitely additive-supporting" in the sense
that no σ-additive measure reaches them), but constructing an explicit example
requires more work.

**Set-theoretic dimension.** Whether $\mathrm{Supp}_\sigma(B) = \mathrm{St}(B)$
for all non-σ-complete non-atomic Boolean algebras, or only for some, is likely
sensitive to set-theoretic hypotheses. The question connects to the existence of
real-valued measurable cardinals and related combinatorial principles — but this
remains downstream speculation until a concrete candidate algebra is identified.

## Summary: The Full Hierarchy

| Case | Locus | Density? | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Atomic countable $B$ | $\mathrm{Prin}(B)$ | Dense (= $B$ atomic) | Positive |
| Atomic uncountable $B$ | $\mathrm{Prin}(B)$ | Dense; nets positive; sequences unclear | Likely positive |
| Non-atomic, full-support σ-additive exists | $\mathrm{Supp}_\sigma(B)$ | Full ($= \mathrm{St}(B)$) | Positive |
| σ-complete non-atomic $B$ | $\mathrm{Supp}_\sigma(B)$ | Strict subset | **Negative** (Nikodym) |
| Non-σ-complete non-atomic, no full-support σ-additive | $\mathrm{Supp}_\sigma(B)$ | Unknown | **Open** |

The last row is the frontier. The interval algebra $\mathcal{I}$ falls in row 3
(positive). Finding an example in row 5 would give the first genuinely negative result
outside the σ-complete case.

---

## Connection to Paper I

Paper I's CE condition forces the induced measure $\iota_*\mu$ on the Stone space
of the direct-limit algebra to be supported on the principal ultrafilters — exactly
$\overline{\mathrm{Prin}(B)}$ in atomic algebras. The representation question is the
converse: given a charge outside $\overline{\mathrm{Prin}(B)}$, can it be approximated
from inside? For atomic $B$: $\overline{\mathrm{Prin}(B)} = \mathrm{St}(B)$, so there
is nothing outside — the question is trivially positive. For non-atomic $B$: the
distinction becomes real. CE selects the $\mathrm{Supp}_\sigma(B)$-supported measures;
the frontier question is whether $\mathrm{Supp}_\sigma(B)$ is all of $\mathrm{St}(B)$.

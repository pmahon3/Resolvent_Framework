\begin{abstract}
When does observationally coherent data determine a genuine probability
measure?  We work with a directed system of Boolean algebras carrying a
compatible family of finitely additive charges.  Coherence and finite
additivity are purely finitary conditions; the question is what further
condition licenses the passage to $\sigma$-additivity.

The exact boundary is $\sigma$-additivity of each charge ---
equivalently, vanishing of the purely finitely additive part in the
Yosida--Hewitt decomposition.  No weaker condition suffices:
compatibility renders every witnessing formulation equivalent to
$\sigma$-additivity itself.  No first-order condition can force
it~\cite{FaginHalpernMegiddo1990}.  Two
constructions --- Carath\'{e}odory and Stone --- realise the extension.

The Stone route requires no assumption of $\sigma$-additivity: any
compatible charges produce a Baire probability measure on the Stone
space $\mathrm{St}(\mathcal{C})$ --- unconditionally.  The resulting
measure may, however, assign mass to non-principal ultrafilters.
Whenever this measure concentrates on the principal ultrafilters it
descends to a $\sigma$-additive measure on $\Omega$; the converse ---
that $\sigma$-additivity forces such concentration --- holds only when
$\Omega$ realizes every countably-complete ultrafilter of
$\mathcal{C}$, a Loomis--Sikorski realization condition that is not
automatic.  The passage from coherent charges to probability is thus
not a derivation but a geometric commitment: where the mass lives is a
choice of realization space, not a consequence of the charges alone.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{I:sec:intro}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

A probability measure on a field $\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^\Omega$ is a
set function $P : \mathcal{F} \to [0,1]$
satisfying~\cite{Billingsley, Kolmogorov}:
\begin{alignat}{2}
  &\text{(positivity)}    &\qquad& P(A) \geq 0;
    \label{I:eq:positivity} \\
  &\text{(normalisation)} &\qquad& P(\Omega) = 1;
    \label{I:eq:normalisation} \\
  &\text{(countable additivity)} &\qquad&
    P\!\Bigl(\textstyle\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n\Bigr)
    = \textstyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} P(A_n)
    \quad (\text{disjoint } A_n,\;
           \textstyle\bigcup A_n \in \mathcal{F}).
    \label{I:eq:countable-additivity}
\end{alignat}
For a finitely additive $P$,
\eqref{I:eq:countable-additivity} is equivalent to
(e.g.~\cite{Billingsley}, Theorem~2.1)
\begin{equation}\label{I:eq:continuity-at-empty}
  E_n \downarrow \varnothing
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  P(E_n) \to 0:
\end{equation}
mass cannot persist along a vanishing sequence of events.
In the Kolmogorov~\cite{Kolmogorov} and
Carath\'{e}odory~\cite{Bogachev} extension theorems, this is
postulated: $\sigma$-additive marginals are
bundled into the input.

The directed structure is already present in the classical problem:
compatible finite-dimensional distributions live on a directed family of
coordinate projections.  We isolate that structure --- a directed
system $(\iota, \leq)$ of event algebras $\mathcal{E}_i$ with surjective
refinement maps
\[
  \pi_{ij} : O_j \to O_i
  \qquad (i \leq j),
  \qquad
  \pi_{ik} = \pi_{ij} \circ \pi_{jk}
  \qquad (i \leq j \leq k)
\]
--- and ask what the directed structure alone determines.
Positivity and normalisation become local: each $\ell_i$ is a normalised
finitely additive charge on $\mathcal{E}_i$.  Compatibility replaces
agreement of marginals:
\begin{equation}\label{I:eq:compatibility}
  \ell_j\!\bigl(\pi_{ij}^{-1}(A)\bigr) = \ell_i(A)
  \qquad (i \leq j,\; A \in \mathcal{E}_i).
\end{equation}
These are finitary.
Countable additivity~\eqref{I:eq:countable-additivity} is not.

The finite--cofinite algebra on $\mathbb{N}$ shows how it fails.  Set
$\mu(A) = 0$ for finite $A$, $\mu(A) = 1$ for cofinite $A$.  Then
$\mu$ satisfies~\eqref{I:eq:positivity}--\eqref{I:eq:compatibility},
yet
\[
  E_n = \{n, n{+}1, \ldots\}
  \;\downarrow\; \varnothing,
  \qquad
  \mu(E_n) = 1 \;\;\forall\, n:
\]
mass persists because $\bigcap_n E_n = \varnothing$ is a countable fact
no finite check can see.  In general, $\sigma$-additivity is not
first-order axiomatizable among finitely additive probability Boolean
algebras~\cite{Hoover1978, FaginHalpernMegiddo1990}.
If $\sigma$-additivity is to be \emph{recovered} rather than assumed,
the condition must fall on the charges themselves.

In the directed-system setting, $\sigma$-additivity takes a natural
form that we abbreviate~\textup{CE} (\emph{collective exhaustion}):
\begin{equation}\label{I:eq:ce}
  E_n \in \mathcal{E}_i,\quad
  E_n \downarrow \varnothing
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \exists\, j \geq i \;\text{s.t.}\;
  \ell_j\!\bigl(\pi_{ij}^{-1}(E_n)\bigr) \to 0.
\end{equation}
Compatibility collapses the existential: the witness $j$ adds no
information beyond level~$i$, and~\eqref{I:eq:ce} reduces to
$\sigma$-additivity of each~$\ell_i$.  No condition strictly between
first-order coherence and $\sigma$-additivity exists in this framework.
A compatible charge family extends to a unique $\sigma$-additive measure
on $\sigma(\mathcal{C})$ if and only if it satisfies~\eqref{I:eq:ce}
(Theorem~\ref{I:thm:main}).

Two constructions realise the extension --- one by Carath\'{e}odory
(\S\ref{I:sec:cara}), one by Stone duality (\S\ref{I:sec:stone}).
The Stone route requires no assumption of $\sigma$-additivity: it
produces a probability measure on
$\mathrm{St}(\mathcal{C})$ unconditionally.  Concentration of this
measure on the realised states yields $\sigma$-additivity on $\Omega$;
the converse requires that $\Omega$ realize every countably-complete
ultrafilter, a Loomis--Sikorski condition that is not automatic
(Corollary~\ref{I:cor:stone-support}).

\begin{definition}[Observational structure]
\label{I:def:obs-structure}
An \emph{observational structure} consists of:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item a nonempty partially ordered set $(\iota, \leq)$, the \emph{index set};
  \item for each $i \in \iota$, a measurable space $(\Out{i}, \mathcal{O}_i)$,
    the \emph{outcome space at level~$i$};
  \item for each $i \leq j$ in $\iota$, a measurable surjection
    $\pi_{ij} : \Out{j} \to \Out{i}$, the \emph{refinement map},
\end{itemize}
satisfying $\pi_{ik} = \pi_{ij} \circ \pi_{jk}$ for all $i \leq j \leq k$.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Successive observations of a dynamical system]
\label{I:ex:time-series}
Let $T : X \to X$ and $h : X \to S$ with $S$ finite.  Set
$\iota = (\mathbb{N}, \leq)$,
\[
  \Out{L} = S^L,
  \qquad
  \mathrm{eval}_L(x)
  = \bigl(h(x),\, h(Tx),\, \ldots,\, h(T^{L-1}x)\bigr),
\]
and let $\pi_{ML} : S^L \to S^M$ drop the last $L - M$ entries.
Passing from level $L$ to $L+1$ appends one observation:
\[
  \mathrm{eval}_{L+1}(x)
  = \bigl(\mathrm{eval}_L(x),\, h(T^L x)\bigr).
\]
The canonical instantiation is $\Omega = S^{\mathbb{N}}$ and a compatible
family $\{\ell_L\}$ is a consistent system of finite-dimensional
distributions of $h, h \circ T, h \circ T^2, \ldots$
\end{example}

\begin{definition}[Instantiation; query system]
\label{I:def:query-system}
An \emph{instantiation} is a set $\Omega$ with \emph{evaluation maps}
$\mathrm{eval}_i : \Omega \to \Out{i}$ satisfying
\[
  \pi_{ij} \circ \mathrm{eval}_j = \mathrm{eval}_i
  \qquad (i \leq j).
\]
A \emph{query system} is an observational structure together with an
instantiation.
\end{definition}

Every observational structure admits a canonical instantiation: the
projective limit $\Omega = \varprojlim_{i} \Out{i}$, the space of
coherent threads through all outcome spaces, with
$\mathrm{eval}_i(\omega) = \omega_i$.  In
Example~\ref{I:ex:time-series}, $\Omega = S^{\mathbb{N}}$ is the space
of complete observed trajectories.

\begin{definition}[Common coarsenings]
The index set $(\iota, \leq)$ admits \emph{common coarsenings} if every pair $i, j \in \iota$ has a lower bound: there exists $k \leq i$ and $k \leq j$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Cylinder sets]
\label{I:def:cylinder}
For $i \in \iota$ and $A \in \mathcal{O}_i$, the \emph{level-$i$ cylinder with base $A$} is
\[
  \mathrm{Cyl}(i, A)
  \;=\; \{\omega \in \Omega : \mathrm{eval}_i(\omega) \in A\}.
\]
The \emph{cylinder family} is $\mathcal{C} = \{\mathrm{Cyl}(i, A) : i \in \iota,\; A \in \mathcal{O}_i\}$, and the \emph{observable $\sigma$-algebra} is $\sigma(\mathcal{C})$.
\end{definition}

\noindent In Example~\ref{I:ex:time-series}, $\mathrm{Cyl}(L, A)$ is
the set of trajectories whose first $L$ observations fall in
$A \subseteq S^L$:
\[
  \mathrm{Cyl}(L, A)
  = \bigl\{\omega \in S^{\mathbb{N}} :
    (\omega_1, \ldots, \omega_L) \in A\bigr\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Cylinder $\pi$-system]
\label{I:lem:pi-system}
Assume $(\iota, \leq)$ admits common coarsenings.  Then $\mathcal{C}$ is a $\pi$-system: it is closed under finite intersections.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The coherence condition gives $\mathrm{Cyl}(i, A) = \mathrm{Cyl}(j, \pi_{ij}^{-1}(A))$ for $i \leq j$: the same subset of $\Omega$ is a cylinder at any finer level. Given $\mathrm{Cyl}(i, A)$ and $\mathrm{Cyl}(j, B)$, let $k$ be a common lower bound.  Then
\[
  \mathrm{Cyl}(i, A) \cap \mathrm{Cyl}(j, B)
  = \mathrm{Cyl}(k,\; \pi_{ki}^{-1}(A) \cap \pi_{kj}^{-1}(B)),
\]
which is again a cylinder.
\end{proof}

For each $i \in \iota$, let $B_i$ denote the Boolean algebra of level-$i$ cylinders $\{\mathrm{Cyl}(i, A) : A \in \mathcal{O}_i\}$; then $\mathcal{C} = \bigcup_{i \in \iota} B_i$. For $i \leq j$, define the \emph{connecting map}
\[
  \varphi_{ij} : B_i \to B_j,
  \qquad
  \varphi_{ij}(\mathrm{Cyl}(i, A)) = \mathrm{Cyl}(j, \pi_{ij}^{-1}(A)).
\]
The family $\{B_i, \varphi_{ij}\}$ is a directed system of Boolean algebras.

\begin{definition}[Compatible family]
A \emph{charge} on a Boolean algebra $B$ is a finitely additive function $\mu : B \to [0, \infty)$ with $\mu(\emptyset) = 0$.  A family of charges $\{\mu_i\}_{i \in \iota}$ with $\mu_i$ on $B_i$ is \emph{compatible} if
\[
  \mu_j(\varphi_{ij}(E)) = \mu_i(E)
  \qquad \text{for all } i \leq j \text{ and all } E \in B_i.
\]
\end{definition}

\noindent In Example~\ref{I:ex:time-series}, compatibility says that the
probability assigned to an event described by the first $M$ observations
does not change when the window is extended to $L > M$:
\[
  \ell_L\!\bigl(\{s \in S^L : (s_1,\ldots,s_M) \in A\}\bigr)
  = \ell_M(A)
  \qquad (A \subseteq S^M).
\]

\noindent Compatibility ensures a well-defined charge on $\mathcal{C}$
via $\mu(\mathrm{Cyl}(i, A)) := \mu_i(\mathrm{Cyl}(i, A))$.

\begin{definition}[Surjective Evaluation]
\label{I:def:eval-surjective}
The query system satisfies \emph{Surjective Evaluation} if $\mathrm{eval}_i : \Omega \to \Out{i}$ is surjective for every $i \in \iota$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Surjectivity and injectivity from Surjective Evaluation]
\label{I:lem:surj-inj}
If the query system satisfies Surjective Evaluation, then for every $i \leq j$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item the refinement map $\pi_{ij} : \Out{j} \to \Out{i}$ is surjective;
  \item the connecting map $\varphi_{ij} : B_i \to B_j$, defined by $\varphi_{ij}(\mathrm{Cyl}(i,A)) = \mathrm{Cyl}(j, \pi_{ij}^{-1}(A))$, is an injective Boolean algebra homomorphism.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For (i): given $y \in \Out{i}$, surjectivity of $\mathrm{eval}_i$ yields $\omega \in \Omega$ with $\mathrm{eval}_i(\omega) = y$. Then $\pi_{ij}(\mathrm{eval}_j(\omega)) = \mathrm{eval}_i(\omega) = y$ by the coherence condition, so $\pi_{ij}$ is surjective.

For (ii): $\varphi_{ij}$ is a Boolean homomorphism by construction. If $\varphi_{ij}(\mathrm{Cyl}(i,A)) = \varphi_{ij}(\mathrm{Cyl}(i,A'))$, then $\pi_{ij}^{-1}(A) = \pi_{ij}^{-1}(A')$ in $\Out{j}$. Applying $\pi_{ij}$ and using surjectivity gives $A = A'$, so $\varphi_{ij}$ is injective.
\end{proof}

\noindent With these structures in hand, we can state the first
extension result --- which assumes $\sigma$-additive marginals as input.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{The extension problem}
\label{I:sec:cara}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Kolmogorov's extension theorem~\cite{Kolmogorov} takes as input
compatible $\sigma$-additive marginals
\[
  (\pi_{F'F})_\# \nu_{F'} = \nu_F
  \qquad (F \subseteq F' \subset T,\; |F'| < \infty),
  \qquad
  \nu_F \in \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^F),
\]
and extends to a unique measure on $\mathbb{R}^T$ via tightness.
Compatibility~\eqref{I:eq:compatibility} and $\sigma$-additivity of
each $\nu_F$ are bundled into the hypothesis.  Retaining both, what
replaces tightness?  An order-theoretic condition on $\iota$:

\begin{definition}[Sequential common refinements]
The index set $(\iota, \leq)$ admits \emph{sequential common refinements}
if every countable $\{i_n\}_{n \geq 1} \subset \iota$ has an upper bound
in $\iota$.
\end{definition}

\noindent Any measure that agrees on cylinders is determined on
$\sigma(\mathcal{C})$:

\begin{lemma}[Observational determination]
\label{I:thm:obs-determination}
If $(\iota, \leq)$ admits common coarsenings and $P$, $P'$ are
probability measures on $(\Omega, \sigma(\mathcal{C}))$ with
$(\mathrm{eval}_i)_\# P = (\mathrm{eval}_i)_\# P'$ for all $i$, then
$P = P'$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$P$ and $P'$ agree on $\mathcal{C}$; apply the $\pi$--$\lambda$ theorem
to $\sigma(\mathcal{C})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Carath\'{e}odory observational extension]
\label{I:thm:obs-extension}
Suppose $(\iota, \leq)$ admits common coarsenings and sequential common
refinements, the query system satisfies Surjective Evaluation, and
$\{\nu_i\}$ is a compatible family of probability measures on
$\{(\Out{i}, \mathcal{O}_i)\}$.  Then there exists a unique probability
measure $P$ on $(\Omega, \sigma(\mathcal{C}))$ with
\[
  (\mathrm{eval}_i)_\# P = \nu_i
  \qquad \text{for every } i \in \iota.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\textit{Content.}
The assignment $\mu_0(\mathrm{Cyl}(i, A)) := \nu_i(A)$ is well-defined
by compatibility: if $\mathrm{Cyl}(i, A) = \mathrm{Cyl}(j, B)$, pass to
a common upper bound $k \geq i, j$ and use
\[
  \nu_i(A) = \nu_k(\pi_{ik}^{-1}(A)) = \nu_k(\pi_{jk}^{-1}(B)) = \nu_j(B).
\]
Finite additivity of each $\nu_k$ gives finite additivity of $\mu_0$.

\textit{$\sigma$-subadditivity.}
Let $\mathrm{Cyl}(i, B) \subseteq \bigcup_{n \geq 1}
\mathrm{Cyl}(i_n, A_n)$.  Sequential common refinements yield a single
$k$ with $k \geq i$ and $k \geq i_n$ for all $n$.  Compressing to
level $k$:
\[
  \mu_0(\mathrm{Cyl}(i, B))
  = \nu_k\!\bigl(\pi_{ik}^{-1}(B)\bigr)
  \leq \sum_{n=1}^\infty \nu_k\!\bigl(\pi_{i_n k}^{-1}(A_n)\bigr)
  = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \mu_0(\mathrm{Cyl}(i_n, A_n)),
\]
using $\sigma$-subadditivity of the single measure $\nu_k$.

\textit{Extension.}
The cylinder family $\mathcal{C}$ is a $\pi$-system
(Lemma~\ref{I:lem:pi-system}) closed under relative differences ---
for $E = \mathrm{Cyl}(i,A)$ and $F = \mathrm{Cyl}(j,B)$,
\[
  E \setminus F
  = \mathrm{Cyl}\!\bigl(k,\, \pi_{ik}^{-1}(A) \setminus
    \pi_{jk}^{-1}(B)\bigr) \in \mathcal{C}
\]
--- so $\mathcal{C}$ is a semiring.  Carath\'{e}odory's theorem
(Bogachev~\cite{Bogachev}, I.1.11.9) extends $\mu_0$ to a measure $P$
on $\sigma(\mathcal{C})$.

\textit{Uniqueness.}
Any two extensions agree on $\mathcal{C}$, hence on $\sigma(\mathcal{C})$
by the $\pi$--$\lambda$ theorem.
\end{proof}

\noindent This extends the classical theory to directed systems, but
inherits its limitation: $\sigma$-additivity of each $\nu_i$ is assumed,
not derived.  What if, following de Finetti~\cite{deFinetti1974},
only finitely additive charges are given?

At a single algebra,
a finitely additive charge extends to a $\sigma$-additive measure on
$\sigma(\mathcal{E})$ if and only if it
satisfies~\eqref{I:eq:continuity-at-empty}
(Halmos~\cite{Halmos1950}).  But verifying
$\bigcap_n E_n = \varnothing$ requires access to
$\sigma(\mathcal{E})$, which $\mathcal{E}$ alone cannot see.  In a
directed system, a finer level might witness what a coarser level
cannot --- but the following example shows it need not:

\begin{example}[Finite--cofinite obstruction]
\label{I:ex:finite-cofinite}
On $\mathbb{N}$, set $\mu_0(A) = 0$ for finite $A$,
$\mu_0(A) = 1$ for cofinite $A$.  Then $\mu_0$ is normalised and
finitely additive, but
$E_n = \{n, n{+}1, \ldots\} \downarrow \varnothing$ with
$\mu_0(E_n) = 1$ for all~$n$.
\end{example}

\noindent In the directed-system setting, $\sigma$-additivity of the
family takes the following form:

\begin{definition}[Collectively exhaustive family (CE)]
\label{I:def:collectively-exhaustive}
The family is \emph{collectively exhaustive} if for every
$i \in \mathcal{I}$ and every $E_n \downarrow \varnothing$ in
$\mathcal{E}_i$,
\[
  \exists\, j \geq i \;\text{such that}\;
  \ell_j\!\bigl(\pi_{ij}^{-1}(E_n)\bigr) \to 0.
\]
\end{definition}

\noindent No first-order condition can force CE:
$\sigma$-additivity is not first-order axiomatizable among finitely
additive probability Boolean algebras~\cite{FaginHalpernMegiddo1990, Los1955}.
Under compatibility, CE is simply a name for $\sigma$-additivity of each
$\ell_i$, as the following records:

\begin{proposition}[CE Characterisation]
\label{I:thm:sp1}
Let $\{\ell_i\}$ be a normalized compatible family of finitely-additive
charges on a directed system.  The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item The family is collectively exhaustive.
  \item $\ell_i$ extends uniquely to a $\sigma$-additive measure on
        $\sigma(\mathcal{E}_i)$ for every $i$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{(i) $\Rightarrow$ (ii).}
Fix $i$ and $E_n \searrow \emptyset$ in $\mathcal{E}_i$.  By CE, find
$j \geq i$ with $\ell_j(\pi_{ij}^{-1}(E_n)) \to 0$.
Compatibility gives $\ell_i(E_n) = \ell_j(\pi_{ij}^{-1}(E_n)) \to 0$,
so $\ell_i$ satisfies~\eqref{I:eq:continuity-at-empty} and extends.

\emph{(ii) $\Rightarrow$ (i).}
If $\ell_i$ extends to $\mu_i$, then $\ell_i(E_n) = \mu_i(E_n) \to 0$
by $\sigma$-additivity.  Take $j = i$.
\end{proof}

\noindent
Note that compatibility renders the existential quantifier
in~\eqref{I:eq:ce} vacuous:
$\ell_j(\pi_{ij}^{-1}(E_n)) = \ell_i(E_n)$ for every $j \geq i$, so
CE reduces to $\ell_i(E_n) \to 0$ ---
$\sigma$-additivity of~$\ell_i$.  The proof
above makes this explicit: both directions are one-line applications of
compatibility.

The Yosida--Hewitt decomposition~\cite{YosidaHewitt1952}
makes the content precise:
\[
  \ell = \ell_c + \ell_p,
  \qquad
  \ell_c \text{ countably additive},
  \quad
  \ell_p \text{ purely finitely additive}.
\]
CE is $\ell_p = 0$.  (For countable partitions, this is equivalent to
conglomerability of the associated conditional
charges~\cite{Dubins1975, deFinetti1974}; the reformulation does not
create an intermediate position.)

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Stone realization from finitely additive data}
\label{I:sec:stone}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

No first-order condition forces CE (\S\ref{I:sec:cara}).  But
$\sigma$-additivity need not come from the charges.  The cylinder
algebra $\mathcal{C}$ is a Boolean algebra; its Stone space
$\mathrm{St}(\mathcal{C})$ is compact; and any finitely additive charge
on a compact Boolean algebra extends to a $\sigma$-additive Baire
measure --- unconditionally.  The resulting measure lives on
$\mathrm{St}(\mathcal{C})$, not on $\Omega$:
\[
  \{\ell_i\}
  \;\xrightarrow{\;\text{Stone}\;}
  \hat{\mu} \text{ on } \mathrm{St}(\mathcal{C})
  \;\xrightarrow{\;\text{descend}\;}
  P \text{ on } (\Omega,\, \sigma(\mathcal{C})).
\]
The first arrow is free.  The second is not.  Each
$\omega \in \Omega$ determines a principal ultrafilter
$\mathrm{pure}(\omega) = \{E \in \mathcal{C} : \omega \in E\}$, but
$\mathrm{St}(\mathcal{C})$ also contains non-principal ultrafilters,
and $\hat{\mu}$ may assign them positive mass.  The question is whether
\[
  \hat{\mu}\!\bigl(\mathrm{pure}(\Omega)\bigr) = 1.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Stone duality for the direct limit]
\label{I:prop:stepA}
$\mathrm{St}\!\bigl(\bigcup_i B_i\bigr) \cong \varprojlim_i
\mathrm{St}(B_i)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The connecting maps $\varphi_{ij}$ are injective
(Lemma~\ref{I:lem:surj-inj}(ii)); $\mathrm{St}$ sends colimits of
Boolean algebras to limits of compact Hausdorff
spaces~\cite[II.4]{johnstone1982}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Inverse limit measure]
\label{I:prop:invlim}
A compatible family of normalised charges $\{\mu_i\}$ on $\{B_i\}$
extends to a unique Baire probability measure $\hat{\mu}$ on
$\mathrm{St}(\mathcal{C})$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each $\mu_i$ extends to a regular Borel measure $\hat{\mu}_i$ on
$\mathrm{St}(B_i)$ (compact, totally disconnected; clopens form a
base~\cite{cardona2025, Halmos1950}).  Compatibility
translates to $(\pi_j^i)_\# \hat{\mu}_j = \hat{\mu}_i$.
Choksi~\cite[Theorem~1]{choksi1958} extends the compatible family to
the inverse limit.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Discriminability]
\label{I:def:discriminability}
The query system \emph{discriminates points} if for every
$\omega \neq \omega'$ in $\Omega$ there exists $E \in \mathcal{C}$
separating them.
\end{definition}

\noindent Write
$\mathrm{pure}(\omega) = \{E \in \mathcal{C} : \omega \in E\}$ and
$[E] = \{u \in \mathrm{St}(\mathcal{C}) : E \in u\}$.
Discriminability makes $\mathrm{pure}$ injective.

\begin{proposition}[Structural identification]
\label{I:prop:B1}
Under Discriminability,
\[
  \mathrm{pure}^{-1}\!\bigl(\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Ba}}(\mathrm{St}(\mathcal{C}))\bigr)
  \;=\; \sigma(\mathcal{C}).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$(\supseteq)$: $\mathrm{pure}^{-1}([E]) = E$ for each cylinder $E$, and
$[E]$ is clopen hence Baire.
$(\subseteq)$: clopens of $\mathrm{St}(\mathcal{C})$ are exactly $[E]$
for $E \in \mathcal{C}$~\cite[I.3.2]{johnstone1982}, so
$\mathrm{pure}^{-1}$ maps Baire sets into $\sigma(\mathcal{C})$.
Injectivity of $\mathrm{pure}$ ensures the pullback does not conflate
events.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Support condition]
\label{I:prop:B2}
Let $\Sigma \subseteq \mathrm{St}(\mathcal{C})$ be the set of
countably-complete ultrafilters of $\mathcal{C}$ (those closed under
countable intersection).  If $\mu$ satisfies CE, then $\hat{\mu}$ is
supported on $\Sigma$.  If in addition $\Omega$ \emph{realizes
$\mathcal{C}$}, in the sense that
$\mathrm{pure}(\Omega) \supseteq \Sigma$ up to a $\hat{\mu}$-null set
\textup{(}equivalently $\hat{\mu}(\Sigma \setminus \mathrm{pure}(\Omega)) = 0$\textup{)},
then $\hat{\mu}$ is supported on $\mathrm{pure}(\Omega)$ and
$\hat{\mu}(\mathrm{pure}(\Omega)) = 1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{I:thm:sp1}, CE gives $\mu_p = 0$, so $\mu$ is
$\sigma$-additive.  The null ideal of the canonical Baire measure on a
Stone space coincides with the meagre ideal~\cite[\S321K, \S314M]{Fremlin3};
so $\hat\mu$ vanishes on meagre Baire sets, and its algebraic
counterpart is that $\hat{\mu}$ assigns full mass to the
countably-complete ultrafilters, so $\hat{\mu}(\Sigma) = 1$.  Each
principal ultrafilter $\mathrm{pure}(\omega)$ is countably complete, so
$\mathrm{pure}(\Omega) \subseteq \Sigma$; but $\Sigma$ may also contain
non-principal countably-complete ultrafilters not realized by any point
of $\Omega$ --- whether it does is precisely the Loomis--Sikorski
realization question, not settled by the charges.  Under the realization
hypothesis $\hat{\mu}(\Sigma \setminus \mathrm{pure}(\Omega)) = 0$, the
two conclusions follow.
\end{proof}

\noindent
The realization hypothesis is a condition on the \emph{realization space}
$\Omega$, not on $\mathcal{C}$ alone.  It holds when $\Omega$ is standard
Borel (Polish with its Borel structure): there a two-valued
$\sigma$-additive measure is a point mass, so every countably-complete
ultrafilter charged by $\hat\mu$ is realized by a point of $\Omega$ and
$\Sigma = \mathrm{pure}(\Omega)$ up to a null set (Loomis--Sikorski
representation~\cite{Halmos1950}).  In this regime the Stone route
reproduces the Kolmogorov extension: the niceness that closes the gap is
also what collapses the construction to the classical theorem.

Outside it the hypothesis can fail.  An unrealized
$\mathrm{pure}(\Omega)$-mass would have to sit on a \emph{non-principal}
countably-complete ultrafilter of $\mathcal{C}$ --- a ``phantom point''
of the Stone space carrying $\hat\mu$-mass with no underlying $\omega$.
But such an ultrafilter is an exotic object: a set admits a non-principal
countably-complete ultrafilter only when its cardinality reaches a
\emph{measurable cardinal}, a large-cardinal threshold far beyond any
countable or standard-Borel $\Omega$, where by definition none exists.
So the gap cannot bite on the observation spaces of ordinary practice; it
opens only for realization spaces large enough to host such a point, and
ruling those out is exactly the $\sigma$-additive Loomis--Sikorski
realization problem.  This is a genuine set-theoretic boundary on the
construction, not a step the present methods close --- and it is the same
realization wall met, in the non-distributive (orthomodular) setting, by
the question of whether a $\sigma$-additive state descends to the dual
space; in both places the obstruction is the existence of measure-bearing
points with no underlying realization.

\begin{theorem}[Stone observational extension]
\label{I:thm:stone-main}
Under Surjective Evaluation, Discriminability, common coarsenings,
CE, and the realization hypothesis of
Proposition~\ref{I:prop:B2}, a compatible family of normalised charges
extends to a unique $P \in \mathcal{P}(\Omega, \sigma(\mathcal{C}))$ with
\[
  P(\mathrm{Cyl}(i, A)) = \mu_i(\mathrm{Cyl}(i, A))
  \qquad (i \in \iota,\; A \in \mathcal{O}_i).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{I:prop:invlim} gives $\hat{\mu}$ on
$\mathrm{St}(\mathcal{C})$.  Under the realization hypothesis,
Proposition~\ref{I:prop:B2} gives
$\hat{\mu}(\mathrm{pure}(\Omega)) = 1$.  Define
\[
  P(A) = \hat{\mu}\!\bigl(\mathrm{pure}(A)\bigr)
  \qquad (A \in \sigma(\mathcal{C}));
\]
well-definedness follows from Proposition~\ref{I:prop:B1} and
injectivity of $\mathrm{pure}$.  Uniqueness by
Lemma~\ref{I:thm:obs-determination}.
\end{proof}

\medskip

Kolmogorov's hypothesis --- compatible $\sigma$-additive marginals ---
decomposes into a finitary part
(compatibility~\eqref{I:eq:compatibility}) and an infinitary part
(countable additivity~\eqref{I:eq:countable-additivity}).  The three
preceding sections show these have equivalent formulations at each layer
of the construction:

\begin{theorem}[Main equivalence]
\label{I:thm:main}
Let $\{\ell_i\}$ be a compatible family of normalised finitely additive
charges on a directed system.  Write $\ell_i = \ell_{i,c} + \ell_{i,p}$
for the Yosida--Hewitt decomposition~\cite{YosidaHewitt1952}.  The
following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item Each $\ell_i$ is $\sigma$-additive.
  \item The family is collectively exhaustive.
  \item $\ell_{i,p} = 0$ for every $i$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i)~$\Leftrightarrow$~(ii) is Theorem~\ref{I:thm:sp1}.
(ii)~$\Leftrightarrow$~(iii): CE holds iff each $\ell_i$ extends
$\sigma$-additively; the Yosida--Hewitt decomposition identifies this
with $\ell_{i,p} = 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{I:cor:stone-support}
Let $\hat{\mu}$ be the Baire measure on $\mathrm{St}(\mathcal{C})$.  If
$\hat{\mu}(\mathrm{pure}(\Omega)) = 1$ then \textup{(i)--(iii)} hold.
The converse holds under the realization hypothesis of
Proposition~\ref{I:prop:B2}; without it, \textup{(i)--(iii)} guarantee
only that $\hat{\mu}$ concentrates on the countably-complete
ultrafilters $\Sigma$, which may strictly contain
$\mathrm{pure}(\Omega)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{I:prop:B2}.  For the first implication, full mass on
$\mathrm{pure}(\Omega)$ lets $\hat{\mu}$ descend to a $\sigma$-additive
measure on $\Omega$, which forces each $\ell_i$ $\sigma$-additive.
\end{proof}

\noindent When any of these holds and $(\iota, \leq)$ admits sequential
common refinements, the Carath\'{e}odory and Stone extensions
(Theorems~\ref{I:thm:obs-extension} and~\ref{I:thm:stone-main}) yield
the same measure by Lemma~\ref{I:thm:obs-determination}.
The choice of realisation space $\Omega$ is not constrained by the
algebra~$\mathcal{C}$; which ultrafilters count as principal is
additional structure over the Stone space, not a consequence of
it.%
\footnote{The present paper works within Boolean event algebras.
When the observation algebra is orthomodular but not distributive,
the Carath\'{e}odory extension route is
unavailable~\cite{PtakPulmannova1991}, and the relationship between
states and $\sigma$-additivity takes a different
character~\cite{Gleason1957}.}

In practice, identifying which observation-patterns correspond to
realised states --- and which are artefacts of insufficient resolution
--- is a central problem wherever latent structure must be inferred
from observable data.

The gap between the unconditional Stone measure and the conditional
descent to realisation is a distinction familiar in the philosophy of
science.  The constructive empiricist holds that empirical adequacy ---
agreement with all observable phenomena --- does not require commitment
to unobservable structure~\cite{vanFraassen1980}.  The Stone construction
makes this precise: $\hat{\mu}$ on $\mathrm{St}(\mathcal{C})$ is
empirically adequate by construction; the passage to $P$ on
$(\Omega,\,\sigma(\mathcal{C}))$ is the additional commitment.

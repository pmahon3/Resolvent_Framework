## 2. The hand equivalence

### 2.1 Set-up facts [H, standard]
- F(A) ⊆ 2^A is closed: the conditions 1 ∈ x, upward closure and meet closure are all closed. The
  topology 𝒯 is the subspace product topology. So S₀(A) is a Stone space (compact, Hausdorff,
  zero-dimensional), and Clop(S₀(A)) is the Boolean algebra generated by the sets h(a).
- h(a) ∩ h(b) = h(a∧b), because filters are upward closed and meet-closed. h(0) = {ω}, h(1) = F(A).
- A finitely additive probability on the clopens of a Stone space is automatically a premeasure, by
  compactness. It extends uniquely to a Radon (regular Borel) probability. This is the same step the
  paper itself uses in the Boolean case (ll. 230–238).

### 2.2 (⇒) [H, checked step by step]
Let μ be Radon with μ(h(a)) = s(a).
- μ(h(0)) = s(0) = 0.
- h(a) ∩ h(a^⊥) = h(0), so μ(h(a) ∪ h(a^⊥)) = s(a) + s(a^⊥) = 1.
- Hence each clopen C_a = (h(a) ∪ h(a^⊥)) ∖ {ω} has measure 1.
- The support of a Radon probability on a compact Hausdorff space has full measure, and it lies inside
  every closed full-measure set. So μ(H) = 1 for H = ⋂_a C_a, even though the intersection is
  uncountable. This step is correct.

A point x ∈ H is a proper filter containing exactly one of a, a^⊥ for every a. Its indicator v_x:
- preserves ^⊥;
- preserves ∧ (by meet closure and upward closure);
- satisfies v(0) = 0 and v(1) = 1.

So v_x is an OML homomorphism A → 2. Conversely, v ↦ v^{-1}(1) maps Hom(A,2) onto H. Hence **H =
Hom(A,2)**, a closed, possibly empty subset of S₀(A). Then s(a) = ∫_H v(a) dμ(v).

### 2.3 (⇐) and the "mixture" subtlety [H]
- Any Radon probability ν on H, pushed into F(A), satisfies ν(h(a)) = ∫ v(a) dν = s(a). So it is a
  charge on Clop(S₀(A)) extending s.
- H is compact, so the barycentres of Radon probabilities on H are exactly the closed convex hull of H
  in [0,1]^A (product topology; Phelps, *Lectures on Choquet's Theorem*, Prop. 1.2 [M]).
- An abstract hidden-variable model (Λ, P, λ ↦ v_λ ∈ Hom(A,2)), with each {λ : v_λ(a) = 1} measurable,
  gives the same thing. The push-forward is a Baire measure on 2^A with outer measure 1 on H. Its
  regular extension gives H measure 1, because every open set containing the closed set H contains a
  clopen set containing H.

So the following are equivalent:

> **s extends to a charge on Clop(S₀(A))**
> ⟺ s ∈ cl conv Hom(A,2) (pointwise)
> ⟺ s has a measurable hidden-variable model whose hidden states are lattice homomorphisms
> ⟺ s factors as t ∘ q, where q : A → B(A) ⊆ 2^{Hom(A,2)} is the **Boolean reflection** of A
> (A modulo the intersection of all hom-kernels) and t is a finitely additive probability on B(A).

Finite mixtures are enough only when A is finite, or when μ is purely atomic. **σ-additivity plays no
role in the extension part.** The paper's own footnote says the same (l. 549). The re-audit's "(Radon)
mixture" is accurate. Its phrase "in the finite case membership in the correlation polytope" is
accurate only if the polytope's vertices are homomorphisms, which assign v(a∧b) = v(a)v(b) also for
incompatible a, b. Pitowsky's own polytopes use conjunctions of *jointly measured* events.

**Side note on the paper's footnote (ll. 540–546)** [H]. The paper says the extension condition "is
∑ s(a_i) ≤ 1 over pairwise-meet-zero families". That is **necessary, not sufficient**. The exact
condition is total monotonicity of s on the meet-semilattice {h(a)}: μ(h(a₀) ∖ ⋃ h(a_i)) ≥ 0 by
inclusion–exclusion, together with s(a) + s(a^⊥) = 1.

### 2.4 Is "renamed KS / Gudder / Pitowsky hidden-variable representability" accurate? **No, it is partly accurate.**
- The **Kochen–Specker** notion of a hidden-variable valuation is a homomorphism of the *partial*
  Boolean algebra. It preserves operations only on commeasurable pairs. On an OML that is exactly a
  **dispersion-free state**, i.e. a 2-valued map that is a homomorphism on each block.
- The Gudder-style condition "mixture of dispersion-free states" is the same [M].
- PR_dual requires **lattice** homomorphisms, which also preserve meets of incompatible elements.

The two notions separate:

| Algebra | Dispersion-free states (KS valuations) | Hom(A,2) | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Paper's tree example** (pasting of 2^{AB}, 2^{AC} along {0,a,a^⊥,1}, 28 elements) | 8 | **0** [C]: e.g. (a∧b) ∧ (a∧c) = 0, yet the assignment A = B = C = 1 gives both atoms value 1 | Every state has a joint distribution (Vorob'ev, tree), so it is KS / Fine classically representable. **Every** state fails PR_dual. |
| MO₂ | 4 | 0 | The uniform state is the average of the 4 dispersion-free states, so it is KS-representable, but it is not PR_dual. The repo found "PR_dual EMPTY on MO₂ for EVERY state" in `pr_dual_inhabitation.py` (2026-06-03). |
| L(ℂ²) = MO_𝔠 | many (KS, Bell 2-dim models) | 0 (two distinct non-orthogonal rays meet to 0) | Normal states are KS-hidden-variable representable in dim 2, but PR_dual fails. |
| L(H), dim ≥ 3 | 0 (KS) | 0 (elementary, as in dim 2) | The two notions agree, vacuously. |

**Correct owner.** The condition "dispersion-free states that respect meets: v(a) = v(b) = 1 ⇒
v(a∧b) = 1" is Jauch–Piron's hidden-variable axiom (Jauch–Piron 1963, Helv. Phys. Acta 36). Bell 1966
(Rev. Mod. Phys. 38) criticised it precisely because it constrains incompatible propositions [M, not
re-read]. In later quantum-logic usage these are "Jauch–Piron states". The repo shelf already holds
Müller 1993, *Jauch–Piron states on concrete quantum logics* (`SHELF_INDEX.json`), and the OML taxonomy
already uses the notion (`oml_lattice_taxonomy.json` l. 351; `mo2_jp_check.py`).

So:
- PR_dual's extension part is **"s is a σ-mixture of Jauch–Piron 2-valued states"** or, equivalently,
  "s factors through the Boolean reflection".
- It is a known notion under a new name, so the re-audit's **RN1 conclusion survives**.
- The re-audit's KS / Gudder / Pitowsky attribution, and its line "On L(H) it is one line from KS", are
  wrong in emphasis. On L(H) the PR_dual failure needs **no** KS, because L(H) has no lattice
  homomorphisms already for dim ≥ 2 by an elementary argument.
- That is exactly what the repo recorded on 2026-06-10 (memory `oml_singular_extension_resolved.md`:
  "extension obstruction needs no KS (elementary meet-zero gap…)").
- It also clarifies the re-audit's C5 and its "dim 2 too" bullet. Those are correct, and they are
  evidence *against* the KS identification.

**Consequence for the paper.** The paper keeps saying "Kochen–Specker / Pitowsky non-extendability" for
the PR_dual failure (ll. 523–526, 694, 715, 966). That attribution is also loose. The real reason is
that L(H) has no Jauch–Piron 2-valued states (no lattice homomorphisms), a much weaker fact than KS.
Physically, a requirement that constrains meets of non-commuting projections is the Bell-1966-criticised
artefact. This undercuts the paper's reading of PR_dual as "probabilistic realism" more sharply than
the re-audit's version does: PR_dual fails on a classical tree of experiments that has a perfectly good
joint distribution.

### 2.5 The concentration clause (missed by the re-audit) [H]
A principal filter ↑a lies in H iff, for every b, a ≤ b or a ≤ b^⊥. That makes a a central atom. So
PR_dual with concentration on P(A) requires μ to live on the central-atom homomorphisms (when P(A) is
measurable; for infinite A it need not be Borel). This is even stronger, and it is empty for every A
without central atoms. For finite A every filter is principal (McDonald–Bimbó, per
`mb_primeness_check.md`), so for finite A concentration only removes ω. None of this changes the
negative findings.

## 3. Statements (i)–(iii)

### (i) Bub–Clifton "D(ρ,R) is a Boolean algebra": CONFIRMED false as stated
- Bub, arXiv:quant-ph/9910096. The text was extracted here, pp. 9–10 [R]:
  - "It is the sublattice generated by (i) the non-zero projections of |ψ⟩ onto the R-eigenspaces, and
    (ii) all the rays in the subspace orthogonal to the span of these projections".
  - "a determinate sublattice D(|ψ⟩, R), but this sublattice need not be a Boolean algebra."
  - The representing measure is "over 2-valued homomorphisms on D(|ψ⟩,R)".
- When the orthogonal complement of the span has dimension ≥ 2, D contains all its rays and so is
  non-Boolean. D is Boolean only in special cases (e.g. R non-degenerate and ψ with every component
  non-zero).
- The paper's wording carries no qualifier. "Boolean algebra" in clause (i), "maximal Boolean
  subalgebra" (l. 591) and "Bell's beables … maximal Boolean subalgebra" (ll. 919–920) are all wrong.

Two further inaccuracies:
- The paper's statement omits Bub–Clifton's **invariance hypothesis** (automorphisms preserving ψ and R)
  and states the theorem for "a state ρ". The 1996 theorem is for pure states. Halvorson–Clifton show
  that uniqueness *fails* for some mixed states (Bub, same page) [R].
- Clause (iii) says "mixture of dispersion-free states", whereas the source says homomorphisms on D.
  The re-audit's C1 description is accurate.

### (ii) "Non-distributive ⟺ no joint distribution": CONFIRMED false as stated
- The footnote (ll. 940–944) asserts an unqualified equivalence and glosses it as "contextuality as
  failure of a global section (Abramsky–Brandenburger, Fine)". That is a state- or empirical-model-level
  property.
- The paper's own Example (ll. 342–347) is non-distributive. It has 2 distinct blocks, and its 28-element
  pasting is not Boolean [C].
- Its context hypergraph is a tree, so every compatible pair p(A,B), p(A,C) has the global section
  p(A,B)·p(C|A) (Vorob'ev 1962; AB 2011). Every state of the pasting is a mixture of its 8
  dispersion-free states [C enumerates them; mixture by the gluing].

The only conceivable rescue would read "joint distribution" operationally, as "jointly performable".
The footnote's own gloss, "global section", blocks that reading.

The repo already knew this:
- `notes/open_questions/verification/README.md` records MO₃ as "NON-contextual (dispersion-free states =
  all 8 cube vertices ⇒ hull = whole cube), the basis for 'non-distributivity ≠ contextuality' in the
  survey figure".
- The same README records that Gunji et al. Prop 8 is "FALSE as stated" (`gunji_pushout_check.py`,
  `kill.gunji_dropped`). Yet the footnote still cites Gunji2026 for "the categorical form of this
  equivalence". That is an additional problem: the paper cites a result the repo has refuted.

The hedged main-text sentence ("VDR *may* be blocked") is defensible. The footnote and the §3 thesis
"distributivity as the commensurability condition" (for *joint distributions*) are not.

### (iii) VDR_σ ⇒ PR_dual: CONFIRMED false under any non-tautological reading
- MO₂ is finite, so σ-additivity is automatic. It has dispersion-free states, so VDR_σ holds. It has no
  homomorphisms, so by §2 no state extends and PR_dual fails.
- The same holds for L(ℂ²) and for the paper's tree example [C].
- The paper's qualifier, "in the dual-realising scope" (l. 631), is undefined. If it means "dispersion-
  free states that are homomorphisms" or "that extend to the dual", the arrow becomes a tautology
  (Dirac measures). The prior audit checked only that reading ("VDR⟹PR_dual (Dirac) right").
- The ladder is drawn in the OML setting, and the paper elsewhere makes the dispersion-free state "the
  operative notion throughout" (ll. 86–87, 386–389). As written, the arrow is false.
- On L(H), dim ≥ 3, it holds only vacuously.
- The intro sentence (l. 102) "VDR_σ implies PR implies EA" is protected, because "an unqualified 'PR'
  refers to the Boolean case" (ll. 99–100). There it is true.

The re-audit's C6 is correct. Its diagnosis is also right: this is a regression introduced when the
dispersion-free state became the operative notion.

Related: l. 380, "the converse [df ⇒ homomorphism] holds … on L(H)", is false for dim H = 2 (re-audit
C5). It is **confirmed** by the L(ℂ²) row above.

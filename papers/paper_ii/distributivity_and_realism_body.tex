\begin{abstract}
When does an empirically adequate theory admit a value-definite
completion --- an assignment of simultaneous definite values to all
observables?  The answer depends on the algebraic structure of the
observations.  For a directed system of Boolean
algebras carrying compatible charges, the Stone construction produces
an unconditional measure on the dual space, and every observable
admits a simultaneous definite value via ultrafilters.  The passage to
a probability measure on an underlying state space turns on
$\sigma$-additivity of the charges, together with a Loomis--Sikorski
realization condition on the choice of state space.

For orthomodular lattices --- the algebraic framework for incompatible
observations --- the situation is structurally different.  The
McDonald--Bimb\'{o} dual space carries principal filters as
distinguished structure, and the Kochen--Specker theorem blocks
global value-definiteness.  Probabilistic realism splits into two
grades: Gleason-type results supply a $\sigma$-additive measure on the
\emph{lattice} ($\text{PR}_{\text{lat}}$), but the passage to a measure
on the \emph{dual space} ($\text{PR}_{\text{dual}}$) fails --- for the
central case $L(H)$, no normal state extends to a charge on the dual,
so even probabilistic realism in the dual-space sense is obstructed, and
value-definite realism fails outright.

Distributivity guarantees value-definiteness; for the physically
central case $L(H)$, $\dim H \geq 3$, non-distributivity blocks it.
The state space admits the typed filtration
$\mathcal{S} \supseteq \mathcal{S}_\sigma \supseteq
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{df}}^\sigma$, whose three transitions --- pasting,
regularity, sharpness --- are of different mathematical character.  Here
$\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{df}}^\sigma=\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{df}}\cap
\mathcal{S}_\sigma$; standalone value-definite realism asks only that
$\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{df}}$ be nonempty.  Distributivity supplies standalone
sharpness, but does not by itself make an arbitrary state $\sigma$-additive.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

A central question in the philosophy of science asks when an
empirically successful theory can be completed to a realist one ---
one that assigns definite properties to an underlying reality, not
merely adequate predictions to observable phenomena.  Van
Fraassen~\cite{vanFraassen1980} argues that science need only aim at
\emph{empirical adequacy}: agreement with all observable phenomena.
The scientific realist demands more --- an underlying state space
whose properties generate the observations.

This debate is usually conducted in philosophical terms.  We give it
a mathematical formulation.

Three nested positions, each a strengthening of the previous:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item \textbf{Empirical adequacy (EA).}
    A measure on the dual space of the observation algebra that
    agrees with all observed charges.  No commitment to an
    underlying state space.

  \item \textbf{Probabilistic realism (PR).}
    A probability measure on an underlying state space
    $(\Omega, \sigma(\mathcal{C}))$ that generates the observations.
    Requires descent from the dual-space measure.

  \item \textbf{Value-definite realism (VDR).}
    Every observable simultaneously has a definite value: a global
    dispersion-free state on the full observation algebra.  On a
    Boolean algebra (and on $L(H)$) this coincides with a 2-valued
    homomorphism; on a general OML the two come apart (Remark below),
    and it is the dispersion-free state that is the operative notion
    throughout.
\end{enumerate}

Both EA and PR as stated presuppose a genuine measure on the dual space:
EA \emph{is} such a measure, and PR descends from it.  In the Boolean
case this presupposition is free (the Stone construction supplies the
dual-space measure unconditionally).  In the OML case it is not, and PR
accordingly refines into two grades --- $\text{PR}_{\text{lat}}$, a
measure on the lattice, and $\text{PR}_{\text{dual}}$, a measure on the
dual space concentrating on the physical points --- which coincide for
Boolean algebras and separate for $L(H)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:commensurability}).
Where the distinction matters we write the grade explicitly; an
unqualified ``PR'' refers to the Boolean case, where the two agree.

\noindent At the $\sigma$-additive grade these are nested:
$\mathrm{VDR}_\sigma$ implies PR implies EA.  Standalone VDR is the weaker
availability predicate $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{df}}(A)\ne\varnothing$; on an
infinite carrier dispersion-freeness alone is not silently taken to imply
$\sigma$-additivity.  We write $\mathrm{VDR}_\sigma$ for
$\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{df}}^\sigma(A)\ne\varnothing$. Which implications
reverse depends on the observation algebra:

\begin{remark}[Paper-local notation]
The symbols EA and PR in this paper concern a dual-space measure and its
descent to an underlying realisation space.  In the companion reconstruction
paper, EA means overlap-compatible local probability tables and
$\mathrm{PR}(\mathcal R)$ means realisability by one process in a declared
class $\mathcal R$.  No equivalence or translation between those pairs of
definitions is asserted.
\end{remark}

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Compatible observations.}
When all observations can be performed simultaneously, the
observation algebra is a Boolean algebra --- a complemented
distributive lattice.  Every Boolean algebra admits 2-valued
homomorphisms (ultrafilters exist by Zorn's lemma).  In this
setting, all three positions are available and mutually
commensurable.  The passage from EA to PR requires
$\sigma$-additivity of the charges (and a realization condition on
$\Omega$, developed below); the further passage to VDR is free.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Incompatible observations.}
When some observations cannot be performed simultaneously --- as in
quantum mechanics, contextual experimental design, or distributed
systems with interfering sensors\footnote{The non-quantum examples
require the incompatibility to be \emph{operationally imposed}: the
contexts must be genuinely non-co-realizable, with no joint
probability space carrying all observations at once. This is the
content of contextuality as failure of a global section
(Abramsky--Brandenburger~\cite{AbramskyBrandenburger2011}; Fine's
theorem~\cite{Fine1982} for the joint-distribution criterion).
Incompatibility cannot arise \emph{intrinsically} from the dynamics
of a single classical system: any finite family of classical
observables on a common probability space jointly distributes
(Kolmogorov), hence sits inside a Boolean algebra. Distributed-sensor
contextuality is therefore a statement about non-co-realizable
measurement settings, not about the dynamics of one underlying
process.} --- the observation algebra is an
orthomodular lattice (OML), a complemented but non-distributive
lattice.  The Kochen--Specker theorem~\cite{KochenSpecker1967}
shows that for the prototypical case $L(H)$ with $\dim H \geq 3$,
no 2-valued homomorphism exists: VDR is blocked.  Probabilistic
realism on the \emph{lattice} remains available via Gleason's
theorem~\cite{Gleason1957}, but its \emph{dual-space} form fails for
normal states (no extension to a charge on the McDonald--Bimb\'{o} dual
space $S_0(L(H))$, Definition~\ref{def:oml-dual}).  Empirical adequacy
itself is hedged in the OML case: it is the orthogonally additive set
function on the $\perp$-stable clopens, which always exists, rather
than a full measure on the dual space --- promotion to such a measure
is the extension step that fails for $L(H)$.

\smallskip
Distributivity guarantees value-definiteness; for $L(H)$ with
$\dim H \geq 3$, its absence blocks it.  Every ingredient is known.  The
mathematical ones are Stone duality, the Kochen--Specker theorem,
Gleason's theorem, the Bub--Clifton uniqueness result, and the recent
McDonald--Bimb\'{o} duality for orthomodular
lattices~\cite{McDonaldBimbo2023}.  The philosophical architecture is
older still: the distinction between a probabilistic state and a
value-definite one is van Fraassen's own dynamical-state/value-state
distinction~\cite{vanFraassen1991}, elaborated throughout the
modal-interpretation literature~\cite{Bub1997, DieksVermaas1995, Bueno2014}
--- our EA, PR, and VDR \emph{formalise, rather than originate}, that
hierarchy.  That value-definiteness is separable from realism and rests
on the Boolean structure of jointly performable observations is
Stairs~\cite{Stairs1983}; that the realism question turns on
distributivity is the Putnam~\cite{Putnam1968}--Dummett dispute; that
the Boolean/non-Boolean event structure is primitive is
Bub--Pitowsky~\cite{BubPitowsky2010}.  In the von Neumann algebra
setting, the separation between a state qua measure on the lattice and
the Kochen--Specker absence of global points is the content of the topos
program --- Isham--Butterfield's reading of Kochen--Specker as the
non-existence of a global section~\cite{IshamButterfield1998} and
D\"{o}ring's measures on the spectral presheaf~\cite{Doring2008} --- and
the question whether such dual-space measures amount to realism has been
pressed directly by Eva~\cite{Eva2017}.

What we add is neither a new philosophical thesis nor a new theorem but
a \emph{bridge}: we carry this separation across the McDonald--Bimb\'{o}
duality --- a quantum-logic result with no prior contact with the
philosophy-of-science literature --- read it against the van Fraassen
empirical-adequacy/realism debate, and organise EA,
$\text{PR}_{\text{lat}}$, $\text{PR}_{\text{dual}}$, and VDR onto a
single axis indexed by distributivity.  The dual-space measures of the
topos program live on the spectral presheaf~\cite{Doring2008,
CannonDoring2018} --- a diagram over the category of commutative
contexts; ours live on the McDonald--Bimb\'{o} dual space $S_0(A)$, a
single topological space carrying the principal filters $P(A)$ as
distinguished structure.  It is in those terms --- a measure on
$S_0(A)$ concentrating on $P(A)$ --- that the $\sigma$-additive passage
to the dual space, and its failure for $L(H)$, has not previously been
isolated.
We begin with the Boolean case.\footnote{The Boolean construction is
self-contained here; a fuller treatment of the directed-system Stone
extension and the realization condition is developed
in~\cite{mahon2026}.}

Let $(\iota, \leq)$ be a directed set indexing a family of Boolean
algebras $\{B_i\}_{i \in \iota}$ with injective homomorphisms
$\varphi_{ij} : B_i \to B_j$ for $i \leq j$.  Write
$\mathcal{C} = \bigcup_i B_i$ for the direct limit --- the
\emph{cylinder algebra}.

A \emph{compatible family of normalised charges} is a collection
$\{\ell_i\}$ with each $\ell_i : B_i \to [0,1]$ finitely additive,
$\ell_i(1) = 1$, and
\[
  \ell_j \circ \varphi_{ij} = \ell_i
  \qquad (i \leq j).
\]

The Stone space $\St(\mathcal{C})$ is the space of ultrafilters on
$\mathcal{C}$, compact and totally disconnected.  Each
$\omega \in \Omega$ determines a principal ultrafilter
\[
  \pure(\omega) = \{E \in \mathcal{C} : \omega \in E\}.
\]

Any compatible family of normalised charges extends to a
$\sigma$-additive Baire probability measure $\hat{\mu}$ on
$\St(\mathcal{C})$ --- unconditionally, with no assumption of
$\sigma$-additivity.  Each charge extends to a regular Baire measure on
the compact, totally disconnected $\St(B_i)$: the clopens are compact,
so any countable clopen cover of a clopen set has a finite subcover,
making a finitely additive charge automatically countably additive on
the clopen algebra (a premeasure), which Carath\'{e}odory
extends~\cite{rao1983, Halmos1950}; compatibility then
assembles these into $\hat{\mu}$ on the inverse limit
$\St(\mathcal{C})$~\cite[Theorem~1]{choksi1958}.  This is the
\emph{empirically adequate} object: it agrees with all charges on
cylinder events.

The measure $\hat{\mu}$ lives on $\St(\mathcal{C})$, not on
$\Omega$.  Descent to a probability measure $P$ on
$(\Omega, \sigma(\mathcal{C}))$ requires
\[
  \hat{\mu}\!\bigl(\pure(\Omega)\bigr) = 1,
\]
i.e., that $\hat{\mu}$ concentrates on the principal ultrafilters.
Such concentration implies each $\ell_i$ is $\sigma$-additive; the
converse holds when $\Omega$ realizes every countably-complete
ultrafilter of $\mathcal{C}$, a Loomis--Sikorski condition that is
automatic for countably generated systems but not in general.

Every Boolean algebra admits lattice ultrafilters --- maximal proper
filters --- by Zorn's lemma.  On a Boolean algebra, maximality
forces a dichotomy: for every~$a$, either $a \in F$ or
$a^c \in F$.  The proof extends~$F$ to include~$a^c$ whenever
$a \notin F$; the extension remains proper because
$a^c \wedge f \neq \bot$ for all $f \in F$, which follows from
distributivity.  This dichotomy makes every lattice ultrafilter a
2-valued homomorphism, assigning each $a \in \mathcal{C}$ a definite
value in $\{0, 1\}$.  On a non-distributive OML, the extension
argument fails, and maximal proper filters need not be 2-valued.

Moreover, any subset $S \subseteq \St(\mathcal{C})$ that projects
surjectively onto each $\St(B_i)$ can serve as a realisation space:
set $\Omega = S$ with evaluation maps given by the restriction maps.
The algebra imposes no constraint on the choice of $\Omega$.

\begin{proposition}[Boolean commensurability]
\label{prop:boolean-comm}
For a directed system of Boolean algebras with compatible charges:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item EA is unconditional: $\hat{\mu}$ on $\St(\mathcal{C})$
    always exists.
  \item PR requires each $\ell_i$ to be $\sigma$-additive, and is
    equivalent to it under the Loomis--Sikorski realization condition
    on $\Omega$ (automatic for countably generated systems).
  \item VDR holds unconditionally: $\St(\mathcal{C})$ consists
    entirely of 2-valued homomorphisms.
\end{enumerate}
The obstruction at the EA--PR transition is $\sigma$-additivity of the
charges, together with the Loomis--Sikorski realization condition on
$\Omega$ (automatic for countably generated systems); both are
conditions on the charges and the realization, not on the algebra.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Part~(a) is the Stone construction above~\cite{rao1983, choksi1958}.
Part~(b): descent to $\Omega$ requires $\hat\mu(\pure(\Omega)) = 1$,
which forces each $\ell_i$ $\sigma$-additive, and conversely under the
realization condition (discussion above).  Part~(c): ultrafilters on
a Boolean algebra are exactly the 2-valued homomorphisms; their
existence follows from Zorn's lemma applied to the filter generated
by any non-zero element.
\end{proof}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Orthomodular observation algebras}
\label{sec:oml}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Proposition~\ref{prop:boolean-comm} depends on distributivity at
exactly one point: the existence of 2-valued homomorphisms.  When
observations are incompatible, this fails.  The non-distributive
structure need not be postulated: Gunji et
al.~\cite{Gunji2026} show that gluing Boolean algebras along
shared boundaries via categorical pushout produces an orthomodular
lattice, generically non-distributive.  The OML is \emph{forced}
by the context structure.

\begin{definition}
An \emph{ortholattice} is a bounded lattice
$A = (A,\, \wedge,\, \vee,\, {}^\perp,\, 0,\, 1)$ with an
orthocomplementation ${}^\perp : A \to A$ satisfying
$a \wedge a^\perp = 0$, \; $a \vee a^\perp = 1$, \;
$a^{\perp\perp} = a$, and \; $a \leq b \Rightarrow b^\perp \leq a^\perp$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}
An \emph{orthomodular lattice} (OML) is an ortholattice satisfying
the \emph{orthomodular law}:
\[
  a \leq b
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  b = a \vee (a^\perp \wedge b).
\]
\end{definition}

\noindent Every Boolean algebra is an OML (the orthomodular law
follows from distributivity), but the converse fails.  An OML is
Boolean if and only if it is
distributive~\cite[Proposition~2.3]{McDonaldBimbo2023}.

\begin{example}
The lattice $L(H)$ of closed linear subspaces of a separable Hilbert
space $H$ with $\dim H \geq 3$, ordered by inclusion with
$U^\perp = \{x \in H : \langle x, y \rangle = 0 \;\forall y \in U\}$,
is an orthomodular lattice that is not
distributive~\cite[Chapter~1]{Kalmbach1983}.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Incompatible measurements]
Three binary measurements $A$, $B$, $C$, where $(A,B)$ and $(A,C)$
can be performed jointly but $(B,C)$ cannot.  The jointly measurable
pairs generate Boolean subalgebras; the full observation algebra is
orthomodular, not Boolean.
\end{example}

Two elements $a, b$ in an OML \emph{commute} if they generate a
Boolean subalgebra.  A maximal set of pairwise commuting elements
forms a \emph{Boolean context} --- a maximal Boolean subalgebra
of~$A$.

\begin{definition}
A \emph{state} on an OML $A$ is a function $s : A \to [0,1]$ with
$s(1) = 1$ and
\[
  a \perp b
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  s(a \vee b) = s(a) + s(b),
\]
where $a \perp b$ means $a \leq b^\perp$.
\end{definition}

\noindent A state is additive on \emph{orthogonal} pairs only ---
not on arbitrary joins.  This is the OML analogue of a finitely
additive charge on a Boolean algebra.

\begin{definition}
A state $s$ is \emph{dispersion-free} if $s(a) \in \{0,1\}$ for
all $a \in A$.
\end{definition}

\noindent A dispersion-free state assigns a definite truth value
to every proposition in $A$ --- the statistical/dispersion-free
distinction in the standard quantum-logic
sense~\cite{BeltramettiCassinelli1981}.  Every 2-valued homomorphism is
dispersion-free; the converse holds on Boolean algebras (where
dispersion-free states are exactly the ultrafilters) and on
$L(H)$, but \emph{fails} on a general OML: a dispersion-free state
need not be multiplicative across incompatible propositions.  The
minimal witness is $\mathrm{MO}_2$ (the horizontal sum of two Boolean
$2^2$ blocks): assigning $1$ to one atom of each block gives a
dispersion-free state $s$, yet for the gap pair $a, b$ with
$a \wedge b = 0$ and $s(a) = s(b) = 1$ one has
$s(a \wedge b) = 0 \neq 1 = s(a) \wedge s(b)$, so $s$ is not a
homomorphism.  Throughout, the operative notion of value-definiteness
is the dispersion-free state (equivalently, non-emptiness of
$\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{df}}(A)$), not the strictly stronger 2-valued
homomorphism.

\begin{theorem}[Kochen--Specker~\cite{KochenSpecker1967}]
\label{thm:KS}
If $\dim H \geq 3$, there is no dispersion-free state on $L(H)$.
\end{theorem}

\noindent For $\dim H = 2$, dispersion-free states exist; the
obstruction is specific to $\dim H \geq 3$.

\begin{theorem}[Gleason~\cite{Gleason1957}]
\label{thm:gleason}
If $H$ is a separable Hilbert space with $\dim H \geq 3$, every
$\sigma$-additive state on $L(H)$ is of the form
$s(P) = \mathrm{tr}(\rho P)$ for a unique density operator
$\rho$ on~$H$.
\end{theorem}

The analogue of Stone duality for OMLs is the
McDonald--Bimb\'{o} duality~\cite{McDonaldBimbo2023}.

Let $A$ be an OML.  A \emph{filter} on $A$ is a non-empty subset
$x \subseteq A$ that is upward closed ($a \in x$ and $a \leq b$
implies $b \in x$) and closed under finite meets ($a, b \in x$
implies $a \wedge b \in x$).  A filter is \emph{proper} if
$0 \notin x$; the unique \emph{improper} filter is $\omega = A$.

For each $a \in A$, the \emph{principal filter} generated by $a$ is
${\uparrow}a = \{b \in A : a \leq b\}$.  Write $F(A)$ for the
collection of all filters on $A$, and $P(A) \subseteq F(A)$ for the
subcollection of principal filters.

\begin{definition}[McDonald--Bimb\'{o}~\cite{McDonaldBimbo2023}]
\label{def:oml-dual}
Let $A$ be an OML.  The \emph{dual space} of $A$ is the ordered
relational topological space
\[
  S_0(A) = \bigl(F(A),\; \subseteq,\; \perp_A,\; P(A),\; \mathcal{T}\bigr),
\]
where:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item $F(A)$ is the set of all filters on $A$;
  \item $\subseteq$ is set inclusion;
  \item $x \perp_A y$ iff there exists $a \in A$ with $a \in x$ and
    $a^\perp \in y$;
  \item $P(A)$ is the collection of principal filters;
  \item $\mathcal{T}$ is the topology generated by the subbasis
    $\{h(a) : a \in A\} \cup \{F(A) \setminus h(a) : a \in A\}$,
    where $h(a) = \{x \in F(A) : a \in x\}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[McDonald--Bimb\'{o}~\cite{McDonaldBimbo2023}]
\label{thm:oml-duality}
$S_0(A)$ is an orthomodular space (compact, totally disconnected).
The category of OMLs and homomorphisms is dually equivalent to the
category of orthomodular spaces and continuous weak p-morphisms.
Moreover, $A \cong \CO(S_0(A))^\dagger$, the OML of clopen
$\perp$-stable subsets of $S_0(A)$.
\end{theorem}

\noindent
A subset $U \subseteq S_0(A)$ is \emph{$\perp$-stable} if
$U = U^{\perp\perp}$, where
$U^\perp = \{x : x \perp_A y \text{ for all } y \in U\}$ ---
i.e.\ $U$ is closed under the double dual-orthocomplement.
We write $\CO(S_0(A))^\dagger$ for the sub-OML of clopen
$\perp$-stable subsets; its meet is intersection and its complement the
dual orthocomplement, but its join is \emph{not} set union.

The critical differences from Stone duality:

\medskip
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Feature & Boolean (Stone) & OML (McDonald--Bimb\'{o}) \\
\midrule
Dual points & Ultrafilters & All filters \\
Distinguished subset & None & $P(A)$ (principal filters) \\
Clopens & Boolean algebra & OML \\
Orthogonality & Trivial & Non-trivial ($\perp_A$) \\
2-valued homomorphisms & Plentiful (Zorn) & May not exist (KS) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\medskip

\noindent In Stone duality, the principal filters (i.e., the
principal ultrafilters $\pure(\omega)$ for $\omega \in \Omega$) are
\emph{invisible}: they play no distinguished role in the dual space.
Which ultrafilters are principal depends on the choice of $\Omega$.

In the McDonald--Bimb\'{o} duality, $P(A)$ is \emph{part of the
structure} of the dual space: the orthomodular frame conditions
(Definition~\ref{def:oml-dual}) explicitly reference $P(A)$.  This
is the algebraic formalisation: for non-distributive algebras,
the distinction between realised and unrealised observation-patterns
is constrained by the algebra.  Distributivity is the condition
under which this constraint vanishes.

\subsection{Descent to the dual space and its obstruction}
\label{sec:descent}

\noindent
$S_0(A)$ is compact, so the topological machinery applies.
But the algebraic input is weaker.  A state $s$ on $A$ defines
\[
  \mu(h(a)) = s(a)
\]
on $\CO(S_0(A))^\dagger$.

In the Boolean case, $\Clop(\St(\mathcal{C}))$ is a Boolean algebra
of sets, and $s$ is finitely additive on it.  The
Carath\'{e}odory--Choksi extension machinery applies: compactness of
$\St(\mathcal{C})$ gives $\sigma$-subadditivity, and the extension
to a Baire measure is automatic.

In the OML case, $\CO(S_0(A))^\dagger$ is an OML of sets --- closed
under $\cap$ and ${}^\perp$ but \emph{not} under arbitrary $\cup$.
The state $s$ is additive on orthogonal pairs only.
Carath\'{e}odory does not apply: there is no Boolean algebra of
sets on which $s$ is finitely additive.

For the prototypical case $A = L(H)$ with $\dim H \geq 3$,
Gleason's theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:gleason}) supplies a
$\sigma$-additive measure \emph{on the lattice} $L(H)$ for every
$\sigma$-additive state: every such state arises from a density
operator via the Born rule.  This is a measure on $A$ itself, and is
\emph{not} the same as a measure on the dual space $S_0(A)$ --- a
distinction the Boolean case obscures and the OML case forces.  Indeed
the passage to the dual space \emph{fails} for $L(H)$: no normal
(Gleason) state extends to a charge on the clopens of $S_0(L(H))$.  This
is a finitary non-extendability of the
Kochen--Specker~\cite{KochenSpecker1967} and Pitowsky
correlation-polytope~\cite{Pitowsky1989} type, whose mechanism we
sketch rather than prove in full.\footnote{%
Within a single $2$-dimensional subspace $e$, the lines
$p$ and their orthocomplements-within-$e$ furnish, for every $k$,
$2k$ pairwise meet-zero elements whose $\perp$-stable clopen images are
pairwise disjoint, while orthoadditivity gives
$s(p) + s(e \ominus p) = s(e)$.  A charge would then force
$k\,s(e) \leq 1$ for all $k$, so $s(e) = 0$ for every
finite-dimensional $e$ --- excluding every normal state.  This is the
$2$-dimensional case of the Pitowsky correlation-polytope
condition~\cite{Pitowsky1989}; $\sigma$-completeness of the lattice is
irrelevant, the obstruction being finitary.  Only \emph{singular} states
($s(e) = 0$ on all finite-dimensional $e$) escape, and whether one
extends is open.}  For general OMLs the extension problem is
open.\footnote{The extension splits into two axes that the Boolean
case fuses.  The \emph{extension axis} --- passing from the
orthogonally additive state on the OML of $\perp$-stable clopens to
a charge on the full Boolean algebra of clopens of $S_0(A)$ --- is
the classical Boolean extension problem~\cite{HornTarski1948}: since
the representation map preserves meets, meet-zero elements map to
disjoint sets, so the condition is $\sum_i s(a_i) \leq 1$ over
pairwise-meet-zero families, a Pitowsky polytope
condition~\cite{Pitowsky1989} strictly stronger than orthoadditivity
when the OML is non-distributive.  It is not governed by
$\sigma$-additivity, and in the finite case is a decidable linear
program.  The \emph{descent axis} --- whether the resulting measure
concentrates on the physical points --- is what $\sigma$-additivity
governs.  For $L(H)$, Gleason settles the lattice measure, but the
extension axis itself fails (preceding footnote): Gleason does
\emph{not} deliver the dual-space measure.}

The conceptual separation between a measure on the lattice and the
non-existence of a measure concentrated on points is, in the von
Neumann algebra setting, already present in D\"{o}ring's analysis of
the spectral presheaf~\cite{Doring2008}: states correspond to measures
on the clopen subobjects of the presheaf $\Sigma$
($\text{PR}_{\text{lat}}$ in our terms), while the absence of global
sections of $\Sigma$ is the Kochen--Specker obstruction
($\text{PR}_{\text{dual}}$ failing).  Our contribution is not this
separation as such but its transposition: we phrase it through the
McDonald--Bimb\'{o} duality for orthomodular lattices rather than the
topos-theoretic presheaf, identify $\sigma$-additivity as the precise
marker of the descent axis (D\"{o}ring's measures are finitely additive
in general), and read the whole off against the van Fraassen empirical
adequacy/realism debate, a framing absent from the topos literature.

Even when probabilistic descent succeeds, value-definite descent
does not.  The Bub--Clifton
theorem~\cite{BubClifton1996} addresses what \emph{can} be
salvaged:

\begin{theorem}[Bub--Clifton~\cite{BubClifton1996};
Halvorson--Clifton~\cite{HalvorsonClifton1999}]
\label{thm:bub-clifton}
Given a state $\rho$ on $L(H)$ and a preferred observable $R$,
there is a unique maximal sublattice
$D(\rho, R) \subseteq L(H)$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item $D(\rho, R)$ is a Boolean algebra;
  \item $D(\rho, R)$ contains the spectral projections of $R$;
  \item The restriction of $\rho$ to $D(\rho, R)$ is a mixture of
    dispersion-free states.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\noindent In the present vocabulary, condition~(iii) says that
$D(\rho, R)$ is the maximal Boolean subalgebra on which VDR is
available --- restating Halvorson and
Clifton~\cite{HalvorsonClifton1999} in the EA/PR/VDR framework.
It depends on $R$: different preferred observables yield different
maximal Boolean subalgebras.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Distributivity as the commensurability condition}
\label{sec:commensurability}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

The status of the three positions depends on the algebra.  In the OML
case probabilistic realism splits into two grades that the Boolean case
fuses: $\text{PR}_{\text{lat}}$, a $\sigma$-additive measure on the
\emph{lattice} $A$ --- states as measures on the projection lattice in
the sense of the algebraic quantum-logic
tradition~\cite{Redei1998} (Gleason, for $L(H)$) --- and
$\text{PR}_{\text{dual}}$,
a measure on the \emph{dual} $S_0(A)$ concentrating on the physical
points.  We take $\text{PR}_{\text{dual}}$ to be the \emph{stronger}
grade --- a dual-space measure restricts, on the $\perp$-stable clopens,
to a finitely orthoadditive set function recovering $s$; promoting that
to a $\sigma$-additive lattice measure ($\text{PR}_{\text{lat}}$) is free
in the Boolean case but only conditional in general (see~$(\ast)$).  The
relationship the two grades certainly exhibit is a \emph{witnessed
separation}, not a proven nesting: for $L(H)$, $\text{PR}_{\text{lat}}$
holds while $\text{PR}_{\text{dual}}$ fails.  The positions, ordered with
the stronger to the right (a node marked $\dagger$ is not realised for
$L(H)$):
\[
  \underbrace{\text{EA}}_{\text{$\perp$-stable set fn.}}
  \;\Longleftarrow\;
  \underbrace{\text{PR}_{\text{lat}}}_{\text{Gleason }(L(H))}
  \;\overset{(\ast)}{\Longleftarrow}\;
  \underbrace{\text{PR}_{\text{dual}}^{\dagger}}_{\text{fails for }L(H)}
  \;\Longleftarrow\;
  \underbrace{\text{VDR}_{\sigma}^{\dagger}}_{\text{KS-blocked (OML)}}
\]
The implications run weak-from-strong at the stated grade:
$\mathrm{VDR}_{\sigma}$ (a global $\sigma$-additive dispersion-free state in
the dual-realising scope) implies $\text{PR}_{\text{dual}}$ (a dual-space measure);
$\text{PR}_{\text{dual}}$ implies $\text{PR}_{\text{lat}}$ only
conditionally (the restriction of $\hat\mu$ to the $\perp$-stable clopens
is finitely orthoadditive, but its $\sigma$-additivity on the lattice
needs $\hat\mu$ to be null on countable-join defects --- free in the
Boolean case, conditional in general; see~$(\ast)$); and
$\text{PR}_{\text{lat}}$ implies EA.  For
$L(H)$ the two strongest positions are not realised: VDR by
Kochen--Specker, and $\text{PR}_{\text{dual}}$ by the
Kochen--Specker / Pitowsky non-extendability of \S\ref{sec:descent}
--- the \emph{upward} extension construction
$\text{PR}_{\text{lat}} \to \text{PR}_{\text{dual}}$ (build a dual-space
measure from the lattice measure) is obstructed, governed by
distributivity rather than $\sigma$-additivity.  Gleason delivers
$\text{PR}_{\text{lat}}$ but Gleason does not climb to
$\text{PR}_{\text{dual}}$.  The two grades coincide only in the Boolean
case, where the extension is free and the ladder collapses to the single
$\text{EA} \Leftarrow \text{PR}$ of the Boolean case.  ($\ast$)~The arrow
$\text{PR}_{\text{lat}} \Leftarrow \text{PR}_{\text{dual}}$ is free in
the Boolean case but holds only conditionally in general (it requires a
$\sigma$-continuity hypothesis on the dual measure that the finitary
McDonald--Bimb\'{o} duality does not supply); we use it only in the
direction stated.  EA in the OML case is the $\perp$-stable clopen set
function (the hedged reading below), not itself a dual-space measure.

\begin{theorem}[Commensurability]
\label{thm:commensurability}
Let $A$ be a complemented lattice.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item If $A$ is distributive \textup{(}Boolean\textup{)}, then
    EA, PR, and VDR are mutually commensurable:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item EA is unconditional
        \textup{(}Stone construction\textup{)}.
      \item PR requires the charges to be $\sigma$-additive, and is
        equivalent to it under the Loomis--Sikorski realization
        condition on $\Omega$.  Here
        $\text{PR}_{\text{lat}}$ and $\text{PR}_{\text{dual}}$ coincide:
        the $\perp$-stable extension step is free, so a lattice measure
        and a dual-space measure stand or fall together.
      \item VDR holds unconditionally
        \textup{(}ultrafilters exist\textup{)}.
    \end{itemize}
    Moreover, the choice of realisation space $\Omega$ is
    unconstrained by the algebra.

  \item If $A$ is non-distributive \textup{(}OML with
    $A \cong L(H)$, $\dim H \geq 3$\textup{)}, then:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item EA is unconditional \emph{in the $\perp$-stable set-function
        sense}.\footnote{EA here is the orthogonally additive set
        function $\mu(h(a)) = s(a)$ on the $\perp$-stable clopens, which
        always exists.  It is \emph{not} a genuine Baire measure on
        $S_0(A)$: promoting it to one is the extension step, which for
        $L(H)$ \emph{fails} for every normal state
        (\S\ref{sec:descent}) and for general OMLs is open.  The unconditional
        claim is thus about the clopen set function, not the dual-space
        measure.}
      \item $\text{PR}_{\text{lat}}$ holds \textup{(}Gleason,
        Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:gleason})}: a $\sigma$-additive measure
        on the lattice $L(H)$.
      \item $\text{PR}_{\text{dual}}$ \emph{fails} for every normal
        state: no charge on $S_0(L(H))$ \emph{extending $s$} exists
        (Kochen--Specker / Pitowsky non-extendability,
        \S\ref{sec:descent}), so a fortiori none concentrating on
        $P(A)$.  Equivalently,
        the extension step $\text{PR}_{\text{lat}} \to
        \text{PR}_{\text{dual}}$ is obstructed --- the one transition the
        Boolean case leaves free.
      \item VDR fails globally
        \textup{(}Kochen--Specker,
        Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:KS})}.
      \item VDR holds locally within each Boolean context
        \textup{(}Bub--Clifton,
        Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:bub-clifton})}.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Part~(a) is Proposition~\ref{prop:boolean-comm}.  For
part~(b): $\text{PR}_{\text{lat}}$ follows from Gleason's theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:gleason}), which supplies a $\sigma$-additive measure
on the lattice $L(H)$ for any $\sigma$-additive state.
$\text{PR}_{\text{dual}}$ fails for normal states by the
Kochen--Specker / Pitowsky non-extendability of \S\ref{sec:descent}: no
charge on the clopens of $S_0(L(H))$ \emph{extending $s$} exists, so no
such Baire measure on $S_0(L(H))$ and a fortiori none concentrating on
$P(A)$.  EA holds in the $\perp$-stable set-function sense
unconditionally --- the assignment $\mu(h(a)) = s(a)$ on
$\CO(S_0(A))^\dagger$ always exists; note this does \emph{not} go via
$\text{PR}_{\text{dual}}$ (which fails), since for $L(H)$ the genuine
dual-space measure does not exist.  VDR-failure is from
Kochen--Specker; local VDR from Bub--Clifton.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:distributivity}
Distributivity is sufficient for VDR.  For the physically central
case $A = L(H)$ with $\dim H \geq 3$, non-distributivity blocks
VDR.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Sufficiency: Theorem~\ref{thm:commensurability}(a).  Blockage:
Theorem~\ref{thm:commensurability}(b).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The dimensional restriction is necessary.  For $\dim H = 2$,
$L(\mathbb{C}^2)$ is non-distributive but admits dispersion-free
states.  More generally, horizontal sums of Boolean algebras and
certain finite OMLs are non-distributive yet admit dispersion-free
states~\cite[Chapter~4]{Kalmbach1983} --- though, as $\mathrm{MO}_2$ shows,
these dispersion-free states need not be 2-valued homomorphisms.
The sharp characterisation for general OMLs is: VDR is available
iff $A$ admits a dispersion-free state, which is a property of $A$,
not of distributivity alone.  Distributivity is sufficient
(ultrafilters exist) but not necessary in full generality.  For
the physically and epistemologically central case $\dim H \geq 3$,
it is the effective dividing line.
\end{remark}

The state notions underlying the typed containment
$\mathcal{S}(A) \supseteq \mathcal{S}_\sigma(A)
\supseteq \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{df}}^\sigma(A)$ are standard in quantum
logic~\cite{PtakPulmannova1991, Kalmbach1983}.  What we add is the
identification of three transitions of different mathematical
character, and the observation that distributivity controls which
are trivial.

\begin{definition}[Coherence filtration]
\label{def:hierarchy}
Let $A$ be a complemented lattice with a family of charges.
The state space of $A$ admits the descending filtration
\[
  \mathcal{S}(A)
  \;\supseteq\;
  \mathcal{S}_\sigma(A)
  \;\supseteq\;
  \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{df}}^\sigma(A),
\]
where:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\mathcal{S}(A)$ is the set of states on $A$
    (contextual coherence);
  \item $\mathcal{S}_\sigma(A) \subseteq \mathcal{S}(A)$ is the
    subset of $\sigma$-additive states
    (probabilistic coherence);
  \item $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{df}}^\sigma(A):=
    \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{df}}(A)\cap\mathcal{S}_\sigma(A)$ is the subset of
    $\sigma$-additive dispersion-free states (the filtration's
    value-definite endpoint).
\end{itemize}
Standalone VDR remains the predicate
$\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{df}}(A)\ne\varnothing$, with no automatic
$\sigma$-additivity clause.  The two formulations agree on finite carriers
and whenever $\sigma$-additivity is separately established; they are not
identified on an arbitrary infinite carrier.
Beneath the filtration sits a local condition:
\emph{consistency} requires only that each maximal Boolean
subalgebra of $A$ individually admits a compatible state.
\end{definition}

\noindent Each inclusion can be strict.  The three transitions have
different mathematical character:

\medskip
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
Transition & Character & Key & Obstruction \\
\midrule
Consistency $\to$ $\mathcal{S}(A)$
  & Pasting (topological)
  & Do local sections glue?
  & Contextuality \\[3pt]
$\mathcal{S}(A) \to \mathcal{S}_\sigma(A)$
  & Regularity (analytic)
  & Does the state have good limits?
  & $\sigma$-additivity \\[3pt]
$\mathcal{S}_\sigma(A) \to \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{df}}^\sigma(A)$
  & Sharpness (algebraic)
  & Can probabilities collapse to $\{0,1\}$?
  & Kochen--Specker \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\medskip

\noindent Distributivity controls two of the transitions but does not erase
the regularity question.  For a Boolean algebra:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Pasting} is trivial: every local assignment extends
    to a global state (distributivity guarantees compatibility).
  \item \emph{Sharpness} is free: ultrafilters exist (Zorn's lemma),
    so $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{df}}(A) \neq \varnothing$
    unconditionally.  This is standalone VDR; membership in
    $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{df}}^\sigma(A)$ requires the separate
    $\sigma$-additivity clause.
  \item The only non-trivial transition is \emph{regularity}:
    $\sigma$-additivity of the charges (with the realization condition
    on $\Omega$), as developed above.
\end{itemize}
For an OML with $\dim \geq 3$, every transition is non-trivial:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Pasting} is non-trivial: contextuality obstructions
    are possible.
  \item \emph{Regularity} requires Gleason-type results. (This
    filtration grades \emph{internal} states of $A$ --- i.e.\
    $\text{PR}_{\text{lat}}$, the lattice measure, which Gleason
    supplies for $L(H)$; the further passage to a measure on the dual
    space $S_0(A)$, namely $\text{PR}_{\text{dual}}$ --- the extension
    step, free in the Boolean case but blocked by non-distributivity ---
    is a separate question that for $L(H)$ \emph{fails} for normal
    states and for general OMLs is open.)
  \item \emph{Sharpness} is blocked: $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{df}}(A)
    = \varnothing$ (Kochen--Specker).
\end{itemize}

\noindent Distributivity is sufficient for standalone VDR and the Boolean
pasting statement; regularity remains the $\sigma$-additivity step.  For
$L(H)$ with $\dim \geq 3$, Kochen--Specker blocks even standalone VDR.

\begin{remark}[Relation to contextuality hierarchies]
Abramsky and Brandenburger~\cite{AbramskyBrandenburger2011}
introduced a hierarchy of contextuality --- probabilistic,
possibilistic, and strong --- grading the \emph{strength of the
obstruction} to a global section of a presheaf of probability
distributions.  Our filtration is complementary: it grades
\emph{what the algebra can support}, not how badly a global section
fails.  Their axis measures obstruction strength; ours measures
coherence demands.
\end{remark}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

The standard philosophical debate between scientific realism and
constructive empiricism treats the question as uniform: either
theories aim at truth or they aim at empirical adequacy.  The
algebraic analysis above lets us recast that choice as
\emph{parametrised} by the observation algebra.  For Boolean
observations, the mathematics does not prefer one position over
another.  For non-Boolean observations ($\dim \geq 3$), VDR is
algebraically impossible; the constructive empiricist
position~\cite{vanFraassen1980} is the generic one, and
value-definite realism is the special case --- the case in which
the observation algebra happens to be distributive.

That value-definiteness is \emph{separable} from realism --- that the
realist is not, qua realist, entitled to simultaneous definite values,
and that what underwrites them is the Boolean (distributive) structure
of jointly performable observations --- is not new with us.  It is the
conclusion Stairs~\cite{Stairs1983} reached directly: the requirement
that all magnitudes carry classically definite values constrains the
admissible structures for reasons independent of realism, turning on
Boolean representability rather than on realist commitment; his
realism-without-value-definiteness position is, in the present
vocabulary, the $\text{PR}$ layer with $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{df}}$
empty.  The parallel link between realism and distributivity is the
Putnam~\cite{Putnam1968}--Dummett battleground (Putnam revises logic to
keep value-definiteness; we instead read its loss off the algebra), and
the primacy of the Boolean/non-Boolean event structure over any hidden
dynamics is the Bub--Pitowsky~\cite{BubPitowsky2010} thesis, itself
continuous with Pitowsky's reading of quantum mechanics as a
non-classical theory of probability on the projection
lattice~\cite{Pitowsky2006}; the broader algebraic-foundations setting
is surveyed in Landsman~\cite{Landsman2017}.  What the
filtration adds to these is not a new philosophical position but an
organising one: it locates EA, $\text{PR}_{\text{lat}}$,
$\text{PR}_{\text{dual}}$, and VDR on a single axis indexed by
distributivity, and separates the obstructions (pasting, regularity,
sharpness) that the debate treats as one.

Several classical positions translate directly into this vocabulary,
which is offered as a uniform restatement rather than a sharpening of
any one of them.
The EPR argument for completeness~\cite{EPR1935} amounts to the
demand that all observables simultaneously possess definite values
--- i.e., VDR --- which the Kochen--Specker theorem blocks
algebraically for $\dim H \geq 3$.  Bohr's
complementarity corresponds to the non-triviality of the pasting
transition: incompatible contexts cannot be simultaneously
realised.
Bell's beables~\cite{HalvorsonClifton1999} --- the observables
that \emph{can} have definite values --- are the elements of the
maximal Boolean subalgebra $D(\rho, R)$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:bub-clifton}), restating Bub--Clifton in this
vocabulary.  De~Finetti's operationalism is
EA in the Boolean case; Pitowsky~\cite{Pitowsky1989} develops the
quantum analogue via Dutch book arguments on $L(H)$.

The analysis is not specific to quantum mechanics.  Any collection
of individually Boolean observation contexts --- jointly performable
measurements in behavioural
science~\cite{DzhafarovKujala2017}, jointly queryable attributes
in machine learning~\cite{DerrWilliamson2023}, simultaneously
administrable treatments in experimental design --- produces an
observation algebra via categorical
gluing~\cite{Gunji2026}.  If the contexts are mutually compatible,
the colimit is Boolean and VDR is available.  If not, the colimit
is orthomodular and VDR may be blocked.  In principle this suggests a
diagnostic: when a deterministic latent-state model fails, the failure
may be algebraic --- the observation algebra does not support VDR ---
rather than a consequence of noise or insufficient data.  We state this
only to mark its limits.  By the equivalence used
above\footnote{Non-distributivity of the colimit is equivalent to the
non-existence of a joint distribution, i.e.\ to contextuality as failure
of a global section~\cite{AbramskyBrandenburger2011, Fine1982}; the
categorical form of this equivalence, orthomodularity as the obstruction
to a global section, is~\cite{Gunji2026}.} ``the
failure is algebraic, not statistical'' is the same diagnostic as ``the
failure is contextuality, not nuisance,'' which the
Contextuality-by-Default programme~\cite{DzhafarovKujala2017,
CervantesDzhafarov2018} already operationalises on empirical data ---
and operationalises in the harder regime, since the sheaf/orthomodular
formulation requires consistent connectedness (no marginal-selectivity
violation), whereas real data routinely violates it and
Contextuality-by-Default is built to handle that case.  The
contribution here is therefore not a new empirical test; it is the
placement of that already-finitary diagnostic within the filtration,
where the one obstruction it cannot see --- the $\sigma$-additive,
dual-space ($\text{PR}_{\text{dual}}$) layer, which is infinitary ---
becomes visible as a distinct transition.

Several directions remain open:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{The OML extension problem.}
    Does every state on a general OML extend to a $\sigma$-additive
    measure on $S_0(A)$, as in the Boolean case?  For $L(H)$ the answer
    is \emph{negative} for \emph{every} state: Gleason supplies the
    lattice measure but no dual-space charge exists --- the
    Kochen--Specker / Pitowsky non-extendability of \S\ref{sec:descent}
    settles the normal states, and a
    finite family of infinite-dimensional, pairwise-meet-zero subspaces
    (a copy of $\mathrm{MO}_2$ with infinite-dimensional atoms) settles
    the singular states. The general OML case remains open.

  \item \emph{Dynamical structure.}
    If the observation algebra carries a group action (stationarity,
    exchangeability), how does symmetry interact with the coherence
    hierarchy?  The Boolean case is partially understood
    (de~Finetti's theorem for exchangeability); the OML case is
    unexplored.

  \item \emph{Causal inference.}
    Holland's fundamental problem of causal
    inference~\cite{Holland1986} --- that both potential outcomes
    $Y(0)$ and $Y(1)$ cannot be observed for the same unit --- is
    structurally analogous to Bohr complementarity.  Whether partial
    identification constraints generate non-Boolean algebraic
    structure, and whether the commensurability theorem applies to
    counterfactual reasoning, is unexplored.

  \item \emph{Relation to the topos programme.}
    The Bohrification programme~\cite{IshamButterfield1998,
    DoeringIsham2008, HeunenLandsmanSpitters2009} encodes related
    structure more powerfully: the spectral presheaf over the poset
    of Boolean subalgebras captures VDR-failure as absence of global
    sections (Kochen--Specker), and local VDR within each Boolean
    context is built into the presheaf's local sections.  Our
    filtration operates at a coarser level --- distinguishing EA, PR,
    and VDR as properties of the algebra's state space rather than as
    sheaf-cohomological obstructions.  The McDonald--Bimb\'{o} duality
    offers a complementary perspective: a single topological dual
    space (not a presheaf over contexts) in which principal filters
    are structurally distinguished.  Whether the EA/PR/VDR vocabulary
    translates into the topos framework, and whether the filtration
    refines or merely simplifies the sheaf-theoretic picture, remains
    open.
\end{enumerate}

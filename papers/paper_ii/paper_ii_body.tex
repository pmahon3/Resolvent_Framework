\begin{abstract}
Given a probability measure on the observable $\sigma$-algebra — the output of
Paper~I — this paper derives the dynamics of the system.  The central object is
the \emph{predictive kernel}: the regular conditional distribution of the future
observable $F$ given the current query $Q$.  From the predictive kernel we
construct the \emph{minimal predictive query} $Q_*$, a canonical measurable map
from the sample space into the space of probability measures on outcomes.  The
predictive factorization theorem shows that $Q_*$ carries all conditional
information about $F$: conditional expectations of functions of $F$ factor
through $Q_*$.  Indexing $Q_*$ over a discrete time horizon then yields a
family of Markov kernels $\{\Pi_t\}$ and a corresponding family of
\emph{predictive operators} $\{K_t\}$.  The Chapman--Kolmogorov equation for
$\{\Pi_t\}$ is derived from temporal coherence of prediction, not assumed; it
produces the semigroup law $K_{t+s} = K_t \circ K_s$.  The dual
\emph{Koopman--Perron} relation $\int K_t g\, d\mu = \int g\, d(P_t^* \mu)$
connects operator-on-functions and measure-on-states into a single picture.
When the kernels are Dirac measures, the operators reduce to classical Koopman
operators and the semigroup law becomes the flow law $\varphi_{t+s} =
\varphi_t \circ \varphi_s$.  All results are formalized in Lean~4 / Mathlib
with zero sorry obligations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{The problem.}
Paper~I produced a probability measure $P$ on the observable $\sigma$-algebra
from coherent structured observations.  The question Paper~II addresses is:
what does $P$ say about how the system \emph{moves}?  The answer is not an
additional assumption.  Once $P$ is fixed, the temporal structure of prediction
is already determined.

\paragraph{The Koopman picture.}
The classical approach to dynamics centers on the \emph{Koopman operator}
$U_T f = f \circ T$, which acts on observable functions by precomposing with
the dynamics $T$.  This paper takes the dual view: dynamics is what the
observer \emph{predicts} about the future, not what the system \emph{does} in
state space.  The predictive operator $K_t$ takes a function $g$ of future
outcomes and returns the observer's expected value of $g$ at each current
predictive state.  When the dynamics is deterministic, $K_t g = g \circ
\varphi_t$ and the two pictures coincide.  In the stochastic case, $K_t$ is the
more fundamental object.

\paragraph{What this paper shows.}
Starting from a probability space $(\Omega, P)$, a current query $Q : \Omega \to
\alpha$, and a future observable $F : \Omega \to \beta$, we construct:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item the \emph{predictive kernel} $\Pi_Q$, the regular conditional
    distribution of $F$ given $Q$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:predictive-factorization});
  \item the \emph{minimal predictive query} $Q_* = \varphi_Q \circ Q$, which
    encodes the conditional law as a single measurable map into
    $\mathcal{P}(\beta)$ (Definition~\ref{def:minimal-predictive});
  \item a family of predictive operators $\{K_t\}$ satisfying the semigroup
    law $K_{t+s} = K_t \circ K_s$
    (Theorem~\ref{thm:semigroup});
  \item the Koopman--Perron duality between $K_t$ and the push-forward $P_t^*$
    on distributions (Theorem~\ref{thm:koopman-perron});
  \item the deterministic specialization: Dirac kernels recover classical Koopman
    operators (Theorem~\ref{thm:deterministic}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Relation to Paper~I.}
Paper~I took the output of structured observations as input and produced a
probability measure.  Paper~II takes that measure as given and asks what
temporal structure it carries.  The query system language of Paper~I
specializes here to a single probability space $(\Omega, P)$ with two
distinguished measurable functions: $Q$ (the present query) and $F$ (the
future observable).  The formalism is self-contained but the conceptual
motivation flows from Paper~I.

\paragraph{Lean formalization.}
All results in this paper are formalized in Lean~4 / Mathlib in two files:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{PredictiveState.lean} — the predictive kernel, minimal
    predictive query, and predictive factorization theorem (zero sorry
    obligations).
  \item \texttt{PredictiveOperators.lean} — the semigroup, Koopman--Perron
    duality, and deterministic specialization (zero sorry obligations).
\end{itemize}
The key Mathlib ingredient is \texttt{ProbabilityTheory.Kernel.Disintegration},
which provides the Rokhlin disintegration theorem for standard Borel spaces,
and \texttt{Probability.Kernel.Composition.IntegralCompProd}, which gives the
Bochner--Fubini theorem for kernel compositions.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

Throughout this paper, $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)$ is a probability space.  We
fix two measurable functions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a \emph{current query} $Q : \Omega \to \alpha$, where $(\alpha,
    \mathcal{A})$ is a measurable space;
  \item a \emph{future observable} $F : \Omega \to \beta$, where $(\beta,
    \mathcal{B})$ is a standard Borel space.
\end{itemize}
The standard Borel hypothesis on $\beta$ is used once: it is the hypothesis
under which the Rokhlin disintegration theorem guarantees the existence of a
regular conditional distribution.  All other results hold in greater generality.

We write $\mathcal{P}(\beta)$ for the space of probability measures on $\beta$,
equipped with its canonical (Giry) $\sigma$-algebra.  The \emph{joint
pushforward} is the measure $\rho := P \circ (Q, F)^{-1}$ on $\alpha \times
\beta$; its marginals are $\rho_\alpha = P \circ Q^{-1}$ and $\rho_\beta = P
\circ F^{-1}$.

\begin{remark}[Notation]
  In Lean~4 / Mathlib, the joint pushforward is \texttt{P.map (fun o => (Q o,
  F o))}, and the conditional kernel is \texttt{.condKernel} applied to this
  measure.  All Lean definitions use this route.
\end{remark}

\section{The predictive kernel and the minimal predictive query}
\label{sec:predictive}

\subsection{The predictive kernel}

\begin{definition}[Predictive kernel]
\label{def:predictive-kernel}
The \emph{predictive kernel} $\Pi_Q : \alpha \to \mathcal{P}(\beta)$ is the
regular conditional distribution of $F$ given $Q$:
\[
  \Pi_Q(q, A) \;=\; P(F \in A \mid Q = q),
\]
defined as the disintegration kernel $\Pi_Q(q, \cdot) = \rho(\cdot \mid
\alpha)$ of the joint pushforward $\rho = P \circ (Q, F)^{-1}$ over its first
marginal.
\end{definition}

Concretely: $\Pi_Q$ is the unique (up to $P \circ Q^{-1}$-null sets) Markov
kernel $\alpha \to \mathcal{P}(\beta)$ such that
\[
  \int_A \Pi_Q(q, B)\, d(P \circ Q^{-1})(q) \;=\; P(Q \in A,\; F \in B)
\]
for all measurable $A \subseteq \alpha$ and $B \subseteq \beta$.

\begin{proposition}[Predictive compatibility, P2.2]
\label{prop:predictive-compat}
Let $\pi : \alpha \to \alpha'$ be measurable with $Q' = \pi \circ Q$.  Then
for $(P \circ Q^{-1})$-almost every $q \in \alpha$:
\[
  \Pi_Q(q, \cdot) \;=\; \Pi_{Q'}(\pi(q), \cdot).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The joint pushforward $\rho' = P \circ (Q', F)^{-1}$ satisfies $\rho' = \rho
\circ (\pi \times \mathrm{id})^{-1}$.  The claim follows from the
$P \circ Q^{-1}$-a.e.\ uniqueness of the disintegration of $\rho'$ over its
first marginal, combined with the covariance of condKernel under measurable
maps (\texttt{eq\_condKernel\_of\_measure\_eq\_compProd} in Mathlib).
\end{proof}

\subsection{The predictive law map and the minimal predictive query}

The predictive kernel $\Pi_Q$ maps each query value $q$ to a probability
measure on outcomes.  Packaging this as a single measurable map gives:

\begin{definition}[Predictive law map and minimal predictive query]
\label{def:minimal-predictive}
The \emph{predictive law map} is
\[
  \varphi_Q : \alpha \to \mathcal{P}(\beta), \qquad \varphi_Q(q) = \Pi_Q(q, \cdot).
\]
The \emph{minimal predictive query} is the composition
\[
  Q_* := \varphi_Q \circ Q : \Omega \to \mathcal{P}(\beta).
\]
The value $Q_*(\omega)$ is the full conditional law $P(F \in \cdot \mid Q =
Q(\omega))$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Measurability, P2.1/P2.3]
\label{prop:measurability}
The maps $\varphi_Q$ and $Q_*$ are measurable.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\varphi_Q$ is the underlying map of the Markov kernel $\Pi_Q$, which is
measurable by definition of a kernel (\texttt{Kernel.measurable} in Mathlib).
Since $\mathcal{P}(\beta)$ carries the subspace $\sigma$-algebra from the
Giry space of all measures on $\beta$, the wrap as a \texttt{ProbabilityMeasure}
is measurable by \texttt{Measurable.subtype\_mk}.  Measurability of $Q_*$ then
follows from composition.
\end{proof}

The minimal predictive query identifies observers with the same predictive law:

\begin{proposition}[Predictive equivalence, P2.4]
\label{prop:predictive-equiv}
For $\omega, \omega' \in \Omega$:
\[
  Q_*(\omega) = Q_*(\omega') \;\iff\; \Pi_Q(Q(\omega), \cdot) = \Pi_Q(Q(\omega'), \cdot).
\]
\end{proposition}

No quotient construction is needed: the state space is the image of $\varphi_Q$
inside the standard Borel space $\mathcal{P}(\beta)$.

\section{Predictive factorization}
\label{sec:factorization}

The predictive factorization theorem is the central result of this paper.  It
says that the conditional expectation of any bounded measurable function of the
future, given the current query, factors through the minimal predictive query.

\begin{theorem}[Predictive factorization, P2.5]
\label{thm:predictive-factorization}
For every bounded measurable $g : \beta \to \mathbb{R}$:
\[
  E[g(F) \mid \sigma(Q)] \;=\; \Bigl(\omega \mapsto \int g\, dQ_*(\omega)\Bigr)
  \quad P\text{-a.e.}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $h(\omega) := \int g\, dQ_*(\omega) = \int g\, d\Pi_Q(Q(\omega), \cdot)$.
We verify the two defining properties of the conditional expectation.

\emph{Measurability.}  The map $q \mapsto \int g\, d\Pi_Q(q, \cdot)$ is
strongly measurable (Bochner integrability of $g$ against a Markov kernel;
\texttt{StronglyMeasurable.integral\_kernel} in Mathlib).  Since $Q_* = \varphi_Q
\circ Q$, the map $h = (\text{predictive operator applied to } g) \circ Q_*$
is $\sigma(Q)$-measurable.

\emph{Set-integral identity.}  For every $\sigma(Q)$-measurable set $S =
Q^{-1}(A)$:
\begin{align*}
  \int_S h\, dP
  &= \int_{Q^{-1}(A)} \!\! \int g\, d\Pi_Q(Q(\omega), \cdot)\, dP(\omega) \\
  &= \int_A \int g\, d\Pi_Q(q, \cdot)\, d(P \circ Q^{-1})(q)
  \quad\text{(push-forward)} \\
  &= \int_A \int_{A \times \beta} g(f)\, d\rho(q, f)
  \quad\text{(disintegration identity)} \\
  &= \int_{Q^{-1}(A)} g(F(\omega))\, dP(\omega) = \int_S g(F)\, dP.
\end{align*}
The key step is the disintegration identity for $\rho = P \circ (Q, F)^{-1}$,
which in Mathlib is \texttt{Measure.setIntegral\_condKernel}.

By the a.e.\ uniqueness of conditional expectation
(\texttt{ae\_eq\_condExp\_of\_forall\_setIntegral\_eq}), $h = E[g(F) \mid
\sigma(Q)]$ $P$-a.e.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Predictive sufficiency, P2.5]
\label{cor:sufficiency}
$E[g(F) \mid \sigma(Q)] = E[g(F) \mid \sigma(Q_*)]$ $P$-a.e.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Since $Q_* = \varphi_Q \circ Q$, we have $\sigma(Q_*) \subseteq \sigma(Q)$.
The conditional expectation given $\sigma(Q)$ already factors through $Q_*$
by Theorem~\ref{thm:predictive-factorization}, so it is $\sigma(Q_*)$-measurable.
\end{proof}

The canonical predictive operator $K_{Q_*}$ is the map
\[
  (K_{Q_*} g)(q_*) \;=\; \int g\, dq_*, \qquad q_* \in \mathcal{P}(\beta).
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:predictive-factorization} then reads: $E[g(F) \mid \sigma(Q)]
= (K_{Q_*} g) \circ Q_*$ $P$-a.e.  This is the canonical operator associated
with the predictive state.

\begin{proposition}[Properties of $K_{Q_*}$, P2.6]
\label{prop:operator-properties}
The canonical predictive operator $K_{Q_*}$ is:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item \emph{positive}: $g \geq 0 \Rightarrow K_{Q_*} g \geq 0$;
  \item \emph{unital}: $K_{Q_*} \mathbf{1} = \mathbf{1}$;
  \item \emph{linear}: $K_{Q_*}(g + h) = K_{Q_*} g + K_{Q_*} h$,
    $K_{Q_*}(\lambda g) = \lambda K_{Q_*} g$;
  \item \emph{contractive}: $|K_{Q_*} g|(q_*) \leq \|g\|_\infty$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\section{The predictive semigroup}
\label{sec:semigroup}

So far, the setup has been static: a single query $Q$ predicting a single
future observable $F$.  To introduce dynamics, we index over a discrete time
horizon.  For each $t \in \mathbb{N}$, let $F_t : \Omega \to \gamma$ be the
observable at time $t$, and let $\Pi_t$ be the predictive kernel of $F_t$
given the current predictive state.

\subsection{Predictive kernel families}

\begin{definition}[Predictive kernel family and semigroup kernel]
\label{def:kernel-family}
A \emph{predictive kernel family} on a state space $\gamma$ is a family
$\{\Pi_t\}_{t \in \mathbb{N}}$ of Markov kernels $\Pi_t : \gamma \to
\mathcal{P}(\gamma)$ with $\Pi_0 = \delta$ (identity: $\Pi_0(q, \cdot) = \delta_q$).

A \emph{predictive semigroup kernel} is a predictive kernel family satisfying
the Chapman--Kolmogorov equation:
\[
  \Pi_{t+s}(q, \cdot) \;=\; \int_\gamma \Pi_t(q', \cdot)\, d\Pi_s(q, q')
  \quad\text{for all } q \in \gamma,\; t, s \in \mathbb{N}.
\]
In kernel notation: $\Pi_{t+s} = \Pi_t \circ_\kappa \Pi_s$ where $\circ_\kappa$
denotes kernel composition.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Derivation, not assumption]
The Chapman--Kolmogorov equation is \emph{derived} from the temporal coherence
of prediction (the same observable compatibility that determined $P$ in
Paper~I), not assumed.  For a system evolving under dynamics $T$, the kernel at
time $t+s$ sends state $q$ to the distribution of $q$ after $t$ and then $s$
steps of prediction, which is exactly the composition $\Pi_t \circ_\kappa
\Pi_s$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The predictive operator family}

\begin{definition}[Predictive operator family]
\label{def:predictive-op-family}
Given a predictive kernel family $\{\Pi_t\}$, the \emph{horizon-indexed
predictive operator} at horizon $t$ is
\[
  (K_t g)(q) \;:=\; \int g\, d\Pi_t(q, \cdot), \qquad g : \gamma \to \mathbb{R}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Basic properties, P3.1/P3.4]
\label{prop:basic-properties}
For any predictive kernel family $\{\Pi_t\}$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item \emph{Identity at zero}: $K_0 g = g$ for all measurable $g$;
  \item \emph{Positive}: $g \geq 0 \Rightarrow K_t g \geq 0$;
  \item \emph{Unital}: $K_t \mathbf{1} = \mathbf{1}$;
  \item \emph{Positive}: $g \geq 0 \Rightarrow K_t g \geq 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Identity at zero: $K_0 g(q) = \int g\, d\delta_q = g(q)$.  The remaining
properties are immediate from the Markov property (probability preserving) and
non-negativity of integration against a positive measure.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Semigroup property, P3.3]
\label{thm:semigroup}
Let $\{\Pi_t\}$ be a predictive semigroup kernel.  For every bounded measurable
$g : \gamma \to \mathbb{R}$:
\[
  K_{t+s} g \;=\; K_t(K_s g).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Using the Chapman--Kolmogorov equation $\Pi_{t+s} = \Pi_t \circ_\kappa \Pi_s$:
\begin{align*}
  (K_{t+s} g)(q)
  &= \int g\, d\Pi_{t+s}(q, \cdot) \\
  &= \int g\, d(\Pi_t \circ_\kappa \Pi_s)(q, \cdot) \\
  &= \int \Bigl(\int g\, d\Pi_t(q', \cdot)\Bigr)\, d\Pi_s(q, q')
  \quad\text{(Bochner--Fubini for kernel composition)} \\
  &= \int (K_t g)(q')\, d\Pi_s(q, q')
  \;=\; (K_t(K_s g))(q).
\end{align*}
The Bochner--Fubini step is \texttt{Kernel.integral\_comp} from
\texttt{Mathlib.Probability.Kernel.Composition.IntegralCompProd}.  The
boundedness hypothesis on $g$ is needed for integrability of $g$ against the
composed kernel.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Boundedness hypothesis]
The hypothesis that $g$ is bounded (i.e., $\|g\|_\infty < \infty$) is used
to guarantee that $g$ is integrable against the composed Markov kernel, which
has total mass 1.  The result extends to $g \in L^\infty(\gamma)$ by a
monotone class argument.
\end{remark}

\section{Koopman--Perron duality}
\label{sec:duality}

The predictive operators $K_t$ act on functions.  The dual objects act on
measures.

\begin{definition}[Koopman--Perron dual]
\label{def:kp-dual}
Given a predictive semigroup kernel $\{\Pi_t\}$ and a (finite) measure $\mu$
on $\gamma$, the \emph{Koopman--Perron push-forward} is
\[
  P_t^* \mu \;:=\; \mu \mathbin{\star} \Pi_t
  \;=\; \int_\gamma \Pi_t(q, \cdot)\, d\mu(q).
\]
In Mathlib notation: \texttt{$\mu$.bind (ker $t$)}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Koopman--Perron duality, P3.5]
\label{thm:koopman-perron}
For every bounded measurable $g : \gamma \to \mathbb{R}$ and finite measure
$\mu$ on $\gamma$:
\[
  \int_\gamma (K_t g)\, d\mu \;=\; \int_\gamma g\, d(P_t^* \mu).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\begin{align*}
  \int g\, d(P_t^* \mu)
  &= \int g\, d(\mu \mathbin{\star} \Pi_t)
  \;=\; \int g\, d\!\left(\int \Pi_t(q, \cdot)\, d\mu(q)\right) \\
  &= \int_\gamma \int g\, d\Pi_t(q, \cdot)\, d\mu(q)
  \quad\text{(Bochner--Fubini)} \\
  &= \int_\gamma (K_t g)(q)\, d\mu(q).
\end{align*}
The Bochner--Fubini step again uses \texttt{Kernel.integral\_comp}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The two sides of the duality]
$K_t$ acts on the \emph{function algebra}: it takes a present query $g$ and
returns its predicted value $t$ steps hence.  $P_t^*$ acts on the
\emph{distribution}: it takes a current distribution of predictive states and
evolves it $t$ steps forward.  Neither is primary; they are adjoint.  In the
classical (deterministic) specialization below, $K_t$ becomes the Koopman
operator $U_T^t$ and $P_t^*$ becomes the pushforward $T^t_* \mu$.
\end{remark}

\section{Deterministic specialization}
\label{sec:deterministic}

When the dynamics is deterministic, the stochastic picture collapses to the
classical Koopman picture.

\begin{theorem}[Deterministic specialization, P3.6]
\label{thm:deterministic}
Suppose $\Pi_t(q, \cdot) = \delta_{\varphi_t(q)}$ for a measurable map
$\varphi_t : \gamma \to \gamma$.  Then for every measurable $g$:
\[
  K_t g \;=\; g \circ \varphi_t.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$(K_t g)(q) = \int g\, d\delta_{\varphi_t(q)} = g(\varphi_t(q))$.  The
integral against a Dirac measure is \texttt{integral\_dirac'} in Mathlib.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Flow law, P3.6]
\label{cor:flow-law}
If $\Pi_t(q, \cdot) = \delta_{\varphi_t(q)}$ for all $t$, then
\[
  \varphi_{t+s} \;=\; \varphi_t \circ \varphi_s.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
From $\Pi_{t+s} = \Pi_t \circ_\kappa \Pi_s$ and the Dirac hypothesis:
\begin{align*}
  \delta_{\varphi_{t+s}(q)}
  &= \Pi_{t+s}(q, \cdot)
  \;=\; (\Pi_t \circ_\kappa \Pi_s)(q, \cdot) \\
  &= \Pi_s(q, \cdot) \mathbin{\star} \Pi_t
  \;=\; \delta_{\varphi_s(q)} \mathbin{\star} \Pi_t
  \;=\; \Pi_t(\varphi_s(q), \cdot)
  \;=\; \delta_{\varphi_t(\varphi_s(q))}.
\end{align*}
Since Dirac measures at distinct points are distinct (under
\texttt{SeparatesPoints}), we conclude $\varphi_{t+s}(q) = \varphi_t(\varphi_s(q))$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Classical Koopman]
In the deterministic case, $K_t g = g \circ \varphi_t = U_T^t g$ where $U_T$
is the classical Koopman operator.  The semigroup law $K_{t+s} = K_t \circ K_s$
becomes $U_T^{t+s} = U_T^t \circ U_T^s$, and the Koopman--Perron duality
$\int K_t g\, d\mu = \int g\, d(P_t^* \mu)$ recovers the change-of-variables
formula $\int g \circ T^t\, d\mu = \int g\, d(T^t_* \mu)$.
\end{remark}

\section{The observable dynamical system}
\label{sec:obs-dyn-sys}

The results above assemble into a single structure.

\begin{definition}[Observable dynamical system]
\label{def:obs-dyn-sys}
An \emph{observable dynamical system} is a triple
$(O_{Q_*},\, \nu_{Q_*},\, \{K_t\}_{t \in \mathbb{N}})$
where:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $O_{Q_*} = \mathcal{P}(\beta)$ is the \emph{predictive state space}
    (the image of $Q_*$);
  \item $\nu_{Q_*} = P \circ Q_*^{-1}$ is the \emph{predictive state
    distribution} (the pushforward of $P$ along $Q_*$);
  \item $\{K_t\}$ is the \emph{predictive operator family} on $O_{Q_*}$,
    satisfying the semigroup law and Markov properties above.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

The predictive state distribution $\nu_{Q_*}$ is stationary under $P_t^*$: if
$(\Omega, P)$ is stationary (i.e., $P \circ T^{-1} = P$), then $P_t^*
\nu_{Q_*} = \nu_{Q_*}$ for all $t$.  This connects the observer's prediction
to invariant measure theory; the details are deferred to the continuous-time
setting.

\section{Relation to Paper III}
\label{sec:bridge}

Paper~II has constructed the dynamics entirely from the probability measure $P$
and the query-future pair $(Q, F)$.  The predictive state space $O_{Q_*}$ is a
subset of $\mathcal{P}(\beta)$, equipped with the semigroup $\{K_t\}$.

Paper~III asks the converse question: \emph{when does the sequence of
time-delayed observations $\{Q \circ T^{-n} : n \in \mathbb{N}\}$ determine the
full state space?}  The answer — under a \emph{cyclic vector condition} on the
Koopman operator — is that the Stone space of the observable algebra generated
by time-delayed queries is isomorphic (as a measure space) to the original
state space.

The connection to Paper~II is direct: the Koopman operator that appears in the
cyclic vector condition is precisely the operator on $L^2(\Omega, P)$ induced by
the dynamics $T$, and the predictive operators $K_t$ are its compression to the
observable subalgebra.  When the cyclic vector condition holds, no information
about the state is lost in the observable layer, and $O_{Q_*}$ is (up to
measure-theoretic identification) the full state space.

\begin{remark}[Continuous-time deferred]
All results above are stated for discrete time $t \in \mathbb{N}$.  The
continuous-time ($t \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$) versions require the
infinitesimal generator of a $C_0$-semigroup and the Hille--Yosida theorem.
These are deferred to a subsequent treatment; the discrete-time results proved
here are the foundation.
\end{remark}

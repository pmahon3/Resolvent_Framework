\section{Introduction}

A finite system of measurement contexts may admit a consistent assignment of
truth values to events that no single underlying state realises --- the
algebraic signature of quantum contextuality. When the contexts are countable
and one demands countable additivity, a sharper question arises: can a
\emph{finitely} coherent pattern fail to extend to any global $\sigma$-additive
state, with the failure visible only at the $\sigma$-level? This paper isolates
that phenomenon for concrete orthomodular lattices, reduces it to an irreducible
set-theoretic core, and locates that core against the measure-theoretic and
operator-algebraic literature. We work throughout with two-valued states, the
non-distributive analogue of a $\{0,1\}$-valued measure.

A \emph{concrete $\sigma$-complete orthomodular lattice} (concrete $\sigma$-OML)
is a family $\Lo \subseteq \PO(\Omega)$ with
\[
  \varnothing, \Omega \in \Lo; \qquad
  A \in \Lo \implies A\orth := \Omega\setminus A \in \Lo; \qquad
  \{A_n\}\subseteq\Lo \text{ pairwise disjoint} \implies \textstyle\bigcup_n A_n \in \Lo,
\]
ordered by inclusion. Equivalently it is a \emph{$\sigma$-class}~\cite{NavaraPtak1983},
a Gudder concrete quantum logic~\cite{Gudder1969,PtakPulmannova1991}: the maximal
Boolean subalgebras (``blocks'') overlap but their union need not be Boolean. A
\emph{two-valued state} is $s\colon\Lo\to\{0,1\}$ with $s(\Omega)=1$ that is
additive over orthogonal joins lying in its domain: whenever $\{A_n\}\subseteq\Lo$
are pairwise disjoint with $\bigcup_n A_n\in\Lo$,
\[
  s\!\left(\textstyle\bigcup_n A_n\right)=\textstyle\sum_n s(A_n)
\]
(in particular $s(A)+s(A\orth)=1$ on complement pairs); it is \emph{$\sigma$-additive}
when this holds for countable such families. The same additivity is required of states
on a sub-orthoposet $B\subseteq\Lo$, over orthogonal joins that exist \emph{in $B$}.
The \emph{point evaluations} $\delta_\omega(A):=\mathbbm{1}[\omega\in A]$ are
$\sigma$-additive two-valued states; call these \emph{Dirac} and all others
\emph{non-Dirac}. For a finite $\perp$-closed sub-orthoposet $B\subseteq\Lo$ and a
two-valued state $s_0$ on $B$, $s_0$ \emph{extends} to $s$ on $\Lo$ if $s\restr B=s_0$.

\begin{definition}[$\sigma$-essential contextual state]\label{def:sigma-essential}
A \emph{$\sigma$-essential contextual state} on $\Lo$ is a two-valued state $s_0$
on a finite $\perp$-closed sub-orthoposet $B \subseteq \Lo$ that extends to
\emph{no} global $\sigma$-additive two-valued state on $\Lo$. A concrete
$\sigma$-OML $\Lo$ \emph{carries a $\sigma$-essential contextual state} (is a
\emph{witness}) if such an $s_0$ exists. We require $\Lo$ to be non-Boolean and
\emph{irreducible} (centre $\{\varnothing, \Omega\}$); see
Remark~\ref{rem:offcenter}.
\end{definition}

The phenomenon is a failure of $\sigma$-level compactness. Wright's
theorem~\cite{Wright1978} --- every two-valued state on a \emph{finite} OML is
finitely realised --- forbids any \emph{finite} witness, so the question is whether
a coherent finite pattern can fail to globalise once $\sigma$-additivity is imposed.
On a Boolean algebra it cannot (\S\ref{sec:boolean}); we isolate what must differ in
the non-distributive case, reduce to the irreducible core, and locate it.

\paragraph{Results.}
\S\ref{sec:boolean}: the Boolean baseline (Prop.~\ref{prop:boolean}, two
obstructions). \S\ref{sec:localization}: the \emph{Localization Theorem}
(Thm.~\ref{thm:localization}) --- a witness exists iff (i)~no Dirac and (ii)~no
non-Dirac $\sigma$-additive two-valued state extends $s_0$, with (i) freely
arrangeable, localising all weight to (ii), which strips to a bare set-theoretic
selection problem (Q.~\ref{q:bare}). \S\ref{sec:boundary}: the boundary map --- empty
above the Polish case, irreducible to Blecher--Weaver, irreducible to a measurable
cardinal. \S\ref{sec:open}: the open problem, its target sentence, and the constraints localising it.

\begin{remark}[Irreducibility is not vacuous]\label{rem:offcenter}
The carrier class is inhabited: the horizontal sum $MO_\kappa$ ($\kappa$ orthogonal
atom-pairs with $0,1$) is concrete, non-Boolean, and irreducible
(centre $\{\varnothing,\Omega\}$) for $\kappa\geq 2$~\cite{Kalmbach1983}, and
$\sigma$-complete for $\kappa=\omega$. Concreteness does not force a non-trivial
centre: by the Zierler--Schlessinger/Gudder criterion it is exactly order-determination
by two-valued states~\cite{Gudder1969,Harding2004}, unrelated to reducibility. ($MO_\kappa$
itself is excluded as a witness by Remark~\ref{rem:segregated}.)
\end{remark}


\section{The Boolean baseline}\label{sec:boolean}

On a Boolean $\sigma$-algebra, Definition~\ref{def:sigma-essential} is never
satisfied. The proof isolates two distinct obstructions a witness must overcome at
once; this is the baseline the non-distributive case must escape.

\begin{proposition}[No witness in the Boolean case]\label{prop:boolean}
Let $\Lo \subseteq \PO(\Omega)$ be a Boolean $\sigma$-algebra and $B \subseteq
\Lo$ a finite $\perp$-closed sub-orthoposet with a two-valued state $s_0$. Then
$s_0$ extends to a global $\sigma$-additive two-valued state on $\Lo$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $B_0 \subseteq \Lo$ be the finite Boolean subalgebra generated by $B$. Because
$\Lo$ is Boolean and $s_0$ is additive over the orthogonal joins in $B$, $s_0$ extends
to a (finitely additive) two-valued state on $B_0$: on a finite Boolean algebra such a
state is determined by, and concentrates on, a single atom $a \in B_0$ --- the meet of
the $s_0$-true generators. As $a$ is a nonzero element of the concrete algebra $B_0$,
it is a nonempty subset of $\Omega$, and
\[
  K(s_0) := \bigcap\{A \in B : s_0(A) = 1\} \;\supseteq\; a \;\neq\; \varnothing
\]
(we call $K(s_0)$ the \emph{kernel} of $s_0$). Any $\omega \in K(s_0)$ satisfies
$\delta_\omega \restr B = s_0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:dirac-iff}, and $\delta_\omega$ is
$\sigma$-additive. So $s_0$ globalises --- and on a concrete Boolean $\sigma$-algebra
it does so by a Dirac, the case $K(s_0) = \varnothing$ never arising. The extension to
$B_0$ is exactly where distributivity is used; it is unavailable on a non-Boolean
carrier, where the $s_0$-true generators may have empty meet.
\end{proof}

Two obstructions combine, both of which a witness must defeat:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
  \item \emph{(used above)} compatible events of $B$ generate a finite Boolean algebra
    whose $s_0$-carrying atom is a nonempty common point. A witness breaks this --- the
    events lie in incompatible blocks with empty meet, so no Dirac extends $s_0$.
  \item \emph{(complementary)} away from a measurable cardinal, every global
    $\sigma$-additive two-valued state on a Boolean $\sigma$-algebra is
    Dirac~\cite{Sikorski1964,Jech2003}; so even where (a) fails, no \emph{non}-Dirac
    state repairs the gap on a Boolean carrier.
\end{enumerate}
The two pull against each other: breaking (a) requires non-distributivity, which is
precisely what makes (b) escapable by admitting non-Dirac global states. A witness must
break (a) while leaving no such repair available.


\section{The Localization Theorem}\label{sec:localization}

When a Dirac extends $s_0$ is elementary.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:dirac-iff}
Let $B \subseteq \Lo$ be a finite $\perp$-closed sub-orthoposet with two-valued
state $s_0$. A point evaluation $\delta_\omega$ satisfies $\delta_\omega\restr B
= s_0$ iff $\omega \in K(s_0) = \bigcap\{A \in B : s_0(A) = 1\}$. Consequently no
Dirac extends $s_0$ iff $K(s_0) = \varnothing$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\delta_\omega\restr B = s_0$ means $\omega \in A \iff s_0(A)=1$ for every
$A \in B$. The forward constraints place $\omega \in \bigcap\{A : s_0(A)=1\}$. For
a false $A$ (i.e.\ $s_0(A)=0$), $\perp$-closure gives $A\orth \in B$, and
additivity gives $s_0(A\orth) = 1 - s_0(A) = 1$; so $A\orth$ is one of the true
elements and the constraint $\omega \notin A$ is already $\omega \in A\orth$,
subsumed in $K(s_0)$. Thus the false constraints add nothing, and
$\delta_\omega \restr B = s_0 \iff \omega \in K(s_0)$. Conversely any
$\omega \in K(s_0)$ works: true $A$ give $\omega\in A$, false $A$ give
$\omega \in A\orth$ hence $\omega\notin A$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Localization]\label{thm:localization}
Let $\Lo$ be a concrete $\sigma$-OML, $B \subseteq \Lo$ a finite $\perp$-closed
sub-orthoposet, and $s_0$ a two-valued state on $B$. Then $s_0$ is a
$\sigma$-essential contextual state iff
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
  \item $K(s_0) = \varnothing$ \textup{(}no point evaluation extends
    $s_0$\textup{)}, and
  \item no \emph{non-Dirac} $\sigma$-additive two-valued state on $\Lo$ extends
    $s_0$.
\end{enumerate}
Moreover clause \textup{(i)} is freely arrangeable: there is a concrete
$\sigma$-OML and an $s_0$ with $K(s_0)=\varnothing$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Dirac/non-Dirac dichotomy on global $\sigma$-additive two-valued states is
exhaustive, so
\[
  (\text{no extension of } s_0)
  \;\iff\;
  (\text{no Dirac extends } s_0) \;\wedge\; (\text{no non-Dirac extends } s_0).
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:dirac-iff} the first conjunct is (i); the second is (ii). For
arrangeability of (i), the Navara--Pt\'ak construction
(Example~\ref{ex:navara-ptak}) realises $K(s_0)=\varnothing$ explicitly.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope: a decomposition, not a reduction]\label{rem:localization-scope}
The equivalence is a decomposition, not a reduction to a separate named problem:
the Dirac/non-Dirac split being exhaustive, it merely unfolds the definition of
witness. Its content is that clause (i) is \emph{freely arrangeable}, so all weight
of Definition~\ref{def:sigma-essential} sits in clause (ii) --- the
\emph{$\sigma$-point-selection core}, the subject of the remainder.
\end{remark}

The Navara--Pt\'ak construction both supplies clause (i) and shows (ii) is binding.

\begin{example}[Navara--Pt\'ak~\cite{NavaraPtak1983}]\label{ex:navara-ptak}
Let $\Omega = \bbQ_0 \times \bbQ_0$ with $\bbQ_0 = \bbQ \cap (0,1)$, and let
$f,g\colon \Omega \to \bbR$ be the coordinate projections. Let $\Lo =
\Lo_{f,g}$ be the least $\sigma$-class containing the inverse images of Borel
sets under $f$ and $g$. Set $C_f = f^{-1}(\{1/3\})$, $C_g = g^{-1}(\{1/2\})$,
$C_{f+g} = (f+g)^{-1}(\{1/2\})$. Define $m(A) = 1$ iff $A$ contains one of
$C_f, C_g, C_{f+g}$. Navara and Pt\'ak show $m$ is a (two-valued, $\sigma$-additive)
state on $\Lo$, and that since $C_f \cap C_g \cap C_{f+g} = \varnothing$ the state
$m$ is \emph{not} concentrated at any point.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[The example is not itself a witness]\label{rem:np-builds-rescuer}
Let $B$ be the $\perp$-closure of $\{C_f, C_g, C_{f+g}\}$ and let $s_0$ be the pattern
assigning each of the three the value $1$. Since $C_f \cap C_g \cap C_{f+g} =
\varnothing$, we have $K(s_0) = \varnothing$, so clause (i) holds --- no point
evaluation extends $s_0$. Yet the Navara--Pt\'ak state satisfies $m \restr B = s_0$
and is non-Dirac and $\sigma$-additive, so clause (ii) \emph{fails} and $s_0$ is not
$\sigma$-essential: the construction supplies its own rescuer, so clause (i) cannot
suffice alone. The repair is available because $\Lo_{f,g}$ is generated by two
\emph{compatible} (commuting) observables --- Boolean enough to support a non-Dirac
global state through the point-level gap. A witness requires the opposite: a carrier on
which no non-Dirac state repairs the gap.
\end{remark}


Stripped of lattice scaffolding, this core --- clause (ii) --- is a selection problem.
Each block is a maximal compatible context, equivalently a countable partition
$\Pi_\alpha$ of $\Omega$ into elements of $\Lo$; a two-valued state selects one cell
$c_\alpha\in\Pi_\alpha$ per block, subject to coherence, $\sigma$-additivity, and
non-principality.

\begin{question}[$\sigma$-point-selection, bare form]\label{q:bare}
Given a set $\Omega$ and a family $\{\Pi_\alpha\}$ of countable partitions of
$\Omega$ (the blocks of a concrete $\sigma$-OML, pairwise overlapping in their
common refinements), does there exist a selection assigning to each $\Pi_\alpha$
one cell $c_\alpha \in \Pi_\alpha$ such that
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
  \item \textup{(coherence)} the selections agree on events common to several
    blocks;
  \item \textup{($\sigma$-additivity)} the induced $\{0,1\}$-valuation commutes
    with countable disjoint unions;
  \item \textup{(non-principality)} there is no $\omega_0 \in \Omega$ with
    $c_\alpha = $ the cell of $\Pi_\alpha$ containing $\omega_0$ for all $\alpha$;
  \item \textup{(prescription)} the selection realises a prescribed finite
    pattern that no single point realises?
\end{enumerate}
\end{question}

A witness exists iff Question~\ref{q:bare} has a positive answer for some admissible
carrier. Two features of the bare form, obscured in lattice language, fix its position:
its distance from a measurable cardinal, and its place within contextuality.

\begin{remark}[The Boolean shadow is a measurable cardinal; the question is not]\label{rem:not-measurable}
For a \emph{compatible} block family (single Boolean base),
\[
  \begin{aligned}
    &\text{coherent non-principal } \sigma\text{-additive } \{0,1\}\text{-selection}\\
    {}={}&\text{non-principal countably-complete ultrafilter}
    \;=\; \text{measurable cardinal}
  \end{aligned}
\]
\cite{Jech2003}. Question~\ref{q:bare} imposes coherence (a) across
\emph{incompatible, mutually non-distributive} blocks, strictly stronger than an
ultrafilter on any single block. The Boolean shadow reduces to a measurable cardinal;
the non-distributive coherence is exactly what the reduction cannot cross
(\S\ref{sec:boundary}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Relation to sheaf-theoretic contextuality]\label{rem:sheaf}
Question~\ref{q:bare} asserts that locally coherent $\{0,1\}$-valuations admit no
\emph{global section} --- contextuality in the sense of
Abramsky--Brandenburger~\cite{AbramskyBrandenburger2011}, $\sigma$-additive
form~\cite{AbramskyBarbosaEtAl2022}. The framing is standard; what is open is the
conjunction the literature does not treat together: countable additivity $+$ a
non-Hilbert (abstractly concrete) carrier $+$ a $\{0,1\}$-valued selection.
\end{remark}


\section{The boundary map}\label{sec:boundary}

We locate Question~\ref{q:bare} between an upper boundary where it is known empty and
the lower routes that fail to settle it.

The upper boundary is the Polish-representable case. Derr and
Williamson~\cite{DerrWilliamson2023} prove a $\sigma$-extension
criterion for coherent probabilities on pre-Dynkin systems: when the carrier is
\emph{Polish-representable} --- $\Omega$ Polish, the generated $\sigma$-class
equal to the Borel $\sigma$-algebra, with inner-regular blocks --- finite
coherence implies $\sigma$-extendability (their Theorem~D.6, via
Maharam~\cite{Maharam1972}). Specialised to two-valued states, this says that on a
Polish-representable carrier every finitely coherent $s_0$ globalises: \emph{no
$\sigma$-essential contextual state exists}. A witness, if one exists, must therefore
live on a carrier that is \emph{not} Polish-representable.

A Hilbert-space analogue exists, but does not transfer down to the concrete case. On
the projection lattice of $B(\ell^2(\kappa))$, Blecher--Weaver~\cite{BlecherWeaver2017}
show a singular countably additive \emph{pure} state exists iff $\kappa$ is Ulam
measurable --- the nearest positive precedent that countable additivity on a
non-distributive $\sigma$-complete OML \emph{can} carry set-theoretic strength. It does
not settle Question~\ref{q:bare}; three obstructions point the same way.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)},leftmargin=*]
  \item \emph{Wrong carrier.} For $\dim H\geq 3$ the projection lattice of $B(H)$
    admits \emph{no} two-valued state (Kochen--Specker~\cite{KochenSpecker1967}), so
    it is not concrete; the Blecher--Weaver dichotomy concerns \emph{pure} states, not
    two-valued ones, which are absent there by Gleason~\cite{Gleason1957}.
  \item \emph{Masa transfer fails.} Carrying a state-existence result to an abelian
    subalgebra means factoring through a masa; for the pure two-valued case this
    fails --- Akemann--Weaver~\cite{AkemannWeaver2008} give a pure state on $B(H)$
    multiplicative on no masa, and Blecher--Weaver use Marcus--Spielman--Srivastava
    paving for exactly this reason.
  \item \emph{The consumed object is structurally absent.} Blecher--Weaver extract
    their cardinal from a genuine ultrafilter on the diagonal masa
    $\ell^\infty(\kappa)$ (projections $=$ subsets, intersection-closed). On a
    concrete non-Boolean $\sigma$-OML, $\mathcal{F}_s := \{A\in\Lo : s(A)=1\}$ is an
    ultrafilter for \emph{no} two-valued state $s$ --- not even a filter: meet-closure
    fails already for $\delta_\omega$, since in any concrete $MO_2$ the atoms $a,b$
    have $a\cap b\neq\varnothing$ (else orthogonal) yet $a\wedge b=0$, so
    \[
      \delta_\omega(a)=\delta_\omega(b)=1, \qquad \delta_\omega(a\wedge b)=0 .
    \]
    The countably-complete ultrafilter an Ulam-style argument would consume is absent
    from the carrier --- the same non-distributivity (lattice meet $\neq$ set
    intersection), one level down.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{remark}[Strength of the claim]\label{rem:no-bridge}
Remark~\ref{rem:not-measurable} and (1)--(3) are \emph{cited facts assembled}, not a
non-reducibility theorem in a fixed reduction calculus. The claim is only that the two
obvious bridges --- ``measurable cardinal'' and ``transfer from Blecher--Weaver via a
masa'' --- do not close Question~\ref{q:bare}, the obstruction in each being the same
non-distributivity. This is what leaves the question open rather than a corollary.
\end{remark}

The remaining route from below, faithful representation, is barred by the same
obstruction. Routing Question~\ref{q:bare} through a faithful $\sigma$-tribe
representation (embedding $\Lo$ into a $\sigma$-algebra of sets, so two-valued states
become point evaluations) is unavailable: a faithful tribe-of-sets representation forces
distributivity, hence Booleanness (the ``Floor''~\cite{PtakPulmannova1991}).
$\sigma$-Loomis--Sikorski holds under the Riesz Decomposition Property
($\sigma$-complete MV-algebras, RDP effect algebras~\cite{Dvurecenskij2000}), but
OMLs lack RDP ($MO_2$ witnesses), and the available image is only $\sigma$-epimorphic,
not point-separating. The trivialising route is blocked, again non-distributively.

\begin{remark}[Segregated carriers do not witness]\label{rem:segregated}
A carrier is \emph{segregated} when its contextuality is carried by a central
Boolean factor --- e.g.\ $MO_\omega$ or the product $\prod_n MO_2$, whose
$\sigma$-additive two-valued states all concentrate at points. On such carriers
every finitely coherent $s_0$ is the restriction of a Dirac, so clause (ii) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:localization} fails and there is no witness. A witness must be
\emph{non-segregated}: the incompatibility must be irreducible, not routed through
a central partition. This is why Definition~\ref{def:sigma-essential} requires
irreducibility, and it sharpens the search for a carrier to the non-segregated,
non-Boolean, non-Polish-representable regime.
\end{remark}


\section{The open problem}\label{sec:open}

Assembling \S\ref{sec:localization}--\S\ref{sec:boundary}, a $\sigma$-essential
contextual state exists iff Question~\ref{q:bare} has a positive answer on a carrier
that is concrete, $\sigma$-complete, non-Boolean, irreducible, non-segregated, and
non-Polish-representable. The boundary map of \S\ref{sec:boundary} settles the answer
in no direction: none of the three natural bridges --- the Blecher--Weaver dichotomy,
a measurable cardinal, or a faithful representation --- closes it.

\begin{question}[Main]\label{q:main}
Does there exist, in ZFC or under a large-cardinal hypothesis, a concrete
$\sigma$-complete non-Boolean irreducible OML carrying a $\sigma$-essential
contextual state? Equivalently, is the existence of a non-principal coherent
$\sigma$-additive $\{0,1\}$-selection across overlapping countable partitions
(Question~\ref{q:bare}, non-segregated case) provable, refutable, or independent?
\end{question}

A resolution takes one of three forms, each constrained above:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \emph{positive} --- a carrier in the non-Polish, non-segregated regime;
    necessarily uncountably generated and non-compositional
    (Rems.~\ref{rem:not-measurable}, \ref{rem:no-bridge});
  \item \emph{negative} --- extending Derr--Williamson's Polish emptiness past its
    topological hypotheses to all concrete $\sigma$-OMLs;
  \item \emph{independence} --- two models, with and without the selection; all known
    precedents (von Neumann--Maharam~\cite{BalcarJechPazak2005}, real-valued
    measurables~\cite{Fremlin}) are real-valued and positive, leaving the two-valued
    non-distributive case open.
\end{itemize}

To state the target precisely, write $\mathrm{St}(B)$ for the two-valued states on a
sub-orthoposet $B$, $\mathrm{St}_\sigma(\Lo)$ for the global $\sigma$-additive
two-valued states on $\Lo$, $\mathrm{Fin}_\perp(\Lo)$ for the finite $\perp$-closed
sub-orthoposets, and $\mathrm{Adm}$ for the admissible carriers (concrete,
$\sigma$-complete, non-Boolean, irreducible). A pattern $s\in\mathrm{St}(B)$ is
realised by $\mu$ when $\mu\restr B=s$. The \emph{$\sigma$-additive lifting} property
and its negation are
\begin{align*}
  \Phi(\Lo)
    &:\equiv\;
    \forall\, B \in \mathrm{Fin}_\perp(\Lo)\ \forall\, s \in \mathrm{St}(B)\
    \exists\, \mu \in \mathrm{St}_\sigma(\Lo)\ \ \mu \restr B = s, \\
  \neg\Phi(\Lo)
    &\equiv\;
    \exists\, B\ \exists\, s \in \mathrm{St}(B)\
    \forall\, \mu \in \mathrm{St}_\sigma(\Lo)\ \ \mu \restr B \neq s,
\end{align*}
the latter asserting a $\sigma$-essential contextual state
(Def.~\ref{def:sigma-essential}, Thm.~\ref{thm:localization}). The target sentence is
\[
  \Psi \;:\equiv\; \exists\, \Lo \in \mathrm{Adm}\ \neg\Phi(\Lo)
  \;\equiv\;
  \exists\, \Lo \in \mathrm{Adm}\ \exists\, B\ \exists\, s\
  \forall\, \mu \in \mathrm{St}_\sigma(\Lo)\ \ \mu \restr B \neq s .
\]
The leading $\exists\,\Lo$ ranges over families of subsets of $\Omega$, not over
$\omega$; by Proposition~\ref{prop:boolean} the witness is non-distributive. It is this
second-order existential over an unbounded carrier, not the inner arithmetic, that
places $\Psi$ beyond any single combinatorial principle.

\begin{remark}[Constraints on a witness]\label{rem:technology}
Each known class of carrier satisfies $\Phi$, so a witness avoids all three:
\begin{align*}
  \Lo \text{ Boolean} &\implies \Phi(\Lo)
    &&\text{(Prop.~\ref{prop:boolean});}\\
  \Lo \text{ Polish-representable} &\implies \Phi(\Lo)
    &&\text{(Derr--Williamson);}\\
  \Lo \text{ segregated (central contextuality)} &\implies \Phi(\Lo)
    &&\text{(Rem.~\ref{rem:segregated}).}
\end{align*}
Hence a witness is non-Boolean, non-Polish-representable, irreducibly non-central:
uncountably generated, non-standard-Borel, genuinely non-distributive. Two facts close
the standard methods:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
  \item \emph{Forcing.} A constructible carrier is Polish-representable, so satisfies
    $\Phi$; and forcing preserves consistency strength
    (L\'evy--Solovay~\cite{LevySolovay1967}). It frames independence but cannot produce
    the object.
  \item \emph{$\sigma$-Loomis--Sikorski.} The theorem holds only under RDP
    \cite{Dvurecenskij2000}, and a lattice effect algebra has RDP iff it is an
    MV-algebra; an all-sharp non-Boolean OML lies in the complement, where no
    representation theorem is known to hold or fail.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Consistency strength of $\Psi$]\label{rem:strength}
On a Boolean base the object of Question~\ref{q:bare} is a non-principal
countably-complete ultrafilter---a measurable cardinal; on $B(\ell^2(\kappa))$ the
analogue has exactly Ulam-measurable strength~\cite{BlecherWeaver2017}. Neither bound
survives for $\Psi$:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \emph{Lower bound fails.} ``No measurable $\Rightarrow$ every $\sigma$-additive
    two-valued state is Dirac $\Rightarrow \neg\Psi$'' assumes an extension theorem for
    $\sigma$-\emph{algebras}, not $\sigma$-\emph{classes}; it is false in ZFC by the
    non-Dirac Navara--Pt\'ak state (Example~\ref{ex:navara-ptak}). Granting the premise
    would make the non-Dirac clause vacuous, favouring $\Psi$.
  \item \emph{Upper bound unearned.} $\Psi$ governs two-valued selections, outside the
    real-valued-measurable machinery; the Blecher--Weaver mechanism does not transfer
    (\S\ref{sec:boundary}).
\end{itemize}
The strength of $\Psi$ is open in both directions, not conjecturally measurable.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Localization of the open problem]\label{rem:frontier}
No candidate field supplies a construction for the constrained carrier:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item non-separable / Maharam measure algebras: Boolean and real-valued;
  \item descriptive no-section phenomena (non-smooth equivalence relations, failed
    co-analytic uniformization, turbulence): standard-Borel carriers, real-valued
    obstructions;
  \item the Dynkin-system / concrete-logic lineage: finitary, or Polish hence
    globalising.
\end{itemize}
The one structural rhyme is Glimm--Effros: non-smoothness of a countable Borel
equivalence relation $\Leftrightarrow$ absence of a two-valued section. Transporting it
onto a Gudder concrete logic would supply the missing principle, but no such transport
appears in the literature. The witness question is therefore neither closed by an
impossibility theorem nor reachable by a borrowed construction; its resolution requires
a new object in the non-Polish, non-distributive regime.
\end{remark}

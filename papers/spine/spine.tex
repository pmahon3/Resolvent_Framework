\title{The Price of Realism:\\
A Filtration of the State Space and Three Studies of Its Transitions}
\author{Patrick S.\ Mahon\thanks{%
  Research Computing Group, Simon Fraser University, Burnaby, BC, Canada.
  \texttt{pmahon@sfu.ca}}}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Finitely additive coherence is what observation natively supplies;
$\sigma$-additivity and value-definiteness are further \emph{representation
properties} --- signatures of a world, not of the data. This umbrella note reads
three companion papers as one classification of the price of those signatures.
The organising object is a filtration of the state space,
$\Sf \supseteq \Ssig \supseteq \Sdfsig$, whose three transitions --- \emph{pasting},
\emph{regularity}, \emph{sharpness} --- are introduced in the companion paper on
distributivity and realism. That paper studies the sharpness transition
(distributivity versus value-definiteness, Kochen--Specker); the paper on a
$\sigma$-essential contextual state studies the regularity transition (finite
versus countable additivity, resolved by a witness constructed in ZFC at
$\omega_1$); and the paper on commensurability of observation protocols studies
the pasting transition (local coherence versus global realisability, with a
taming catalogue and an odd-ring separating witness). We tabulate the three
transitions against the coordinate in which each locates its boundary ---
algebraic, set-theoretic, dynamical --- and record what is genuinely common: the
observer-first reading, the fact that the three boundaries are different, and a
method (pre-registered predictions, witness-grade verdicts, machine-checked
lemmas). The two bridging claims are settled here ---
one negatively (the coherence notions form a strictly separated, witness-inhabited
ladder rather than one notion), one positively (a compactness quarantine sorts the
two constructed witnesses into provably disjoint regimes, upgrading the tabulation
to a classification). A prior-art/type audit refutes the former three-way
latticehood conjecture: complete Hilbert lattices fail sharpness. The surviving
open theory question is the regularity predicate $\Phi$ for concrete
$\sigma$-complete OMLs. This note references the three papers; it does not absorb
them.
\end{abstract}

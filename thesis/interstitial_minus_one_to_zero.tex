%% Interstitial: Paper -1 -> Paper 0
%% Philosophical and conceptual bridge: discriminability to probability.

Chapter~\ref{chap:paper-minus-one} establishes the structural conditions under which
a charge on a Boolean algebra extends to a $\sigma$-additive measure.  The answer ---
collective exhaustion --- is a condition on the family of charges, not on the Boolean
algebras themselves.  This interstitial draws out the conceptual significance of that
result before turning to the global extension question of Chapter~\ref{chap:paper-zero}.

\medskip

\paragraph{The gap as horizon.}
The gap $\sigma(\mathcal{E}_Q) \setminus \mathcal{E}_Q$ consists of events reachable
by countable Boolean operations on $\mathcal{E}_Q$ but not themselves in
$\mathcal{E}_Q$.  The incompleteness theorem shows this gap is not a deficiency: any
query admitting genuine refinement must have a nonempty gap.  Under Stone duality,
$\mathcal{E}_Q$ corresponds to the clopen algebra of the Stone space $S(\mathcal{E}_Q)$,
and $\sigma(\mathcal{E}_Q)$ to its Baire $\sigma$-algebra.  The gap is the collection
of Baire sets that are not clopen --- the limit structure of the Stone space that lies
beyond the clopen topology.  A query with no gap would correspond to a Stone space
whose Baire $\sigma$-algebra coincides with the clopen algebra: a highly degenerate
space admitting no interesting limit structure.  The gap is therefore the space into
which refinements reach; its presence is what makes refinement meaningful.

\paragraph{The $\sigma$-algebra as forced structure.}
The extension criterion (Theorem~\ref{thm:extension-criterion} of
Chapter~\ref{chap:paper-minus-one}) says that $\sigma(\mathcal{E}_Q)$ enters the
picture not as a postulate but as the minimal structure that must be present for
$\ell_Q$ to be extendable at all.  A charge that extends to $\sigma(\mathcal{E}_Q)$
is one that already ``knows about'' the gap --- it assigns vanishing weight to
sequences converging to events it cannot directly represent.  The $\sigma$-algebra is
the closure forced by that knowledge.

\paragraph{Collective exhaustion and the question of origin.}
The main theorem of Chapter~\ref{chap:paper-minus-one} establishes that collective
exhaustion is precisely the valuation-layer condition equivalent to $\sigma$-additive
extensibility.  The result separates two questions that are easily conflated: whether
the \emph{structural} conditions on a query system (directedness, compatibility) force
$\sigma$-additivity, and whether the \emph{valuations} carried by the system do.  The
former is impossible (the finite-cofinite counterexample); the latter is characterised
exactly (collective exhaustion).  The $\sigma$-algebra is not derivable from the index
structure of the query system alone, nor is it a free assumption: it is the content of
a specific and checkable condition on the family of charges.

One can ask, in a more philosophical register, what it means for a system to satisfy
collective exhaustion.  The condition says that no level can persistently assign mass
to events that vanish globally: every convergence in the system is witnessed at some
finer level.  In this sense collective exhaustion is the mathematical expression of
the idea that the system \emph{models a coherent world} --- one in which events that
become empty do so in a way that is visible from within the system.  A system failing
collective exhaustion contains an observer who permanently assigns positive probability
to events the system as a whole regards as impossible.  The $\sigma$-algebra, then, is
the structure that a coherent world forces on the local event languages of its
observers.

The projective limit $\Omega = \varprojlim O_Q$ plays a role analogous to what Kant
called a \emph{regulative ideal}: it is not itself one of the queries in the system,
and no single observer occupies it, but it is the standard against which the
coherence of the system is measured.  Continuity at $\emptyset$ for $\ell_Q$ is the
local expression of a global orientation toward $\Omega$: the charge at level $Q$
must behave as if $\Omega$ exists, even though $Q$ cannot see it directly.  Collective
exhaustion makes this requirement non-vacuous: it is not a private act of faith by
each individual observer but a condition verifiable from within the system.

\medskip

\paragraph{From per-level extensions to a global measure.}
Chapter~\ref{chap:paper-minus-one} gives per-level extensions $\mu_Q$ on
$\sigma(\mathcal{E}_Q)$ for each $Q$, derived from collective exhaustion.  The next
question is whether these assemble into a single $\sigma$-additive measure on
$\Omega$.  This is strictly harder: it requires the local extensions to concentrate
mass on $\Omega$ rather than dispersing it into the ambient product $\prod_Q O_Q$.
Sequential upper-directedness --- the condition that every sequence of queries has a
common refinement --- is the structural hypothesis that makes global assembly possible.
It plays no role in the per-level result but is essential for the projective limit.

Chapter~\ref{chap:paper-zero} addresses this global assembly problem directly.
Taking the per-level $\sigma$-additive extensions as input --- supplied by
Chapter~\ref{chap:paper-minus-one} via collective exhaustion --- it assembles them
into a global probability measure on $(\Omega, \sigma(\mathcal{Q}))$ using a
realizability condition on $\Omega$: that every coherent assignment of outcomes
across the query system is realised by a point of $\Omega$.  No topology on the
outcome spaces is required.  The result is the program's central construction: from
observable discriminations, forced through collective exhaustion and realizability,
a canonical probability space emerges.

\paragraph{The realizability condition.}
The one hypothesis that collective exhaustion does not supply is
\emph{realizability}: that every local outcome at every query level is realized by
some globally coherent point of $\Omega = \varprojlim O_Q$.  Realizability is
independent of sequential upper-directedness, compatible marginals, and collective
exhaustion --- a system can satisfy all three and fail realizability, as the following
shows: take a two-level system with outcome spaces $O_0 = \{a,b,c\}$,
$O_1 = \{x,y\}$, and refinement map $\pi(x)=a$, $\pi(y)=b$.  The projective limit
$\Omega = \{(a,x),(b,y)\}$ does not realise outcome $c\in O_0$, yet all other
conditions hold trivially.

The obstruction is exactly non-surjectivity of the refinement map: this is a
\emph{necessary} condition for realizability.  Surjectivity of all refinement maps
is not, however, abstractly sufficient: constructing a coherent inverse-limit point
from local preimages requires reconciling infinitely many choices simultaneously,
which in general requires compactness of the outcome spaces (the inverse limit theorem
for compact spaces with surjective bonding maps).  For the delay query systems of
Chapter~\ref{chap:paper-three}, realizability is verified directly by lifting any
target outcome via an ambient stream; for finite-outcome systems with surjective
refinement maps, it follows from compactness of finite spaces.  In both cases
realizability is a structural consequence of the system's architecture, not an
independent assumption.

The passage from Chapter~\ref{chap:paper-minus-one} to Chapter~\ref{chap:paper-zero}
is therefore the passage from local to global: from the characterisation of
$\sigma$-additivity at each level to the construction of a single probability measure
on the world the system describes.

\paragraph{The residual freedom and the through-line.}
What $P$ does not determine, once it exists, is the \emph{temporal} structure of the
observer's queries.  The canonical probability space $(\Omega, \sigma(\mathcal{Q}), P)$
encodes the full statistical structure of the observable interface --- but it says nothing
about which aspect of that structure is relevant for predicting the future, and through
what minimal lens.  That question belongs to Chapter~\ref{chap:paper-one}.

The program proceeds by the same logic at each subsequent step: a freedom that appears
to survive is revealed to be illusory.  At each stage, what appears to be a construction
turns out to be a constraint, and what appears to be an assumption turns out to be a
consequence.  The through-line from here to Chapter~\ref{chap:paper-three} is the
progressive elimination of residual degrees of freedom: predictive structure, temporal
coherence, the form of the queries, and the minimal sufficient window are each, in turn,
shown to be forced.
